FastBERT: Self-Distilling BERT with Adaptive Inference Time

Architectures AIMech:FastBERTSelfDistillingBERTWithAdaptiveInferenceTime DRAFT
Aliases: FastBERT, FastBERT Self-Distilling Adaptive-Inference Transformer, Self-Distilling BERT with Normalized-Entropy Early Exit
A sample-wise adaptive Transformer architecture and three-stage training paradigm that appends a teacher classifier to the final layer L-1 and lightweight student branch classifiers (each comprising a 768->128 projection, 128-dim self-attention, 128->128 dense layer, and N-class softmax) to intermediate layers 0..L-2, fine-tunes the backbone and teacher classifier on task labels with branches disabled, freezes the backbone and teacher to self-distill all L-1 student classifiers by minimizing sum_{i=0}^{L-2} D_KL(p_{s_i}, p_t), and terminates inference for any input sample at layer i when its normalized prediction entropy Uncertainty = (sum_{c=1}^N p_{s_i}(c) log p_{s_i}(c)) / log(1/N) falls below a tunable threshold Speed in [0, 1].
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Introduced by Weijie Liu, Peng Zhou, Zhe Zhao, Zhiruo Wang, Haotang Deng, and Qi Ju (ACL 2020, pp. 6035-6044, arXiv:2004.02178, doi:10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.537). Distinct from cross-model knowledge distillation methods (DistilBERT Round 803, TinyBERT Round 800, MobileBERT Round 804) that execute a fixed number of layers for every input, and from Patience-Based Early Exit (PABEE, Round 808), which jointly trains linear internal classifiers and exits based on cross-layer prediction agreement patience rather than frozen-backbone branch self-distillation and single-layer normalized entropy thresholding.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #r807-claim-decoupled-branch-self-distillation
To prevent intermediate exit branches from corrupting backbone representations during supervised fine-tuning, FastBERT first fine-tunes the L-layer Transformer backbone and top-layer teacher classifier p_t = Teacher_Classifier(h_{L-1}) (Eq. 3) with all student branches disabled, and then freezes the backbone and teacher classifier while training the L-1 student branch classifiers p_{s_i} = Student_Classifier_i(h_i) (Eq. 4) on labeled or unlabeled data by minimizing the total KL-divergence self-distillation loss Loss(p_{s_0}, ..., p_{s_{L-2}}, p_t) = sum_{i=0}^{L-2} D_KL(p_{s_i}, p_t) (Eqs. 5-6).

  • supports Source: FastBERT: a Self-distilling BERT with Adaptive Inference Time · Locator: self_distillation_loss verification in main()
Mechanism #r807-claim-normalized-entropy-uncertainty-and-luha-exit
During adaptive inference, FastBERT evaluates the normalized Shannon entropy Uncertainty = (sum_{i=1}^N p_s(i) log p_s(i)) / log(1/N) in [0, 1] (Eq. 7) at each executed layer's student classifier and halts inference immediately whenever Uncertainty < Speed (Hypothesis 1, LUHA: 'the Lower the Uncertainty, the Higher the Accuracy'), incurring only 46.1M FLOPs per student classifier evaluation compared to 1809.9M FLOPs per Transformer block.
  • supports Source: FastBERT: a Self-distilling BERT with Adaptive Inference Time · Locator: Section 3.3 (Adaptive inference, Equation 7 & Table 1), Page 4
  • supports Source: FastBERT: a Self-distilling BERT with Adaptive Inference Time · Locator: normalized_entropy_uncertainty and sample_adaptive_flops verification in main()
Application #r807-claim-tunable-speedup-across-twelve-datasets
Evaluated across six Chinese datasets (ChnSentiCorp, Book review, Shopping review, LCQMC, Weibo, THUCNews) and six English datasets (AG News, Amazon Full, DBpedia, Yahoo Answers, Yelp Full, Yelp Polarity), FastBERT achieves a continuously tunable 1x to 12x FLOPs speedup over 12-layer BERT by varying Speed in [0.1, 0.8], delivering 2x to 5x speedup without accuracy loss at Speed = 0.1 and outperforming 6-layer, 3-layer, and 1-layer DistilBERT models at matched speedup ratios.
  • supports Source: FastBERT: a Self-distilling BERT with Adaptive Inference Time · Locator: Section 4 (Experimental results, Tables 2-3 & Figures 3-7), Pages 5-8
Limitation #r807-claim-classification-entropy-and-nonlinear-speed-curve-limitation
Because FastBERT's Uncertainty criterion (Eq. 7) is defined as Shannon entropy normalized by log(1/N) over N discrete class probabilities, it applies directly only to classification tasks with N >= 2 classes (not continuous regression), and the relationship between the threshold Speed and the realized computational Speedup ratio is non-linear and dataset-dependent.
  • supports Source: FastBERT: a Self-distilling BERT with Adaptive Inference Time · Locator: Section 3.3 (Equation 7), Figure 3(b)/(e), and Section 6 (Future work: 'linearizing the Speed-Speedup curve'), Pages 4, 6, & 9

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The normalized entropy Uncertainty criterion requires a discrete N-class probability distribution (N >= 2) and does not directly support continuous regression tasks.
  • The mapping from the user-specified threshold Speed in [0, 1] to the actual FLOPs speedup factor is non-linear and varies with dataset complexity.
