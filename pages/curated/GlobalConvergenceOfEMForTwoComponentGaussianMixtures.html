Global Convergence and Stationary-Point Characterization of EM for Two-Component Gaussian Mixtures

Mathematical Methods AIMech:GlobalConvergenceOfEMForTwoComponentGaussianMixtures DRAFT
Aliases: Global Convergence of EM for Two-Gaussian Mixtures, Xu-Hsu-Maleki Global EM Trajectory and Saddle-Hyperplane Analysis, Two-Gaussian Mixture EM Global Basin and Angle Monotonicity
A global dynamical and statistical consistency analysis (introduced by Ji Xu, Daniel J. Hsu, and Arian Maleki in 2016) of the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm for balanced two-component Gaussian mixtures $0.5\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_1^\star,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}) + 0.5\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_2^\star,\boldsymbol{\Sigma})$ with known covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ and arbitrary non-zero separation $\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star = (\boldsymbol{\mu}_2^\star - \boldsymbol{\mu}_1^\star)/2 \ne \mathbf{0}$. Re-parameterizing the iterates by the mean-shift error $\boldsymbol{a}^{\langle t\rangle} = \frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_1^{\langle t\rangle} + \boldsymbol{\mu}_2^{\langle t\rangle}}{2} - \frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_1^\star + \boldsymbol{\mu}_2^\star}{2}$ and half-separation $\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle t\rangle} = \frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_2^{\langle t\rangle} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_1^{\langle t\rangle}}{2}$ (Equation 11), Lemmas 6–8 prove that all population EM updates lie in the two-dimensional subspace $\mathrm{span}(\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle 0\rangle},\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star)$ and that the angle $\beta^{\langle t\rangle} = \arccos(\frac{\langle\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle t\rangle},\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star\rangle}{\|\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle t\rangle}\|\|\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star\|})$ decreases monotonically: (1) for any initialization outside the measure-zero orthogonal hyperplane $\langle\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle 0\rangle},\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star\rangle \ne 0$, Theorems 1, 3, and 4 prove linear contraction $\sin(\beta^{\langle t+1\rangle}) \le \kappa_\beta^t\sin(\beta^{\langle 0\rangle})$, $\|\boldsymbol{a}^{\langle t+1\rangle}\|^2 \le \kappa_a^2\|\boldsymbol{a}^{\langle t\rangle}\|^2 + \frac{\|\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star\|^2\sin^2(\beta^{\langle t\rangle})}{4}$, and $\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle t\rangle} \to \mathrm{sgn}(\langle\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle 0\rangle},\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star\rangle)\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star$; (2) if $\langle\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle 0\rangle},\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star\rangle = 0$, Theorems 2 and 5 prove $(\boldsymbol{a}^{\langle t\rangle},\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle t\rangle}) \to (\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})$, which is the unique non-global stationary point (a local minimum for $d=1$ and a saddle point for $d > 1$, Corollaries 3–4); and (3) Theorem 7 proves that sample-based EM initialized at any $(\boldsymbol{\mu}_1^{\langle 0\rangle},\boldsymbol{\mu}_2^{\langle 0\rangle})$ with $\langle\boldsymbol{\mu}_2^{\langle 0\rangle} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_1^{\langle 0\rangle},\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star\rangle \ne 0$ is globally statistically consistent ($\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_i^{\langle t\rangle} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_i^{\langle t\rangle}\| \xrightarrow{p} 0$ as $n\to\infty$).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Resolved against all 442 pinned AIO concepts and 697 maintained AIMech concepts. Distinct from `AIMech:PopulationToSampleContractionAndFirstOrderStabilityEM` (Balakrishnan, Wainwright, & Yu 2017, which proves only local contraction inside a high-SNR neighborhood $\mathbb{B}_2(\|\theta^*\|_2/4;\theta^*)$) and `AIMech:OverSpecifiedGaussianMixtureEMSingularityAndAnnulusLocalization` (Dwivedi et al. 2020, which analyzes the degenerate $\theta^* = 0$ over-specified null case rather than global convergence from arbitrary non-orthogonal initializations for $\theta^* \ne 0$).

Mechanism #claim-two-dimensional-subspace-invariance-and-monotone-angle-contraction
Under the re-parameterization $\boldsymbol{a}^{\langle t\rangle} = \frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_1^{\langle t\rangle}+\boldsymbol{\mu}_2^{\langle t\rangle}}{2} - \frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_1^\star+\boldsymbol{\mu}_2^\star}{2}$ and $\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle t\rangle} = \frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_2^{\langle t\rangle}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_1^{\langle t\rangle}}{2}$ (Equation 11), Lemma 7 proves that $\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle t\rangle}\in \mathrm{span}(\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle 0\rangle},\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star)$ for all $t\ge 0$, Lemma 8 proves that the angle $\beta^{\langle t\rangle}$ between $\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle t\rangle}$ and $\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star$ strictly decreases via $\cot\alpha^{\langle t\rangle} = \cot\beta^{\langle t\rangle} + \frac{R(\|\tilde{\boldsymbol{b}}^{\langle t\rangle}\|,\tilde{a}_1^{\langle t\rangle}-\theta_{\langle t\rangle,1}^\star) + R(\|\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle t\rangle}\|,\tilde{a}_1^{\langle t\rangle}+\theta_{\langle t\rangle,1}^\star)}{\tilde{\gamma}_{\langle t\rangle,2}^{\langle t+1\rangle}} > \cot\beta^{\langle t\rangle}$ (Equation 29), and Theorem 3 establishes $\sin(\beta^{\langle t+1\rangle}) \le \kappa_\beta\sin(\beta^{\langle t\rangle})$ and $\|\boldsymbol{a}^{\langle t+1\rangle}\|^2 \le \kappa_a^2\|\boldsymbol{a}^{\langle t\rangle}\|^2 + \frac{\|\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star\|^2\sin^2(\beta^{\langle t\rangle})}{4}$ with $\kappa_a,\kappa_\beta\in(0,1)$.

Mechanism #claim-complete-stationary-point-landscape-and-orthogonal-hyperplane-saddle
Lemma 1 and Corollaries 1–4 prove that the population log-likelihood $G(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})$ has no spurious local maxima: its only stationary points are the two symmetric global maximizers $(\frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_1^\star+\boldsymbol{\mu}_2^\star}{2}, \pm\frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_2^\star-\boldsymbol{\mu}_1^\star}{2})$ — reached from every initialization with $\langle\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle 0\rangle},\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star\rangle \ne 0$ (Theorems 1 & 4) — and the single degenerate point $(\frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_1^\star+\boldsymbol{\mu}_2^\star}{2}, \frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_1^\star+\boldsymbol{\mu}_2^\star}{2})$ (or $\boldsymbol{\theta} = \mathbf{0}$ in Model 1), which is a strict saddle point for $d > 1$ (and local minimum for $d = 1$) whose basin of attraction is exactly the measure-zero orthogonal hyperplane $\{\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle 0\rangle}: \langle\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle 0\rangle},\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star\rangle = 0\}$ (Theorems 2 & 5).

  • supports Source: Global Analysis of Expectation Maximization for Mixtures of Two Gaussians (Crossref metadata) · Locator: DBLP bibliographic record for NeurIPS 2016 pp. 2676-2684
Scope #claim-global-sample-based-em-consistency-via-finite-horizon-population-tracking
By coupling pointwise convergence in probability $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_i^{\langle T_0\rangle} \xrightarrow{p} \boldsymbol{\mu}_i^{\langle T_0\rangle}$ at a finite iteration $T_0$ where the population trajectory enters a local contraction neighborhood $\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{0},\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star}$ (Theorem 6) with uniform Rademacher concentration of the sample EM operator inside $\mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{0},\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star}$ (Lemmas 20–23), Theorem 7 proves that sample-based EM initialized at any $\langle\boldsymbol{\mu}_2^{\langle 0\rangle}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_1^{\langle 0\rangle},\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star\rangle \ne 0$ satisfies $\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_i^{\langle t\rangle} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_i^{\langle t\rangle}\| = O_p(\sqrt{d/n}) \to 0$ as $n\to\infty$.
  • supports Source: Global Analysis of Expectation Maximization for Mixtures of Two Gaussians · Locator: Section 2.2, Theorems 6 & 7, p. 7; Appendix A.9-A.10, Lemmas 20-23, pp. 40-55
Limitation #claim-limitation-two-component-equal-weight-symmetry-requirement
The absence of spurious local maxima and global convergence from arbitrary non-orthogonal initializations rely on the symmetry of two equally weighted Gaussian components with known covariance; for mixtures of $k \ge 3$ Gaussians, the population log-likelihood possesses genuine spurious local maxima that trap randomly initialized EM with constant probability.
  • supports Source: Global Analysis of Expectation Maximization for Mixtures of Two Gaussians · Locator: Section 1.2 & Section 3 Concluding Remarks, pp. 2-3, 8-9

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Applies to balanced two-component Gaussian mixtures with known covariance matrix $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$; initializations lying exactly on the measure-zero hyperplane $\langle\boldsymbol{b}^{\langle 0\rangle},\boldsymbol{\theta}^\star\rangle = 0$ converge to the saddle point $(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})$, and mixtures with $k \ge 3$ components can exhibit bad local maxima.
