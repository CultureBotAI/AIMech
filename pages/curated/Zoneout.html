Zoneout

Algorithms AIMech:Zoneout DRAFT
A recurrent-network regularization procedure that randomly preserves selected state components at their previous values instead of applying their proposed updates during training.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Zoneout is a stochastic transition-modification procedure, not a particular RNN architecture or trained sequence model. Its identity branch retains previous activations; it differs from setting recurrent states to zero, dropping only input-gate contributions, fixed periodic update schedules and always skipping an entire recurrent layer. LSTM cells and hidden states can have separate preservation probabilities and masks. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
At a recurrent timestep, form the model’s proposed next-state values and use a Bernoulli selector for each targeted component to choose between that proposal and its previous value. Defining z as the preservation probability, the update has the form mask times previous state plus one-minus-mask times proposed state, with a mask value of1 selecting preservation. This introduces direct identity paths through time for the preserved components. The source’s LSTM account usually uses separate selectors for memory cells and hidden states and also studies shared selectors. At testing, replace the random selector by its expectation, producing a weighted combination of previous and proposed values at each step. Selection probabilities, targeted state components and the underlying recurrent transition are configured choices; the procedure itself does not require a new learned gate.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The generic operator formula in Section3.1 uses d for selecting the normal transition, whereas the LSTM formulas in Section3.2 use d for selecting preservation. The record defines its own preservation convention explicitly; those source masks are complements, not interchangeable symbols.
  • The printed LSTM hidden-state proposal uses the unzoned cell-update expression inside tanh. An implementation that instead uses the already-mixed cell state can differ when cell and hidden selectors differ. This general method record does not certify all LSTM code as equivalent or invent an uninspected implementation rule.
  • Replacing selectors by expectations gives a deterministic local mixture; nonlinear recurrence means it is not generally the exact ensemble-averaged state or output over complete stochastic trajectories. Preserved components pass their old values directly, but this does not guarantee that the entire network avoids vanishing/exploding gradients or processes every input in every component.
  • Very high preservation can suppress adaptation to new inputs. State/cell probabilities, sharing and other regularizers matter; the source’s task-specific results and conjectures do not establish universal improvement. No exact trained-model replication, parameter sweep or author-code execution is asserted.
