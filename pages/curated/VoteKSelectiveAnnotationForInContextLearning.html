Vote-k Selective Annotation for In-Context Learning

Aliases: Vote-k Selective Annotation, Vote-k Graph-Based Selective Annotation, Selective Annotation with Vote-k
A graph-based selective annotation algorithm for few-shot in-context learning that constructs a directed k-nearest-neighbor graph over dense sentence embeddings of an unlabeled dataset, iteratively selects an initial fraction (M/10) of examples to annotate by maximizing a discounted in-degree score where each vertex's vote is exponentially decayed by the number of its already-selected neighbors, evaluates the language model's predictive confidence on the remaining unlabeled pool using those initial annotations, and selects the remaining 9M/10 examples via the discounted vote-k score stratified across confidence-percentile buckets.

Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from test-time prompt retrieval methods such as KATE (AIMech:KATEkNNAugmentedInContextExampleSelection) and EPR (AIMech:EPREfficientPromptRetrievalForInContextLearning), which assume the entire training pool is already annotated and select prompts per test query, whereas Vote-k operates before annotation to choose a compact, diverse, and representative subset L of size M from an unlabeled pool X to be human-annotated. Also distinct from pure facility-location or greedy embedding-diversity coreset selection, which do not combine exponentially discounted kNN graph voting with in-context LM confidence stratification.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #votek-discounted-knn-graph-scoring-mechanism
Vote-k builds a directed graph G = (V, E) connecting each unlabeled instance to its k nearest cosine-similarity neighbors in Sentence-BERT embedding space and scores each candidate u by summing s(v) = rho^{-|{l in L | (v, l) in E}|} (rho > 1) over all remaining vertices v that have an edge to u, promoting both high neighborhood density and coverage diversity.
  • supports Source: Selective Annotation Makes Language Models Better Few-Shot Learners · Locator: Section 2.1 & Appendix G.1 Algorithm 1 (Lines 2-7), pp. 3 & 21
Mechanism #votek-confidence-bucket-stratification-mechanism
After annotating the first M/10 examples selected by Vote-k, the algorithm uses them as in-context demonstrations to compute average log-probability confidence scores on all remaining unlabeled instances, partitions the unlabeled pool into M equal-sized confidence percentile buckets, and selects the highest-scoring Vote-k instance from each of the first 9M/10 buckets while discarding the top 10% most confident buckets.
  • supports Source: Selective Annotation Makes Language Models Better Few-Shot Learners · Locator: Section 2.1 & Appendix G.1 Algorithm 1 (Lines 8-18), pp. 3 & 21
Application #votek-annotation-efficiency-and-domain-shift-application
Paired with similarity-based prompt retrieval across 10 classification, commonsense reasoning, dialogue state tracking, semantic parsing, QA, and summarization benchmarks, Vote-k achieves a 12.9% and 11.4% relative gain over random annotation under budgets of 18 and 100 examples and matches supervised fine-tuning with 10-100x fewer annotations.
  • supports Source: Selective Annotation Makes Language Models Better Few-Shot Learners · Locator: Section 3.3 & Section 4.1-4.3, pp. 5-8, Tables 2-3 & Figure 2
  • supports Source: Selective Annotation Makes Language Models Better Few-Shot Learners (Crossref metadata) · Locator: DBLP bibliographic record Hongjin Su et al. ICLR 2023
Limitation #votek-similarity-retrieval-coupling-limitation
The diversity and representativeness gains of Vote-k selective annotation depend critically on pairing the annotated pool with similarity-based test-time prompt retrieval; when prompts are sampled randomly at test time from the Vote-k pool, performance drops and fails to improve over random annotation.
  • supports Source: Selective Annotation Makes Language Models Better Few-Shot Learners · Locator: Section 4.4, p. 8, Table 4

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The full Vote-k algorithm requires running language model inference over the remaining unlabeled pool after the initial M/10 annotations to compute confidence percentiles (though Fast Vote-k omits this step).
  • Directly fine-tuning a language model on the Vote-k selected subset without outlier removal does not yield the same gains observed in similarity-retrieved in-context learning.
