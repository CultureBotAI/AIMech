A source-scoped SAT-search algorithm described by the literal composition of the 1995 GenSAT stages: a probability-controlled choice between false-clause variables and globally best-scoring flips, followed by HSAT’s least-recently-flipped selector. The paper’s generic random-walk prose does not uniquely certify this composition as its experimental executable.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #printed-gensat-search-context
In the inspected 1995 GenSAT framework, begin an attempt from an initialized complete Boolean assignment; the GSAT initialization described in Section 2 is random. Before each permitted flip, test whether the assignment satisfies the CNF formula. Otherwise select a candidate set, pick one variable, flip its truth value and continue within the per-attempt flip budget and outer attempt budget. HRSAT is introduced as a variant within this framework.
  • supports Source: Unsatisfied Variables in Local Search · Locator: PDF pp. 2–4, Section 2, Figure 1 and Section 4 introducing HRSAT.
Mechanism #probability-controlled-candidate-set
In the literal printed select stage, choose with probability p the set of all variables occurring in currently unsatisfied clauses. With probability 1−p, choose the variables whose single-variable flips give the greatest change toward more satisfied clauses, as in the stated GSAT hclimb rule. A greatest change need not be positive. The focused branch is available even when an improving flip exists; Section 5.3 treats allowing walks only when hill climbing is unavailable as a separate variant.
  • supports Source: Unsatisfied Variables in Local Search · Locator: PDF pp. 2–3, Section 2 and Figure 1; PDF p. 8, Section 5.3, the separately restricted walk variant.
Mechanism #literal-history-pick-composition
After select returns its candidate set, the printed driver applies pick to that set. For HRSAT, compose this with the HSAT strategy described in Section 4: choose a candidate whose last flip was longest ago. The same section explicitly contrasts this history-based deterministic picking with random picking. This is a source-scoped reading of the printed stages, not a claim that the paper’s generic random-walk prose fully specifies or certifies the experimental implementation.
  • supports Source: Unsatisfied Variables in Local Search · Locator: PDF p. 3, Figure 1 and HSAT pick definition in Section 4; PDF pp. 4–5, HRSAT introduction and deterministic/history-picking discussion; PDF p. 8, generic random-picking prose retained as a qualification.
Mechanism #focused-moves-and-endpoints
A focused flip changes a variable appearing in a false clause and can reduce the total number of satisfied clauses. Under the literal composition and the same history, tie and outer-loop conventions, p=0 recovers the HSAT candidate/pick combination, while p=1 always uses the false-clause variable pool but retains the history selector. The latter endpoint is not a uniform random draw from that pool or a clause-first random walk.
  • supports Source: Unsatisfied Variables in Local Search · Locator: PDF pp. 1–3, discussion of downward focused flips, Section 2 and Figure 1; PDF pp. 3–5, history picking. Endpoint statements are direct consequences of the stated probability branch and composition.
Scope #archived-hwsat-version-distinction
The inspected UBCSAT 1.0.0 archive registers HWSAT as HSAT with random walk and cites the Gent–Walsh paper. Its unweighted PickHWSat walk branch instead chooses an unsatisfied clause uniformly and then a literal uniformly from that clause, flipping the corresponding variable; its other branch delegates to PickHSat. This is a separately versioned implementation policy, not evidence that HWSAT is an exact alias of the literal HRSAT composition curated here.
  • supports Source: UBCSAT 1.0.0 source archive: HWSAT selector in src/hwsat.c · Locator: Archive member src/hwsat.c, AddHWSat lines 28–41 and unweighted PickHWSat lines 56–77. The explicit uniform clause/literal comment accompanies the two RandomInt selections.
  • supports Source: Unsatisfied Variables in Local Search · Locator: PDF p. 3, Figure 1; PDF pp. 3–5, HRSAT history selector, for the original composition being contrasted.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The 1995 printed select/pick stages and deterministic-history discussion support this literal composition, while the paper also uses generic random-walk and random-picking prose. This record preserves that ambiguity; it does not claim to reconstruct the exact experimental executable or silently substitute a uniform focused selector.
  • The later HWSAT name does not establish identical transition rules. The inspected UBCSAT 1.0 clause-first sampling differs from both history picking over the full focused pool and uniform sampling of the duplicate-free variable union. The complete delegated HSAT helper, solver infrastructure and later releases are not certified by this comparison.
  • The 1995 summary does not restate every initialization, never-flipped tie and restart convention from the earlier HSAT work. Those choices must be specified for an operational implementation; no convention is imported here from a maintained neighbor as though it were fresh primary evidence. A history preference is not a tabu-tenure exclusion.
  • The probability parameter lies in [0,1], and a focused flip needs an eligible variable. Empty clauses or an empty eligible set require explicit handling outside that selector. Clause/literal multiplicity and normalization can alter score counts or clause-first sampling; representation choices are not silently standardized.
  • Finite unsuccessful search is not an UNSAT certificate. Figure 1 tests satisfaction before each flip, so a model reached on the final permitted flip is not rechecked inside that literal loop. An added final test must be declared rather than treated as printed behavior.
  • The reported parameter studies and random/structured benchmarks do not establish a universal p, restart budget, speedup or asymptotic complexity bound. Difficult structured examples and the dependence on encoding and parameter choices remain within the source’s scope. No solver or benchmark was executed for this record.
  • Focused moves need not improve the score, and the history selector does not give every eligible variable a positive uniform probability at each step. General random-walk or 2-SAT convergence bounds therefore do not automatically apply. Endpoint agreement at p=0 does not identify the full parameterized method with ordinary HSAT.
