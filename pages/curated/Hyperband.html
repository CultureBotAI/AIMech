Hyperband

Algorithms AIMech:Hyperband DRAFT
A hyperparameter resource-allocation algorithm that runs successive-halving brackets with different initial configuration counts and starting budgets, balancing broad early screening against more conservative evaluation.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Hyperband is the bracketed allocation procedure, distinct from one successive-halving run, independent random search, TPE proposal fitting and PBT weight inheritance. The inspected finite-horizon algorithm samples configurations independently; the paper also discusses other samplers and a separate infinite-horizon formulation. Pure-exploration bandit terminology does not make this an RL-trained language model. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Choose a maximum per-configuration resource R and reduction factor eta. In the finite-horizon Algorithm1, set s_max=floor(log_eta(R)) and B=(s_max+1)*R. For each bracket s from s_max down to zero, start with n=ceil((B/R)*eta^s/(s+1)) configurations and r=R*eta^(−s) resource units. Evaluate current candidates at geometrically increasing resource levels and retain the best approximately 1/eta fraction after each comparison. The s=0 bracket evaluates its candidates directly at R. Algorithm1 returns the configuration with the smallest intermediate loss observed, rather than specifying selection only among maximum-budget evaluations. Brackets address uncertainty about how many configurations to start and how long to evaluate before elimination. Resources can denote iterations, time, data or features; actual computational cost and whether work can be resumed depend on that choice.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Table1 is not a numeric instantiation of the printed Algorithm1 count formula: for R=81 and eta=3, Algorithm1 starts with 81,34,15,8,5 configurations, while Table1 lists 81,27,9,6,5. These conventions are retained separately; approximate bracket budgets are not asserted as exact equality.
  • Poor early rankings can discard a configuration that would improve later. The inspected theoretical overview does not establish an unconditional finite-budget optimum or universal speedup.
  • The record describes the finite-horizon method. The paper’s separate infinite-horizon version and alternative samplers are not interchangeable implementations of the exact recorded pseudocode.
