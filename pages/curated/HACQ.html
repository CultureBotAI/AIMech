HAC-Q network clustering

Algorithms AIMech:HACQ DRAFT
Aliases: HAC-Q
A hierarchical network-clustering variant that uses a degree-corrected modularity contribution to guide group merges and retains hierarchical group structure for link prediction rather than using only a modularity-selected flat partition.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #modularity-merge-guide
The merge score sums observed cross-group adjacency minus the degree-product expectation under the graph’s degree-based null model. Equivalently it is the observed edge count minus the product of the two groups’ total degrees divided by twice the number of edges. Unlike edge density, it depends on the graph-wide edge total and vertex degrees.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Different merging scores, PDF p. 4, Equation 11
Scope #cnm-and-gain-boundary
The authors describe the score-driven algorithm as essentially CNM while emphasizing a different retained representation for link prediction. Under the standard normalized undirected modularity convention, the normalized modularity change for merging two fixed groups is this score divided by the graph’s number of edges. Greedy local gain is not a guarantee of a globally optimal partition or hierarchy.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Other methods and Different merging scores, PDF p. 4; curator-derived modularity identity in research/discovery/hacq-boundaries.py
Scope #hierarchy-versus-flat-prediction
For hierarchical methods the study estimates densities across the two sides of tree joins; for CNM it estimates within- and between-group densities for the selected flat clustering. Retaining a hierarchy can therefore produce different link estimates even when related merge criteria are used. A finite tree-versus-partition example illustrates this representation distinction conditionally, without asserting uninspected stopping or collapse behavior.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Different merging scores, PDF p. 4; Link prediction, p. 5; curator conditional representation example in research/discovery/hacq-boundaries.py
Limitation #score-domain-boundary
The degree-corrected score may be negative, and its printed degree-product denominator is undefined for a graph with no edges. It is a merge contribution rather than an edge probability. The record does not invent handling of edgeless graphs, negative-gain completion or implementation-specific tie and termination policies.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Different merging scores, PDF p. 4, Equation 11; curator-derived score examples in research/discovery/hacq-boundaries.py
Scope #evaluation-scope
The study compares this named variant with HAC-ML and other methods on biological interaction networks using held-out-edge prediction. Positive test pairs are balanced by an equal number of sampled holes, and maximum F scores summarize ranked predictions. This selection changes the negative prevalence relative to deployment. The reported comparisons support a setting-specific empirical assessment, not universal superiority or calibrated real-world link probabilities.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Performance Evaluation and Link prediction, PDF pp. 4–5; Table 2 and Figure 1, p. 6

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • HAC-Q does not introduce a distinct general modularity objective. Its source-defined scope preserves hierarchical representation for link prediction, separately from the CNM flat-cut baseline.
  • The three-vertex path calculation is a conditional mathematical example, not a claim about the paper’s degree-filtered evaluation network or about whether its uninspected code would retain that entire binary tree.
  • This record covers a named 2011 score variant in Park and Bader’s network-clustering study, not a fitted interactome or the entire family of agglomerative clustering methods.
  • The paper defines each alternative by replacing the merge guide. It does not separately specify every candidate-restriction, tie, stopping and terminal-collapse convention for each variant. The detailed HAC-ML implementation and its complexity are not automatically asserted for this variant.
  • The experiments use undirected, unweighted interaction graphs with iterative removal of isolated and degree-one vertices. Missing observed edges can include unobserved biological interactions; sampled holes are not established biological negatives.
  • No author implementation, data or supplementary archive was audited or executed. Finite constructed checks assess local scores and algebraic distinctions, not complete clustering runs, benchmark replication or global optimality. DRAFT remains.
