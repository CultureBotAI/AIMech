Adaptive Metropolis

Algorithms AIMech:AdaptiveMetropolis DRAFT
Aliases: Adaptive Metropolis algorithm
A history-adaptive random-walk Metropolis sampler whose Gaussian proposal covariance is estimated from the accumulated retained states, including repetitions after rejected proposals.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record identifies covariance adaptation from the full retained history, distinguished from a fixed random-walk kernel, finite sliding-window adaptation, scale-only tuning and RAM direction-based adaptation. The original empirical-covariance form and later recursive modifications share this core but differ in update timing and regularization. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Start at a state with positive target density. In the original form, after an initial period with a chosen positive-definite C0, propose Y from N(X_(t−1), C_t), where C_t=s_d[Cov(X_0,...,X_(t−1))+epsilon I], epsilon>0 and s_d>0. The empirical covariance uses every retained state, with denominator t−1 for t available states; choose the adaptation start after at least two states. Accept with min(1,pi(Y)/pi(X_(t−1))) and otherwise repeat the old state. The fixed-covariance Metropolis rule is used at each iteration, but adapting C_t from the sampled history requires a separate validity argument.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The 2010 source explicitly analyzes a recursive modification, not an exact restatement of the original unbiased sample covariance. Its displayed outline also shifts the covariance index; the original timing is stated explicitly here.
  • The original full-method text was read through a public indexed PDF. Direct download returned 403 and no visible page images were obtained; no original-PDF hash or visual inspection is claimed. The 2010 method pages were read and visually checked.
  • The original bounded-support result and the later regular superexponential-tail results have different hypotheses. Neither licenses arbitrary targets or unrestricted adaptive variants.
  • The usual dimension-based scale is a tuning heuristic under particular target assumptions, not a universal optimum. Empirical covariance estimation can be unsuitable for distributions without finite second moments.
  • The sampled-state sequence alone is history dependent; invariant kernels at frozen settings do not by themselves prove adaptive convergence.
