Ball vector machine

Algorithms AIMech:BallVectorMachine DRAFT
Aliases: BVM
An approximate SVM training procedure that uses fixed-radius enclosing-ball center updates in a transformed kernel feature space, with multiscale tolerance and radius-refinement variants that avoid CVM’s repeated core quadratic programs.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the Tsang-Kocsor-Kwok BVM procedure. Its fixed-radius core and optional refinement are distinguished explicitly; a feasible enclosing radius is not assumed to be the minimum radius. Claims & Grounded Evidence

7 Claims
Mechanism #fixed-radius-center-update
For a finite feature set, 0<epsilon<=1 and a supplied positive radius r>=R_star, initialize the center at a feature point. If a point z lies beyond (1+epsilon)*r, set beta=r/||c-phi(z)|| and c_new=phi(z)+beta*(c-phi(z)); otherwise terminate after checking all points. The update is the smallest center movement that places z on the radius-r boundary and is a convex combination. Any violating point suffices. Kernel coefficients can represent the center, and no inner quadratic program is required.
  • supports Source: Simpler Core Vector Machines with Enclosing Balls · Locator: Sections 3.1-3.2, printed pp912-914 / PDF pp2-4, Algorithms 2-3 and Equations 5-6
Limitation #iteration-guarantees-and-search-cost
The fixed-radius procedure requires at most O(1/epsilon²) violating-point updates under r>=R_star and the stated initialization. A multiscale version halves tolerance successively and warm-starts centers; its O(1/epsilon) iteration result is proved for r=R_star. That stronger rate is not established for every larger feasible radius. These are update bounds: finding a violator or certifying all-point coverage, kernel-evaluation costs and input storage must also be accounted for. A sampled stopping check is not an exhaustive certificate.
  • supports Source: Simpler Core Vector Machines with Enclosing Balls · Locator: Sections 3.1-3.2, printed pp913-914 / PDF pp3-4, Propositions 1 and 3; curator separation of search work
Mechanism #l2-transformed-kernel-and-feasible-radius
For binary L2 SVM training, use Ktilde_ij=y_i*y_j*(k_ij+1)+delta_ij/C. If k has constant diagonal kappa, the transformed diagonal is kappa_tilde=kappa+1+1/C, as follows from Equation 3, including its bias coordinate. The radius sqrt(kappa_tilde) is a feasible upper bound on the optimal MEB radius, since all transformed points have that norm. It need not be the minimum radius. For finite n and C>0, alpha^T*Ktilde*alpha>=1/(n*C)>0 on the simplex, hence R_star²<kappa_tilde. The nearby prose diagonal that omits +1 is not substituted for the displayed kernel.
  • supports Source: Simpler Core Vector Machines with Enclosing Balls · Locator: Sections 2 and 3.4, printed pp912,914-915 / PDF pp2,4-5, Equations 3 and 12; curator diagonal and finite-slack-term derivation
  • supports Source: Core Vector Machines: Fast SVM Training on Very Large Data Sets · Locator: Section 3.2.2, printed pp369-370 / PDF pp7-8, Equations 12-17 and following transformed-feature display
Limitation #fixed-radius-not-minimum-center
Reducing epsilon at a radius strictly larger than R_star does not in general recover the MEB center. For unit-norm points (1,0) and (-7/25,24/25), the fixed-r=1 update from the first point stops at (13/25,9/25), with distances 3/5 and 1. The true MEB center is (9/25,12/25), radius 4/5, and the center error remains 1/5 as tolerance tends to zero. This exact geometric illustration concerns Algorithm 2, not a historical SVM benchmark or the full radius-refinement procedure.
  • supports Source: Simpler Core Vector Machines with Enclosing Balls · Locator: Sections 3.1-3.2, printed pp912-914 / PDF pp2-4, Algorithms 2-3 and Equations 5-6
  • supports Source: Simpler Core Vector Machines with Enclosing Balls · Locator: Section 3.2, printed pp913-914 / PDF pp3-4, Proposition 2; exact curator example in research/discovery/core-ball-vector-boundaries.py
Mechanism #optional-radius-refinement
The source’s Algorithm 4 augments every feature by a zero coordinate and starts the center with an extra coordinate r. After an approximate radius-r enclosure, let u be the final extra center coordinate. If u²>(2*epsilon+epsilon²)*r², replace r by sqrt((1+epsilon)²*r²-u²) and repeat; otherwise stop. The paper’s experiments explicitly use only one iteration of Algorithm 4. This refinement is distinct from assuming the initial feasible radius is already optimal.
  • supports Source: Simpler Core Vector Machines with Enclosing Balls · Locator: Section 3.3, printed p914 / PDF p4, Algorithm 4; Section 4 footnote 4, printed p915 / PDF p5
Limitation #asymptotic-and-prediction-scope
The paper motivates near equality of the feasible and optimal radii using high-dimensional Gaussian data and a Gaussian kernel. Its limiting argument additionally needs the class-count imbalance divided by n to vanish; merely having two reasonably populated classes does not ensure that. The center-distance bounds retain their dependence on r/R_star, and coefficient or score proximity does not by itself guarantee identical predicted labels or test accuracy. No finite-sample equality R_star=sqrt(kappa_tilde) is asserted.
  • supports Source: Simpler Core Vector Machines with Enclosing Balls · Locator: Sections 3.2 and 3.4, printed pp913-915 / PDF pp3-5, Propositions 2-3 and Equations 9-12; curator finite-radius qualification
Scope #procedure-identity-and-evaluation
BVM denotes the enclosing-ball approach to the stated SVM formulation, with multiscale and radius-refinement variants described in the same paper. It is separate from CVM’s repeated core MEB fits, generic SVM, support-vector domain estimation and sphere-based cluster labeling. The ten-dataset experiments use particular kernels, C values, tolerances, versions, hardware, time cutoffs and multiclass availability; they do not establish a universal speed or accuracy ranking.
  • supports Source: Simpler Core Vector Machines with Enclosing Balls · Locator: Sections 2-5, printed pp912-918 / PDF pp2-8; Tables 1-4 and Figures 2-4
