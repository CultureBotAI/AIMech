Swarm intelligence

Computational Paradigms AIMech:SwarmIntelligence DRAFT
A computational design paradigm, in the broadened 1999 formulation, that develops algorithms or distributed problem-solving devices inspired by collective behavior in social insects and other animal societies, emphasizing interactions among relatively simple agents from which system-level behavior emerges.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Selects the computational design paradigm in the inspected 32-page preview of Bonabeau, Dorigo and Theraulaz 1999. It organizes relatively simple agents and local interactions to obtain collective behavior. It is broader than an individual optimizer and does not require stigmergy in every implementation. Scope #collective-design-scope
The 1999 formulation broadens swarm intelligence to the design of algorithms or distributed problem-solving devices inspired by the collective behavior of social insects and other animal societies. This selects an organizing approach rather than one prescribed computational procedure.
  • supports Source: Swarm Intelligence: From Natural to Artificial Systems · Locator: Section 1.1, PDF p. 20 / printed p. 7, the authors’ explicitly broadened definition; Preface, PDF p. 12 / printed p. xi.
Mechanism #individual-behavior-and-interaction
Designing the described collective behavior requires specifying both individual agent behavior and the interactions among agents or with their environment. The source’s self-organization account explains global patterns arising from interactions based on local information, rather than from a global pattern explicitly imposed on each constituent.
  • supports Source: Swarm Intelligence: From Natural to Artificial Systems · Locator: Section 1.1, PDF p. 20 / printed p. 7, programming emergent behavior; Section 1.2.2, PDF p. 22 / printed p. 9, definition of self-organization.
Scope #engineering-modeling-distinction
The source distinguishes modeling a biological collective from designing an artificial problem-solving system. An engineering design may extend parameters beyond biologically relevant ranges or add nonbiological features while retaining useful organizing principles; biological plausibility is not the same criterion as engineering usefulness.
  • supports Source: Swarm Intelligence: From Natural to Artificial Systems · Locator: Section 1.1, PDF p. 20 / printed p. 7, artificial variations; Section 1.2.1, PDF p. 21 / printed p. 8, modeling and designing.
Mechanism #direct-and-mediated-interactions
The inspected account permits direct interactions and indirect interactions in which an individual modifies the environment and later activity responds to that change. It identifies the latter pathway as stigmergy and discusses combining it with self-organization; the pathway is distinct from the broader swarm-inspired design approach.
  • supports Source: Swarm Intelligence: From Natural to Artificial Systems · Locator: Section 1.2.3, PDF p. 27 / printed p. 14, direct and indirect interactions; PDF p. 29 / printed p. 16, environmental mediation and operational examples.
Application #algorithm-and-device-applications
The source situates the approach in combinatorial optimization, communication networks and robotics, and includes both algorithms and distributed problem-solving devices in its definition. These application classes do not supply a single shared learning objective, optimizer update or implementation protocol.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The selected identity follows the explicitly broadened 1999 formulation. The Beni and Wang 1993 cellular-robotics chapter was inspected only through its publisher abstract and metadata. Its full methods and the priority of earlier uses were not verified; the historical definitions are not asserted to be identical.
  • The acquired source is a 32-page preview containing front matter and part of the introduction, ending midway through Section 1.4 at printed page 19. The complete introduction, later book chapters and the original experiments cited there were not inspected through this artifact; their detailed methods or quantitative results are not asserted.
  • This organizing approach does not fix a complete executable algorithm. Agent state, interaction topology, scheduling, communication, stochastic rules, objectives and stopping conditions must be specified for a concrete design before its behavior or reproduction can be assessed.
  • Flexibility, robustness, speed and other engineering benefits are motivations or conditional examples, not universal guarantees. The source emphasizes that programming emergent collective behavior is difficult; local rules alone do not establish convergence, optimality, fault tolerance or a favorable scaling law.
  • Self-organization and stigmergy are distinct concepts, and the source permits direct as well as indirect interactions. Environmental traces, pheromone reinforcement, evaporation and reinforcement learning are not mandatory for every design within the selected scope; evidence about a particular application does not automatically generalize to the paradigm.
  • The selected approach is not every multiagent system, distributed computation, biological collective phenomenon or population optimizer. A source-supported organizing relationship and an explicit boundary from particular implementations are needed; a shared name or metaphor alone does not establish membership or equivalence.
