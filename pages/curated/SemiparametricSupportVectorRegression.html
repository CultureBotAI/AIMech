Semiparametric support vector regression

Algorithms AIMech:SemiparametricSupportVectorRegression DRAFT
Aliases: Semiparametric SV regression
A supervised regression algorithm that jointly fits an unpenalized finite expansion of supplied basis functions and a kernel component, minimizing fixed epsilon-insensitive residual loss plus a squared feature-space norm penalty on the kernel component.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

7 Claims
Mechanism #joint-function-objective
For scalar responses y_i, a positive-semidefinite kernel k with feature map psi, fixed epsilon>=0 and lambda>0, use f(x)=w.psi(x)+sum_j beta_j*phi_j(x). The finite functions phi_j are supplied; the real beta_j are unpenalized. Jointly minimize sum_i max(0,abs(y_i-f(x_i))-epsilon)+(lambda/2)*||w||^2. Constant phi=1 gives an intercept, but no extra intercept is implicit when it is omitted from the supplied basis. The component changes the fitted function rather than learning an input-dependent tube.

Mechanism #normalized-dual
After dividing the primal objective by lambda, write a_i=alpha_i-alpha_star_i. Maximize y.a-(1/2)*a^T*K*a-epsilon*sum_i(alpha_i+alpha_star_i), subject to 0<=alpha_i,alpha_star_i<=1/lambda and sum_i a_i*phi_j(x_i)=0 for every supplied basis function. Recover w=sum_i a_i*psi(x_i) and f(x)=sum_i a_i*k(x_i,x)+sum_j beta_j*phi_j(x). The printed Equation 6 uses this normalized convention: its optimal value equals the raw Equation 5 primal value divided by lambda, not the raw value itself.

Mechanism #coefficient-recovery
The supplied-basis coefficients can be recovered through the primal/dual KKT conditions or as equality-constraint multipliers of the normalized dual; the paper uses an interior-point method. For a free alpha_i, y_i-f(x_i)=epsilon; for a free alpha_star_i, f(x_i)-y_i=epsilon. Recovering every beta from such equations needs sufficient independent information. Free coefficients, full basis rank and unique beta are not guaranteed.

Limitation #sequential-fit-boundary
Fitting the parametric component once and holding it fixed while fitting residuals need not minimize the joint objective. A curator-derived specialization uses x=y=(1,3), epsilon=0, lambda=1/2 and f(x)=w*x+beta. The joint optimum w=1,beta=0 has raw objective 1/4. A first-stage absolute-loss median beta=2, followed by optimal fitting with beta fixed, gives w=1/3 and objective 49/36. This supports the paper’s one-pass counterexample; it does not establish failure of every iterative backfitting or coordinate-descent procedure.

Limitation #supplied-basis-limits
The kernel norm does not penalize the supplied finite component. Appropriate basis choice can encode prior structure, but the paper does not establish automatic recovery of the true basis, globally identifiable coefficients or a universal accuracy benefit. Overlapping or rank-deficient supplied functions can make beta nonunique. Section 3 changes the method when it adds an L1 penalty to beta, replacing zero dual basis moments with bounds; that penalized variant is outside this record.
  • supports Source: Semiparametric Support Vector and Linear Programming Machines · Locator: Sections 1–3, PDF pp. 1–4 / printed pp. 585–588, especially Equations 8 and 11
Limitation #evaluation-scope
The empirical study is a synthetic regression proof of concept: sin(x)+sinc(2*pi*(x-5)), x uniform on [0,10], uniform additive noise with standard deviation 0.2, Gaussian RBF width 1/4 and fixed epsilon=0.05. Unless stated otherwise, 100 datasets contain 50 observations each. The paper compares a supplied sin(x),cos(x),1 basis, an incorrect-frequency sin(2x),cos(2x),1 basis and nonparametric SV regression. Regularization is selected separately by 10-fold cross-validation; L1 and L2 errors are evaluated over [0,10] with uniform measure. Table 1 parentheses count trials in which a model was best. Its plus/minus uncertainty is not explicitly identified as standard deviation or standard error. No numerical gain, universal superiority or guarantee that a wrong basis cannot harm is asserted here.

Scope #extension-and-proof-limits
The discussion connects the model to smoothing splines and representer results and describes polynomial augmentation for conditionally positive-definite kernels. The core record uses the positive-semidefinite feature-space formulation actually specified above. Proposed compound-class risk bounds, hypothesis testing and Gaussian-process extensions are future directions; the cited external kernel and representer proofs were not independently audited.
