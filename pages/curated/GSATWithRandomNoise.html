GSAT with random noise

A GSAT variant that mixes a random flip drawn from all formula variables with a globally best GSAT flip, without restricting the random choice to variables in currently unsatisfied clauses.
Definition Evidence
  • supports Source: Noise Strategies for Improving Local Search · Locator: The Random Walk Strategy, PDF2 / printed338; random-noise variation and experimental parameter context, PDF3 / printed339
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the random-noise strategy explicitly contrasted with the focused random-walk GSAT strategy in the 1994 primary paper. The defining difference is the random branch’s eligible variable set. It is not simulated annealing, Gaussian input noise, neural weight noise, or WalkSAT’s clause-first selection. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Start from a complete assignment for a CNF formula and test whether it is satisfying. At a search step, with probability p choose a formula variable uniformly and flip it, irrespective of whether it appears in an unsatisfied clause or improves the clause count. With probability 1−p take a globally best GSAT flip, using the largest increase in the number of satisfied clauses and random tie handling. Apply the chosen outer flip and attempt budgets with random restarts as in the GSAT experiments. This unrestricted noise replaces the eligible-variable restriction in GSAT with random walk. It can perturb a variable whose current occurrences are all in satisfied clauses, and it uses no temperature-dependent acceptance test.
  • supports Source: Noise Strategies for Improving Local Search · Locator: The Random Walk Strategy, PDF2 / printed338; random-noise variation and experimental parameter context, PDF3 / printed339

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A noise move need not help satisfy a currently violated clause and can break previously satisfied clauses. Conversely, the mechanism does not assert that every random move is uphill.
  • Finite search failure is inconclusive about satisfiability. Empirical comparisons on the paper’s selected instances and tuned settings are not universal performance orderings or general runtime bounds.
  • At p=0 the move policy reduces to basic GSAT, while p=1 selects only unrestricted random flips; the entire parameterized policy is not identical to either endpoint. The formula’s variable universe and treatment of empty clauses/candidate sets must be explicit.
  • The record describes the source’s noise policy, not an executed reference implementation, a weighted MAX-SAT extension or automatic parameter adaptation. It does not silently repair the original GSAT final-flip check boundary.
