Aliases: CTM
A mixed-membership topic model that draws each document's topic proportions from a logistic-normal distribution and generates its words through latent topic assignments and shared categorical topic-word distributions.

Identity Notes & Concept Scope Scoped to the Blei-Lafferty correlated topic model introduced at NIPS 2005 and developed in the 2007 journal article. The defining change is the logistic-normal document mixture, not a word-interaction MRF, dynamic topic evolution, sparse topic graph or the particular variational solver. Topic graphs are optional analyses of fitted latent coordinates. This is not the Dynamic Conditional Correlation econometric model. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #generative-law
For fixed K topics beta_k on a finite vocabulary, draw document logits eta_d from N(mu,Sigma), set theta_d=softmax(eta_d), then independently draw each topic assignment z_dn from theta_d and its word from beta_zdn, conditional on these variables. The logistic-normal prior allows associations that a single Dirichlet proportion prior cannot express, including positive pairwise proportion covariance when at least three components are available. Dirichlet proportions are not literally independent: their sum-to-one constraint gives negative off-diagonal covariance for positive concentration parameters. Common shifts of all logits leave proportions unchanged; unrestricted K-logit mean/covariance parameters are not uniquely identified by the induced composition. Logit covariance, proportion covariance and causal relationships are distinct.
  • supports Source: Correlated Topic Models · Locator: Section 2, PDF pp.2-3, Figure 1 and Equation (3)
  • supports Source: A Correlated Topic Model of Science · Locator: Section 2, PDF/reprint pp.4-6, Figure 1 and softmax nonminimality note
Mechanism #variational-inference
The original inference scheme approximates each document posterior by independent univariate Gaussian logits and categorical topic assignments. An auxiliary positive scalar bounds expected log-sum-exp from above, yielding a tractable lower bound no greater than the exact mean-field evidence lower bound. Coordinate updates alternate the auxiliary scalar, assignment probabilities, Gaussian means and positive variances; variational EM fits topics and Gaussian parameters from approximate sufficient statistics. Nonconjugacy and the additional bound make this approximate inference, not exact posterior computation or a guarantee of global marginal-likelihood maximization. The journal correction restores entropy in the originally printed corpus bound; the inspected v2 reprint already includes it, and the corpus objective sums expected log joint plus entropy for each document.
  • supports Source: Correlated Topic Models · Locator: Section 2.1, PDF pp.4-5; Appendix A, p.8, Equations (4)-(17)
  • supports Source: A Correlated Topic Model of Science · Locator: Section 3.1-3.2, PDF/reprint pp.7-9; Appendix, pp.16-18, Equations (9)-(20)
  • supports Source: Correction: A Correlated Topic Model of Science · Locator: Entire one-page correction, printed p.634, referring to original journal p.24

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Fixed topic count, finite vocabulary and conditional bag-of-words assumptions remain. Flexible logistic-normal associations do not remove compositional constraints, identify unique semantic topics or establish that all Dirichlet laws are exact special cases.
  • The source assertion that one observed document leaves no advantage for a nondiagonal variational covariance is not a general theorem. Posterior dependence can remain. Substituting the auxiliary bound into an expected log probability, as displayed with equality in the appendices, must be read as a bound rather than an exact expectation.
  • The journal topic graph regresses standardized fitted logit means using lasso and combines neighborhoods. Gaussian precision statements concern specified Gaussian coordinates; graph estimates do not automatically establish conditional independence of proportions, gauge-invariant structure, causal links or consistency from noisy variational means.
  • Reported Science-corpus comparisons use importance sampling or partially observed-document prediction under particular fits. No universal performance or semantic-interpretability advantage is asserted. No author code or benchmark was executed.
