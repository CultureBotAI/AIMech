Complete Dictionary Recovery Over the Sphere and Riemannian Trust-Region Method

Algorithms AIMech:CompleteDictionaryRecoveryOverTheSphereAndRiemannianTrustRegionMethod DRAFT
Aliases: Complete Dictionary Recovery Over the Sphere (Sun-Qu-Wright 2017), Log-Cosh Spherical Sparsity Minimization and Riemannian Trust-Region Method, Three-Zone Spherical Landscape Analysis for Complete Dictionary Learning
A nonconvex spherical optimization formulation and second-order *Riemannian Trust-Region (RTR)* algorithm over the unit sphere $\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$ (introduced by Ju Sun, Qing Qu, and John Wright in 2015/2017) for recovering a complete invertible dictionary $A_0\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ and sparse coefficient matrix $X_0\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times p}$ from $Y = A_0 X_0$ up to scale and permutation when $X_0$ follows an i.i.d. Bernoulli-Gaussian($\theta$) distribution with constant sparsity $\theta\in (0, 1/3)$. Observing that $\mathrm{row}(Y) = \mathrm{row}(X_0)$ so the $n$ rows of $X_0$ are the sparsest vectors in $\mathrm{row}(Y)$, the method sequentially finds directions $q\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$ by minimizing the smooth log-cosh sparsity surrogate $f(q; Y) = \frac{1}{p}\sum_{k=1}^p \mu\log\cosh(q^\top y_k / \mu)$ on $\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$ (Equation 1.5). Theorem 2.1 proves that when $p \ge C n^2\log^2 n/(\mu^2\theta)$ (down to $\theta = \Omega(1)$ where $O(n)$ nonzeros per column defeat existing $\ell_1$/SOS dictionary recovery methods), $f(q; X_0)$ has *no spurious local minima* across the entire sphere $\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$: in each of the $2n$ spherical caps $S_{\pm}^{(i)}$ mapped to $B(\sqrt{1 - 1/(4n)})$ via $q(w) = (w, \sqrt{1 - \|w\|^2})$, $g(w) = f(q(w))$ exhibits (1) strong convexity $\nabla^2 g(w) \succeq \frac{c_*\theta}{\mu}I$ in the core $R_1 = \{\|w\| \le \frac{\mu}{4\sqrt{2}}\}$ containing a unique local minimizer $w_*$ within $O(\mu\sqrt{\frac{n\log p}{\theta p}})$ of $e_i$ (Proposition 2.5, Lemma 2.3), (2) strictly positive radial gradient $\frac{w^\top \nabla g(w)}{\|w\|} \ge c_*\theta$ in the annulus $R_2 = \{\frac{\mu}{4\sqrt{2}} \le \|w\| \le \frac{1}{20\sqrt{5}}\}$ (Proposition 2.6), and (3) strictly negative radial curvature $\frac{w^\top \nabla^2 g(w) w}{\|w\|^2} \le -c_*\theta$ in the equatorial collar $R_3 = \{\frac{1}{20\sqrt{5}} \le \|w\| \le \sqrt{\frac{4n-1}{4n}}\}$ (Proposition 2.7), enabling the Riemannian Trust-Region algorithm with exponential-map retraction $R_q(\delta) = q\cos\|\delta\| + \frac{\delta}{\|\delta\|}\sin\|\delta\|$ and linear-programming rounding ($\min_q \|q^\top Y\|_1$ s.t. $\langle r, q\rangle = 1$, Lemma 4.2) to recover all $n$ dictionary atoms.

Mechanism #claim-three-zone-hemisphere-landscape-without-spurious-local-minima
Covering $\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$ by $2n$ overlapping spherical caps $S_{\pm}^{(i)} = \{q\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1} : q_i^2 \ge \frac{1}{4n}\}$ parameterized by $q(w) = (w, \sqrt{1 - \|w\|^2})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}$, Theorem 2.1 and Propositions 2.5–2.7 prove that with high probability when $\theta\in (0,1/3)$, $\mu \le c_a\min(\theta, 1/\sqrt{n})$, and $p \ge C n^2\log^2 n/(\mu^2\theta)$, the pullback $g(w) = f(q(w); X_0)$ on every cap satisfies: (i) $\nabla^2 g(w) \succeq \frac{c_*\theta}{\mu} I$ on $R_1 = \{\|w\| \le \frac{\mu}{4\sqrt{2}}\}$ with a unique local minimizer $w_*$ obeying $\|w_*\| \le C\frac{\mu}{\theta}\sqrt{\frac{n\log p}{p}}$ (Lemma 2.3), (ii) $\frac{w^\top \nabla g(w)}{\|w\|} \ge c_*\theta$ on $R_2 = \{\frac{\mu}{4\sqrt{2}} \le \|w\| \le \frac{1}{20\sqrt{5}}\}$, and (iii) $\frac{w^\top \nabla^2 g(w) w}{\|w\|^2} \le -c_*\theta$ on $R_3 = \{\frac{1}{20\sqrt{5}} \le \|w\| \le \sqrt{\frac{4n-1}{4n}}\}$, proving that $f(q;X_0)$ has no spurious local minima anywhere on $\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$.

Mechanism #claim-riemannian-trust-region-over-the-sphere-and-quadratic-local-convergence
Equipping $\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$ with tangent space $T_q\mathbb{S}^{n-1} = \{\delta : q^\top\delta = 0\}$, exponential-map retraction $R_q(\delta) = q\cos\|\delta\| + \frac{\delta}{\|\delta\|}\sin\|\delta\|$ (Equation 3.4), Riemannian gradient $\mathrm{grad}\,f(q) = P_{T_q\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\nabla f(q)$, and Riemannian Hessian $\mathrm{Hess}\,f(q) = P_{T_q\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(\nabla^2 f(q) - \langle q,\nabla f(q)\rangle I)P_{T_q\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}$ (Equations 3.14–3.15), the Riemannian Trust-Region algorithm solves the quadratic subproblem $\min_{\delta\in T_q\mathbb{S}^{n-1}, \|\delta\|\le\Delta} f(q) + \langle\mathrm{grad}\,f(q),\delta\rangle + \frac{1}{2}\langle\mathrm{Hess}\,f(q)[\delta],\delta\rangle$ (Equation 3.8); Theorem 3.1 proves global polynomial-time convergence from an arbitrary spherical initialization to $R_1$ followed by quadratic convergence $\|q^{(k+1)} - q_*\|_2 \le C\|q^{(k)} - q_*\|_2^2$ inside $R_1$.

Scope #claim-scope-lp-rounding-and-orthogonal-deflation-for-full-complete-dictionary-recovery
Given an approximate local minimizer $r\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}$ produced by RTR on preconditioned data $\overline{Y} = (\frac{1}{p\theta}Y Y^\top)^{-1/2} Y$ satisfying $\langle r, q_*\rangle \ge 249/250$, Lemma 4.2 proves that the linear program $\min_q \|q^\top \overline{Y}\|_1$ subject to $\langle r, q\rangle = 1$ recovers the exact dictionary row $q_*$ with high probability when $p \ge C n^2\log n$, and Algorithm 4.1 recovers all $n$ rows via sequential orthogonal subspace projection (Theorem 4.1).

Limitation #claim-limitation-complete-dictionary-square-invertibility-and-smoothing-bias
The row-space equivalence $\mathrm{row}(Y) = \mathrm{row}(X_0)$ requires the dictionary $A_0\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ to be complete (square and invertible) rather than overcomplete ($m > n$), and for finite $p$ the smoothed $\mu\log\cosh(\cdot/\mu)$ minimizer $w_*$ incurs a finite-sample shift $\|w_*\|_2 = O(\frac{\mu}{\theta}\sqrt{\frac{n\log p}{p}})$ away from the exact basis vector $e_n$ prior to LP rounding.
  • supports Source: Complete Dictionary Recovery Over the Sphere I: Overview and the Geometric Picture · Locator: Section 1.2, p. 3; Section 2.2, Lemma 2.3, p. 9; Section 5.1, pp. 23-24

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Restricted to complete (square invertible) dictionaries $A_0\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ where $\mathrm{row}(Y) = \mathrm{row}(X_0)$, requires smoothing parameter $\mu \le c_a\min(\theta, 1/\sqrt{n})$, and needs LP rounding or $\mu\to 0$ to eliminate the $O(\frac{\mu}{\theta}\sqrt{\frac{n\log p}{p}})$ finite-sample minimizer shift.
