Tractable unnormalized Barber–Agakov bound

Mathematical Methods AIMech:TUBABound DRAFT
Aliases: TUBA, TUBA bound
A tractable unnormalized Barber–Agakov lower-bound parameterization that uses a real critic and a positive output-dependent normalizer to replace an intractable log partition term by a linear upper bound.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope TUBA is the named parameterization with separate critic f(x,y) and normalizer a(y)>0. It is algebraically NWJ evaluated at T=f−log(a)+1; retaining it separately records its explicit normalizer and optimization interpretation, not a claim of inequivalent unrestricted variational families. Fixing a=e gives NWJ with the same f, while a held-fixed scalar moving average explains the modified MINE gradient. Mechanism #method
Let Z(y)=E_p(x)[exp(f(X,y))]. For normalized q(x|y)=p(x)exp(f(x,y))/Z(y), the unnormalized Barber–Agakov expression is E_joint[f]−E_p(y)[log Z(y)]. Apply log Z<=Z/a+log a−1 for a(y)>0 to obtain L_TUBA=1+E_joint[f(X,Y)−log a(Y)]−E_product[exp(f(X,Y))/a(Y)]<=I(X;Y). The inequality replacing log Z is tight at a(y)=Z(y); full information tightness additionally requires a critic that represents the conditional-to-marginal relationship. Critic and positive normalizer parameters can be optimized toward the same objective. Reparameterizing T=f−log a+1 recovers exactly the NWJ expression. With fixed functions and appropriate independent joint/product sampling and integrability, the linear expectations admit unbiased Monte Carlo estimates of this bound.

Limitation #implementation-scope
The pinned notebook subtracts a row-wise log baseline in its TUBA function. Its separable critic returns Y-indexed rows and X-indexed columns, while the concatenation critic transposes a matrix initially tiled with Y-indexed rows. These paths therefore do not share one score orientation. This is a source-level observation supporting caution about a blanket implementation-equivalence claim; it does not change the stated population formula or claim an executed runtime defect.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The normalizer must remain positive and must match the variable/sampling convention of the formula. A batch-dependent normalizer cannot be inserted without checking the extended sampling argument.
  • TUBA and NWJ have the same unrestricted family under the stated critic transformation. Different parameterizations, restrictions or optimizers can behave differently; no universal superiority is claimed.
  • Replacing the normalizer by a running statistic has a fixed-denominator gradient interpretation. It does not automatically yield unbiased population Donsker–Varadhan gradients, especially when the statistic depends on the current batch.
  • Finite samples can exceed true information, and exponential moments can be unstable. The inspected notebook has inconsistent matrix-orientation documentation/implementations; no blanket equivalence of all code paths or runtime correctness is asserted.
