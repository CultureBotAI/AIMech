Eppstein–Wang RAND closeness approximation

Algorithms AIMech:EppsteinWangRANDClosenessApproximation DRAFT
Aliases: Eppstein-Wang closeness approximation
A randomized graph-analysis algorithm that estimates closeness centrality from independent uniformly sampled source vertices, averaging scaled shortest-path distances to estimate mean distance and taking its reciprocal where defined.

This is the author-qualified RAND procedure for closeness centrality: iid uniform source draws estimate inverse centrality by a scaled distance average. It is not the centrality measure alone, exact Brandes betweenness, Brandes–Pich’s distinct-pivot multistrategy comparison, PageRank, HITS or an arbitrary random sampler. The bare acronym RAND is not adopted as a globally unique alias. Graph-based social-network analysis is the scoped relevance; the record does not claim a learned predictor or that every graph algorithm is an AI method.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Scope #domain-and-estimand
The maintained formulation uses a connected undirected graph with n>1 and positive edge lengths. For vertex u, mean distance is mu(u)=sum over v of d(v,u)/(n-1), and closeness is c(u)=1/mu(u). The self-distance is zero. This convention is inverse average distance, not inverse unnormalized distance sum, betweenness or eccentricity centrality. It describes graph structure without establishing causal influence.
  • supports Source: Fast Approximation of Centrality · Locator: Section 1, PDF/printed p. 1, closeness definition and footnote; Section 2, pp. 1–2; positive connected undirected domain is an explicit local restriction
Mechanism #independent-sampling-and-estimator
For k positive integer iterations, draw sources v_i independently and uniformly from all n vertices and solve a single-source shortest-path problem from each source. Repeated vertices are allowed. Form mu_hat(u)=n/[k(n-1)] times sum_i d(v_i,u); define c_hat(u)=1/mu_hat(u) only when the denominator is positive. The mean-distance estimator satisfies E[mu_hat(u)]=mu(u). This does not imply that the reciprocal estimator is unbiased.
  • supports Source: Fast Approximation of Centrality · Locator: Section 2, Algorithm RAND steps 1–3 and following expectation statement, PDF/printed p. 1; independence required by Lemma 2.1, p. 2
Scope #additive-mean-distance-bound
For diameter Delta and xi>0, the independent bounded contributions give P(|mu_hat(u)-mu(u)|>=xi) <= 2 exp(-2k xi^2/[n Delta/(n-1)]^2). Applying a union bound over n targets, a sufficient sample count for simultaneous additive mean-distance error below epsilon Delta with failure probability at most delta is k >= ceil(n^2 ln(2n/delta)/[2(n-1)^2 epsilon^2]), where epsilon>0 and 0<delta<1. This explicit sufficient count is curator algebra from the displayed bound, not an optimal sample size or a claim that target errors are independent.
  • supports Source: Fast Approximation of Centrality · Locator: Section 2, Lemma 2.1, probability display and union-bound discussion, PDF/printed p. 2; algebra documented in research/discovery/rand-closeness-boundaries.py
Limitation #reciprocal-error-conditions
Converting additive mean-distance error to multiplicative closeness error requires a positive lower bound on the mean relative to the allowed error. If |mu_hat-mu|<=eta mu for 0<=eta<1, then c_hat/c lies between 1/(1+eta) and 1/(1-eta). Thus for rho>0 a two-sided factor 1+rho is ensured when eta<=rho/(1+rho). A uniform lower bound mu(u)>=a Delta with a>0 supplies this condition when epsilon<=a eta. Bounded weighted diameter alone does not supply an n-independent a for arbitrary positive weights.
  • supports Source: Fast Approximation of Centrality · Locator: Section 1, small-world and inverse-centrality discussion, PDF/printed p. 1; Section 2 additive analysis, p. 2; explicitly qualified curator reciprocal algebra and weighted-star examples in research/discovery/rand-closeness-boundaries.py
Limitation #zero-denominator-and-repetitions
For any target u, all k independent sources can equal u, with probability n^(-k); then mu_hat(u)=0 and its reciprocal is undefined in this record. No fallback is specified by the inspected manuscript. Taking k=n does not ensure every vertex was selected or that the estimate is exact. Traversing every vertex exactly once would recover the mean, but is a different sampling design. On a three-vertex unit path with k=2, enumerating all nine source pairs gives zero reciprocal denominator with probability 1/9 at each target.
  • supports Source: Fast Approximation of Centrality · Locator: Section 2, Algorithm RAND, PDF/printed p. 1; independent-variable analysis, p. 2; curator consequences and finite enumeration in research/discovery/rand-closeness-boundaries.py
Scope #computation-and-evidence-scope
The algorithm requires k single-source traversals. On connected unit-length graphs, breadth-first traversal gives O(k(n+m)) work, equivalently O(km) for n>1; positive weighted graphs admit the cited O(k(n log n+m)) bound with the appropriate shortest-path implementation. With the paper’s high-probability choice k=Theta(log n/epsilon^2), this yields its stated asymptotic inverse-distance approximation cost. The inspected source is a theoretical analysis, without a numerical benchmark, observed ranking-accuracy comparison or empirical runtime validation.
  • supports Source: Fast Approximation of Centrality · Locator: Section 1, shortest-path algorithm context and references 13/19, PDF/printed p. 1; Section 2, runtime paragraph, PDF/printed p. 2

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The explicit record is restricted to connected undirected graphs with positive edge lengths and n>1. Disconnected distances, negative or zero lengths, directed conventions and alternative centrality normalizations need separate evidence.
  • Formal source support uses the two-page author-hosted SODA manuscript. The seven-page journal version was read as web-extracted text only; local download returned HTTP 403 and mathematical page images were not available to inspect. Exact equivalence between versions is not asserted.
  • The source’s informal equivalence between constant diameter and a bounded minimum/maximum distance ratio is not adopted as a general theorem for arbitrary positive weighted graphs. Relative centrality guarantees here require the stated mean-to-diameter or relative-error condition.
  • The source’s page 2 bound prose writes d(i,u); Algorithm RAND defines sampled vertices v_i. The maintained contribution is d(v_i,u), consistent with its stated random experiment.
  • Finite arithmetic illustrates error and degeneracy; it is not a replication of author software, a new empirically established confidence guarantee, or proof by enumerating all possible graphs. No model fitting, paid service or data upload was used. DRAFT remains after author self-review.
