Breadth-first beam search

Aliases: Beam search
A heuristic search procedure that advances by depth layers, retaining at most a chosen beam width of the best-ranked candidate nodes for each next layer and discarding the other candidates.
Definition Evidence
  • supports Source: Beam-Stack Search: Integrating Backtracking with Beam Search · Locator: Background: Beam search, PDF p. 1; breadth-first scope on PDF p. 2
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the conventional breadth-first form of beam search. Wider historical uses of beam search also include global best-first frontier truncation and other heuristic pruning procedures. A width of one follows a single surviving continuation; it is not the unbounded global-frontier algorithm curated as greedy best-first search. No normalized identity match was found across the 442 AIO and 130 prior native records; ignored-inclusive text hits in the main checkout were prior discovery leads, source citations and search-order contrasts, plus incidental lightbulb and wet-bulb dependency substrings, not existing concept records.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #search-procedure
Expand the retained nodes in the current depth layer, rank their eligible successors with the chosen heuristic or evaluation rule, and retain at most the beam width for the next layer. Candidates removed by this width rule are not systematically recovered by the basic procedure.
  • supports Source: Beam-Stack Search: Integrating Backtracking with Beam Search · Locator: Background: Beam search, PDF p. 1; breadth-first scope on PDF p. 2
Limitation #policy-boundaries
The comparative study distinguishes layer-limited and whole-frontier beam policies, and shows why duplicate handling must be specified separately. Its non-unit-cost examples challenge a universal claim that widening a beam always improves the returned solution.
  • supports Source: A Comparison of Greedy Search Algorithms · Locator: Beam Search and Closed Lists, PDF pp. 4–5; Incompleteness of Beam Search, PDF p. 5

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Width pruning can discard every path to a solution or every optimal path, so basic beam search has no general completeness or optimality guarantee.
  • A layer-width bound does not by itself bound all memory: stored predecessor information, retained earlier layers, generated successors and any global duplicate table must also be accounted for.
  • Ranking, ties, goal stopping and duplicate handling are additional choices. Increasing the beam width does not guarantee an improvement in returned solution quality for every implementation and problem.
