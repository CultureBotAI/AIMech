Parameter-Expanded Expectation Maximization

Algorithms AIMech:ParameterExpandedExpectationMaximization DRAFT
Aliases: PX-EM, Parameter-Expanded EM
An expectation-maximization construction that fits an expanded complete-data model with auxiliary parameters, reduces its result to the original observed-data parameterization through a likelihood-preserving map, and resets the auxiliary parameters before the next iteration.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope PX-EM changes the complete-data representation without enlarging the original observed-data model family. Auxiliary parameters are not independently identified from the observed data. It is deterministic likelihood fitting, distinct from parameter-expanded data-augmentation sampling, generic model overparameterization, ECM constrained updates and transformation-based training-data augmentation. 4 Claims
Mechanism #expand-fit-reduce-reset
Specify an expanded complete-data density p_X(y_com|theta_star,alpha) that recovers the original augmentation at alpha_0 and whose observed density equals the original density at theta=R(theta_star,alpha). At each iteration embed the current theta with alpha_0, compute expanded conditional sufficient moments, maximize expanded Q over the expanded parameters, reduce by R, and reset the auxiliary parameter to alpha_0 for the next E step. The observed-density equality makes expanded EM ascent transfer to the original likelihood.
  • supports Source: Parameter Expansion to Accelerate EM: The PX-EM Algorithm · Locator: Sections 3.1-3.2, Equations 3.1-3.2 and Theorem 1, pp.758-759 / PDF pp.5-6
Mechanism #fixed-degrees-t-expansion
For a p-dimensional t distribution with fixed positive degrees of freedom nu, old latent weights are w_i=(nu+p)/(nu+delta_i), with delta_i the old scatter-based squared Mahalanobis distance. Ordinary EM uses the weighted mean and scatter sum_i w_i(y_i-mu_new)(y_i-mu_new)^T/N. Expanding the mean of the latent gamma scale yields alpha_new=sum_i w_i/N. Dividing the expanded scatter by alpha_new reduces to the original t model, giving denominator sum_i w_i instead of N; the next iteration resets alpha to one. This normalization follows from the stated expansion and reduction.
  • supports Source: Parameter Expansion to Accelerate EM: The PX-EM Algorithm · Locator: Section 2, pp.756-758 / PDF pp.3-5
Mechanism #scalar-normal-example
For y|z~Normal(z,1), z~Normal(theta,sigma_squared), with known positive sigma_squared, ordinary EM updates theta to m=(theta_old+sigma_squared*y)/(1+sigma_squared). Expanding the conditional observation mean to z+alpha gives theta_star_new=m and alpha_new=y-m. The reduction theta_star+alpha returns theta_new=y in one iteration, while ordinary EM contracts its error by 1/(1+sigma_squared). This exact example does not establish one-step convergence for other expansions.
  • supports Source: Parameter Expansion to Accelerate EM: The PX-EM Algorithm · Locator: Section 3.4, pp.761-762 / PDF pp.8-9; curator scalar arithmetic
Limitation #local-rate-boundary
The information-matrix comparison linearizes at a converged likelihood solution under the stated differentiability, invertibility and positive-information conditions. It orders the slowest local linear convergence direction relative to the parent EM and permits equality. The paper’s global rate terminology refers to the extremal local direction, not convergence from arbitrary initializations. It does not order every finite iterate or total computational time.
  • supports Source: Parameter Expansion to Accelerate EM: The PX-EM Algorithm · Locator: Section 3.3, pp.760-761 / PDF pp.7-8; Section 5, pp.768-769 / PDF pp.15-16

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Finding a useful likelihood-preserving expansion is problem-specific. Additional free observed-model parameters without a valid reduction do not constitute this PX-EM construction.
  • Monotone bounded likelihood values alone do not prove parameter convergence or global optimality. Approximate expanded maximization and matrix/scatter boundary cases need separate validity and ascent checks.
  • The probit second-moment expression printed on p.766 fails the zero-predictor half-normal check, and the Poisson latent-count expectation printed on p.768 conflicts with the stated Poisson split. These curator-identified discrepancies are retained in the ledger; the record does not assert those example formulas.
  • All seventeen source PDF pages were read and two formulas reread at higher resolution. Finite calculations check scalar reduction, one fixed-nu t update and elementary moment contradictions; no historical fit, source implementation, clinical conclusion or benchmark was reproduced.
