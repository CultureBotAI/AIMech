Supplemented Expectation Maximization

Algorithms AIMech:SupplementedExpectationMaximization DRAFT
Aliases: Supplemented EM, SEM Algorithm (Supplemented EM)
A numerical supplement to expectation maximization that combines expected complete-data information with the local Jacobian of the EM update map to estimate the observed-data asymptotic parameter covariance.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Supplemented EM estimates uncertainty around a converged EM fit; it is distinct from stochastic EM, structural equation modeling, EM acceleration and the fitting algorithm itself. Bare SEM is not asserted as an alias. The full 1995 SECM primary follow-up gives the SEM equations and explicit implementation as a special case, supporting this record despite the inaccessible original 1991 full PDF. Mechanism #information-from-em-map
At a regular interior fit theta*, let Ic be the conditional expected negative complete-data Hessian and D the EM map derivative using input-coordinate rows and output-coordinate columns. The missing-information identity gives Io=(I-D)Ic, so Vobs=Ic^(-1)(I-D)^(-1). Equivalently add Ic^(-1)D(I-D)^(-1) to the complete-data covariance. The factors and derivative convention matter; a single scalar convergence rate cannot replace the full Jacobian in a multiparameter problem.

Mechanism #coordinate-map-probes
Obtain a tightly converged fit and expected complete information. Perturb one input coordinate away from theta*, holding the other input coordinates at theta*, and perform one full ordinary EM update. Divide each output change from theta* by the input perturbation to approximate one row of D, using stabilized ratios as perturbations approach the fit. Section 3 implements SEM by making CMSTEPS a joint Q maximization and setting the separate CM derivative to zero.

Limitation #asymptotic-and-numerical-boundaries
Inverse observed curvature is an asymptotic uncertainty approximation requiring a regular interior fit and nonsingular information. Ratio calculations become unreliable with inaccurate fitting, tiny denominators or missing-information fractions near one. Components reaching their fitted values exactly can require special treatment instead of zero-denominator ratios. Inspecting asymmetry is a useful diagnostic, but symmetric output does not certify the correct covariance or statistical coverage.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The inspected full-text mechanism source is the 1995 primary follow-up, not the original 1991 paper. Original-paper access remains a documented evidence gap; no unseen original proof is claimed.
  • The method additionally requires expected complete-data information. The simple sufficient-statistic implementation assumes an appropriate exponential-family complete model; general expected-curvature calculation may require integration.
  • Boundary optima, nonidentifiability, singular information and incomplete convergence can invalidate the inverse-curvature calculation or its statistical interpretation. It does not provide a full posterior distribution or guaranteed finite-sample interval coverage.
  • The source’s strong informal language about symmetry assuring correctness is not adopted as a theorem. A wrong common information scaling can preserve symmetry while giving wrong variances. Finite Gaussian matrix checks are not a calibration study or replication of the source examples.
