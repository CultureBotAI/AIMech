Continuous-time Bayesian network

A finite-state continuous-time model family combining a separate initial Bayesian-network distribution with a directed, possibly cyclic graph of current-parent-dependent conditional intensity matrices, whose single-variable jump rates amalgamate into a homogeneous joint Markov generator.
Definition Evidence

Identity Notes & Concept Scope Scoped to the finite homogeneous CTBN model of Nodelman, Shelton and Koller: an arbitrary separate initial BN and a possibly cyclic current-parent CIM dynamics graph. This is distinct from hidden/observed emission-model variants, discrete memory mixtures, undirected static potentials, timestamp-density topic models and continuous-state linear layers. The pinned BayesianNetwork DAG does not identify the dynamics graph; MarkovChain supplies a qualified process association. Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #initial-law-and-local-intensities
Specify finitely many finite-state variables, an initial joint distribution represented by an ordinary Bayesian network, and a separate directed dynamics graph. The dynamics graph may have cycles. For every variable X and current parent assignment u, specify a conditional intensity matrix Q_(X|u): off-diagonal entries are finite nonnegative transition rates, and each diagonal is the negative sum of that row’s outgoing rates. A parent change can select a different local matrix. The joint model is homogeneous because these rates are fixed functions of the current complete state, rather than explicit functions of clock time. The initial-law graph and dynamics graph have different roles and need not be the same.
  • supports Source: Continuous Time Bayesian Networks · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.2, PDF p. 2; Section 3.1 and Example 3.1, pp. 3–4; Definition 3.2, p. 4
Mechanism #single-coordinate-amalgamation
For distinct joint states x and y differing only in coordinate k, set Q(x,y) to the local rate for x_k to y_k under the parent values in x. Set Q(x,y)=0 if more than one coordinate differs, and set Q(x,x)=-sum_(y!=x) Q(x,y). This assembles the local generators without multiplying their entries. The resulting finite matrix has nonnegative off-diagonal entries and zero row sums. The absence of a single jump changing multiple variables is a defining restriction of this construction; continuous time alone does not impose it on every Markov process.
  • supports Source: Continuous Time Bayesian Networks · Locator: Section 3.3 and Example 3.4, PDF pp. 4–5; Section 3.4, p. 5; original finite amalgamation and joint-jump boundary checks in research/discovery/ctbn-boundaries.py
Mechanism #event-rates-and-zero-exits
While the complete state x remains fixed, let lambda=-Q(x,x). If lambda>0, its next-event waiting time is exponential with rate lambda, and destination y has probability Q(x,y)/lambda. This can be factored into choosing a variable in proportion to its local exit rate and choosing its new value in proportion to that local row’s off-diagonal rates. A variable with current exit rate zero contributes no event and must not divide by that rate. It may become enabled after a parent changes. If the total joint exit rate is zero, the joint state is absorbing. These guards qualify the printed destination/event formulas; the paper does not supply a guarded implementation for every zero-rate case. A child’s unconditional holding time need not be exponential at its initial local rate when its parents can change first.
  • supports Source: Continuous Time Bayesian Networks · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.2, PDF p. 2; Section 3.4 generative procedure, p. 5; original competing-event and parent-enabling checks in research/discovery/ctbn-boundaries.py
Mechanism #finite-interval-transitions
For the joint homogeneous generator, the transition matrix over duration delta is exp(delta*Q), and a row-vector distribution propagates as p(t)=p(s)*exp((t-s)*Q). Two-time joint probabilities instead have cells p_i(s)*[exp((t-s)*Q)]_ij; the source’s row-vector product labeled a two-time joint law is only the later marginal. A zero multi-coordinate generator entry does not forbid sequential changes during a positive interval. For two independent irreversible binary switches with rates 1 and 2, Q(00,11)=0 but P(00 to 11 in time t)=(1-exp(-t))*(1-exp(-2*t))>0 for t>0. This is a curator mathematical example, not a reported empirical result.
  • supports Source: Continuous Time Bayesian Networks · Locator: Section 2.3, PDF p. 3; Section 3.4, p. 5; Section 4.1, p. 6; original finite-interval calculation and rational exponential bounds in research/discovery/ctbn-boundaries.py
Limitation #initial-dependence-boundary
The dynamics graph alone does not establish independence of complete variable trajectories under the arbitrary initial BN allowed by the definition. A curator counterexample uses two binary variables with no dynamics edges, rate-one flips in both directions, and initial law (3/8,1/8,1/8,3/8) in state order (00,01,10,11). Already P(00)=3/8 differs from the marginal product 1/4. At time t the joint law is (1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4) plus exp(-4*t)*(1/8,-1/8,-1/8,1/8), so the zero-state indicators have covariance exp(-4*t)/8 at every finite t>=0. Thus the unqualified trajectory-independence statement in Theorem 3.9 is not adopted. No replacement general graphical-independence theorem is asserted.
  • supports Source: Continuous Time Bayesian Networks · Locator: Definition 3.2, PDF p. 4; Definitions 3.7–3.8 and Theorem 3.9, p. 5, qualified by the original full-support correlated-initial counterexample in research/discovery/ctbn-boundaries.py
Scope #representation-and-inference-scope
The local rate factorization can represent the model compactly, but its joint state space has the product of the local state cardinalities. Marginalizing variables need not leave a homogeneous Markov process on the remaining variables; parent trajectory information can matter beyond a one-time parent marginal. Sparse instantaneous dependencies also need not remain sparse over a nonzero observation interval. The paper’s clique-tree approximation, linearized marginalization and subsystem approximation are inference choices, not defining parts of this model family. No claim that every equivalent finite-time DBN is fully connected, or that the model guarantees cheap exact inference, is made.
  • supports Source: Continuous Time Bayesian Networks · Locator: Sections 4.1–4.2, PDF pp. 6–7; Sections 5–5.2.2, pp. 7–9; Section 7, pp. 9–10

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The maintained scope is finite variables and finite, time-independent conditional rates with one local transition owner per variable. Time-dependent rates, infinite state spaces and simultaneous multi-variable jump extensions require separate definitions. Zero rates and reducibility are permitted; irreducibility and convergence to a unique stationary law independently of the initial distribution are not guaranteed.
  • This is a stochastic model representation, not a particular simulator, learning procedure or approximate-inference algorithm. Hidden variables, emission distributions, action/reward control and intervention semantics are not required by this definition.
  • Ancillary displayed inconsistencies in the corrected source remain outside the core construction: Example 2.2 on PDF p. 2 reverses two probabilities in the third embedded-chain row; the first marginalization display on p. 8 mixes its initial/destination eliminated-state indices; and Section 5.2.2 on p. 9 labels an expected holding time as an intensity, although its example uses the reciprocal. No repaired approximate-inference implementation or author-issued erratum is claimed.
  • The author-hosted PDF is labeled Corrected without an established correction date or comparison to the original proceedings bytes. Original finite calculations support bounded examples; nonrational exponential probabilities are enclosed by rational bounds, not represented as exact rational values. No author-code execution, experiment replication, clinical validation or universal performance claim is included.
