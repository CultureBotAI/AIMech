Author-recipient-topic model

Model Families AIMech:AuthorRecipientTopicModel DRAFT
Aliases: ART
A generative message model with a topic distribution for each ordered sender-recipient pair, in which each word selects a recipient uniformly from the message's observed recipient set, a topic from that pair, and a word from the selected topic.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Scoped to McCallum, Corrada-Emmanuel and Wang's ART model in the December 2004 technical report and IJCAI 2005 paper. Sender and recipient have distinct roles in selecting a pair-specific topic vector. The model differs from author-topic modeling over undifferentiated participants, document-specific LDA, network-edge generation and the later latent-role RART variants. ART is a source acronym within this scope, not a universal expansion of that acronym. Mechanism #ordered-pair-mixture
For a fixed topic count and vocabulary, each topic has a categorical word distribution phi_z with a Dirichlet prior, and each ordered sender-recipient pair (a,r) has a topic vector theta_ar with a Dirichlet prior. Given sender a_d and nonempty observed recipient set R_d, each token selects x uniformly from R_d, z from theta_(a_d,x), and w from phi_z. Conditional on these parameters, the message topic mixture averages the eligible pair vectors. The reversed pair can have a different vector. Sender identity, recipient sets and message lengths are conditioned on; this direction-sensitive content model does not itself generate network edges or establish causal influence.

Mechanism #collapsed-pair-inference
Integrating the shared pair-topic and topic-word vectors gives a collapsed token update. For eligible recipient r, topic z and observed word v, its weight is proportional to (n_(a_d,r),z^(-i)+alpha_z)/(n_(a_d,r),.^(-i)+sum(alpha)) times (n_zv^(-i)+beta_v)/(n_z.^(-i)+sum(beta)). Counts exclude the token and pool the corpus; the uniform recipient factor cancels among eligible choices. Joint recipient/topic sampling or compatible separate conditional updates target the collapsed assignment posterior. Finite samples approximate posterior summaries and do not certify convergence. Sharing random parameters across messages requires joint corpus integration; a product of separately integrated message marginals is generally different.
  • supports Source: The Author-Recipient-Topic Model for Topic and Role Discovery in Social Networks: Experiments with Enron and Academic Email · Locator: Section 2.1, printed/PDF pp.4-6; compare final corpus display p.4 with global count integration p.5

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The corpus-probability display on report p.4 factors separately integrated document marginals despite shared random parameters. The subsequent corpus-count derivation on p.5 preserves sharing. The record uses the shared model and qualifies that display; no failure of unseen implementation is inferred.
  • Pair-specific parameters can be weakly informed for sparse or unobserved pairs. Fixed topic count, uniform recipient selection, stationary pair preferences, finite vocabulary and bag-of-words emissions constrain the model. Suggestions to smooth toward sender-only vectors are not part of the basic specification.
  • Reported role similarities use descriptive topic distributions, divergence matrices and selected email examples with manual entity resolution and heuristic text cleaning. They do not establish universal predictive superiority, actual job roles, expertise or causal social relationships. Topic and role labels remain interpretations.
  • RART introduces additional latent sender/recipient roles and has token-level and message-level variants. It is a distinct extension; preliminary staged fitting after fixing an ART sample is not full joint posterior inference. No author code, email corpus or benchmark was executed.
