Multiple-try Metropolis within Gibbs

Algorithms AIMech:MultipleTryMetropolisWithinGibbs DRAFT
Aliases: MTM-Gibbs, Griddy Gibbs multiple-try Metropolis
A coordinate-update sampler that applies a multiple-try Metropolis transition to a conditional target while holding all other coordinates fixed.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the MTM-Gibbs construction in Liu–Liang–Wong Section 5.2, a specific multiple-candidate Metropolis-within-Gibbs kernel. It need not draw exactly from a full conditional in one update and does not replace that conditional with a gridded interpolation. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a chosen coordinate i, fix x_−i and use the unnormalized scalar target pi_i(z)=pi(x_−i,z). Draw k independent yj from T(x_i,·), compute w(yj,x_i)=pi_i(yj)T(yj,x_i)lambda(yj,x_i), and select y in proportion to these weights. Draw k−1 reference values from T(y,·), append the old x_i, and accept using the forward-to-reference total-weight ratio of MTM. On acceptance replace only coordinate i; otherwise keep the full state. T may depend on the fixed remaining coordinates; its forward/reverse support and symmetric lambda requirements hold within that conditional update.
  • supports Source: The Multiple-Try Method and Local Optimization in Metropolis Sampling · Locator: Section 5.2, MTM-Gibbs steps 1–3, printed p. 126 / PDF p. 6, with Section 2 Equation (3), p. 122 / PDF p. 2

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This is conditional target invariance, not an exact independent conditional draw or independence of successive full states.
  • Use the complete backward-reference correction. Keeping only the weighted candidate selection defines a different procedure.
  • Zero forward total weight is handled by retaining the current coordinate; finite, meaningful target/proposal weights are required.
  • Support, coordinate scheduling and irreducibility determine whether the full chain can explore the target. No benchmark advantage over Gibbs or other conditional kernels is asserted.
