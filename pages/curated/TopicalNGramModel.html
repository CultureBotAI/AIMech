Topical n-gram model

Model Families AIMech:TopicalNGramModel DRAFT
Aliases: TNG
A topic-conditioned sequence model that chooses whether each word uses a topic-specific unigram distribution or a topic-and-previous-word bigram distribution, with that choice depending on the preceding word and its topic.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Scoped to Wang, McCallum and Wei's ICDM2007 TNG model in Figure 1(c). The Bernoulli gate uses the previous token's topic and word; the emission uses the current topic. Consecutive bigram choices form longer phrases, but the local generative law remains first order. This differs from the always-bigram topic model, the topic-independent LDA collocation components and generic tokenization. TNG is the source acronym. Topic assignments need not be identical within an extracted phrase. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #topic-conditioned-gate
Each document has a Dirichlet topic vector theta. Each topic has a categorical unigram vector phi, each topic/previous-word pair has a categorical bigram vector sigma, and each previous-topic/previous-word pair has a Bernoulli gate psi with a Beta prior. At position i, x_i is drawn from psi_(z_(i-1),w_(i-1)), z_i from theta, and w_i from phi_z_i if x_i=0 or sigma_(z_i,w_(i-1)) if x_i=1. Initial and other specified boundaries constrain gates to unigrams. Runs of bigram choices induce phrases; the paper labels a phrase by its last word's topic as an output convention. This does not impose a shared latent topic across the phrase.
  • supports Source: Topical N-grams: Phrase and Topic Discovery, with an Application to Information Retrieval · Locator: Sections 2.2-2.3, PDF pp.2-4, boundary handling, Figure 1(c) and topic-consistency discussion
Mechanism #next-gate-inference-boundary
The generative law makes z_i a parent of the next gate x_(i+1). Consequently a topic update conditioned on the other assignments must account for that outgoing gate as well as the current emission and document mixture. The full joint in Appendix A includes those factors, but the printed joint/topic conditional displays on pp.4 and10 omit the next-gate contribution. A collapsed implementation must remove all affected factors and preserve shared-parameter count corrections before evaluating candidates. A finite integrated example yields a topic conditional of (2/3,1/3), whereas dropping that contribution gives (4/7,3/7). This qualifies the printed inference expression, without asserting that uninspected author software used it.
  • supports Source: Topical N-grams: Phrase and Topic Discovery, with an Application to Information Retrieval · Locator: Figure 1(c), PDF p.3; Section 2.3 p.4; Appendix A joint p.9 and conditionals p.10

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The model represents long phrases through successive first-order bigrams, not an unrestricted higher-order n-gram table. Fixed topic count, vocabulary, boundary rules and many topic/word parameter rows constrain the model and require smoothing.
  • The optional same-topic phrase modification on p.4 has an if/else inconsistent with its verbal intention. The record follows the main Figure 1(c) construction. Printed minus-one counts must be interpreted as removing the old assignment, not subtracting one from every candidate category.
  • The retrieval section uses a plug-in first-order query score that averages the previous-topic gate under document weights. It is not generally exact filtering or posterior prediction for the full latent sequence. Query-level mixture and term-level interpolation are different scoring rules; the reported study uses term-level interpolation.
  • The NIPS/TREC experiments use selected preparations, fixed hyperparameters and model-specific interpolation weights selected for best performance. Topic/phrase labels and retrieval comparisons do not establish linguistic ground truth or universal superiority. The finite counterexample audits a printed equation, not source implementation or benchmark execution.
