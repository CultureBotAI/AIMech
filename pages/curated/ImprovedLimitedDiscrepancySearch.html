Improved limited discrepancy search

Algorithms AIMech:ImprovedLimitedDiscrepancySearch DRAFT
Aliases: ILDS
Korf’s discrepancy-search variant that uses a supplied maximum search depth and remaining discrepancy count to generate each path to that depth in the pass corresponding to its exact number of nonpreferred choices, avoiding the original method’s repeated maximum-depth paths.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope ILDS is the 1996 improvement to LDS, not a generic label for any later discrepancy-search modification. Its exact-count guarantee concerns leaves at the specified maximum depth; shallower leaves and internal nodes can still be revisited. The supplied depth bound differs from a bound on the depths at which discrepancies are allowed. Mechanism #search-procedure
For each budget k from zero through the supplied maximum depth, recursively track remaining depth D and discrepancies k. In the binary Figure 3, visit the preferred child when D>k, leaving k unchanged; visit the nonpreferred child when k>0, reducing k by one. Both moves reduce D by one. When D=k, only nonpreferred choices remain possible along a path that reaches the depth bound. The printed procedure visits the preferred branch before the nonpreferred branch when both are allowed.

Limitation #repeated-prefixes
Maximum-depth paths are generated once across discrepancy passes, but shorter leaves and internal nodes can recur. The paper’s uniform-tree overhead analysis and its negative number-partitioning results are distinct from a universal performance guarantee.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A valid upper bound on maximum depth is an input. A loose bound or pruning that creates shallower leaves reduces the benefit because those leaves may be visited on several passes.
  • Visiting each maximum-depth leaf once does not mean visiting every internal node once; repeated prefixes remain.
  • The complete uniform-tree expansion analysis is conditional on that tree shape. It does not establish an unconditional wall-clock advantage on pruned trees or early-terminated searches.
  • The procedure’s discrepancy priority does not guarantee that the first goal is shortest or globally minimum-cost. No universal superiority over DFS is asserted.
  • The 1996 prose gives a depth-three original-LDS path count inconsistent with its own displayed counting formula and the original paper’s trace. No literal 19-path count is adopted.
