Square root graphical model

Model Families AIMech:SquareRootGraphicalModel DRAFT
Aliases: SQR, Square root Markov random field
A family of finite undirected graphical probability models that retains univariate base measures while coupling specified square roots of sufficient statistics through unary terms and a symmetric quadratic form, with parameters restricted to a finite joint partition function.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope SQR denotes the interaction construction of Inouye et al., not a square-root preprocessing pipeline or a radial-basis neural network. Its Poisson and exponential instantiations retain distinct supports, base measures and validity regimes. For ordinary nonnegative statistics the root is nonnegative; the paper explicitly adopts the signed convention s(x)=x when T(x)=x^2 to recover the Gaussian family. That convention is part of the specification, not the identity sqrt(x^2)=x for arbitrary real x. Indicator statistics remain unchanged by their nonnegative root, so the binary/discrete special case is not automatically a new identity. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a finite product domain with specified univariate measure mu, log base measure B and nonnegative scalar sufficient statistic T, set s(x)=sqrt(T(x)). The paper makes one explicit signed-root exception: use s(x)=x for T(x)=x^2 on the real line to recover its Gaussian special case. Let Phi be a finite symmetric matrix, including its diagonal. Define p(x)=Z^(-1)*exp[sum_i theta_i*s(x_i)+sum_(i,j) Phi_ij*s(x_i)*s(x_j)+sum_i B(x_i)] with 0<Z<infinity relative to the product measure. The log partition is A=log Z. The site-i full-conditional kernel is exp[Phi_ii*T(t)+(theta_i+2*sum_(j!=i) Phi_ij*s(x_j))*s(t)+B(t)]. Nonedge matrix entries vanish. With theta=0 and diagonal Phi, the law factors into the original independent univariate exponential family at valid diagonal parameters. Normalizability otherwise depends on the chosen base measure and parameter regime.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This is a construction with an explicit finite-partition requirement, not a theorem that every exponential-family base allows all finite interactions. The unrestricted Poisson case and the restricted continuous exponential case require separate arguments.
  • Using the ordinary nonnegative root of x^2 gives |x| and does not recover a general Gaussian with a linear x term. The documented signed convention is essential for that special case.
  • Several displayed definitions in the source omit log even while calling A a log partition. The record uses Z for the integral or sum and A=log Z, preventing confusion between a normalizer and its logarithm.
  • The graph is finite and Phi is symmetric; an off-diagonal matrix entry contributes twice in the quadratic form. Separately estimated node regressions need not agree on one symmetric joint parameterization.
  • The node conditionals usually have two sufficient statistics and differ from the original univariate base family. A base-family name is not a claim about every conditional or marginal distribution, and an edge parameter is not a universal marginal-correlation or causal-effect statement.
  • The printed radial formulas omit the Jacobian needed to interpret x=z*v as a change of continuous coordinates. In p dimensions the L1-radius transformation contributes z^(p-1); a bare ray kernel is not generally the radius distribution. Discrete directions require a counting-measure treatment. The normalization arguments stated here use direct joint domination instead.
  • The paper proposes nodewise l1-penalized fitting, numerical conditional partitions and annealed importance sampling. Model existence does not establish finite-data graph recovery, sampler mixing, numerical accuracy or optimizer convergence; no author code, empirical fitting or benchmark was reproduced.
  • Reported synthetic and airport-delay experiments are contextual. The paper’s synthetic description prints a positive diagonal while calling the matrix negative definite, an inconsistent sign convention that must be resolved before reproducing that generator. Airport associations and fitted likelihoods do not establish causal effects or held-out predictive performance.
