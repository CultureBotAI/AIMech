Aliases: Finite-Sample Analysis of M-Estimators Using Self-Concordance, Canonically Self-Concordant and Pseudo-Self-Concordant Empirical Risk Minimization, Dikin-Ellipsoid Calibrated-Design Finite-Sample M-Estimator Excess Risk Theory, Fenchel-Conjugate Canonically Self-Concordant Pseudo-Huber and Classification Losses
Self-Concordant and Pseudo-Self-Concordant Finite-Sample M-Estimation is a non-asymptotic statistical estimation and excess-risk analysis methodology for empirical risk minimizers (ERM) theta_hat_n in Argmin_{theta in Theta} L_n(theta) and l_1-penalized M-estimators theta_hat_{lambda,n} in Argmin_{theta in R^d} {L_n(theta) + lambda ||theta||_1} in random-design linear prediction models ell_Z(theta) = ell(Y, X^T theta) that characterizes the critical sample size n needed to achieve the Wilks-Fisher chi-square-type excess risk bound L(theta_hat_n) - L(theta_*) <= C K_1^2 d_eff log(e/delta) / n where d_eff = Tr(H^{-1/2} G H^{-1/2}) is the effective dimension, H = nabla^2 L(theta_*) = E[ell''(Y, X^T theta_*) X X^T], and G = E[nabla ell_Z(theta_*) nabla ell_Z(theta_*)^T]. By exploiting either pseudo-self-concordance (|ell'''(y, eta)| <= ell''(y, eta), Assumption SCa) or canonical self-concordance (|ell'''(y, eta)| <= 2 [ell''(y, eta)]^{3/2}, Assumption SCb, including novel Fenchel-conjugate canonically self-concordant pseudo-Huber and classification surrogate losses) together with local sub-Gaussian calibrated-design assumptions at theta_* or over the population Dikin ellipsoid Theta_r(theta_*) = {theta in R^d : ||theta - theta_*||_{H(theta_*)} <= r}, the methodology localizes theta_hat_n without global empirical-process chaining and establishes critical sample sizes O(rho d d_eff) or O(d d_eff) under pointwise local assumptions at theta_* and near-linear critical sample sizes O(max{rho d_eff, d log d}) or O(max{d_eff, d log d}) via an epsilon-net covering argument over the Dikin ellipsoid.

Mechanism #pseudo-and-canonical-self-concordance-and-fenchel-conjugate-losses
For linear prediction losses ell_Z(theta) = ell(Y, X^T theta) that are convex and C^3 in eta = X^T theta, Ostrovskii and Bach (2021) distinguish two third-derivative control regimes: pseudo-self-concordance (Assumption SCa: |ell'''(y, eta)| <= ell''(y, eta), satisfied by logistic regression with c = 1, Poisson regression with c = 1, and smooth pseudo-Huber contrasts log(cosh(t)) and sqrt(1+t^2)-1 with c = 3) and canonical self-concordance (Assumption SCb: |ell'''(y, eta)| <= 2[ell''(y, eta)]^{3/2}, satisfied by exponential-response GLMs). Moreover, using Fenchel conjugation of the normalized log-barrier phi(u) = -(1/2) log(1 - u^2) on (-1, 1) and the duality identity phi''(varphi'(t)) * varphi''(t) = 1 (Eq. 71), they construct the canonically self-concordant pseudo-Huber loss varphi(t) = (1/2)[sqrt(1 + 4 t^2) - 1 + log((sqrt(1 + 4 t^2) - 1)/(2 t^2))] (Eq. 21), which satisfies varphi(0) = 0, varphi''(0) = 1, |varphi'(t)| < 1, and |varphi'''(t)| <= 2 sqrt(2) [varphi''(t)]^{3/2}, together with a canonically self-concordant classification surrogate loss (Eq. 22).

Mechanism #self-concordant-taylor-bracketing-and-pointwise-critical-sample-size
Along any line segment theta_t = theta_0 + t(theta_1 - theta_0) of a C^3 convex objective F: Theta -> R with H_0 = nabla^2 F(theta_0) > 0 and directional scale S = |<W, theta_1 - theta_0>|, Proposition B.3 (Lemmas B.1-B.3) establishes exact Taylor remainder bracketing under Case (a) |phi_F'''(t)| <= S phi_F''(t) (Eqs. 60-61) and Case (b) |phi_F'''(t)| <= (S/(1 - S t)) phi_F''(t) (Eqs. 62-63), and Proposition B.4 proves that whenever ||W||_{H_0^{-1}} ||nabla F(theta_0)||_{H_0^{-1}} <= 1/4, F has a unique minimizer theta_tilde satisfying ||theta_tilde - theta_0||_{H_0} <= 4 ||nabla F(theta_0)||_{H_0^{-1}}. Applying this under pointwise sub-Gaussian assumptions at theta_* (Assumptions D0-D2 and compatibility Sigma <= rho H, Assumption C) yields ||theta_hat_n - theta_*||_H^2 <= C ||nabla L_n(theta_*)||_{H^{-1}}^2 <= C' K_1^2 d_eff log(e/delta) / n and the same restricted-risk bound once n >= C max{K_2^4(d + log(1/delta)), rho K_0^2 K_1^2 d_eff d log(e d/delta)} for pseudo-self-concordant losses (Theorem 3.1) or n >= C max{K_2^4(d + log(1/delta)), K_1^2 K_2^2 d_eff d log(e d/delta)} for canonically self-concordant losses (Theorem 3.2, eliminating rho and Assumption D0).

Mechanism #dikin-ellipsoid-covering-near-linear-critical-sample-size-and-gaussian-logistic-scaling
Under Assumption D2* requiring the calibrated design X_tilde(theta) = [ell''(Y, X^T theta)]^{1/2} X to satisfy ||H(theta)^{-1/2} X_tilde(theta)||_{psi_2} <= K_bar_2(r) over the r-radius Dikin ellipsoid Theta_r(theta_*), Theorems 4.1 and 4.2 combine population-risk Hessian stability on Theta_{1/(2 c_bar)}(theta_*) ((4/9) H(theta_*) <= H(theta) <= 4 H(theta_*), Eq. 84) with individual-loss self-concordance on fine balls of radius varepsilon = O(1/(d^2 log(e n/delta))) (Eq. 88) and a union bound of sub-Gaussian covariance concentration over an varepsilon-net N_varepsilon of size |N_varepsilon| <= (3/(2 c_bar varepsilon))^d (Eq. 86) to establish uniform empirical Hessian bracketing 0.09 H(theta_*) <= H_n(theta) <= 32 H(theta_*) (Eq. 92) and unrestricted excess-risk bound L(theta_hat_n) - L(theta_*) <= C K_1^2 d_eff log(e/delta) / n for near-linear critical sample size n >= C max{K_bar_2^4(r) d log(e d/delta), K_1^2 K_bar_2^6(r) d_eff log(e/delta)} (with r >= 1 under SCb, Theorem 4.1) or n >= C max{K_bar_2^4(r) d log(e d/delta), rho K_0^2 K_1^2 K_bar_2^4(r) d_eff log(e/delta)} (with r >= 1/sqrt(rho) under SCa, Theorem 4.2). For logistic regression with Gaussian design X ~ N(0, Sigma), Proposition D.1 proves tight polynomial scaling rho <= C(1 + ||theta_*||_Sigma^3) and K_2 ~ K_bar_2(1/sqrt(rho)) <= C(1 + log(1 + ||theta_*||_Sigma)) sqrt(1 + ||theta_*||_Sigma).

Scope #high-dimensional-l1-penalized-self-concordant-m-estimation
In high dimensions (d >> n) where |theta_*|_0 <= s << d and Assumption C* holds (Sigma = I, G <= kappa_1 I, H <= kappa_2 I with condition number Q = rho max(kappa_1, kappa_2)), Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 prove that for regularization parameter K_1 sqrt(kappa_1 log(e d/delta) / n) <= C lambda <= c / (rho K_0 s sqrt(log(e d n/delta))) (or with K_2 sqrt(kappa_2) in place of K_0 under SCb), the l_1-penalized M-estimator theta_hat_{lambda,n} satisfies the restricted cone property ||Delta_{S_c}||_1 <= 3 ||Delta_S||_1 (Eq. 105), ||theta_hat_{lambda,n} - theta_*||_1 <= C rho s lambda, ||theta_hat_{lambda,n} - theta_*||_H^2 <= C rho s lambda^2, and restricted excess risk L_E(theta_hat_{lambda,n}) - L_E(theta_*) <= C rho s lambda^2 = O(Q s log(e d/delta) / n) whenever n >= C max{Q s, rho Q s^2} log(e d n/delta) (under SCa, Theorem 5.1) or n >= C max{Q s, Q^2 s^2} log(e d n/delta) (under SCb, Theorem 5.2), showing that lack of affine invariance of the l_1 penalty prevents canonical self-concordance from eliminating the curvature factor rho in high dimensions.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Sub-Gaussian Calibrated-Design Assumptions and Exponential Adversarial Design Scaling: Assumptions D1-D2 and D2* can fail when the second-derivative scaling [ell''(Y, X^T theta)]^{1/2} grows super-polynomially (such as in Poisson regression where a''(eta) = e^eta, Remark 2.2), and for adversarial discrete design distributions (Hazan, Koren, & Levy 2014) the local curvature parameter rho and critical sample size can grow as exp(R D) in the predictor-parameter product (Section 6, Figure 4).
  • Loss of Affine Invariance Under L1 Regularization in High Dimensions: In Theorems 5.1-5.2, the l_1 norm and coordinate sparsity depend on the choice of basis and are not affine-invariant, so canonical self-concordance (Assumption SCb) does not eliminate the local curvature factor rho from the estimation error or the O(Q^2 s^2 log(e d/delta)) critical sample size.
  • Primary-Source Typographical Notes in Ostrovskii & Bach (2020/2021, arXiv:1810.06838v2): Verified five self-correcting typographical slips in the primary manuscript: (1) Theorem 1.2 (p. 8) writes 'In addition to the premise of Theorem 1.2' instead of 'In addition to the premise of Theorem 1.1'; (2) Section 2.1.3 (p. 11, after Eq. 21) writes '|varphi_tau'''(t)| <= (2/tau)[varphi_tau''(t)]^{3/2}', omitting the sqrt(2) factor from |varphi'''(t)| <= 2 sqrt(2) [varphi''(t)]^{3/2} (the exact rescaled constant is 2 sqrt(2)/tau); (3) Section 5 (p. 19, sentence preceding Theorem 5.1) states that Theorem 5.1 characterizes an l_1-penalized M-estimator with a 'canonically self-concordant loss', whereas Theorem 5.1 assumes SCa (pseudo-self-concordance) and Theorem 5.2 assumes SCb (canonical self-concordance); (4) Section 6 (p. 22, paragraph 1) states 'Thus, the logistic model for Y|X is well-specified in the second case' after defining the second case as probit regression (where the logistic model is misspecified, as noted in the second bullet on p. 22); and (5) Appendix B.1 (p. 34, final line) writes '|varphi'''(u)| <= c[varphi''(u)]^{3/2}' instead of '|varphi'''(t)| <= c[varphi''(t)]^{3/2}'.
