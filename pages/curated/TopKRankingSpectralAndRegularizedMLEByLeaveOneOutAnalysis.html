Top-K Ranking by Spectral Method and Regularized Maximum Likelihood via Leave-One-Out Analysis

Mathematical Methods AIMech:TopKRankingSpectralAndRegularizedMLEByLeaveOneOutAnalysis DRAFT
Aliases: Leave-One-Out Entrywise Optimal Spectral and Regularized MLE Top-K Ranking, Chen-Fan-Ma-Wang Minimax Top-K BTL Ranking, Reversible Markov Chain Perturbation and Leave-One-Out BTL Ranking
A unified pairwise-comparison ranking and entrywise $\ell_\infty$ estimation framework (established by Yuxin Chen, Jianqing Fan, Cong Ma, and Kaizheng Wang in 2019) proving that both the spectral method (Rank Centrality stationary distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ of the empirical comparison transition matrix $\mathbf{P}$) and $\ell_2$-regularized maximum likelihood estimation ($\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \mathcal{L}_\lambda(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ with $\mathcal{L}_\lambda(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}, i>j}[-y_{j,i}(\theta_i - \theta_j) + \log(1 + e^{\theta_i - \theta_j})] + \frac{1}{2}\lambda\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_2^2$) achieve minimax-optimal entrywise $\ell_\infty$ relative error and exact top-$K$ item identification under the Bradley–Terry–Luce (BTL) model $y_{i,j}^{(l)} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(w_j^*/(w_i^* + w_j^*))$ on an Erdős–Rényi comparison graph $\mathcal{G}_{n,p}$ with $L$ repeated comparisons per edge. Theorem 8 establishes a sharp stationary-distribution perturbation bound for reversible Markov chains in the $\boldsymbol{\pi}^*$-weighted norm $\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\boldsymbol{\pi}^*} = \sqrt{\sum_i (x_i^2 / \pi_i^*)}$ (for row distributions): $\|\boldsymbol{\pi} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*\|_{\boldsymbol{\pi}^*} \le \|\boldsymbol{\pi}^*(\mathbf{P} - \mathbf{P}^*)\|_{\boldsymbol{\pi}^*} / (1 - \rho^* - \|\mathbf{P} - \mathbf{P}^*\|_{\boldsymbol{\pi}^*})$. Combining Theorem 8 with a leave-one-out transition matrix $\mathbf{P}^{(m)}$ (replacing item $m$'s random comparisons by their population means, Section 4.2, Theorem 3) and a leave-one-out gradient-descent trajectory $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{t,(m)}$ for the regularized MLE (Section 4.3, Theorem 4) proves $\|\boldsymbol{\pi} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*\|_\infty / \|\boldsymbol{\pi}^*\|_\infty \lesssim \sqrt{\log n / (npL)}$ and $\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_\infty \lesssim \sqrt{\log n / (npL)}$, achieving exact top-$K$ identification whenever the total sample size satisfies $n^2 p L / 2 \gtrsim n\log n / \Delta_K^2$ where $\Delta_K = (w_K^* - w_{K+1}^*) / w_{\max}^*$ (Theorems 1–2).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Resolved against all 442 pinned AIO concepts and 678 maintained AIMech concepts. Distinct from `AIMech:EntrywiseFirstOrderEigenvectorApproximationAndLeaveOneOutPerturbationAnalysisForLowExpectedRankRandomMatrices` (Abbe, Fan, Wang, & Zhong 2020, which analyzes symmetric low-expected-rank matrices rather than asymmetric reversible Markov chain stationary distributions $\boldsymbol{\pi}^T\mathbf{P} = \boldsymbol{\pi}^T$ and $\ell_2$-regularized BTL logistic MLE trajectories) and from `AIMech:RankCentralityIterativeRandomWalkSpectralRankingFromPairwiseComparisons` (Negahban, Oh, & Shah 2017, which bounds only the aggregate Euclidean $\ell_2$ error $\|\boldsymbol{\pi} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*\|_2 / \|\boldsymbol{\pi}^*\|_2$, suboptimal by a factor of $\Theta(n)$ in sample complexity for top-$K$ identification compared to the entrywise $\ell_\infty$ leave-one-out bounds of Chen, Fan, Ma, & Wang 2019).

Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #claim-reversible-markov-chain-stationary-perturbation-bound
For a Markov chain $\mathbf{P}$ reversible with respect to stationary distribution $\boldsymbol{\pi}^*$ ($\pi_i^* P_{i,j}^* = \pi_j^* P_{j,i}^*$), Theorem 8 proves that any perturbed stochastic matrix $\widehat{\mathbf{P}}$ with stationary distribution $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}$ satisfies $\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\pi}} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*\|_{\boldsymbol{\pi}^*} \le \|\boldsymbol{\pi}^*(\widehat{\mathbf{P}} - \mathbf{P}^*)\|_{\boldsymbol{\pi}^*} / (1 - \rho^* - \|\widehat{\mathbf{P}} - \mathbf{P}^*\|_{\boldsymbol{\pi}^*})$ whenever $\|\widehat{\mathbf{P}} - \mathbf{P}^*\|_{\boldsymbol{\pi}^*} < 1 - \rho^*$, where $\rho^* = \max\{\lambda_2(\mathbf{P}^*), -\lambda_n(\mathbf{P}^*)\}$ (Section 4.1, Theorem 8, Appendix A).
  • supports Source: Spectral Method and Regularized MLE Are Both Optimal for Top-K Ranking · Locator: Section 4.1, Theorem 8 (p. 2219), Appendix A (pp. 2226-2227)
Mechanism #claim-leave-one-out-entrywise-linfinity-spectral-and-regularized-mle
Constructing for each item $m \in [n]$ a leave-one-out transition matrix $\mathbf{P}^{(m)}$ and a leave-one-out regularized log-likelihood $\mathcal{L}_\lambda^{(m)}(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ by replacing comparison outcomes incident to $m$ with their population means decouples statistical dependence on item $m$'s comparisons and proves entrywise $\ell_\infty$ bounds $\|\boldsymbol{\pi} - \boldsymbol{\pi}^*\|_\infty / \|\boldsymbol{\pi}^*\|_\infty \lesssim \kappa\sqrt{\log n / (npL)}$ (Theorem 3) and $\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}} - \boldsymbol{\theta}^*\|_\infty \lesssim \kappa^2\sqrt{\log n / (npL)}$ for $\lambda \asymp \sqrt{np\log n / L}$ (Theorem 4, Sections 3.1 and 4.2–4.3).
  • supports Source: Spectral Method and Regularized MLE Are Both Optimal for Top-K Ranking · Locator: Section 3.1 (Theorems 3-4, pp. 2212-2213), Sections 4.2-4.3 (pp. 2220-2225)
Application #claim-minimax-optimal-top-k-ranking-sample-complexity
Under the BTL model with condition number $\kappa = w_{\max}^*/w_{\min}^* = O(1)$ and relative top-$K$ separation $\Delta_K = (w_K^* - w_{K+1}^*)/w_{\max}^*$, Theorem 1 proves that both the spectral method and regularized MLE recover the exact top-$K$ set $[K]$ with probability $1 - O(n^{-5})$ whenever $n^2 p L / 2 \ge C(n\log n) / \Delta_K^2$, matching the information-theoretic Fano/KL minimax lower bound of Theorem 2 up to universal constants (Sections 2.2–2.3, Theorems 1–2).
  • supports Source: Spectral Method and Regularized MLE Are Both Optimal for Top-K Ranking · Locator: Sections 2.2-2.3, Theorems 1-2 (pp. 2209-2211)

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The optimal sample complexity bounds in Theorems 1, 3, and 4 assume a bounded BTL dynamic range kappa = w^*_max / w^*_min = O(1) and a uniform Erdos-Renyi comparison graph G_{n,p} with p >= C0 log(n)/n; when kappa grows polynomially with n or the comparison graph has a small spectral gap, the sample complexity scales polynomially in kappa.
  • Calculating the regularized MLE via gradient descent (Algorithm 2 / Section 4.3) requires tuning the L2 regularization parameter lambda ~ sqrt(n p log n / L), whereas the spectral method is tuning-free.
