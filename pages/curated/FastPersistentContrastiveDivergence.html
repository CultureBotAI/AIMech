Fast persistent contrastive divergence

Algorithms AIMech:FastPersistentContrastiveDivergence DRAFT
Aliases: FPCD
A persistent-chain RBM learning algorithm that adds rapidly updated, rapidly decaying auxiliary parameters to the sampling energy while retaining separate regular parameters for the model distribution.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is fast PCD from Tieleman and Hinton, not a generic fast implementation of PCD. Its temporary energy overlay is separate from the parameters defining the learned model. It differs from momentum alone, partial smoothing of positive gradients, ordinary persistent sampling and fast-weight memory architectures used for prediction. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Maintain regular parameters theta, auxiliary fast parameters phi and persistent visible configurations. Compute positive statistics using hidden conditional probabilities under theta given the data. Compute negative statistics at the fantasy configurations using hidden conditionals under theta+phi, and advance the persistent chains with the combined sampling parameters. Use the same positive-minus-negative statistic difference g to increment theta by eta_regular*g and set phi to rho*phi+eta_fast*g. The paper’s pseudocode uses rho=19/20, initializes fast parameters at zero and regular parameters at small random values, and uses 100 initially zero visible chains. Its initial experiments keep the fast learning rate constant while reducing the regular rate, and later examine alternative schedules. These numerical settings instantiate the method rather than defining every FPCD variant. The reported model probability is defined by theta alone; the fast overlay shapes fantasy-particle exploration and decays quickly.
  • supports Source: Using Fast Weights to Improve Persistent Contrastive Divergence · Locator: Abstract, PDF p.1; Sections 2–4, temporary sampling overlay and fast-weight updates, pp.2–3; Section 6 pseudocode, p.4; parameter alternatives and limitations, Sections 7–8, pp.5–8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The negative phase uses a changing overlay and is not an unbiased equilibrium estimate for the base theta model merely because each conditional is tractable. Fast parameters intentionally change the sampling landscape; improved mixing is a motivation supported by particular experiments, not a universal guarantee.
  • Section 6 computes h-minus as a vector of conditional probabilities and then writes a visible draw conditioned on h-minus while calling this a full Gibbs update. Exact binary block Gibbs also requires drawing a hidden state; using the probability vector directly gives a distinct mean-field-style transition. This record preserves the intended combined-parameter sampling mechanism without certifying an uninspected implementation or silently resolving that pseudocode ambiguity.
  • The pseudocode computes negative statistics at the current particles before advancing them, then updates both parameter sets. Changing the ordering or using regular instead of combined parameters in the negative conditionals changes the stated procedure. Final conditional expectations for statistics and sampled states for transitions must be distinguished.
  • Fast decay, learning-rate schedules, particle count and initialization influence the procedure. The paper tests alternatives, and its 19/20 decay and 100 chains are not universal optimal settings. Fast PCD does not mean that fast parameters contribute to the final base model’s normalized density.
  • The source’s mixing discussion and restricted MNIST/Micro-NORB experiments do not certify global convergence, asymptotic unbiasedness, universally better likelihood or optimal general hyperparameters. No author-code or empirical-performance reproduction was performed.
