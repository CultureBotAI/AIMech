Diffusion Maps

Algorithms AIMech:DiffusionMaps DRAFT
Aliases: Diffusion Map
A family of spectral embedding algorithms that normalizes a nonnegative symmetric affinity kernel into a reversible Markov operator and represents points by its nontrivial eigenfunctions scaled by powers of their eigenvalues.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the Markov-spectral dimensionality-reduction construction. It is not a denoising generative diffusion model, Isomap shortest-path embedding, or merely a graph Laplacian eigensystem. Kernel choice and diffusion time define the diffusion geometry being represented. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method-scope
Choose an affinity kernel with positive degrees, normalize its rows to transition probabilities, and compute nontrivial eigenpairs of the resulting Markov operator. At diffusion time t, use coordinates λ_l^t ψ_l(x). Under the stated reversible-chain spectral assumptions, squared Euclidean distances in the full coordinates equal a stationary-distribution-weighted squared difference between t-step transition distributions; retaining only leading coordinates gives a truncated approximation. Optional density normalization changes the kernel before row normalization.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The kernel, its bandwidth, density normalization and diffusion time determine the geometry. Ordinary row normalization does not generally remove sampling-density effects.
  • Connectivity and aperiodicity support the usual unique-stationary and decaying-mode interpretation; disconnected graphs or periodic walks need qualified treatment.
  • Coordinate truncation discards spectral contributions. The record does not assert the paper’s relative-error shorthand as a universal pairwise bound, or that every diffusion distance separates distinct points for a rank-deficient transition matrix.
  • Continuum Laplace–Beltrami claims require the stated manifold, sampling and bandwidth limits; they do not establish exact finite-data geometry or universal noise robustness.
