HAC-E network clustering

Algorithms AIMech:HACE DRAFT
Aliases: HAC-E
A hierarchical network-clustering variant that greedily guides agglomeration by the observed edge density between two vertex groups and uses the resulting group hierarchy for link-probability estimation.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #edge-density-guide
The merge guide is the observed number of edges between two disjoint nonempty vertex groups divided by their number of possible cross-group pairs. Replacing HAC-ML’s profile-likelihood guide with this density gives HAC-E. The score favors dense cross-connections and does not directly compare agreement with all external groups.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Different merging scores, PDF p. 4, Equation 9; HAC-ML Equation 5, p. 3
Scope #average-linkage-boundary
For a binary adjacency matrix, the density is exactly mean cross-group adjacency; its complement is average dissimilarity when pair dissimilarity is one minus adjacency. The score after a merge therefore uses group-size weights, rather than an unweighted mean of the two old scores. This algebra identifies a shared linkage criterion, not equivalence of every implementation, search restriction or stopping rule.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Preliminary definitions, PDF p. 2; Equation 9, p. 4; curator derivation in research/discovery/hace-boundaries.py
Scope #score-versus-prediction
The density-based merge guide is distinct from the hierarchical model’s later link estimates. The study estimates densities between left and right vertex groups at tree joins for hierarchical methods. A score comparison alone does not establish a calibrated probability of a missing edge, a complete merge sequence or recovery of true biological communities.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Link prediction, PDF p. 5; Different merging scores, p. 4
Scope #evaluation-scope
The study compares this named variant with HAC-ML and other methods on biological interaction networks using held-out-edge prediction. Positive test pairs are balanced by an equal number of sampled holes, and maximum F scores summarize ranked predictions. This selection changes the negative prevalence relative to deployment. The reported comparisons support a setting-specific empirical assessment, not universal superiority or calibrated real-world link probabilities.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Performance Evaluation and Link prediction, PDF pp. 4–5; Table 2 and Figure 1, p. 6

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The average-adjacency criterion is mathematically related to average linkage. This record preserves the source-named network variant without claiming a novel general linkage function.
  • On a three-vertex path, density favors adjacent singleton pairs while the HAC-ML profile guide favors the nonadjacent pair with identical external connectivity. This is a local score distinction; candidate restrictions and a complete execution were not modeled.
  • This record covers a named 2011 score variant in Park and Bader’s network-clustering study, not a fitted interactome or the entire family of agglomerative clustering methods.
  • The paper defines each alternative by replacing the merge guide. It does not separately specify every candidate-restriction, tie, stopping and terminal-collapse convention for each variant. The detailed HAC-ML implementation and its complexity are not automatically asserted for this variant.
  • The experiments use undirected, unweighted interaction graphs with iterative removal of isolated and degree-one vertices. Missing observed edges can include unobserved biological interactions; sampled holes are not established biological negatives.
  • No author implementation, data or supplementary archive was audited or executed. Finite constructed checks assess local scores and algebraic distinctions, not complete clustering runs, benchmark replication or global optimality. DRAFT remains.
