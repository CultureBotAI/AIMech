Persistent contrastive divergence

Aliases: PCD
A learning algorithm that estimates an energy model’s negative-phase statistics with Markov chains whose states persist across parameter updates, rather than restarting those chains at the current data minibatch.

Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is persistent contrastive divergence as presented for RBMs by Tieleman. Persistence refers to the fantasy-particle sampling state across learning updates, not continual-learning tasks or recurrent sequence-model state. It is an instance of persistent-chain stochastic-approximation learning, not the original data-restarted CD-k rule. Fast PCD adds temporary sampling parameters and remains a separate variant. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Maintain a collection of fantasy-particle configurations independently of which data minibatch is currently presented. Compute positive-phase sufficient statistics from that minibatch, using exact hidden conditional expectations for an RBM. Advance the existing fantasy particles by a small number of Gibbs transitions under the current model parameters and estimate negative-phase statistics from those particles. Update the model parameters from the positive-minus-negative difference and retain the particle states for subsequent iterations. The 2008 experiments use one full Gibbs step per update, one particle per minibatch member and no chain resets; those are specified experimental choices, while persistence is the defining distinction. If the chain distribution tracks the current equilibrium sufficiently closely, its negative statistics approximate those of the model. For fixed particle-distribution R, the instantaneous update can be expressed as the negative derivative of KL(q_data||p_theta)-KL(R||p_theta), with R held fixed; this does not make the whole coupled adaptive process descent on one fixed objective.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Finite transitions, correlated particles and changing parameters can leave negative statistics biased for the current equilibrium expectation. The small-learning-rate justification depends on mixing relative to parameter drift; it is not a guarantee at every finite step size.
  • A persistent chain is neither an independent model sample at each iteration nor a proof of rapid mixing. The original paper discusses difficulties when strong dependencies or large weights slow exploration, and the regularization and learning-rate schedule affect behavior.
  • Exact positive-phase inference is available for the binary RBM formulation inspected here; that property does not transfer automatically to models with interacting latent variables. The paper also examines fully visible MRFs with different update costs and sampling structure.
  • The fixed-R contrastive expression is an instantaneous derivative description. Particle evolution and parameter adaptation remain coupled; the ordinary data-restarted contrastive-divergence objective does not describe that entire process.
  • The retained particles and one-step experimental choice do not imply that conditional models with changing conditioning inputs have the same tracking problem as an unconditional RBM. No universal superiority, global convergence or author-code behavior is certified.
