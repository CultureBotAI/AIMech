ABRA-s-fix fixed-sample betweenness approximation

Algorithms AIMech:ABRASFixedSampleBetweenness DRAFT
Aliases: ABRA-s-fix, ABRA fixed-sample betweenness approximation
A fixed-sample static-graph betweenness algorithm that averages shortest-path fractions over a user-specified number of uniform endpoint pairs and returns both the estimates and a simultaneous Rademacher-based additive error bound, regardless of the size of that bound.

Ten complete AIO contexts and five full native neighbors were assessed. ABRA-s-fix takes a fixed independent positive sample count and returns its achieved bound unconditionally. It is distinct from ABRA-s’s confidence-based progressive stopping, the forced-stop pseudodimension variant and source-pivot or current-flow sampling.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Scope #domain-and-sample-contract
The maintained domain is a finite simple directed or undirected static graph with n>=3, unweighted edges or strictly positive edge lengths, and exact shortest-path counts. The user supplies a positive integer S fixed independently of the samples and a failure budget delta in (0,1). The algorithm takes exactly S independent uniform ordered pairs of distinct vertices, with replacement. Epsilon is not an input stopping threshold.
  • supports Source: ABRA: Approximating Betweenness Centrality in Static and Dynamic Graphs with Rademacher Averages · Locator: Section 4.4, PDF p. 24; Section 4.1 input domain, p. 10; the strict-positive-weight restriction is maintained to avoid unresolved zero-weight path conventions
Mechanism #ordered-pair-fraction-estimator
For every sampled pair (u,v), an internal vertex w contributes f_w(u,v)=sigma_uv(w)/sigma_uv. Source, target and unreachable-pair contributions are zero; unreachable pairs remain part of the n(n-1) population. Return bhat(w)=S^(-1) sum_j f_w(u_j,v_j). Shared shortest-path predecessor and backward suffix-count computations obtain these fractions without choosing just one path or substituting raw path counts.
  • supports Source: ABRA: Approximating Betweenness Centrality in Static and Dynamic Graphs with Rademacher Averages · Locator: Definition 3.1, PDF p. 5; Equation 11 and Algorithms 1–2, pp. 10–13; Section 4.4, p. 24
Mechanism #fixed-sample-error-bound
Retain the distinct node sample vectors V_S and bound their conditional Rademacher average by omega=inf over r>0 of log(sum_{v in V_S} exp(r^2 ||v||_2^2/(2S^2)))/r. For a one-shot failure allocation eta, let L=ln(3/eta) and xi=2omega+[L+sqrt(L(L+4S omega))]/S+sqrt(L/(2S)). Retaining Algorithm 1’s first-stage allocation uses eta=delta/2 and gives an at-least-1-delta guarantee conservatively. A direct one-shot use of Theorem 3.3 may instead use eta=delta; these two bound conventions are explicitly distinguished. Numerical evaluation must conservatively bound omega, and equal vector norms alone do not identify vectors.
  • supports Source: ABRA: Approximating Betweenness Centrality in Static and Dynamic Graphs with Rademacher Averages · Locator: Theorems 3.3–3.4 and Equations 4–6, PDF p. 7; Algorithm 1 lines 19–21, p. 12; Section 4.4 and Theorem 4.10, p. 24; one-shot allocation distinction is explicit curator interpretation
Limitation #output-and-guarantee
After exactly S samples, return the full node estimates and xi even when xi is large. With the stated sampling and conservative-bound assumptions, all absolute errors are simultaneously at most xi with probability at least 1-delta. The achieved tolerance is determined by the sample; it is not guaranteed to meet a user-selected epsilon or preserve ranks. A returned xi greater than one is possible and supplies a vacuous bound for these [0,1] scores. This fixed-size method has no data-dependent termination or stage-wise union requirement.
  • supports Source: ABRA: Approximating Betweenness Centrality in Static and Dynamic Graphs with Rademacher Averages · Locator: Section 4.4 and Theorem 4.10, PDF p. 24; range in Definition 3.1, p. 5; explicit bound examples in research/discovery/abra-fixed-boundaries.py
Limitation #fixed-expectation-boundaries
For the fixed independent sample count, each estimate is unbiased for the n(n-1)-normalized target and has variance Var(f_w)/S, at most 1/(4S); these are explicit curator consequences of the sampling law. On an undirected three-node path, the middle contribution is Bernoulli with mean 1/3. At S=2 the estimate is zero with probability 4/9 despite true score 1/3, and its variance is 1/9. Finite exact enumeration verifies S=1,2,4. Unbiasedness does not imply exact values in one run or carry over automatically to a stopped progressive estimate.
  • supports Source: ABRA: Approximating Betweenness Centrality in Static and Dynamic Graphs with Rademacher Averages · Locator: Equation 11 and independent uniform sampling, PDF pp. 10–11; Section 4.4, p. 24; explicit curator arithmetic in research/discovery/abra-fixed-boundaries.py

It differs from progressive ABRA-s, the pseudodimension-based forced-stop variant and relative top-k or dynamic variants. Section 6.4 and Figure 4/Table 10 compare the fixed method with RK under the manuscript’s historical implementation and datasets; no universal advantage, replicated timing, causal influence or learned prediction is asserted. ⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Fixed positive S is independent of sampled values. Adaptive selection of the final count requires a different validity argument.
  • Unreachable pairs contribute zero without being removed from the population; endpoints do not contribute, but the denominator remains n(n-1).
  • Strict positive edge lengths or unweighted graphs and exact path counts are maintained. Zero-weight cycles, approximate distances/counts and numerical ties need separate conventions.
  • The first-stage delta/2 allocation and one-shot delta allocation are distinguished. Any numerical Rademacher upper bound must remain conservative. No preselected accuracy, rank preservation or generally useful bound at small S is promised.
  • The author manuscript retains placeholder journal fields; no publisher-byte identity is asserted. Full text, selected mathematical pages and all figures were inspected, but not every proof equation visually checked. No author software or benchmark was executed.
  • The finite calculations test selected mathematical boundaries. The record remains DRAFT after author self-review.
