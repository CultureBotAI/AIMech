AdaSum Adaptive Summation for Distributed Training

Algorithms AIMech:AdaSumAdaptiveSummationForDistributedTraining DRAFT
Aliases: AdaSum, Adaptive Summation for Distributed Training, ADASUMRVH
A hyperparameter-free distributed gradient and model-update reduction operator that replaces naive all-reduce summation across data-parallel workers by recursively combining pairs of layer-wise gradients or optimizer parameter deltas (g_1, g_2) via Adasum(g_1, g_2) = (1 - (g_1^T g_2) / (2 ||g_1||_2^2)) g_1 + (1 - (g_1^T g_2) / (2 ||g_2||_2^2)) g_2, derived by averaging the first-order Taylor sequential SGD emulations of executing minibatch b_1 before b_2 and b_2 before b_1 under a rank-one outer-product Fisher Hessian approximation H_i approx g_i g_i^T and locally optimal step size alpha = 1 / ||g_i||_2^2.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from standard synchronous AllReduce summation/averaging, from delay-compensated asynchronous SGD (DC-ASGD, which uses only diagonal g_i odot g_i and requires a tuned hyperparameter), and from per-layer norm trust-ratio optimizers (LARS, LAMB): AdaSum operates across workers via a two-phase recursive vector-halving collective (ADASUMRVH) after local optimizer steps, reducing to the exact sum g_1 + g_2 when g_1 and g_2 are orthogonal and to the exact average (g_1 + g_2)/2 when g_1 = g_2.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #c-r623-sequential-emulation-and-orthogonality-interpolation
Approximating the second-step gradient g_2(w_0 - alpha g_1) by its first-order Taylor expansion g_2 - alpha H_2 g_1 with Fisher Hessian H_2 approx g_2 g_2^T and optimal quadratic step alpha = 1 / ||g_2||_2^2 yields w_{1,2} = w_0 - alpha (g_1 + (1 - (g_2^T g_1)/||g_2||_2^2) g_2); averaging both minibatch execution orders (b_1, b_2) and (b_2, b_1) produces Adasum(g_1, g_2), which equals g_1 + g_2 when g_1^T g_2 = 0 (orthogonal) and (g_1 + g_2)/2 when g_1 = g_2 (parallel).
  • supports Source: Scaling Distributed Training with Adaptive Summation · Locator: Section 3.1-3.5, Eq. (1)-(5), p. 3-4; Appendix A.1-A.2, Eq. (6)-(10), p. 14-15
Mechanism #c-r623-pseudogradient-angle-and-norm-bounds
For independent gradient samples a, b ~ X with mean r = E[X], E[Adasum(a, b)] = (2I - E[a a^T / ||a||_2^2]) r satisfies ||E[X]||_2 <= ||E[Adasum(a, b)]||_2 <= 2 ||E[X]||_2 (Lemma A.3) and forms an angle eta with E[X] satisfying cos(eta) >= min_gamma (2 - cos^2(gamma)) / sqrt(4 - 3 cos^2(gamma)) = 2 sqrt(2) / 3 approx 0.9428 > 0.942 (eta <= 0.108 pi, Lemma A.2), establishing pseudogradient convergence across binary-tree reductions (Theorem A.4).
  • supports Source: Scaling Distributed Training with Adaptive Summation · Locator: Appendix A.3 Lemma A.2, Lemma A.3, Theorem A.4, Eq. (11)-(12), p. 15-16
Application #c-r623-adasumrvh-and-large-batch-scaling
Implemented via a two-phase recursive vector-halving collective (ADASUMRVH, Algorithm 1) applied per layer to post-optimizer parameter deltas with FP64 dot-product accumulation, AdaSum scales Momentum-SGD on ResNet-50 and Adam on BERT-Large from 16K to 64K effective batch size and LAMB on BERT-Large from 64K to 128K effective batch size while converging in up to 30% fewer steps at 64K.
  • supports Source: Scaling Distributed Training with Adaptive Summation · Locator: Section 4.1-4.4 Algorithm 1 & Figure 3, p. 5-8; Section 5.1-5.3 Tables 2-4, p. 8-11
Limitation #c-r623-non-streaming-dot-product-and-parallel-gradient-limitation
Because Adasum(a, b) requires global inner products a^T b and squared norms ||a||_2^2, ||b||_2^2 across full layer tensors before scaling, it cannot be executed as a single-pass elementwise MPI reduction and requires power-of-two recursive vector-halving with extra subgroup dot-product allreduces; moreover, if all N worker gradients are strictly parallel, its convergence rate reduces to 1/N of sequential SGD.
  • supports Source: Scaling Distributed Training with Adaptive Summation · Locator: Section 3.4, p. 4; Section 4.2.1 Algorithm 1, p. 6; Appendix A.3-A.4, p. 16

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Cannot be implemented as a streaming elementwise MPI AllReduce operator because each tree level requires a subgroup allreduce of partial dot products [a . b, a . a, b . b] across full layer slices before applying the scaling factors.
  • Requires double-precision (FP64) accumulation for inner products and squared norms plus dynamic loss scaling on effective gradients when training in FP16 to prevent numerical underflow/overflow.
