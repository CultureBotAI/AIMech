GSAT

Algorithms AIMech:GSAT DRAFT
A randomized local-search SAT procedure that repeatedly flips a Boolean variable giving the largest change toward more satisfied CNF clauses, breaks tied choices randomly, and restarts from a random complete assignment under specified flip and attempt budgets.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the Selman–Levesque–Mitchell GSAT model-finding procedure. It evaluates single-variable flips across the current assignment, rather than first choosing a conflicted variable and then its minimum-conflict domain value. It is not strict hill climbing, a resolution proof procedure, a generic language-model reasoner or every later GSAT/WalkSAT extension. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For up to MAX-TRIES attempts, generate a random complete truth assignment for the input CNF clauses. During each attempt, test the current assignment for satisfaction and, while within MAX-FLIPS, choose a variable whose flip gives the largest increase in the number of satisfied clauses, breaking equally good choices randomly, then flip it. The argmax does not require a positive gain: sideways moves remain possible, and the source also acknowledges occasional decreasing moves. Restart after the per-attempt budget and report search failure after all attempts if no tested satisfying assignment was found. A returned assignment is a directly checkable model; an unsuccessful search does not prove unsatisfiability. Figure1 checks before each flip, so the assignment produced by the last allowed flip is not checked inside its literal loop; a concrete implementation must state whether it adds that final check.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • GSAT is an incomplete model finder under finite budgets. A satisfiable formula may be missed, and the source discusses families that can require exponentially many attempts. Search failure must not be reported as an UNSAT proof.
  • Allowing plateau and occasionally decreasing flips distinguishes the method from strict-improvement hill climbing. Replacing these moves with immediate restarts changes the specified procedure.
  • The Figure1 pre-flip test leaves a final-flip boundary: with one allowed flip on a single positive unit clause, an initially false assignment can become satisfying after the last check. The record exposes this literal pseudocode behavior rather than silently certifying a corrected implementation.
  • MAX-FLIPS, MAX-TRIES, randomization, tie handling and CNF encoding affect outcomes. Experiments on selected satisfiable instances and particular distributions are not universal runtime or success guarantees; no numerical benchmark result is curated.
