Hidden Markov random field

Model Families AIMech:HiddenMarkovRandomField DRAFT
Aliases: HMRF, Hidden Markov field
A latent-variable graphical-model family whose unobserved label field has the Markov property on a specified neighborhood graph, with observations conditionally independent across sites given the labels and each depending only on its corresponding hidden state.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The classical independent-emission construction specifies a hidden label field X and observed field Y, rather than only a generic MRF. It is a restricted hidden–observed pair Markov field: its hidden prior must retain the stated graph and its conditional observation law factors by site. A chain-shaped hidden graph recovers an ordinary HMM, but general spatial graphs are not subtypes of sequential Markov chains. A Gaussian observation distribution does not make the discrete hidden label field a continuous Gaussian random field. This model identity is separate from Gibbs sampling, ICM, EM and the paper’s HMRF-EM fitting framework. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
On a finite graph, choose a proper hidden prior p(x)=Z^(-1)*exp(-sum_c V_c(x_c)) and normalized site emission laws b_i(y_i|x_i). Their joint law is p(x,y)=p(x)*product_i b_i(y_i|x_i). At fixed observed y with positive evidence, p(x|y) is proportional to the same clique prior times the local emission factors. Its negative log density is the prior energy plus sum_i[-log b_i(y_i|x_i)] up to a constant, preserving the hidden neighborhood graph. A local conditional label update conditions on neighboring hidden labels and the local observation. A site posterior marginal instead sums over all other labels given the complete observation field. With independent hidden labels and common class weights the model reduces to independent finite-mixture observations. MAP estimates a joint labeling. In loopy graphs these inference objectives need not admit the exact noniterative recursions available for a tree.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The finite-state Gibbs prior needs a finite positive partition function, and emissions must be normalized with valid support. Gaussian emissions require positive variance. Conditional independence is a modeling restriction, not a demonstrated property of every image or downsampled observation.
  • The report’s Equation(2) gives independence across paired sites for its finite-mixture setup; applying that product to an interacting HMRF would discard the defining label dependencies. The later Equation(8) and Section2.3 joint factorization specify the supported HMRF model.
  • The expanded Gaussian likelihood on PDF p.8 contains a quadratic-sign inconsistency. Equation(18) gives the consistent negative log-emission energy. Figure5 step4 adds likelihood and prior although MAP requires their product, or a sum of log terms as in Equation(14). These printed algorithm displays are not certified implementations.
  • The report’s fitted HMRF-EM uses ICM-estimated neighboring labels in local responsibilities. Those plug-in responsibilities generally differ from exact full-field posterior marginals. Ideal EM monotonicity and exact global MAP are not guaranteed for this approximation. ICM can stop at a local optimum; initialization, empty classes and vanishing Gaussian variances require care.
  • The bias-field correction framework adds assumptions and alternating updates beyond the generic model. A positive multiplicative bias before logarithms and an additive log-bias must be distinguished. No clinical validity, universal segmentation advantage, runtime guarantee or author-code reproduction is asserted.
  • The inspected source is the 20-page FMRIB technical report TR00YZ1, not the later 2001 journal article. Its self-description as novel does not establish first invention; earlier hidden-field literature and the existing pair-field record supply historical context.
