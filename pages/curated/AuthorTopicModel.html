Author-topic model

Model Families AIMech:AuthorTopicModel DRAFT
A generative document model in which each author has a shared topic-proportion distribution and each word is produced by uniformly selecting an author from the document's observed author set, selecting a topic from that author, and selecting a word from that topic.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Scoped to Rosen-Zvi, Griffiths, Steyvers and Smyth's UAI 2004 author-topic model, whose arXiv deposit occurred in 2012. It conditions on a document's authors and pools topic preferences across their documents. It is distinct from document-specific LDA, a pure author-word mixture, author-recipient-topic models and personalized large language models. Latent token-author assignments are model variables, not observed intellectual contributions. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #author-conditioned-law
For fixed topic count T, draw each author topic vector theta_a from a symmetric Dirichlet(alpha) and each topic word vector phi_t from a symmetric Dirichlet(beta). Given a nonempty observed author set A_d, each token draws x uniformly from A_d, z from theta_x, and w from phi_z. Conditional on theta, the document-topic mixture is the arithmetic mean of its authors' topic vectors. Ordinary smoothed LDA is recovered when every document is assigned its own distinct single author and the priors match; sharing one author across documents instead pools their proportions. The author-word model requires deterministic unique topic assignment per author, a constrained degenerate case rather than a typical positive-Dirichlet draw.

Mechanism #collapsed-sampling
The published Gibbs sampler jointly updates each token's author-topic pair after integrating theta and phi. For observed word v, eligible author a in A_d and topic t, the conditional weight is proportional to (n_vt^(-i)+beta)/(n_.t^(-i)+V*beta) times (n_at^(-i)+alpha)/(n_a.^(-i)+T*alpha). Counts exclude the current token; assignments outside A_d have zero weight. The uniform author-selection factor is constant among eligible pairs and cancels in normalization. Dirichlet posterior means conditional on sampled assignments estimate the author-topic and topic-word vectors; averaging across sampled assignments approximates marginal inference. Finite sampling and posterior-mean plug-ins are not exact multiword posterior predictive integration.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Known author sets, uniform token-level author choice, fixed topic count, bag-of-words observations and stationary author preferences constrain the model. The author set is conditioned on, not generated. Coauthors with indistinguishable participation can be unidentifiable, and inferred assignments do not establish actual authorship contributions or expertise.
  • Topic and author probability vectors are conditionally independent given assignments under the conjugate construction, but their marginal posterior is generally coupled. The product of separate marginal posterior factors displayed in Equation (8) must not be taken as a general exact factorization. Reported prediction averages plug-in conditional means, which need not equal integrated multiword probabilities.
  • The historical held-out NIPS split ensures test authors already occur in training and can incorporate observed words from each test document. It does not demonstrate cold-start prediction for unseen authors. LDA overtakes the author-topic model in reported settings as more document words become available; no blanket superiority is asserted.
  • Symmetric-KL author similarity and topic entropy are descriptive applications. They do not validate reviewer selection, establish causal collaboration or supply calibrated measures of author competence. Finite Gibbs runs and restarts do not prove mixing, and no author software or benchmark was executed.
