Conjugate-gradient Monte Carlo

Algorithms AIMech:ConjugateGradientMonteCarlo DRAFT
Aliases: CGMC
A population Markov sampler that obtains a local-optimization anchor from one population member and updates another member along the anchor line with a multiple-try transition targeting a radial-Jacobian-adjusted density.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is Liu–Liang–Wong Section 4.2 CGMC, not the conjugate-gradient linear solver or direct optimization as a substitute for sampling. Its anchor comes from a different stream. The product target is an invariant population law, not a statement that transient streams are independent. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Maintain m≥2 population members targeting a product of pi. Choose a reference member x_r uniformly at random, and deterministically optimize along a gradient or conjugate-gradient direction of log pi to obtain anchor A=g(x_r). Choose x_c uniformly among the other members. For A≠x_c, define e=(A−x_c)/||A−x_c|| and the scalar line target f(r) proportional to |r|^(d−1)pi(A+r e), r on the whole real line. Apply a valid MTM transition along this line, starting at the scalar coordinate of x_c, and replace x_c by A+r_new e while retaining the other members. A scalar proposal and weight construction must respect the same unoriented line when traversed in reverse; a centered Gaussian scalar random-walk proposal of fixed variance with MTM-II target-density selection weights is a concrete compatible choice. The source’s product-invariance argument uses the anchor’s independence from the moved member under the product target.
  • supports Source: The Multiple-Try Method and Local Optimization in Metropolis Sampling · Locator: Sections 4.1–4.2, CGMC steps 1–3, Equation (11), Theorem 2 and Lemma 1, printed pp. 124–125 / PDF pp. 4–5; population diagnostic caveat and discussion, p. 133 / PDF p. 13; Section 2 MTM-II choice, p. 122 / PDF p. 2

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The factor |r|^(d−1) is essential for an anchor line selected through the current point. Replacing the line target with pi(A+r e) alone is not this construction.
  • The optimization anchor must be independent of the moved member under the product stationary law. Optimizing that same member and copying this acceptance scheme is not justified.
  • The zero-length line A=x_c requires a defined degenerate-case policy; the displayed normalized direction is not evaluated at zero. Differentiability and the chosen deterministic optimization procedure have their own requirements.
  • The compatible scalar-proposal statement is an explicit implementation constraint inferred from reversing the unoriented line, not a new algorithm attributed to the source.
  • Invariance does not guarantee population exploration, convergence, independent transient streams or universally reliable convergence diagnostics. Optimization and multiple trials add cost.
