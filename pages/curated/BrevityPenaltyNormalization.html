Brevity-penalty normalization for sequence scoring

Mathematical Methods AIMech:BrevityPenaltyNormalization DRAFT
Aliases: BP-Norm
A sequence rescoring rule that adds a predicted-length brevity penalty in log space to average token log probability: S(x,y)/|y| + min(1−Lpred(x)/|y|,0).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope BP-Norm is Yang et al.’s predicted-length decoding score. It resembles the algebraic structure of BLEU’s brevity penalty but does not compute n-gram precision, use an actual reference translation at inference, or equal the BLEU evaluation metric. It differs from plain average log probability and additive word rewards. Mechanism #method
Predict a target length from the input using the paper’s encoder-state length predictor. For a nonempty hypothesis of length n, combine average log probability S/n with log brevity penalty min(1−Lpred/n,0). For decoding with a genuine finite maximum output length R, a current raw log score S_t≤0 yields an upper bound S_t/R on descendant BP-Norm scores. Compare the maximum active-prefix bound with the best completed BP-Norm score before stopping.

Limitation #predicted-versus-maximum
Equation (13) defines R as a maximum generation length, whereas the experiments identify it with predicted length. The bound is curated only when that value truly bounds the permitted completion lengths; prediction alone is not a mathematical upper bound.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Lpred is a prediction, not a reference length or a proven upper bound on output length. The S_t/R stopping bound requires that admissible completions actually have length at most R.
  • The experiments set R to the predicted length. This supports the stopping guarantee only under an enforced length cap or another justification that all allowed completions obey that bound; a forecast alone is insufficient.
  • The formula requires nonempty hypotheses and a valid length prediction. A BOS-only prefix needs separate handling; length and EOS conventions must be specified.
  • The score is neither a normalized probability nor BLEU itself. Improved search score does not imply better human translation quality.
  • The source’s hyperparameter-free comparison concerns removal of a separately tuned reward constant, not absence of trained models, a length predictor or decoding choices. Finite beam pruning remains approximate.
