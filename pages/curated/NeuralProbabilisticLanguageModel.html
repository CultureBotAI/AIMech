Neural probabilistic language model

Architectures AIMech:NeuralProbabilisticLanguageModel DRAFT
Aliases: NPLM
A neural finite-context language architecture that jointly learns a shared word-embedding lookup and a nonlinear predictor of the next-word distribution using concatenated context embeddings and a vocabulary softmax.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record is scoped to Bengio, Ducharme, Vincent and Jauvin’s published 2003 architecture, especially its tanh feed-forward formulation with optional direct input-to-output weights. It does not designate every neural probabilistic model of language, or assert historical priority over the earlier work acknowledged by the paper. It differs from LBL’s tied bilinear output embeddings and Morin–Bengio’s tree-node-conditioned nonlinear predictor. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Look up each preceding word in a shared matrix C and concatenate the n-1 context embeddings in their specified positions into x. Compute vocabulary logits y=b+W x+U tanh(d+H x), where direct weights W may be set to zero. Apply the vocabulary softmax and multiply next-word conditionals to obtain sequence probabilities under the chosen context convention. Jointly optimize the embeddings and network parameters by stochastic gradient ascent on the corpus log likelihood with regularization; a weight-decay term added to a maximized objective has the negative penalty sign. The input embedding lookup is shared across context positions, while the output matrices and biases are separately learned parameters. Contributions from repeated appearances of the same context word accumulate in its embedding row. The paper also discusses mixing the resulting predictions with an interpolated trigram model, which is an additional ensemble choice.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The specified feed-forward formulation has a finite context window and a fixed vocabulary. It is not a recurrent state model or a tree-normalized output architecture.
  • The flat vocabulary softmax incurs vocabulary-dependent computation even when only the observed next-word likelihood is required. Joint learning is nonconvex and does not guarantee optimality or universally meaningful semantic distances.
  • An input-embedding row absent from an example’s context has zero gradient from that example’s likelihood, even if its word is the target. This statement excludes regularization, which can still affect unused rows. Output-word parameters remain separate and can receive gradients.
  • Sharing the lookup matrix does not discard context order: concatenation places embeddings in different slots with distinct subsequent weights. Optional direct weights change the parameterization but not probability normalization.
  • The paper warns that regularizing W and H without C can permit a compensating growth of embedding scale. Smooth feature sharing motivates generalization, but does not prove correct probabilities for every unseen combination. No benchmark superiority or author-code behavior is certified.
