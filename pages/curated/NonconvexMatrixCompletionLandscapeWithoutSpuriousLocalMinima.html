Nonconvex Matrix Completion Optimization Landscape Without Spurious Local Minima

Mathematical Methods AIMech:NonconvexMatrixCompletionLandscapeWithoutSpuriousLocalMinima DRAFT
Aliases: No Spurious Local Minima in Nonconvex Matrix Completion, Ge-Lee-Ma Strict-Saddle Landscape for Factored Matrix Completion, Global Landscape of Burer-Monteiro Matrix Completion with Row-Norm Regularization
A global nonconvex optimization landscape characterization (introduced by Rong Ge, Jason D. Lee, and Tengyu Ma in 2016) proving that the Burer–Monteiro factored matrix completion objective $f(\boldsymbol{X}) = \frac{1}{2p}\|\mathcal{P}_\Omega(\boldsymbol{M} - \boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^\top)\|_F^2 + \lambda R(\boldsymbol{X})$ over $\boldsymbol{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times r}$ with quartic row-norm incoherence regularizer $R(\boldsymbol{X}) = \sum_{i=1}^d (\|\boldsymbol{X}_i\|_2 - \alpha)_+^4$ (Equation 5.1) has **no spurious local minima** and obeys the global $(\theta,\gamma,\zeta)$-strict saddle property (Definition 2.1). Specifically, when a positive semidefinite rank-$r$ matrix $\boldsymbol{M} = \boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{Z}^\top$ satisfies $\mu$-incoherence ($\|\boldsymbol{Z}_i\|_2 \le \mu\sqrt{r/d}\sigma_1(\boldsymbol{Z})$) and entries are observed independently with probability $p \ge \tilde{\Omega}(\frac{\mu^4 r^6 \kappa^4}{d\varepsilon^2})$, Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 5.3 prove that every critical point $\nabla f(\boldsymbol{X}) = \boldsymbol{0}$ is either (1) a global minimum satisfying $\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^\top = \boldsymbol{M}$ (up to $\varepsilon$ in the noisy case), or (2) a strict saddle point where the Hessian exhibits strictly negative curvature $\lambda_{\min}(\nabla^2 f(\boldsymbol{X})) \le -\Omega(\mu^2 \sigma_r(\boldsymbol{M}) r/d) < 0$. The proof constructs an explicit escape direction $\boldsymbol{\Delta} = \boldsymbol{X} - \boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{R}$ aligned to $\boldsymbol{Z}$ via the Procrustes rotation $\boldsymbol{R}$ and combines concentration of the sampling operator $\|\mathcal{P}_\Omega(\boldsymbol{A}) - p\boldsymbol{A}\|_F^2$ on low-rank incoherent tangent matrices with the directional convexity inequality $\langle \nabla R(\boldsymbol{X}), \boldsymbol{\Delta}\rangle \ge 0$ (Claim C.3, Lemma 5.7) to show that $\langle \boldsymbol{\Delta}, \nabla^2 f(\boldsymbol{X})\boldsymbol{\Delta}\rangle \ge 0$ combined with $\nabla f(\boldsymbol{X}) = \boldsymbol{0}$ forces $\|\boldsymbol{M} - \boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^\top\|_F = 0$.

Distinct from `AIMech:DebiasedNoisyMatrixCompletionInferenceAndUncertaintyQuantification` (Chen, Fan, Ma, & Yan 2019) and `AIMech:ImplicitRegularizationInNonconvexStatisticalEstimationByLeaveOneOutTrajectoryAnalysis` (Ma, Wang, Chi, & Chen 2020), which analyze local convergence from a spectral initialization rather than proving the global absence of spurious local minima and strict-saddle geometry across the entire factor space $\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}$ from arbitrary random initialization.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #claim-strict-saddle-negative-curvature-along-procrustes-alignment-direction
For the full-observation factored objective $g(\boldsymbol{X}) = \frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{M} - \boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^\top\|_F^2$ with $\boldsymbol{M} = \boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{Z}^\top$, Proposition 4.1 and Lemma 4.2 prove that along the optimal Procrustes alignment difference $\boldsymbol{\Delta} = \boldsymbol{X} - \boldsymbol{U}$ (where $\boldsymbol{U} = \boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{R}$ minimizes $\|\boldsymbol{X} - \boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{R}\|_F$ over orthogonal $\boldsymbol{R}\in\mathbb{O}_r$), the directional Hessian quadratic form satisfies $\langle\boldsymbol{\Delta}, \nabla^2 g(\boldsymbol{X})\boldsymbol{\Delta}\rangle = \|\boldsymbol{\Delta}\boldsymbol{X}^\top + \boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{\Delta}^\top\|_F^2 - 2\langle \boldsymbol{M} - \boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^\top, \boldsymbol{\Delta}\boldsymbol{\Delta}^\top\rangle = 2\langle \nabla g(\boldsymbol{X}), \boldsymbol{\Delta}\rangle - \|\boldsymbol{M} - \boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^\top\|_F^2 + 3\|\boldsymbol{\Delta}\boldsymbol{\Delta}^\top\|_F^2 \le 2\langle \nabla g(\boldsymbol{X}), \boldsymbol{\Delta}\rangle - \frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{M} - \boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^\top\|_F^2$, certifying strictly negative curvature whenever $\|\nabla g(\boldsymbol{X})\|_F$ is small and $\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^\top \ne \boldsymbol{M}$ (Sections 3 and 4, Claims 1f–2f, Proposition 4.1, Lemma 4.2).

Mechanism #claim-quartic-row-norm-incoherence-regularizer-directional-monotonicity
For the smooth quartic row-norm regularizer $R(\boldsymbol{X}) = \sum_{i=1}^d (\|\boldsymbol{X}_i\|_2 - \alpha)_+^4$ with threshold $\alpha \ge 2\mu\sqrt{r/d}\sigma_1(\boldsymbol{Z})$, Proposition 5.2, Lemma 5.7, and Claim C.3 prove that for any target factor $\boldsymbol{U}$ with $\|\boldsymbol{U}_i\|_2 \le \alpha/2$, the gradient inner product satisfies $\langle \nabla R(\boldsymbol{X}), \boldsymbol{X} - \boldsymbol{U}\rangle \ge 2 R(\boldsymbol{X}) \ge 0$ and the directional Hessian satisfies $\langle \boldsymbol{\Delta}, \nabla^2 R(\boldsymbol{X})\boldsymbol{\Delta}\rangle \le 6\langle \nabla R(\boldsymbol{X}), \boldsymbol{\Delta}\rangle$, ensuring that every point with small gradient has incoherent rows $\|\boldsymbol{X}_i\|_2 \le 2\alpha$ without introducing any spurious stationary points (Section 5, Equation 5.1, Proposition 5.2, Lemma 5.7, Appendix C, Claim C.3).

Application #claim-global-convergence-of-random-initialized-stochastic-gradient-descent
Because $f(\boldsymbol{X})$ satisfies the global $(\theta,\gamma,\zeta)$-strict saddle property with no spurious local minima whenever $p \ge \tilde{\Omega}(\mu^4 r^6 \kappa^4 / d)$ (Theorems 1.1 and 5.3) and extends to asymmetric rectangular matrices $\boldsymbol{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_1\times d_2}$ via symmetric lifting (Section 6, Theorem 6.1), perturbed/stochastic gradient descent from an arbitrary random initialization converges in polynomial time to a global minimum $\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^\top = \boldsymbol{M}$ without requiring spectral initialization (Section 1, Theorem 1.1; Section 2, Theorem 2.2; Section 5, Theorem 5.3; Section 6, Theorem 6.1).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The global landscape sample complexity bound p >= Omega_tilde(mu^4 r^6 kappa^4 / d) has higher polynomial dependence on the rank r and condition number kappa = sigma_1(M) / sigma_r(M) than local-convergence bounds starting from a spectral initialization.
  • Requires explicit quartic row-norm regularization R(X) when p < 1 to prevent iterates from escaping into high-coherence spikes aligned with the sparse sampling mask Omega.
