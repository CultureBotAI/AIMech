Straight-through Gumbel-Softmax estimator

Mathematical Methods AIMech:StraightThroughGumbelSoftmax DRAFT
Aliases: ST Gumbel-Softmax, ST Gumbel estimator
A surrogate-gradient estimator that uses a hard one-hot argmax of a Gumbel-Softmax sample in the forward computation and substitutes the corresponding continuous softmax relaxation’s derivative in the backward computation.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is Jang and colleagues’ straight-through Gumbel estimator, which retains a discrete forward sample while using a temperature-controlled continuous backward surrogate. It is distinct from using soft Gumbel-Softmax vectors in both passes, from exact differentiation of argmax, from a score-function estimator, and from the broader family of straight-through estimators for other stochastic or quantized units. Mechanism #method
Draw independent Gumbel noise and compute y=softmax((log(alpha)+G)/tau) with positive finite weights and tau>0. In the forward pass, use z as the one-hot vector at argmax(y), equivalently argmax(log(alpha)+G). In the backward pass, approximate the derivative of z with respect to its parameters by the derivative of y, while the downstream computation has received z. Thus the downstream gradient evaluated at the hard forward state is multiplied by the relaxed sample’s Jacobian. For log-weight coordinates ell and fixed noise/temperature, that Jacobian has entries y_i(delta_ij−y_j)/tau. Hard output remains one-hot even at large positive temperature, and its categorical sampling law is independent of tau; temperature changes the backward surrogate. This is a deliberately biased surrogate, not the ordinary derivative of the hard argmax or generally the gradient obtained by evaluating the whole loss on y.

Scope #hard-versus-soft
The source discretizes the relaxed vector by argmax for the forward pass and uses the continuous approximation for backpropagation. Its hard samples remain sparse at high temperature, unlike the positive-temperature relaxed vectors themselves. This separates the forward representation from the surrogate derivative.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Exact categorical forward sampling does not establish an unbiased gradient of the discrete expected loss. The forward/backward mismatch can persist even when the noise distribution is sampled exactly.
  • The downstream computation must supply a usable derivative with respect to the hard vector, typically through a differentiable extension. A purely discrete black-box reward does not acquire such a derivative automatically.
  • The derivative of hard argmax is zero almost everywhere and undefined at ties; substituting the soft Jacobian changes that derivative rather than proving argmax differentiable.
  • Positive temperature and positive finite weights are required by the stated relaxed Jacobian. Temperature annealing, zero-mass categories, finite-precision ties and stability require explicit choices; there is no universal unbiasedness or variance guarantee.
  • The estimator does not equal all straight-through rules or all Concrete training objectives. No author implementation, learned model or empirical speed/quality result is certified.
