ROCK clustering

Algorithms AIMech:ROCKClustering DRAFT
Aliases: ROCK, Robust Clustering using linKs
An agglomerative clustering procedure that merges clusters using shared-neighbor link counts divided by a cluster-size normalization derived from a neighborhood model.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope ROCK is Guha et al.’s RObust Clustering using linKs procedure, designed for categorical or other similarity-based data. Links count common neighbors rather than direct similarities or graph cut weights. The normalized goodness is a heuristic and is not simply maximum shared-neighbor count. The record follows the 1999 conference article, not an uninspected expanded journal version. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
On the clustering sample choose a symmetric similarity in [0,1] and threshold theta. Define N(i)={j!=i:sim(i,j)>=theta} and link(i,j)=|N(i) intersect N(j)|; excluding each point from its own neighbor list reproduces the paper’s worked link counts. For clusters A,B set Link(A,B)=sum_(i in A,j in B)link(i,j). Starting from singletons, merge the pair maximizing g(A,B)=Link(A,B)/[(|A|+|B|)^(1+2f)−|A|^(1+2f)−|B|^(1+2f)]. The paper’s experimental configuration uses f(theta)=(1−theta)/(1+theta); use 0<=theta<1 so f>0 and the denominator is positive. Update cross-cluster links additively after a merge. Stop at the requested cluster count or when every remaining cross-cluster link count is zero. The optional large-data procedure clusters a random sample and labels other observations using representative subsets L_i, choosing the cluster maximizing its threshold-neighbor count divided by (|L_i|+1)^f.
  • supports Source: ROCK: A Robust Clustering Algorithm for Categorical Attributes · Locator: Sections 3.1–3.3 and 4.1–4.6, PDF pp. 4–8 (printed 515–519), Figures 3–4; worked common-neighbor example in Section 3.2, p. 5; experimental f(theta) in Section 5, p. 8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The paper introduces a neighborhood-size model and chooses f(theta) for its experiments; the normalization is not a universal theorem for every supplied similarity or data distribution. theta=1 makes the displayed experimental-normalization denominator zero.
  • The graph and shared-neighbor counts depend on the supplied sample, similarity and threshold. Categorical attribute-value encoding with Jaccard similarity is one stated construction; shared-neighbor links do not guarantee semantic clustering.
  • The source’s prose explicitly allows termination above the requested count when all cross-links vanish. Forcing arbitrary zero-link merges changes that stopping convention.
  • Sampling and outlier details omitted from the conference article are deferred to its cited full report. This record does not invent those procedures or claim a benchmark/runtime advantage. No clustering implementation was executed.
