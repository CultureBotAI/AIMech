Aliases: RTA*, Real-time A-star
A real-time heuristic search algorithm that moves to a neighbor with minimum edge-cost-plus-heuristic estimate and stores the second-best such estimate at the state it leaves, using bounded lookahead or initial heuristics for previously unvisited states.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Maintain a table for states actually visited. At the current non-goal state, enumerate neighboring states and evaluate edge cost plus the stored heuristic of a visited neighbor or a heuristic estimate, optionally produced by bounded minimin lookahead, for a new neighbor. Move to a neighbor with minimum resulting score, resolving ties among minima, and store the second-best score at the old state. With tied best alternatives, the second-best score may equal the best. The table persists during the search and allows revisits and backtracking. Only roots actually visited need enter this table; lookahead descendants need not all be retained. The 1990 paper proves eventual goal finding for a finite problem space with positive edge costs, finite heuristic values and a goal reachable from every state. Its local-optimality theorem concerns decisions relative to the cumulative searched frontier on a tree, not globally shortest returned paths.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A finite reachable goal from the initial state alone is insufficient if an irreversible move can enter a region with no route to any goal. Infinite spaces, zero/negative cost cycles and infinite heuristic barriers are outside the stated completeness theorem.
  • The 1990 tree local-optimality theorem does not extend unchanged to graphs with cycles. The 1988 paper separately sketches a recursive correction of stored values and omits its proof for space; the 1990 article says significant modifications would be required. That variant is not silently included in basic RTA*.
  • Real-time work depends on a fixed lookahead/resource policy and manageable branching and heuristic evaluation. The method is not a uniform constant-time implementation for arbitrary successor sets or lookahead depths, and the visited-state table can grow with execution.
  • The second-best rule assumes alternatives from which that value can be obtained. The inspected method description does not state a general single-successor fallback; an executable implementation must declare this boundary rather than invent an undocumented convention here.
  • Second-best backups can overestimate remaining goal distance when reused across trials. The source motivates LRTA* as a separate multi-trial learning modification. No puzzle benchmark or complete graph-correction proof was audited.
