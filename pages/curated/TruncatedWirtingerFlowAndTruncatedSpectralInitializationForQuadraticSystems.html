Truncated Wirtinger Flow and Truncated Spectral Initialization for Random Quadratic Systems

Algorithms AIMech:TruncatedWirtingerFlowAndTruncatedSpectralInitializationForQuadraticSystems DRAFT
Aliases: Truncated Wirtinger Flow (TWF), Chen-Candes Truncated Poisson Phase Retrieval, Truncated Spectral Initialization and Adaptive Gradient Truncation for Quadratic Sensing
An adaptive nonconvex phase-retrieval and quadratic-sensing algorithm (introduced by Yuxin Chen and Emmanuel J. Candès in 2017) that solves $m$ random quadratic equations $y_i = |\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\boldsymbol{x}|^2$ (or noisy Poisson intensity observations $y_i \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(|\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\boldsymbol{x}|^2)$) in $O(m n \log(1/\epsilon))$ total flops from an information-theoretically optimal sample size $m \asymp n$. Rather than minimizing the heavy-tailed quartic intensity residual, Truncated Wirtinger Flow (TWF, Algorithm 1) maximizes the Poisson log-likelihood $\sum_{i=1}^m [y_i\log(|\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\boldsymbol{z}|^2) - |\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\boldsymbol{z}|^2]$ using two statistically adaptive truncation mechanisms: (1) *Truncated Spectral Initialization* (Equations 2.6–2.7), which computes the leading eigenvector of $\boldsymbol{Y} = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m y_i\boldsymbol{a}_i\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\mathbb{I}_{\{|y_i| \le \alpha_y^2 (\frac{1}{m}\sum_l y_l)\}}$ discarding measurements where $|y_i|$ exceeds $\alpha_y^2\|\boldsymbol{x}\|^2$ so that the summands are bounded and concentrate at $m \asymp n$ via matrix Bernstein; and (2) *Truncated Wirtinger Gradient Updates* $\boldsymbol{z}^{(t+1)} = \boldsymbol{z}^{(t)} + \frac{\mu_t}{m}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{E}_1^{(t)}\cap\mathcal{E}_2^{(t)}} \frac{y_i - |\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\boldsymbol{z}^{(t)}|^2}{\boldsymbol{z}^{(t)*}\boldsymbol{a}_i}\boldsymbol{a}_i$ (Equations 2.4–2.5, 2.8–2.10), where $\mathcal{E}_1^{(t)} = \{\alpha_z^{\mathrm{lb}} \le \frac{|\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\boldsymbol{z}^{(t)}|}{\|\boldsymbol{z}^{(t)}\|} \le \alpha_z^{\mathrm{ub}}\}$ prevents division by small inner products $|\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\boldsymbol{z}^{(t)}|$ and $\mathcal{E}_2^{(t)} = \{|y_i - |\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\boldsymbol{z}^{(t)}|^2| \le \alpha_h K_t \frac{|\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\boldsymbol{z}^{(t)}|}{\|\boldsymbol{z}^{(t)}\|}\}$ (with empirical median residual $K_t = \mathrm{median}_i(|y_i - |\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\boldsymbol{z}^{(t)}|^2|)$) suppresses high-leverage residual outliers. Theorems 1–2 prove linear convergence $\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{z}^{(t)},\boldsymbol{x}) \le \delta_0(1-\rho)^t\|\boldsymbol{x}\|$ with constant step size $\mu_t = \mu_0 \asymp 1$ and minimax-optimal noise stability $\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{z}^{(t)},\boldsymbol{x}) \lesssim \frac{\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_2}{\sqrt{m}\|\boldsymbol{x}\|}$.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Resolved against all 442 pinned AIO concepts and 685 maintained AIMech concepts. Distinct from `AIMech:WirtingerFlowPhaseRetrievalAndSpectralInitialization` (Candès, Li, & Soltanolkotabi 2015, which minimizes the untruncated quartic intensity loss with untruncated spectral initialization, requiring $m \gtrsim n\log n$ samples and vanishing step size $\mu \lesssim 1/n$ due to fourth-moment heavy tails, whereas Chen & Candès 2017 introduce the Poisson log-likelihood formulation, Truncated Spectral Initialization $\mathbb{I}_{\{|y_i| \le \alpha_y^2\lambda_0^2\}}$, and the dual-event gradient truncation rules $\mathcal{E}_1^{(t)} \cap \mathcal{E}_2^{(t)}$ achieving $O(n)$ sample complexity and $O(1)$ step size).

Mechanism #claim-truncated-spectral-initialization-order-n-concentration
Replacing the untruncated weighted covariance matrix by the truncated estimator $\boldsymbol{Y} = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m y_i\boldsymbol{a}_i\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\mathbb{I}_{\{|y_i| \le \alpha_y^2\lambda_0^2\}}$ (Equation 2.7) caps the spectral norm of each summand while preserving the rank-1 population gap $\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{Y}] = \beta_1\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{x}^* + \beta_2\|\boldsymbol{x}\|^2\boldsymbol{I}_n$ with $\beta_1 = 2 - (\alpha_y^4 + 2\alpha_y^2 + 2)e^{-\alpha_y^2/2} > 0$ (Equations B.1–B.2), so Proposition 1 proves $\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{z}^{(0)},\boldsymbol{x}) \le \delta\|\boldsymbol{x}\|$ with $m \ge c_0 n$ Gaussian measurements (Section 2.1, Equations 2.6–2.7, Appendix B, Proposition 1).

Mechanism #claim-dual-event-truncated-poisson-gradient-regularity-condition
By restricting each Poisson score summand $2(\frac{|\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\boldsymbol{z}|^2 - y_i}{\boldsymbol{z}^*\boldsymbol{a}_i})\boldsymbol{a}_i$ to the intersection $\mathcal{E}_1^{(t)} \cap \mathcal{E}_2^{(t)}$ (Equations 2.8–2.10), the denominator $|\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\boldsymbol{z}|$ is bounded away from zero and the residual is adaptively clipped at $\alpha_h K_t |\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\boldsymbol{z}|/\|\boldsymbol{z}\|$ where $0.6\|\boldsymbol{h}\| \le K_t / \|\boldsymbol{z}\| \le 1.2\|\boldsymbol{h}\|$ (Lemma 1); Lemmas 2–6 prove that the truncated gradient $- \nabla\ell_{\mathrm{tr}}(\boldsymbol{z})$ satisfies the local Regularity Condition $RC(\mu,\lambda,\delta)$ with constant parameters $\zeta_1 = 2[1 - \sqrt{2/\pi}\alpha_z^{\mathrm{lb}}e^{-(\alpha_z^{\mathrm{lb}})^2/2}]$ and $\zeta_2 = \mathbb{E}[\xi^2\mathbb{I}_{\{|\xi| \ge \sqrt{1.99}\alpha_h/(2\alpha_z^{\mathrm{ub}})\}}]$ satisfying $2(1 - \zeta_1 + \zeta_2) < 1.99$ (Table 1, Equation 3.8, Sections 5.1–5.5, Lemmas 1–6, Proposition 2).

Application #claim-linear-convergence-in-linear-time-and-minimax-noise-stability
With constant step size $\mu_t \equiv \mu_0$ (e.g. $\mu_0 = 0.2$ for real Gaussian vectors) and $m \ge c_0 n$, Theorem 1 proves that TWF contracts as $\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{z}^{(t)},\boldsymbol{x}) \le \frac{1}{10}(1-\nu)^t\|\boldsymbol{x}\|$ in $O(m n \log(1/\epsilon))$ total flops, and under bounded or Poisson noise $y_i = |\boldsymbol{a}_i^*\boldsymbol{x}|^2 + \eta_i$, Theorem 2 proves $\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{z}^{(t)},\boldsymbol{x}) \lesssim \frac{\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_2}{\sqrt{m}\|\boldsymbol{x}\|} + (1-\nu)^t\|\boldsymbol{x}\|$, matching the information-theoretic Cramér–Rao lower bound of Proposition 3 (Section 2.3, Theorems 1–2, Proposition 3, Section 6).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Theorem 1's O(n) sample complexity proof relies on independent sub-Gaussian measurement vectors a_i; for structured Fourier / Coded Diffraction Pattern (CDP) measurements, an extra polylogarithmic factor in n is still needed for uniform spectral concentration.
  • When the signal-to-noise ratio is extremely low so that the median residual K_t is dominated by large outliers across more than half of the measurements, the empirical quantile clipping threshold can be inflated.
