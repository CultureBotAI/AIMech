Mixtures of Probabilistic Principal Component Analyzers

Model Families AIMech:MixturesOfProbabilisticPCA DRAFT
Aliases: Mixtures of Probabilistic PCA, MPPCA, Mixtures of Probabilistic Principal Component Analysers
A finite Gaussian mixture whose components each have a linear standard-normal latent factor and component-specific isotropic observation noise, giving covariance W_i W_i^T+sigma_i^2 I and probabilistic assignments to local principal-component models.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The normalized mixture distribution is distinct from a single PPCA model, reconstruction-error-only local PCA, a diagonal-noise mixture of factor analyzers, GTM, and predictive mixtures of experts. The model family and its EM or eigendecomposition fitting variants share this record. Mixture components need not share a meaningful latent coordinate system. Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #mixture-density-and-posterior
For nonnegative weights summing to one, p(t)=sum_i pi_i Normal(t;mu_i,C_i), with C_i=W_i W_i^T+sigma_i^2 I and positive component noise variance. Sampling selects a component before drawing its latent factor and observation. Its posterior responsibility is pi_i p(t|i)/p(t); conditional on that component, the latent posterior is Gaussian with mean M_i^(-1)W_i^T(t-mu_i) and covariance sigma_i^2 M_i^(-1), where M_i=W_i^T W_i+sigma_i^2 I. Permuting component labels or rotating a component latent basis leaves the observation distribution unchanged.
  • supports Source: Mixtures of Probabilistic Principal Component Analysers · Locator: Section 3.1, Equations 3-9, p. 5; Section 4, Equations 19-21, p. 9; Appendix A.6, p. 27; Appendix C, Equations 69-72, p. 28
Mechanism #weighted-local-fitting
With component assignments as missing data, EM computes responsibilities from the old mixture, updates each weight by its responsibility mass divided by sample count, and updates the mean by the weighted data mean. It forms a responsibility-weighted covariance around that new mean. A PPCA eigensolution supplies each covariance update when its rank and residual-variance conditions hold. Alternatively, the paper gives a two-stage generalized EM update using one inner latent-variable EM step, with the new mean, old loading/noise parameters and fixed outer responsibilities. This differs from simultaneously substituting new parameters into every posterior calculation.
  • supports Source: Mixtures of Probabilistic Principal Component Analysers · Locator: Section 4, Equations 21-24, p. 9; Appendix C, coupled Equations 75-78 and two-stage Equations 79-84, pp. 29-30
Scope #assignment-objective
Responsibilities depend on the mixing weight, covariance determinant, in-subspace Mahalanobis distance and noise-scaled orthogonal residual. They need not select the component with the smallest orthogonal reconstruction error. Thus likelihood fitting supplies a density and soft partition, not a guarantee of minimum reconstruction error or a unique global manifold parameterization. Posterior latent means retain PPCA shrinkage within each component.
  • supports Source: Mixtures of Probabilistic Principal Component Analysers · Locator: Section 3.3, pp. 6-7; Section 5.2, Equations 25-33 and Figure 3 discussion, pp. 10-14; Section 7, pp. 19-20; curator assignment counterexample
Limitation #fitting-degeneracy
Well-defined exact EM or the specified generalized EM step makes likelihood nondecreasing, but this does not guarantee convergence to a finite local or global maximum from every initialization. Zero loadings remain zero under the displayed iterative loading update. Without constraints preventing covariance collapse, a component centered on an observation can shrink its isotropic variance toward zero and make mixture likelihood unbounded. Empty responsibility masses, rank-deficient covariances and tied eigenvalues also require treatment.
  • supports Source: Mixtures of Probabilistic Principal Component Analysers · Locator: Appendix A.4-A.5, pp. 25-26; Appendix C, Equations 82-84, p. 30; curator deductions and finite collapse calculation qualifying the source’s local-maximum wording
Scope #covariance-family
Latent dimension controls the component covariance restriction. The zero-factor case is spherical Gaussian, while q=d-1 can represent any positive-definite d-dimensional Gaussian covariance by using its smallest eigenvalue for isotropic noise. Intermediate dimensions impose a repeated residual eigenvalue. Component count and latent dimension are model-selection choices; a low-rank loading contribution is compatible with full-rank positive-noise covariance.
  • supports Source: Mixtures of Probabilistic Principal Component Analysers · Locator: Section 6, internal/PDF pp. 16-18; covariance Equation 7 and eigensolution Equations 14-15, pp. 5-6

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Components and latent axes are not uniquely labeled. Local density components need not correspond to semantic classes or physical factors; mixture fitting is nonconvex and sensitive to initialization.
  • The source’s unqualified local-maximum statements are bounded here by stationary starts and the unconstrained mixture-collapse counterexample. Positive variances at finite iterates do not impose a positive lower bound on them.
  • Image compression may use reconstruction-based assignments after density fitting, and digit posterior classification uses a different decision rule. Historical comparisons do not establish universally better compression, clustering or recognition.
  • All 30 primary reprint pages were read. Finite calculations check weighted eigensolution and two-stage EM likelihood increases, assignment disagreement and variance collapse. No historical benchmark or author software was reproduced.
