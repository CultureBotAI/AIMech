SamplePairing

Algorithms AIMech:SamplePairing DRAFT
An image-classification augmentation procedure that averages an anchor image with a randomly selected training image using equal pixel weights and trains against the anchor image’s label, with a training schedule that includes phases without pairing.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope SamplePairing is the equal-image-weight, anchor-label procedure of Inoue, distinct as a training protocol from the standard Beta-distributed input/target MixUp construction. This distinction does not imply that every expected objective differs: under exchangeable pairing and a loss linear in targets, averaging the anchor-label losses over both orders matches the half-and-half target loss for the same averaged image. The paper itself discusses this relationship. It is also distinct from learned Smart Augmentation and from spatial patch replacement in CutMix. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a training anchor, randomly select a partner from the training set without requiring the same class. Apply ordinary image augmentations to the inputs when used, then average corresponding pixels and channels with equal weights. Train on the mixed image using only the anchor label; the partner label is not required by this rule. The published training schedule starts without SamplePairing, enables it after a warm-up, intermittently disables it during training, and finishes with a no-pairing fine-tuning phase. The source uses one warm-up epoch for ILSVRC and 100 for the other datasets, then alternates 300,000 paired / 100,000 unpaired images for ILSVRC or eight paired / two unpaired epochs otherwise. These numerical intervals are experimental choices, not the identity of every use. A learned augmenter, a random mixing ratio, same-class pairing and label interpolation are not required by the original procedure.
  • supports Source: Data Augmentation by Pairing Samples for Images Classification · Locator: Section 3 and Figure 1, PDF pp. 2–3; relationship to MixUp and between-class learning in Section 2, PDF p. 2

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Averaged images can contain incompatible classes; the assigned anchor label is a training convention, not a claim that the mixture has a unique correct semantic label.
  • The paper’s N-squared count describes ordered pairing choices. Commutativity and duplicate inputs can make different pairings produce the same averaged image, so it is not a guarantee of N-squared distinct visual samples.
  • The expected-loss relation to half-and-half soft labels requires the stated symmetry and loss assumptions. It does not equate individual sampled gradients, arbitrary pair distributions, the full training schedules or the original randomly weighted MixUp procedure.
  • The source’s training-accuracy ceiling discussion is idealized and does not establish a universal bound for every finite training run. No benchmark advantage, training-cost equivalence or medical-task result is imported.
