RBFS-kth-root

Algorithms AIMech:RBFSKthRoot DRAFT
Aliases: RBFSkthrt
A bounded-suboptimal recursive best-first search variant that divides a quality budget w between a heuristic factor w^((k-1)/k) and a competitive-threshold factor w^(1/k), using the baseline recursive regeneration and backup organization under the source’s qualified frontier and cost assumptions.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the 2015 variant coupling heuristic factor w^((k-1)/k) to competitive-threshold factor w^(1/k). It is distinct from additive epsilon, histogram-controlled re-expansion, ordinary weighted A* and baseline RBFS as parameterized procedures. The source-local RBFSkthrt label is retained; endpoint coincidences and a shared w-quality bound do not establish aliases or identical visits. Nonnegative path cost, goal normalization, admissible heuristic and optimal-path frontier qualifications remain explicit, along with finite w>=1 and positive integer k. The formula modifies the continuation comparison rather than repeatedly scaling every recursive bound assignment. Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #split-weight-and-threshold
For a finite quality budget w>=1 and positive integer split choice k, the described variant uses f_prime(n)=g(n)+w^((k-1)/k)*h(n) and relaxes the competitive comparison to F(best)<=w^(1/k)*B. For k=2 the two factors are both sqrt(w): part of the budget weights the heuristic and part relaxes the competitive threshold.
  • supports Source: Recursive Best-First Search with Bounded Overhead · Locator: Author PDF p.3 / official p.1153, second variant paragraph and displayed generalization
Mechanism #baseline-structure-with-modified-evaluation
The proposal changes the baseline evaluation and the upper-bound comparison, while retaining the RBFS organization of conditional backed-up-value inheritance, best-child recursion and return-value backup using the modified evaluation. It does not instruct an implementation to multiply every recursive bound assignment repeatedly; the baseline finite-F and empty-successor conditions remain part of the scoped interpretation.
  • supports Source: Recursive Best-First Search with Bounded Overhead · Locator: Author PDF p.2 / official p.1152, Figure 1; author p.3 / official p.1153, kth-root paragraph identifying line 9
Scope #source-stated-quality-bound
Theorem 1 states that the goal expanded by RBFS-kth-root is w-admissible with an admissible heuristic. Under the explicit nonnegative-path-cost, goal-normalization and optimal-path frontier premises used in its algebra, the two factors combine to bound the returned cost by w times optimal cost. This is a qualified report of the source theorem, without an independent implementation-correctness certification.
  • supports Source: Recursive Best-First Search with Bounded Overhead · Locator: Author PDF p.3 / official p.1153, Theorem 1 and proof
Scope #parameter-and-optimal-endpoint
For its stated w-quality guarantee, the source presents kth-root splitting without a separate epsilon or the additive variant’s post-first-goal branch-and-bound requirement. The choices w and k remain parameters. At w=1 both factors equal 1, so the additional weighting and threshold relaxation disappear algebraically; the source’s emphasis on suboptimal search does not make that endpoint undefined.
  • supports Source: Recursive Best-First Search with Bounded Overhead · Locator: Author PDF p.3 / official p.1153, paragraph before Theorem 1; algebraic specialization of the displayed factors

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The printed theorem explicitly states heuristic admissibility; the complete algebra also uses a finite w>=1, positive integer k, nonnegative accumulated path costs, h(goal)=0 and a suitable optimal-path frontier witness. These are explicit interpretive premises here, not a claim that every one is printed in the theorem statement. Arbitrary negative-cost or incorrectly normalized-goal cases are outside the reported guarantee.
  • The crucial comparison to an unresolved optimal-path frontier value is part of the source proof. Its preservation through all duplicate-handling, cycle, regeneration and tie cases is not independently certified by this draft. A bounded algebra check is not a full correctness, completeness or termination proof for arbitrary graphs.
  • The method is described through changes to Figure 1 rather than its own complete listing. Root initialization, finite/infinite arithmetic, dead ends, tie handling and the propagation of uninflated competitive bounds require explicit conventions. Repeatedly multiplying every recursive bound assignment is not specified by the published line-9 modification.
  • The method is not parameter-free: w and the split choice k remain. Its w=1 and k=1 algebraic specializations do not establish a global alias or a monotonic performance law; the source’s k=5 experimental choice is not a universal default or an automatic selection rule.
  • No O(N) regeneration-overhead theorem is attached to this variant. The histogram-controlled method’s extra values, distribution/count assumptions and Theorem 2 are a separate construction. Fewer backtracks or a better time/quality tradeoff on every domain is not guaranteed by the split formula.
  • Depth-linear path/sibling storage assumes bounded branching and per-node data. Duplicate paths, cycles, heuristic/state storage and optional transposition tables need separate analysis; the Dockyard study’s unlimited table does not demonstrate a path-only total-memory bound.
  • The unit-cost and square-root-cost puzzle and Dockyard observations use particular heuristics, k values and historical implementations. They do not establish universal or current-hardware speedups, and the paper’s anytime RBFS-versus-RBFS_CR figure is not a kth-root result. No algorithm or benchmark has been executed for this draft.
  • The two 2015 PDF artifacts represent the same work despite byte and pagination differences. The 2024 ILBFS epsilon/RBFS_CR terminology, prior RBFS versions and exact code/experimental reproduction remain separate source questions; neither shared terminology nor endpoint coincidences justify merging kth-root with the additive or histogram variants.
