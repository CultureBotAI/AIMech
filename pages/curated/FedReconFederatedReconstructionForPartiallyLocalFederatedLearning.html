FedRecon (Federated Reconstruction for Partially Local Federated Learning)

Aliases: FedRecon, Federated Reconstruction, Stateless Partially Local Federated Learning via Reconstruction
A stateless partially local federated learning framework (Singhal, Sidahmed, Garrett, Wu, Rush, & Prakash 2021, Sections 3-4, Equations 1-2, Algorithm 1, Proposition 4.1) that partitions model parameters into shared global parameters g in G and private local parameters l_i in L without requiring clients to persist local state across rounds: in each round t, each selected client i in S^{(t)} splits its local dataset D_i into a reconstruction support set R_i and an update query set U_i, initializes local parameters l_i^{(t,0)} fresh, performs k_r gradient descent steps on R_i with frozen g^{(t)} to reconstruct l_i^{(t)} = R(D_i, g^{(t)}, L_i) = argmin_{l_i in L} L_i(g^{(t)}, l_i) (Equation 1), performs k_u gradient descent steps on U_i with frozen reconstructed l_i^{(t)} to update g_i^{(t)}, discards l_i^{(t)}, and returns only the global parameter update Delta_i^{(t)} = g^{(t)} - g_i^{(t)} to the server (Algorithm 1).

Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from stateful partially local methods such as FedPer (AIMech:FedPerFederatedLearningWithPersonalizationLayers, Arivazhagan et al. 2019), FedRep (AIMech:FedRepExploitingSharedRepresentationsForPersonalizedFederatedLearning, Collins et al. 2021), and LG-FedAvg (AIMech:LgFedAvgLocalGlobalRepresentationsFederatedLearning, Liang et al. 2020), all of which require clients to persistently store their private parameters across communication rounds and cannot directly evaluate on unseen stateless cross-device clients without a reconstruction step; FedRecon discards local parameters after every round and reconstructs them on the fly via support/query splitting, naturally generalizing to unseen clients at inference time via ReconEval.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #r481-stateless-on-the-fly-reconstruction-and-reconevaluation-mechanism
Instead of maintaining persistent client state l_i across rounds, FedRecon reconstructs l_i^{(t)} from scratch on each active client's reconstruction split R_i via k_r gradient steps with frozen global parameters g^{(t)} before updating g_i^{(t)} on update split U_i and discarding l_i^{(t)}; at inference time (ReconEval), any unseen client reconstructs its personal parameters l_i from R_i in k_r steps and evaluates on U_i (Singhal et al. 2021, Section 3, Algorithm 1, pp. 3-4).
  • supports Source: Federated Reconstruction: Partially Local Federated Learning · Locator: Section 3 (Equations 1-2, Algorithm 1), pp. 3-4
Scope #r481-first-order-maml-equivalence-and-bilevel-convergence-scope
Proposition 4.1 proves that for one reconstruction step (k_r = 1 on R_i with step size alpha_r) and one global update step (k_u = 1 on U_i), FedRecon optimizes the bi-level meta-learning objective min_g (1/|C|) sum_i F_i(g) = L_i(g, l_i - alpha_r nabla_l L_i(g, l_i)) using the first-order MAML gradient nabla_g L_{U_i}(g, l_i^{(t)}) (omitting the expensive second-order Hessian cross-term alpha_r nabla_g nabla_l L_i) and converges to an O(alpha_r)-stationary point at rate O(1/sqrt(T) + alpha_r) in smooth non-convex settings (Singhal et al. 2021, Section 4 & Appendix C).
  • supports Source: Federated Reconstruction: Partially Local Federated Learning · Locator: Section 4 (Proposition 4.1), pp. 5-6, and Appendix C (Proposition C.4), pp. 14-16
Application #r481-federated-matrix-factorization-and-next-word-prediction-application
Applied to federated collaborative filtering via matrix factorization on MovieLens-1M (where user embeddings U_i are reconstructed locally and item embeddings V are aggregated globally) and personalized next-word prediction on StackOverflow with user-specific vocabulary bias/head layers (Singhal et al. 2021, Section 5, Figures 2-4, Tables 1-2).
  • supports Source: Federated Reconstruction: Partially Local Federated Learning · Locator: Section 5.1-5.2 (Figures 2-4, Tables 1-2), pp. 6-9
Limitation #r481-support-split-requirement-for-reconstruction-limitation
Requires every participating and evaluated client to hold a labeled support subset R_i with enough local examples to reconstruct l_i^{(t)} in k_r steps without overfitting before global updates or inference can be performed (Singhal et al. 2021, Section 3 & Section 5.1, pp. 4 & 7).
  • supports Source: Federated Reconstruction: Partially Local Federated Learning · Locator: Section 3 (Dataset Splitting), p. 4, and Section 5.1, p. 7

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Requires splitting each client's local dataset D_i into separate reconstruction (R_i) and update (U_i) subsets, reducing the sample size available for each phase.
  • Performs k_r reconstruction gradient steps in every round from scratch, adding local compute overhead compared to stateful partially local methods.
