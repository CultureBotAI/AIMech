CANITA Compressed Accelerated Gradient Method for Distributed Convex Optimization

Algorithms AIMech:CanitaCompressedAcceleratedGradientMethodForDistributedConvexOptimization DRAFT
Aliases: CANITA, Compressed Accelerated Gradient Method CANITA, CANITA Distributed Convex Optimization Algorithm
A compressed Nesterov-style accelerated gradient algorithm for distributed smooth convex optimization across n machines (Algorithm 1) that couples three momentum-coupled iterate sequences (x^t, y^t, z^t) with a randomized loopless control-variate reference point w^t (updated to z^t with probability p_t) and worker-side DIANA-style gradient shifts h_i^{t+1} = h_i^t + alpha_t Q_i^t(nabla f_i(w^t) - h_i^t) under randomized unbiased (omega)-compressors, achieving the accelerated O(sqrt((1 + omega) L / eps) + omega * (L / (n * eps))^{1/3} + (omega * L / (n * eps))^{1/2}) communication complexity in general smooth convex regimes without strong convexity.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from non-accelerated DIANA (Mishchenko et al., 2019; O((1 + omega/n) L / eps) in general convex settings), ADIANA (Li et al., ICML 2020; requires strong convexity mu > 0), and uncompressed ANITA (Li, 2021). CANITA (Li & Richtarik, NeurIPS 2021, arXiv:2107.09461) is the first distributed method with communication compression to achieve the accelerated O(sqrt(L/eps)) leading term on general smooth convex objectives.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #canita-dual-compressed-difference-estimator-with-probabilistic-anchor-mechanism
At each communication round t, CANITA forms the extrapolated query point y^t = theta_t x^t + (1 - theta_t) w^t, each worker i transmits two compressed gradient differences Q_i^t(nabla f_i(y^t) - h_i^t) and Q_i^t(nabla f_i(w^t) - h_i^t) to form the global gradient estimator g^t = h^t + (1/n) sum_i Q_i^t(nabla f_i(y^t) - h_i^t) and shift update h_i^{t+1} = h_i^t + alpha_t Q_i^t(nabla f_i(w^t) - h_i^t), updates x^{t+1} = x^t - (eta_t/theta_t) g^t and z^t = theta_t x^{t+1} + (1 - theta_t) w^t, and resets w^{t+1} = z^t with probability p_t (or keeps w^t with probability 1 - p_t).
  • supports Source: CANITA: Faster Rates for Distributed Convex Optimization with Communication Compression · Locator: Section 4 Algorithm 1 & Equations (3)-(4) p. 5
Scope #canita-accelerated-general-convex-communication-complexity-scope
Under L-average smoothness (Assumption 1) and unbiased omega-compression (Definition 1) with theta_t = 3(1+b)/(t + 9(1+b+omega)), p_t = 1/(1+b), alpha_t = 1/(1+omega), and b = min{omega, sqrt(omega(1+omega)^2 / n)} (Theorem 2 & Corollary 1), CANITA achieves an eps-suboptimal solution f(z^T) - f(x^*) <= eps in O(sqrt((1+omega) L / eps) + omega * (L / (n * eps))^{1/3} + (omega * L / (n * eps))^{1/2}) communication rounds, achieving strictly O(sqrt(L/eps)) round complexity when n >= omega^3.
  • supports Source: CANITA: Faster Rates for Distributed Convex Optimization with Communication Compression · Locator: Section 5 Theorem 1, Theorem 2 & Corollary 1 pp. 6-7
  • supports Source: CANITA: Faster Rates for Distributed Convex Optimization with Communication Compression (arXiv abstract) · Locator: Abstract
Limitation #canita-unbiased-compressor-and-double-message-per-round-limitation
CANITA's convergence analysis requires randomized unbiased compression operators satisfying E[Q(x)] = x and E[||Q(x) - x||^2] <= omega ||x||^2 (excluding contractive biased compressors such as Top-k without error feedback) and transmits two compressed vectors per worker per round (for y^t and w^t).
  • supports Source: CANITA: Faster Rates for Distributed Convex Optimization with Communication Compression · Locator: Section 3 Definition 1 p. 4 & Algorithm 1 Line 5 p. 5
Application #canita-distributed-convex-logistic-and-linear-regression-application
Applied to distributed regularized logistic regression and unregularized convex learning across multiple worker nodes using randomized sparsification (Rand-k) and quantization (QSGD).
  • supports Source: CANITA: Faster Rates for Distributed Convex Optimization with Communication Compression · Locator: Section 3 Examples 1-2 p. 4 & Section 6 p. 9

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Requires unbiased omega-compression operators (excluding contractive biased compressors without error feedback).
  • Transmits two compressed gradient differences per worker in each communication round.
