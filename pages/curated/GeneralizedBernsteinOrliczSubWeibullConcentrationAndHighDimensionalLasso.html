Generalized Bernstein-Orlicz sub-Weibull concentration and high-dimensional misspecified Lasso

Mathematical Methods AIMech:GeneralizedBernsteinOrliczSubWeibullConcentrationAndHighDimensionalLasso DRAFT
Aliases: Generalized Bernstein-Orlicz (GBO) Norm and Sub-Weibull Concentration, Sub-Weibull GBO Norm and High-Dimensional Covariance and Lasso Framework, Marginal Sub-Weibull High-Dimensional Covariance Estimation and Misspecified Lasso
Generalized Bernstein-Orlicz sub-Weibull concentration and high-dimensional misspecified Lasso is a non-asymptotic heavy-tailed probability and high-dimensional statistical estimation methodology introduced by Kuchibhotla and Chakrabortty (2022, Information and Inference: A Journal of the IMA 11(4):1389-1456, arXiv:1804.02605v4, DOI:10.1093/imaiai/iaac012). It unifies sub-Gaussian (alpha = 2), sub-exponential (alpha = 1), and heavy-tailed Weibull-type (alpha > 0, including non-convex Orlicz regimes alpha < 1 where moment-generating functions need not exist) random variables via the sub-Weibull(alpha) Orlicz quasi-norm ||X||_{psi_alpha} := inf{eta > 0 : E[psi_alpha(|X|/eta)] <= 1} with psi_alpha(x) = exp(x^alpha) - 1 (Definitions 2.1-2.2), and introduces the Generalized Bernstein-Orlicz (GBO) quasi-norm ||X||_{Psi_{alpha,L}} := inf{eta > 0 : E[Psi_{alpha,L}(|X|/eta)] <= 1} defined by its inverse function Psi_{alpha,L}^{-1}(t) := sqrt(log(1 + t)) + L(log(1 + t))^{1/alpha} for alpha > 0, L >= 0 (Definition 2.3, Equation 2.5). The GBO norm is equivalent up to factor 2 to the closed-form two-regime Orlicz quasi-norm ||X||_{phi_{alpha,L}} with phi_{alpha,L}(x) = exp(min{x^2, (x/L)^alpha}) - 1 (Proposition A.1), yields the sharp two-regime Gaussian-plus-Weibull tail inequality P(|X| >= ||X||_{Psi_{alpha,L}}{sqrt(t) + L t^{1/alpha}}) <= 2 exp(-t) (Proposition A.3), satisfies Latala-type and Adamczak-Hoffmann-Jorgensen variance-scaling concentration bounds for sums of independent random variables (Theorems 3.1-3.3) and high-dimensional maxima improving Chernozhukov-Chetverikov-Kato (Theorem 3.4, Remark 3.2), obeys a Holder-type product Orlicz inequality ||prod_{i=1}^k W_i||_{psi_beta} <= prod_{i=1}^k ||W_i||_{psi_{alpha_i}} for 1/beta = sum_{i=1}^k 1/alpha_i (Proposition D.2), extends to suprema of unbounded empirical processes with sub-Weibull envelopes (Theorem B.1), and formally separates marginal sub-Weibull tails ||X||_{M,psi_alpha} := max_{1<=j<=q} ||X(j)||_{psi_alpha} from joint sub-Weibull tails ||X||_{J,psi_alpha} := sup_{||theta||_2=1} ||X^T theta||_{psi_alpha} (Definitions 2.4-2.5) to establish non-asymptotic rates for elementwise covariance estimation (Theorems 4.1-4.2), k-sparse operator norm RIP_n(k) (Theorem 4.3), unified Restricted Strong Convexity (RSC) and free-sparsity s-optimized Restricted Eigenvalue RE(k) sample complexity (Theorem 4.4, Section 4.3.1), and hard-sparsity-free oracle inequalities for high-dimensional misspecified Lasso under sub-Weibull or polynomial-tailed errors (Theorems 4.5-4.6, Proposition E.1).

Section 2 and Appendix A (pp. 4-8, 15, 30-32, 48-55, Definitions 2.1-2.5, Equation 2.5, Propositions A.1-A.7) define the sub-Weibull(alpha) Orlicz quasi-norm ||X||_{psi_alpha} := inf{eta > 0 : E[exp(|X|^alpha / eta^alpha)] <= 2} for alpha > 0 and the Generalized Bernstein-Orlicz (GBO) quasi-norm ||X||_{Psi_{alpha,L}} := inf{eta > 0 : E[Psi_{alpha,L}(|X|/eta)] <= 1} with inverse Psi_{alpha,L}^{-1}(t) := sqrt(log(1 + t)) + L(log(1 + t))^{1/alpha}, proving: (i) equivalence ||X||_{Psi_{alpha,L}} <= ||X||_{phi_{alpha,L}} <= 2||X||_{Psi_{alpha,L}} with the closed-form two-regime Orlicz function phi_{alpha,L}(x) = exp(min{x^2, (x/L)^alpha}) - 1 (Proposition A.1); (ii) the sharp two-regime tail bound P(|X| >= ||X||_{Psi_{alpha,L}}{sqrt(t) + L t^{1/alpha}}) <= 2 exp(-t) (Proposition A.3) and moment equivalence sup_{p>=1}(E|X|^p)^{1/p}/(sqrt(p) + L p^{1/alpha}) ~ ||X||_{Psi_{alpha,L}} (Proposition A.4); (iii) the quasi-norm triangle inequality ||X + Y||_{Psi_{alpha,L}} <= Q_alpha(||X||_{Psi_{alpha,L}} + ||Y||_{Psi_{alpha,L}}) with Q_alpha = 1 for alpha >= 1 and Q_alpha = 2e(4/alpha)^{1/alpha} for alpha in (0, 1) (Proposition A.5) and finite/infinite maximal inequalities (Propositions A.6-A.7); and (iv) for random vectors X in R^q, strict separation between the marginal sub-Weibull norm ||X||_{M,psi_alpha} := max_{1<=j<=q} ||X(j)||_{psi_alpha} and the joint sub-Weibull norm ||X||_{J,psi_alpha} := sup_{||theta||_2=1} ||X^T theta||_{psi_alpha}, where under collinearity X(1) = ... = X(q), ||X||_{J,psi_alpha} = sqrt(q) ||X(1)||_{psi_alpha} diverges as sqrt(q) while ||X||_{M,psi_alpha} = ||X(1)||_{psi_alpha} remains dimension-free (Section 4, Equation 4.1).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Quasi-Norm Triangle Factor Q_alpha and Effective Exponent alpha^* = min{alpha, 1} for alpha < 1: When alpha in (0, 1), psi_alpha(x) = exp(x^alpha) - 1 is non-convex near the origin, so ||.||_{psi_alpha} and ||.||_{Psi_{alpha,L}} are quasi-norms satisfying the triangle inequality only up to Q_alpha = 2e(4/alpha)^{1/alpha} > 1 (Proposition A.5), and the heavy-tail exponent for sums of independent sub-Weibull(alpha) random variables is alpha^* = min{alpha, 1} rather than alpha (Theorems 3.1-3.3).
  • Quadratic vs. Linear Sparsity Sample Complexity Under Marginal vs. Joint Sub-Weibull Tails: Under purely marginal sub-Weibull tails ||X_i||_{M,psi_alpha} <= K_{n,p} without a joint sub-Weibull bound, the k-sparse operator norm bound Xi_{n,k}^{(M)} = k Delta_n scales linearly in k (Theorem 4.3(a)), so optimizing the free parameter s in Theorem 4.4 selects s = 1 and requires sample size n >> k^2 log p for the Restricted Eigenvalue condition RE(k), whereas joint sub-Weibull tails achieve n >> k log(ep/k) by selecting s proportional to k (Section 4.3.1, Equations 4.24-4.27).
  • Primary-Source Typographical Notes in arXiv:1804.02605v4: Verified five self-correcting typographical slips in the primary manuscript: (1) Theorem 3.1 (p. 9) prints 'b = (a_1 ||X_1||_{psi_alpha}, ..., a_n ||X_i||_{psi_alpha})' where 'X_i' in the last entry should be 'X_n'; (2) Proof of Proposition C.1 (p. 49) prints an extraneous factor 'L' in 'e C_2 L (log(1+t)^4)^{1/alpha}'; (3) Proof of Proposition D.2 (p. 57) prints '|W_1/alpha_1|^{alpha_1}(beta/alpha_1) + |W_2/eta_2|^{alpha_1}(beta/alpha_2)' where 'alpha_1' in the first denominator should be 'eta_1' and the exponent 'alpha_1' on the second term should be 'alpha_2'; (4) Proof of Theorem 4.5 (p. 60) prints 'max_{1<=i<=n} ||X_i(j) varepsilon_j||_{psi_gamma}' where 'varepsilon_j' should be 'varepsilon_i'; and (5) Proposition E.1 (p. 63) prints '||beta^*(S^c)||_1^2' for '||beta_0(S^c)||_1^2' and Equation (E.11) on p. 64 (line 1) prints '32||beta_0(S^c)||_2^2' before writing '1728(Xi_{n,|S|}^{(M)}/|S|) ||beta_0(S^c)||_1^2'.
