Power-of-Choice (Biased Loss-Based Client Selection Strategies for Federated Learning: pow-d, cpow-d, and rpow-d)

Algorithms AIMech:PowerOfChoiceBiasedLossClientSelectionForFederatedLearning DRAFT
Aliases: Power-of-Choice Client Selection, pow-d, cpow-d, and rpow-d Federated Client Selection, Biased Client Selection with Selection Skew in Federated Learning
A biased client-selection framework and theoretical convergence analysis for partial-participation federated learning (Cho, Wang, & Joshi 2022, Sections 3-4, Definitions 3.1-3.2, Equations 4-8, Theorem 3.1, Theorem A.1, Appendix F Algorithms 1-2) that characterizes any client selection policy pi mapping the global model w to an active cohort S(pi, w) of size m = max(C K, 1) via the local-global objective gap Gamma = F^* - sum_{k=1}^K p_k F_k^* >= 0 (Definition 3.1, Equation 4) and the selection skew rho(S(pi, w), w') = E_{S(pi,w)}[(1/m) sum_{k in S(pi,w)} (F_k(w') - F_k^*)] / (F(w') - sum_{k=1}^K p_k F_k^*) with extremal bounds rho_bar = min_{w,w'} rho(S(pi, w), w') and rho_tilde = max_w rho(S(pi, w), w^*) (Definition 3.2, Equations 5-6). Under L-smooth, mu-strongly convex objectives, Theorem 3.1 proves that biasing selection toward higher-loss clients (rho_bar > 1) accelerates the O(1 / (T rho_bar)) vanishing error term by a factor of rho_bar while introducing a bounded solution bias Q(rho_bar, rho_tilde) = (8 L Gamma / (3 mu)) (rho_tilde / rho_bar - 1) (Equation 7). To span this speed-bias trade-off efficiently, Power-of-Choice (pi_{pow-d}) first samples a candidate set A of d clients (m <= d <= K) without replacement in proportion to dataset sizes p_k, evaluates or estimates their local losses F_k(w_bar^{(t)}), and selects the m clients in A with the largest local losses, with resource-saving variants pi_{cpow-d} (mini-batch loss estimation, Algorithm 1) and pi_{rpow-d} (cached cumulative local training loss piggybacked on model uploads without extra communication, Algorithm 2).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from unbiased partial-participation FedAvg (McMahan et al. 2017; Li et al. 2020), which corresponds to the d = m special case (rho_bar = rho_tilde = 1, zero selection bias but no O(1/rho_bar) convergence acceleration); and distinct from q-Fair Federated Learning (AIMech:QFairFederatedLearningAlphaFairResourceAllocation, Li et al. 2020), which reweights the global objective to sum_k (p_k/(q+1)) F_k(w)^{q+1} under unbiased client sampling rather than altering the client selection policy via power-of-d highest-loss candidate filtering.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #r491-claim-1
Theorem 3.1 (Equation 7) and Theorem A.1 (Equation 8) prove that for L-smooth, mu-strongly convex local objectives with local-global objective gap Gamma = F^* - sum_{k=1}^K p_k F_k^* and selection skew bounds rho_bar <= rho_tilde (Definitions 3.1-3.2, Equations 4-6), FedAvg with decaying step size eta_t = 1 / (mu(t + gamma)) satisfies E[F(w_bar^{(T)})] - F^* <= (1 / (T + gamma)) [4 L (32 tau^2 G^2 + sigma^2/m) / (3 mu^2 rho_bar) + 8 L^2 Gamma / mu^2 + L gamma ||w_bar^{(0)} - w^*||^2 / 2] + (8 L Gamma / (3 mu)) (rho_tilde / rho_bar - 1), accelerating transient convergence by rho_bar.
  • supports Source: Client Selection in Federated Learning: Convergence Analysis and Power-of-Choice Selection Strategies · Locator: Section 3, pp. 4-5, Definitions 3.1-3.2, Equations 4-7, Theorem 3.1; Appendix A, p. 12, Theorem A.1
Mechanism #r491-claim-2
Power-of-Choice (pi_{pow-d}) samples d candidate clients (m <= d <= K) without replacement with probabilities p_k and selects the m clients with highest local loss F_k(w_bar^{(t)}), while pi_{cpow-d} estimates losses on a random mini-batch of size b (Algorithm 1) and pi_{rpow-d} eliminates selection communication overhead by ranking candidates using their latest piggybacked local training loss (1 / (tau b)) sum_{l=t-tau+1}^t sum_{xi in xi_k^{(l)}} f(w_k^{(l)}, xi) (Algorithm 2).
  • supports Source: Client Selection in Federated Learning: Convergence Analysis and Power-of-Choice Selection Strategies · Locator: Section 4, pp. 5-6; Appendix F, p. 20, Algorithms 1-2
Limitation #r491-claim-3
Increasing the candidate set size d increases the selection skew rho_bar and initial convergence speed but can also increase the ratio rho_tilde / rho_bar and the non-vanishing solution bias Q(rho_bar, rho_tilde) = (8 L Gamma / (3 mu))(rho_tilde / rho_bar - 1) under severe data heterogeneity (small Dirichlet alpha), where a moderate d outperforms choosing d = K.
  • supports Source: Client Selection in Federated Learning: Convergence Analysis and Power-of-Choice Selection Strategies · Locator: Sections 3.2, 4, & 5, pp. 5-7, Figures 2 & 4(b)
Application #r491-claim-4
Applied to strongly convex quadratic optimization, logistic regression on Synthetic(1,1), and DNN training on non-IID Dirichlet-partitioned Fashion-MNIST, achieving up to 3x faster convergence and 10% higher test accuracy than unbiased random selection pi_rand and outperforming Active Federated Learning (pi_afl).
  • supports Source: Client Selection in Federated Learning: Convergence Analysis and Power-of-Choice Selection Strategies · Locator: Section 5, pp. 6-8, Table 1, Figures 2-5

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Excessively large candidate pool sizes d under high data heterogeneity (large Gamma) can increase the asymptotic solution bias Q(rho_bar, rho_tilde) = (8 L Gamma / (3 mu))(rho_tilde / rho_bar - 1).
  • Base pow-d requires sending the current global model to d >= m candidate clients to evaluate local losses before selecting m active trainers unless approximated via cpow-d or rpow-d.
