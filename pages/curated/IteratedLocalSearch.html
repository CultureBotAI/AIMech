Iterated local search

Algorithms AIMech:IteratedLocalSearch DRAFT
Aliases: ILS
A metaheuristic that builds a single search trajectory among solutions returned by an embedded optimizer by perturbing the current accepted solution, rerunning that optimizer, and applying an acceptance criterion to the resulting candidate.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The ILS framework includes the common local-minimum formulation but permits a more general, possibly stochastic or history-dependent embedded optimizer. Historical names such as iterated Lin–Kernighan and large-step Markov chains denote particular developments, not exact aliases of every ILS configuration. Single-chain search distinguishes the framework from population selection and distribution refitting; optional restarts and hybrids require their own declared choices. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Generate an initial solution and apply the embedded LocalSearch routine to obtain a current optimized solution. Repeat: perturb that current solution, apply LocalSearch to the perturbed intermediate point, then use an acceptance criterion comparing the current and candidate optimized solutions, potentially with search history, to choose the next current solution. Repeat until the declared termination condition. In the simplified deterministic memoryless setting, LocalSearch maps candidates into a reduced set of local optima, and perturbation followed by local search induces transitions among them. The general framework need not explicitly enumerate neighborhoods in that reduced space. Accepted variants include retaining only strict improvements, always accepting the newly optimized candidate, or temperature-dependent acceptance; improvement-only selection is not a universal requirement. Perturbation and acceptance may use history, and the embedded procedure can itself be stochastic or use memory.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Perturbations that are too weak can return repeatedly to the same optimizer output; independent large perturbations remove the current solution’s influence from proposed starts and approach random-restart behavior. The full behavior still depends on the acceptance rule and the embedded optimizer.
  • A deterministic memoryless embedded routine supports the simplified basin-of-attraction picture. General stochastic or history-dependent routines need not define the same fixed partition; history-free transition descriptions cannot be assumed for every ILS variant.
  • Transitions between optimized solutions need not be reversible, and deterministic perturbations can produce short cycles. The framework alone is neither a target-invariant MCMC sampler nor a global-optimality or cycle-freedom guarantee.
  • Initialization, perturbation, local-search termination, acceptance, best-so-far bookkeeping and restart conventions must be declared for an executable instance. The source reports sensitivity to module interactions, including settings worse than random restart; no universal performance ranking or empirical result is curated.
