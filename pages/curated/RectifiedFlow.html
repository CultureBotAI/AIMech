Rectified flow

Algorithms AIMech:RectifiedFlow DRAFT
Aliases: RF (rectified flow)
A transport-learning method that regresses a velocity field on endpoint differences along straight interpolations of coupled samples and uses the resulting ordinary differential equation to transport the source distribution, optionally repeating rectification using the learned endpoint coupling.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The ignored-inclusive identity search covered both checkouts, all 442 AIO labels and scoped synonyms, and 90 preceding native identities. This record denotes the straight-interpolation regression and rectification procedure of Liu, Gong and Liu, including optional reflow. It overlaps with particular conditional flow-matching objectives, but does not denote every probability-path choice in flow matching, a particular trained image generator, or mandatory distillation.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #generative-mechanism
Given coupled endpoints X0 and X1, training samples t and regresses v((1−t)X0+tX1,t) toward X1−X0 using squared error. The ideal minimizer is the conditional mean endpoint difference at the interpolated state. Integrating dZt/dt=v(Zt,t) from the source sample induces a new endpoint coupling. Reflow optionally trains another rectified flow on that coupling; endpoint-map distillation is a separate optional step.
  • supports Source: Flow Straight and Fast: Learning to Generate and Transfer Data with Rectified Flow · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.2, equations (1)–(2), Algorithm 1 and transport-cost discussion, PDF pp. 4–6
Limitation #optimal-transport-boundary
A later analysis of gradient-constrained rectification exhibits nonoptimal zero-loss fixed points with disconnected intermediate supports; its corrected equivalence theorem additionally requires full intermediate support, rectifiability and smooth potential fields. Straightness or imposing a potential alone does not establish globally optimal transport.
  • supports Source: On the Relation between Rectified Flows and Optimal Transport · Locator: Sections 3.1–3.2, PDF pp. 5–6; Section 4.1, Proposition 10, Theorem 11 and Remark 12, PDF pp. 7–8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Marginal preservation and transport-cost statements concern ideal regression with a well-posed ODE, not arbitrary finite neural estimates or numerical solvers.
  • Straight training interpolations do not make the learned marginal trajectories exactly straight; one-step accuracy is not universal. Reflow and distillation are optional.
  • The original paper explicitly does not guarantee a globally cost-optimal coupling in general. Later analysis also gives nonoptimal zero-loss fixed points for a gradient-constrained rectification variant when required support or regularity assumptions are absent.
  • The later optimal-transport counterexamples limit stronger equivalence claims; they do not refute the original least-squares construction. No executable solver, numerical benchmark or full proof verification is supplied.
