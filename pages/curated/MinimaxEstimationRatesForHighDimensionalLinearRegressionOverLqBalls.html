Aliases: Minimax Rates of Estimation for High-Dimensional Linear Regression Over l_q-Balls (Raskutti, Wainwright, & Yu 2011), Information-Theoretic Minimax l_p-Parameter and l_2-Prediction Risk Over l_q-Balls, Nullspace Kernel Diameter and Metric Entropy Minimax Linear Regression Framework, Raskutti-Wainwright-Yu l_q-Ball Minimax Regression Rate Theory
Minimax Estimation Rates for High-Dimensional Linear Regression Over l_q-Balls is an information-theoretic and empirical-process statistical analysis methodology introduced by Raskutti, Wainwright, and Yu (2011, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 57(10):6976-6994, DOI:10.1109/TIT.2011.2165799, arXiv:0910.2042v1) that characterizes the non-asymptotic minimax l_p-parameter estimation error M_p(B_q(R_q)) := inf_{beta_hat} sup_{beta* in B_q(R_q)} ||beta_hat - beta*||_p^p (p in [1, 2]) and l_2-prediction error M_{pred}(B_q(R_q)) := inf_{beta_hat} sup_{beta* in B_q(R_q)} (1/n) ||X(beta_hat - beta*)||_2^2 for high-dimensional linear regression Y = X beta* + w (w ~ N(0, sigma^2 I_n)) over exact-sparsity hard l_0-balls B_0(s) := {beta in R^d : sum_{j=1}^d I[beta_j != 0] <= s} and weak-sparsity l_q-balls B_q(R_q) := {beta in R^d : sum_{j=1}^d |beta_j|^q <= R_q} for q in (0, 1]. The framework establishes matching minimax lower bounds (Theorems 1 and 3, via generalized Fano and Yang-Barron mutual-information bounds coupled with l_q-ball metric entropy Lemma 3, q-convex hull entropy Lemma 4, and ternary Hamming packing Lemma 5) and upper bounds via l_q-constrained least squares beta_hat in argmin_{beta in B_q(R_q)} ||Y - X beta||_2^2 (Theorems 2 and 4, via Gaussian width bounds over l_q-balls and q-convex hulls in Lemmas 6-8): for parameter estimation, M_p(B_q(R_q)) scales as max{diam_p^p(N_q(X)), R_q ((sigma^2 / kappa_c^2) (log d)/n)^{(p-q)/2}} for q in (0, 1] and max{diam_p^p(N_0(X)), s^{p/2} ((sigma^2 / kappa_u^2) (log(d/s))/n)^{p/2}} for q = 0 (where N_q(X) := B_q(R_q) cap ker(X) is the intersected nullspace, bounded by diam_2(N_q(X)) <= f_l(R_q, n, d)/kappa_l under restricted curvature Assumption 2 in Lemma 1), whereas for prediction error, M_{pred}(B_q(R_q)) scales as R_q (sigma^2 (log d)/n)^{1 - q/2} for q in (0, 1] and sigma^2 s log(d/s)/n for q = 0 without any lower restricted curvature condition, exhibiting an exact role reversal of design matrix assumptions between parameter and prediction estimation.

Under Assumption 2(q, R_q, f_l) requiring (1/sqrt(n)) ||X theta||_2 >= kappa_l ||theta||_2 - f_l(R_q, n, d) for all theta in B_q(2 R_q) (Equation 8), Lemma 1 (p. 5; proved in Appendix A, pp. 23-24) proves diam_2(N_q(X)) <= f_l(R_q, n, d)/kappa_l. ⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Computational Intractability of Non-Convex l_q-Ball Constrained Least Squares for q in [0, 1): As emphasized in Section 2.2 (p. 6) and Section 3.3 (pp. 11-15), the minimax upper-bound estimator beta_hat in argmin_{beta in B_q(R_q)} ||Y - X beta||_2^2 is a non-convex optimization problem for q in [0, 1) (requiring exhaustive subset search over (d choose s) supports when q = 0), whereas polynomial-time convex relaxations such as the Lasso and Dantzig selector require the strictly stronger l_1 Restricted Eigenvalue condition Assumption 3(gamma; s) to achieve the same rates.
  • Sub-Linear Sparsity Regime Requirement s = o(d) for Matching Hard-Sparsity Bounds: As noted in Remark (a) after Theorem 2 (p. 6) and Section 2.3 (p. 7), for q = 0 the lower bounds in Theorems 1(b) and 3(b) scale with s log(d/s)/n under s <= d/4, whereas the upper bounds in Theorems 2(b) and 4(b) can be stated with s log(d - s)/n or s log(d/s)/n, matching up to constant factors across the sub-linear sparsity regime s = o(d).
  • Primary-Source Typographical Notes in Raskutti, Wainwright, & Yu (2011, arXiv:0910.2042v1): Verified five self-correcting typographical details during full-text inspection: (1) Section 1.1 (p. 3, Equation 5) prints |beta_hat_j - beta*_j|_p^p with an extra subscript p on the scalar absolute value; (2) Section 3.1 (p. 9, Corollary 1, Equation 22) omits the radius factor R_q in the Gaussian sequence model display (absorbing R_q into the constants c'_q, c_q); (3) Section 3.2 (p. 9, Equation 23) defines rho(Sigma) := max_j Sigma_{jj} (the maximum diagonal variance) and prints max_j ||X_j||_2 / sqrt(n) <= rho(Sigma) (1 + sqrt(32 log d / n)) without a square root on rho(Sigma), whereas Equation (25) and Appendix F (p. 34) use the dimensionally consistent sqrt(rho(Sigma)); (4) Section 4.2.1 (p. 18, Proof of Theorem 1b) writes (delta_n / sqrt(s)) H_tilde right after defining sqrt(2/s) delta_n H_tilde, and Section 4.2.2 (p. 20, Proof of Theorem 3b) writes (delta_n^2 / (2s)) H_tilde and includes an extra 1/n in the denominator (s/(2n)) log((d - s)/(s/2)) before canceling n in the final line delta_n^2 = (kappa_l^2 / (64 kappa_u^2)) sigma^2 (s/n) log((d - s)/(s/2)); and (5) Section 4.3.1 (p. 21, Proof of Theorem 2a) substitutes tau = (2 sigma kappa_c / kappa_l^2) sqrt(3 log d / n) into 4 max{b^2, |c|} = 8 R_q tau^{2-q}, where tau^{2-q} carries factor 12^{1-q/2} (giving 8 * 12^{1-q/2} R_q), whereas p. 21 and Equation (12) print prefactor 24 = 8 * 3 (omitting 4^{1-q/2} from 2^2 inside tau^2), and at the bottom of p. 21 (Proof of Theorem 2b) the display omits the factor 2 before squaring to 6.
