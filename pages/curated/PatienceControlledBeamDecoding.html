Patience-controlled FCFS beam decoding

Algorithms AIMech:PatienceControlledBeamDecoding DRAFT
Aliases: FCFS beam decoding with controlled patience
A generalization of first-come, first-served sequence beam decoding that retains beam width k for continuing hypotheses but uses a positive patience factor p to stop when the accumulated completed-hypothesis count reaches at least k·p.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the algorithmic stopping-policy variant introduced by Kasai and colleagues, not a standalone model, learned parameter or generic training patience rule. Setting p=1 recovers their FCFS baseline; changing p separates its finished-output threshold from its continuing-beam width. No normalized identity match was found across the 442 AIO and 141 prior native records; ignored-inclusive text hits in the main checkout were prior discovery leads and incidental substrings such as builds in records, source text and dependencies, not existing concept records.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #search-procedure
Keep the FCFS candidate selection and separate persistent finished set, but replace the termination threshold k with k·p. Return the highest-scoring stored completion once that threshold is reached, subject to the method’s maximum-length limit. With integer completion counts, the displayed inequality corresponds mathematically to reaching ceil(k·p); this is an interpretation of the paper’s inequality, not a claim about rounding in any software library.
  • supports Source: A Call for Clarity in Beam Search: How It Works and When It Stops · Locator: Figure 1 line 13, printed p. 77 / PDF p. 1; Patience Factor for FCFS, Section 2, printed p. 79 / PDF p. 3
Limitation #patience-scope
The paper separates stopping depth from beam breadth and reports task-dependent behavior; its limitations explicitly restrict summarization evidence to English news. More extensive search and better generated-text quality are not equivalent claims.
  • supports Source: A Call for Clarity in Beam Search: How It Works and When It Stops · Locator: Section 2, Section 3.3 and Limitations, printed pp. 79–81 / PDF pp. 3–5

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The finite maximum length can end decoding before the completion threshold is reached. Empty finished sets and exhausted candidate pools need an implementation-specific outcome.
  • Increasing patience can expose more completions but does not guarantee better task quality, a globally optimal sequence, or equivalence to vanilla beam search.
  • The parameter is a stopping threshold, not a score upper bound or a proof that no unfinished hypothesis can improve the incumbent.
  • The source evaluates specific translation and news-summarization settings. English news summarization does not establish benefits for all languages, domains or tasks.
  • No current library default, runtime gain, numerical quality improvement or trained-model execution is claimed.
