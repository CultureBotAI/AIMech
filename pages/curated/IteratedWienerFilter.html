Iterated Wiener Filter

Algorithms AIMech:IteratedWienerFilter DRAFT
Aliases: IWF
An iterative MAP image-denoising algorithm that majorizes a fixed Product of Student-t log-potential and alternates response-dependent precision updates with a Gaussian Wiener linear solve.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the explicitly named inference procedure in Welling, Osindero and Hinton 2002, not the PoT probability family, a one-pass Wiener filter, a learned denoising autoencoder or a training algorithm for the filters. Its iterates estimate the latent clean observation under fixed model parameters. It is separately represented from generic sparse coding already covered by AIO:SparseLearning. Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #majorization-update
For noisy y, positive-definite noise covariance Sigma, fixed filters J, positive exponents alpha and nonnegative grouping weights W, minimize F(x)=0.5(y-x)^T Sigma^(-1)(y-x)+sum_i alpha_i log[1+0.5 sum_j W_ij(J_j^T x)^2]. Ungrouped factors use identity W. At the current x set gamma_i=[1+0.5 sum_j W_ij(J_j^T x)^2]^(-1), form D=diag(W^T(alpha*gamma)), and solve [Sigma^(-1)+J D J^T]x_new=Sigma^(-1)y. The bound log(t)<=gamma*t-log(gamma)-1 is tight at gamma=1/t; exact bound minimization therefore does not increase the fixed objective. Sigma positive definite ensures the linear solve is unique even if J is rank deficient. Interpreting F as a proper-prior MAP objective additionally requires the PoT prior to normalize.
  • supports Source: Learning Sparse Topographic Representations with Products of Student-t Distributions · Locator: Section 6, Equations 10-12, PDF pp. 6-7; curator majorization and positive-definiteness consequences
Limitation #optimization-boundaries
The log-potential makes the full objective generally nonconvex. Monotone exact updates do not guarantee its global minimum, and MAP is not generally the squared-error-optimal posterior mean. A scalar proper-prior calculation with alpha=10, noise variance 1 and y=8.5 reaches distinct strict local minima from starting values 0 and 8.5, with objectives approximately 32.03555 and 32.08788. The higher solution cannot be globally optimal. This is a curator counterexample to a universal optimization guarantee, not a reported author experiment.
  • supports Source: Learning Sparse Topographic Representations with Products of Student-t Distributions · Locator: Section 6, Equations 10-13, PDF pp. 6-7; curator finite calculation from the stated objective
Scope #special-case-and-use
In the source special case of square orthonormal filters, isotropic noise and identity grouping, the stationary condition separates into scalar cubic equations. Other structured cases require their own derivation. Multiple stationary roots require objective comparison; a root alone is not a global-optimum certificate. The source applies the procedure to overlapping transformed image patches and averages restored patches, using a complete set of filters learned on natural images. This patch-processing and preprocessing context is part of the reported denoising experiment, not a definition of all Wiener filters or evidence of general benchmark superiority.
  • supports Source: Learning Sparse Topographic Representations with Products of Student-t Distributions · Locator: Equation 13 and Section 6.1, PDF pp. 7-8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The algorithm fixes filters, exponents and grouping weights while optimizing the clean observation. It does not fit the prior, estimate a noise model automatically or sample posterior uncertainty.
  • Descent follows exact auxiliary updates and exact linear solves for the same fixed objective. Approximate solves, changing parameters or a different noise model need separate analysis.
  • Nonconvexity allows initialization-dependent local minima. MAP and posterior-mean estimates optimize different decision criteria; the source description of MAP as optimal is not an unconditional minimum-MSE theorem.
  • The source reports a single displayed noisy-image comparison with known Gaussian noise, overlapping patches and matched whitening/DC handling. It does not establish universal denoising gains or the proposed future benefits of overcomplete/grouped priors.
  • No author software or empirical denoising experiment was run. Finite arithmetic checks verify mathematical boundaries, not historical implementation correctness.
