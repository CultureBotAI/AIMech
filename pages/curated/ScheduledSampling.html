Scheduled sampling

Algorithms AIMech:ScheduledSampling DRAFT
An autoregressive sequence-training procedure that chooses between reference and model-produced previous outputs as feedback, using a schedule that progressively reduces the probability of reference feedback.

Scheduled sampling mixes supplied-reference and model-produced feedback under a changing probability; full teacher forcing is its all-reference endpoint, not an alias for the mixed procedure. Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #mixed-prefix-training
At each training prediction, use the current mixed prefix to form the model conditional, score the actual reference target, and choose whether the output fed to the next prediction is the reference token or a model-produced token. The original experiments flip a coin for each token with reference probability epsilon_i at training iteration i. The model alternative may be sampled from the conditional or selected by argmax. The target in the loss remains the reference; this is not simply training the model to imitate its own sampled target.
  • supports Source: Scheduled Sampling for Sequence Prediction with Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section2.4, PDF/printed pp.3–4; Figures1–2 and decision-gradient convention, p.4
Scope #schedule-and-endpoints
The all-reference choice epsilon=1 reproduces ordinary true-prefix training, whereas epsilon=0 uses generated feedback. The source proposes decreasing schedules, including a linear decay with a floor, exponential decay and an inverse-sigmoid form. These are configurable curricula rather than one mandatory formula or an assertion that every practical schedule starts exactly at one or reaches zero. The paper reports poor results for some tasks when generated feedback is used from the start; the all-generated endpoint is not an automatic optimization improvement.
  • supports Source: Scheduled Sampling for Sequence Prediction with Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section2.4, PDF/printed pp.3–4; Figures1–2 and decision-gradient convention, p.4
  • supports Source: Scheduled Sampling for Sequence Prediction with Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Always Sampling comparisons in Sections4.1–4.3, PDF pp.5–8
Mechanism #decision-gradient-convention
In the reported experiments, gradients pass through the recurrent computations and losses but not through the discrete sampled-token decision. A model-dependent sampling distribution contributes additional terms if one differentiates its full expected loss; those terms must not be silently attributed to the reported update. The paper leaves differentiation through selection decisions to future work. A differentiable relaxation or a score-function estimator therefore needs its own algorithm and objective specification.
  • supports Source: Scheduled Sampling for Sequence Prediction with Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section2.4, PDF/printed pp.3–4; Figures1–2 and decision-gradient convention, p.4
  • supports Source: Scheduled Sampling for Sequence Prediction with Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section5 Conclusion, PDF/printed p.8
Limitation #two-token-consistency-boundary
The primary critique analyzes an idealized two-token sampled-prefix criterion. For a fixed first-token model equal to the true marginal P1, reference probability epsilon makes the conditional target at a supplied prefix z equal to epsilon*P(y|z)+(1-epsilon)*P2(y). With separately adjustable second-token conditionals, this mixture need not equal the true conditional when the tokens are dependent. At epsilon=0 it becomes independent of z. This is a bounded consistency counterexample, not a claim that every finite neural training run converges to that distribution.
  • supports Source: How (not) to Train your Generative Model: Scheduled Sampling, Likelihood, Adversary? · Locator: Sections4–4.1, especially the two-symbol formulation and Equation6, PDF/printed pp.3–5; curator direct expected-loss calculation
Scope #constructed-dependent-example
In a constructed binary example with joint masses P(0,0)=P(1,1)=9/20 and P(0,1)=P(1,0)=1/20, both marginals are uniform. Holding the first-token sampler at that marginal, epsilon=1/2 gives optimal second-token probabilities P_model(1|0)=3/10 and P_model(1|1)=7/10 for the mixed-input conditional loss, instead of the true 1/10 and 9/10. Its conditional cross-entropy is lower at the mixed target than at the true conditional, and the partial gradients at the truth are nonzero. The calculation fixes the sampler and does not equate a stopped-decision neural update with the full derivative through a changing sampler.
  • supports Source: How (not) to Train your Generative Model: Scheduled Sampling, Likelihood, Adversary? · Locator: Section4.1 two-token criterion, Equation6, PDF/printed p.4; independently constructed finite enumeration in the discovery calculation
Scope #reported-experiment-scope
The original paper reports selected image-captioning, parsing and speech comparisons under particular capacities, schedules and evaluation settings. All-generated training fails to learn correct tree structure in the reported parsing condition. Speech next-step frame error uses supplied previous labels on validation data, while free-decoding frame error uses beam search on test data; these are not interchangeable measurements or general word-recognition scores. Reported benefits and the idealized consistency critique concern different questions and do not establish either universal superiority or universal empirical failure.
  • supports Source: Scheduled Sampling for Sequence Prediction with Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Sections4.1–4.3 and Tables1–3, PDF/printed pp.5–8; Section5, p.8
  • supports Source: How (not) to Train your Generative Model: Scheduled Sampling, Likelihood, Adversary? · Locator: Discussion of finite-training empirical success after the consistency analysis, PDF/printed p.5

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The record describes Bengio et al2015 and the original omitted decision-gradient convention. Differentiable, confidence-based or later sampling variants require separate evidence and identity assessment.
  • A sampled model prefix can be paired with an original reference continuation that is no longer appropriate for that prefix. The procedure does not query a new expert target for every visited model state.
  • The conditional mixture result fixes the first-token sampler to the true marginal, uses positive-support two-token data and separately adjustable conditionals. It does not certify the dynamics of a shared-parameter neural optimizer, arbitrary schedules, argmax replacements or longer sequences.
  • The source critique’s broader perceptual-quality and generalized-adversarial proposals are not adopted here. Its displayed reformulations are not needed beyond the directly checked expected two-token criterion.
  • All eighteen cited PDF pages were read. Constructed finite probability calculations were executed without fitting; no author code, model training, paid service or benchmark replication was run.
