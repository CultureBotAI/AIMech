Delayed-rejection adaptive Metropolis

Algorithms AIMech:DelayedRejectionAdaptiveMetropolis DRAFT
Aliases: DRAM
An adaptive Metropolis procedure combining history-based Gaussian proposal covariance updates between transitions with corrected delayed-rejection proposal stages within a transition.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope DRAM combines two distinct mechanisms: retained-history covariance learning across iterations and reversed-path acceptance corrections for additional attempts after rejection. The inspected author implementation supplies the explicit Gaussian-stage realization; it is not merely repeated ordinary Metropolis tests. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
At fixed proposal settings for a transition, first propose y1 from N(x,C1) and use the Metropolis target ratio. After rejection propose y2 from N(x,C2), centered at the original x, with C2=C1/d1² for a positive stage scale d1 (d1>1 narrows the proposal). Let q_i(a,b) denote the Gaussian proposal density at b from a and a1(a,b)=min(1,pi(b)/pi(a)). On a reachable rejection path, accept y2 with min(1, [pi(y2)q1(y2,y1)q2(y2,x)(1−a1(y2,y1))]/[pi(x)q1(x,y1)q2(x,y2)(1−a1(x,y1))]). Higher stages use the recursive forward/reverse rejection products and earlier-stage Gaussian proposal ratios. Retain x after all capped stages reject. At scheduled iterations, update the empirical covariance from stored retained states including repeats, scale its Cholesky factor, and recompute stage factors for later transitions. In the inspected implementation diagonal jitter is attempted only when factorization fails; if that attempt also fails, the previous proposal factor is retained.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The original 2006 article metadata was located but its complete method PDF was not obtained. This record is supported by the named author’s pinned implementation and its covariance helper, not a claim to have read that article.
  • The Gaussian factors remain fixed within a delayed-rejection transition. Recomputing adaptation from rejected internal candidates or ignoring reverse-path factors would require different justification.
  • The implementation defaults to two attempts and adaptation every 100 iterations; its usage comment about three stages is inconsistent with those executable defaults. These counts are options, not the concept identity.
  • Optional covariance pseudoweights, fixed nonadapted coordinates, early rejection-rate scaling, variance sampling and hierarchical updates are outside the simple fixed-target account here.
  • Jitter only on factorization failure differs from always adding a positive regularizer. Source-code inspection alone establishes neither correctness for arbitrary targets nor adaptive ergodicity; no MATLAB execution or performance comparison was performed.
  • Initialize within positive target support. Zero reverse-path probability gives zero acceptance; numerical evaluation must handle vanishing densities and log ratios without invalid divisions.
