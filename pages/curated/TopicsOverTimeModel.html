Topics over Time model

Model Families AIMech:TopicsOverTimeModel DRAFT
Aliases: TOT
A topic-model family combining fixed topic-word distributions with topic-specific continuous timestamp densities, so that words and their observed times jointly inform document topic assignments.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Scoped to Wang and McCallum's KDD2006 Topics over Time model, especially its fitted token-level timestamp construction with Beta densities over normalized time. The paper also proposes a distinct single-document-timestamp view. Fixed topic-word distributions distinguish TOT from dynamic topic models with evolving word probabilities; continuous observed time does not imply a Markov process or point-process intensity. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #token-time-generation
The fitted construction gives each document a Dirichlet topic mixture, each topic a corpus-shared categorical word vector, and each topic a Beta timestamp density on a normalized bounded interval. A token draws a topic, a word and a timestamp; fitting copies the document timestamp to every token. Word distributions remain fixed across time, while topic-specific time densities influence assignment. The alternative description generates one timestamp from a document-mixture-weighted combination of topic densities. These are different generative laws: repeating topic-time factors, even with inverse-length weighting, is not generally identical to one mixture density for a document timestamp.
  • supports Source: Topics over Time: A Non-Markov Continuous-Time Model of Topical Trends · Locator: Section 2, PDF pp.2-4, Figure 1(b,c), generative steps and alternative description
Mechanism #time-weighted-inference
With topic timestamp parameters fixed, the collapsed token assignment weight combines leave-one-out document-topic and topic-word predictive factors with the assigned topic's timestamp density. The paper introduces an exponent to balance time and word contributions and updates Beta parameters from empirical moments. These adaptations change the fitting objective and require positive valid shapes and safeguards for empty topics or zero variance. The printed density uses (1-t)^(psi1-1)*t^(psi2-1), whereas its moment estimates assign psi1=mean*c and psi2=(1-mean)*c. A coherent calculation must use one consistent shape convention; for the conventional Beta(a,b), the density is proportional to t^(a-1)*(1-t)^(b-1). No behavior of uninspected historical software is inferred from this notation conflict.
  • supports Source: Topics over Time: A Non-Markov Continuous-Time Model of Topical Trends · Locator: Section 2, PDF pp.3-4, conditional and Algorithm 1; Appendix A p.10, density and moment estimates

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A repeated timestamp provides one density factor per token unless explicitly reweighted. Its restriction to one common timestamp is not automatically a normalized single-time document density. The proposed balancing exponent does not prove equivalence between the two generative descriptions.
  • Bayes inversion can produce a token-topic probability given time using an assumed or estimated topic prior. This is not generally the same quantity as the posterior mean of a random document topic vector, despite notation on p.4. Continuous density values are not probabilities; endpoint handling, normalization range and balancing conventions matter.
  • One Beta per topic limits temporal shapes. Extending the bounded time range for prediction is a modeling choice rather than a guarantee of future extrapolation. Fixed word distributions do not directly model changing word meanings, and topic split/merge interpretations are descriptive.
  • Historical evaluations use selected address, email and NIPS collections with heuristic preprocessing and particular finite samples. Topic names, KL distinctness, thresholded co-occurrence and decade prediction do not establish causal trends, universal semantic accuracy or future forecasting. No author code or benchmark was executed.
