Otsu thresholding

Algorithms AIMech:OtsuThresholding DRAFT
Aliases: Otsu’s method, Otsu threshold selection
A histogram-based threshold-selection algorithm that partitions ordered gray levels by maximizing class-weighted between-class variance, equivalently minimizing within-class variance for a fixed histogram.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The binary method chooses a global intensity threshold without labeled training data or a fitted parametric intensity density. Its ordered multilevel extension jointly selects several thresholds using the same variance decomposition. It is not a supervised classifier, a learned activation threshold, adaptive spatial thresholding or a guarantee of semantically correct image segmentation. The threshold search depends on intensity counts and order, not spatial adjacency. Thresholds with the same partition can be equivalent even when their numeric values differ. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For nonnegative counts n_i at ordered gray levels i with positive total N, use p_i=n_i/N and total mean mu_T=sum_i i*p_i. For every binary threshold k admitting two nonempty classes, set w=sum_(i<=k) p_i and m=sum_(i<=k) i*p_i. Compute between-class variance B(k)=(mu_T*w-m)^2/[w*(1-w)] and choose a global maximizer. Cumulative counts and first moments give an O(L) scan over L gray levels after histogram construction. At fixed total variance T, within-class variance is T-B(k); if T>0, maximizing the separability ratio B(k)/T gives the same maximizers. The source’s multilevel extension jointly partitions the ordered levels into nonempty classes and maximizes sum_c w_c*(mu_c-mu_T)^2; recursive binary splitting is not automatically equivalent to that joint optimum.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • An empty histogram has no probability distribution. A constant-intensity histogram has no admissible nonempty binary split and has zero total variance, so the separability ratio is undefined without an added convention. Implementations must state how they handle these cases.
  • Multiple thresholds can maximize the objective. The paper does not prescribe a universal tie rule such as averaging all maximizing thresholds. Quantization, bin selection and intensity transformations can affect the result; the stated invariance is for corresponding affine intensity transformations, not arbitrary nonlinear remapping.
  • Section III D suggests that the objective is always unimodal while acknowledging no proof. That universal claim is not adopted: the original finite histogram with equal counts at 0, 10, 11, 21 has two separated maximizing threshold plateaus with a lower objective at 10. A full scan preserves the global objective; an assumed-unimodal local search need not.
  • The variance criterion measures statistical separation in intensity space. It does not require visibly bimodal histograms and does not ensure that the selected classes correspond to the desired objects. Spatial connectivity, texture and semantic labels are absent from this objective.
  • The multilevel criterion requires a joint ordered-threshold optimization or another justified optimizer. The linear scan complexity above is for the binary case, not every multilevel search.
  • The three small illustrative character, texture and cell images are application examples, not a modern accuracy benchmark or a universal comparison. The PDF shares pages with unrelated articles and a book review; those passages are excluded from evidence. No author implementation or image evaluation was run.
