Flake focused community crawling

Algorithms AIMech:FlakeFocusedCrawl DRAFT
Aliases: APPROXIMATE-FLOW-COMMUNITY, Flake focused-crawl algorithm
A flow-based focused-crawling family that alternates gathering a local web graph around positive seed pages, extracting a group through seeded maximum flow, promoting selected discovered pages to seeds and recrawling, with version-specific graph augmentation and seed-ranking rules.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

7 Claims
Mechanism #acquisition-and-reseeding
The procedure uses an explicitly supplied seed set, gathers outgoing links and searches for incoming links, performs flow-based grouping on the resulting graph, promotes selected nonseed pages and repeats for a prescribed number of rounds. Graph acquisition and seed expansion are part of the algorithm; the wrapper is distinct from solving a single fixed augmented graph.
  • supports Source: Self-Organization and Identification of Web Communities · Locator: Figure 2b and approximate-flow discussion, printed p. 68 / PDF p. 3
  • supports Source: Efficient Identification of Web Communities · Locator: Sections 3.2–3.3 and Table 1, printed pp. 153–154 / PDF pp. 4–5
Mechanism #kdd2000-variant
The KDD2000 implementation symmetrizes links within the seed and immediate-neighbor region while preserving directed boundary-reference links toward a virtual sink. It explicitly scales only seed-to-neighbor capacities, deviating from the uniform-scaling construction discussed in its theorem. Table 1 promotes community pages tied to selected high in-degree and out-degree scores measured on the induced graph, then recrawls.
  • supports Source: Efficient Identification of Web Communities · Locator: Section 3.2, Figure 2 and implementation-deviation paragraphs; Section 3.3 and Table 1, printed pp. 153–154 / PDF pp. 4–5
Mechanism #ieee2002-variant
The 2002 wrapper describes a fixed-depth crawl in both hyperlink directions, sets k to the seed-set size, invokes EXACT-FLOW-COMMUNITY on the induced graph, ranks returned pages by internal community links and adds highest-ranked nonseeds. These choices differ from the earlier boundary wiring and induced-graph in/out-degree rule; the versions are retained explicitly rather than presented as identical pseudocode.
  • supports Source: Self-Organization and Identification of Web Communities · Locator: Figure 2b and approximate-flow discussion, printed p. 68 / PDF p. 3
  • supports Source: Efficient Identification of Web Communities · Locator: Table 1 and preceding implementation-deviation paragraphs, printed p. 154 / PDF p. 5
Limitation #em-and-convergence-boundary
The earlier source describes reseeding as inspired by expectation maximization, but supplies no statistical expected-log-likelihood objective or corresponding parameter-estimation E-step. It explicitly lacks a proof of convergence to a fixed point and a bound on deviation from the ideal community procedure. A prescribed round count and empirical stabilization are not such guarantees.
  • supports Source: Efficient Identification of Web Communities · Locator: Section 3.3, printed p. 154 / PDF p. 5
Limitation #coverage-and-theorem-boundary
Exact flow on a sampled graph does not establish a result for the complete Web. Missing incoming links, crawl depth, boundary treatment, seed selection and heuristic capacity scaling affect the represented problem. The 2000 paper’s Theorem 2 concerns a specified uniformly scaled virtual-sink graph under a stated inequality, while the authors explicitly describe deviations in the implemented crawler; it is not an unconditional guarantee for either wrapper.
  • supports Source: Efficient Identification of Web Communities · Locator: Section 3.2, Theorem 2 and implementation deviations, printed pp. 153–154 / PDF pp. 4–5
  • supports Source: Self-Organization and Identification of Web Communities · Locator: Approximate-flow discussion, printed p. 68 / PDF p. 3
Scope #historical-evaluation
The KDD2000 examples concern support-vector-machine research, the Internet Archive and Ronald Rivest, with different seeds, iteration counts and handling of same-domain links. The authors report false positives and say network retrieval dominated runtime. The 2002 examples use three individual-scientist seeds and roughly two hundred returned pages per run; these are conditional historical demonstrations, not comparable modern accuracy or throughput benchmarks.
  • supports Source: Efficient Identification of Web Communities · Locator: Section 4, Tables 2–4 and runtime discussion, printed pp. 154–158 / PDF pp. 5–9
  • supports Source: Self-Organization and Identification of Web Communities · Locator: Experimental Results and Figure 3, printed p. 69 / PDF p. 4
Limitation #interpretation-and-ranking
Internal-link counts rank pages rather than estimate calibrated membership probabilities. The 2002 paper’s keyword features and exhaustively selected three-term rules characterize the returned groups after collection; their reported match rates are not a held-out evaluation of a learned predictive model. Link-based retrieval does not establish bias-free or universally correct semantic communities.
  • supports Source: Self-Organization and Identification of Web Communities · Locator: Experimental Results, Figure 3 and Table 1 discussion, printed pp. 69–70 / PDF pp. 4–5

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This family record preserves the distinct 2000 and 2002 graph and ranking conventions. It does not combine their details into a new purported author implementation.
  • Exact-flow core limitations remain relevant: preserving seeds and optimizing a cut do not certify every seed’s degree criterion or recover any arbitrarily preselected community. The separate exact-flow record and finite calculations document those boundaries.
  • The 2002 Figure 2a all-E rescaling ambiguity is inherited by its called core. The prose preexisting-edge convention is the recorded interpretation; author code was not audited.
  • Tie handling, the precise number of promoted pages in the 2002 description and all exceptional candidate-pool cases were not resolved from implementation evidence. No fixed-point or approximation guarantee is asserted.
  • Both complete primary papers, displayed algorithms and result tables were inspected. Their historical URLs were not visited and their descriptions were not independently revalidated. No live Web crawl, author-code execution, model training or benchmark replication was performed; DRAFT remains.
