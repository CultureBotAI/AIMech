Topic-model evocation score

Evaluation Methods AIMech:TopicModelEvocationScore DRAFT
Aliases: Top-m evocation metric
A topic-model evaluation method that orders judged word pairs by model-derived weights and sums their human evocation ratings over a chosen number of highest-ranked pairs, with alternative aggregations across topics.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the top-m scoring procedure and its Evoc-1/Evoc-2 aggregation choices, not the human evocation dataset, a word-similarity embedding, a distance-metric learning algorithm or an empirical score achieved by a trained model. Human evocation measures how strongly one term brings another to mind; it need not mean synonymy. The two aggregations are retained as explicitly different operations within the scoring method rather than asserted as synonymous numerical quantities. Mechanism #score-and-aggregation
Let E be a finite set of word pairs with human scores h_e and model weights M_e, and choose an integer m with 0<=m<=|E|. Order E by descending M using an explicit tie convention, and set Evoc_m(M,h)=sum_{r=1}^m h_{pi_M(r)}. For K topics, Evoc-1 is the unweighted mean of the K separate top-m scores; Evoc-2 is the top-m score after averaging their weight vectors over the same judged pairs. The paper derives APM weights mainly from edge coefficients, with node-based fallback for insufficient nonzero edges, and uses products of topic-word probabilities for independent multinomial topic models. A strictly increasing transformation of one weight vector preserves its rank-based score under the same tie handling. Separately rescaling topics before their weight vectors are averaged can change Evoc-2 even when every individual score and Evoc-1 stays unchanged.
  • supports Source: Capturing Semantically Meaningful Word Dependencies with an Admixture of Poisson MRFs · Locator: Section 4, PDF/printed pp.5-6, Equation (4), ranking invariance, pair weights and topic aggregation
Scope #evaluation-scope
The reported evaluation maps human-rated synsets to word/POS entries, averages two directed ratings to obtain undirected pair scores, and retains 1646 such entries across 4049 BNC documents. Human-scored pairs are split equally between tuning and testing for model selection; this is distinct from holding out all test documents from unsupervised fitting. The reported comparisons use m=50, selected topic counts and model-specific parameter grids. Those design choices specify this historical evaluation and do not establish universal topic coherence, causal relations or performance on unjudged word pairs.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The score depends on the judged-pair subset, rating scale, m and tie convention. Unjudged pairs are excluded rather than automatically assigned zero. A larger sum is not a calibrated probability, a distance satisfying metric axioms or an estimate of all possible word-pair quality.
  • Evoc-1 and Evoc-2 generally differ. Individual rank invariance does not justify independently changing topic scales before computing Evoc-2. The source does not fully specify every fallback and tie-handling detail, so numerical replication requires additional implementation conventions.
  • Human evocation can be directional, whereas the historical evaluation symmetrizes it and collapses word senses to word/POS. These choices discard information and need not fit every application or language.
  • Human annotation and pair selection can bias coverage. Tuning on one half of pair judgments and evaluating on the other does not establish an unseen-document or unseen-vocabulary test. Topic labels, selected word-pair examples and authors superiority claims do not guarantee semantic validity.
  • No evocation dataset was analyzed and no author evaluation script or benchmark was executed. The mathematical witnesses are synthetic checks of ranking and aggregation only.
