Dual-averaging step-size adaptation for Hamiltonian samplers

Algorithms AIMech:DualAveragingHamiltonianStepSize DRAFT
A warmup procedure that tunes the logarithm of a Hamiltonian sampler’s integration step size using cumulative discrepancy from a target acceptance statistic, a shrinkage point and a smoothed sequence of iterates.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is Hoffman and Gelman’s adaptation of dual averaging to HMC/NUTS step-size tuning. It is not the general Nesterov optimization algorithm, trajectory-length adaptation, mass-matrix estimation, or an acceptance test for retaining each NUTS output. Mechanism #method
At warmup iteration m form Hbar_m=(1−1/(m+t0))Hbar_(m−1)+(delta−a_m)/(m+t0), then log epsilon_m=mu−sqrt(m)Hbar_m/gamma. Smooth with log epsilonbar_m=m^(−kappa)log epsilon_m+(1−m^(−kappa))log epsilonbar_(m−1). Here gamma>0, t0≥0 and 1/2<kappa≤1; initialize Hbar_0=0 and the step-size/shrinkage values as specified by the chosen implementation. HMC uses its proposed trajectory’s Metropolis acceptance probability a_m. Original NUTS uses the mean hypothetical Metropolis acceptance probability over states explored in the final doubling, not a binary acceptance of its selected output. Adapt during a finite warmup, then use the averaged step size without continued adaptation for retained sampling.
  • supports Source: The No-U-Turn Sampler: Adaptively Setting Path Lengths in Hamiltonian Monte Carlo · Locator: Sections 3.2–3.2.4, Equations (5)–(6) and Algorithms 5–6, printed pp. 1605–1611 / PDF pp. 13–19

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The convergence discussion assumes suitable regularity, including the relevant expected acceptance statistic decreasing with step size. The update formula alone does not prove convergence on every target.
  • The target delta, warmup duration, initialization and shrinkage controls remain choices. The paper’s experimental defaults are not universal optimal values or current-library defaults.
  • For NUTS the statistic averages potential HMC acceptances over explored states in the final doubling; it is not the proportion of returned positions that changed.
  • Adapting throughout retained sampling requires a separate adaptive-MCMC argument. This record follows the paper’s finite-warmup/freeze practice.
  • The procedure tunes epsilon, not a mass matrix or the complete distribution geometry, and does not guarantee independent samples or adequate posterior exploration.
