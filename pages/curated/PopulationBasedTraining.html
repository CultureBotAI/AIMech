Population Based Training

Algorithms AIMech:PopulationBasedTraining DRAFT
Aliases: PBT
A population-based neural-network training procedure that interleaves parameter updates with performance-guided copying of partially trained models and exploration of hyperparameters, producing training trajectories with changing hyperparameter settings.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope PBT jointly trains model weights and searches hyperparameter schedules. It differs from independent random-search runs, a prediction ensemble and Population Based Augmentation’s specialized augmentation-schedule transfer. Its population updates do not require genetic crossover. General PBT can return the trained model; PBA separately discards its child weights when transferring a schedule. Reinforcement learning is one application, not the definition of PBT. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Maintain population members with model parameters, hyperparameters, an evaluation score and training progress. Each worker trains its parameters with its current hyperparameters and evaluates a chosen performance metric, which need not be differentiable or identical to the local training objective. When a readiness condition holds, an exploitation rule can replace that worker’s parameters and hyperparameters with those of another promising member. An exploration rule then perturbs or resamples the copied hyperparameters, and training continues from the inherited weights. Algorithm 1 calls exploration and re-evaluation only when exploitation changes the weights; its final output is the population member’s parameters with the highest recorded score. Readiness, selection and mutation rules depend on the application. The original procedure is asynchronous: members need access to population scores, parameters and hyperparameters, but no population-wide synchronization barrier is required. Alternating local parameter learning with slower model selection and hyperparameter changes yields schedules along training lineages, rather than restricting each run to one fixed hyperparameter configuration.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The original algorithm specifies application-dependent readiness, exploitation and exploration. PBA’s quartile selection, three-epoch interval, operation probabilities and schedule-only transfer are not general PBT requirements.
  • Asynchrony removes a global training barrier but does not remove the need to exchange model state and performance information. Reusing weights is not weight averaging or an inference-time ensemble.
  • Score-driven selection depends on the evaluation protocol, search space, budget and population behavior. The record asserts no global optimum, universal cost equivalence, wall-clock speedup or generalization guarantee.
  • Only the primary method and declared pages were inspected. Application-specific experiments, appendices and executable implementations are outside this record’s evidence scope.
