Nu support vector regression

Algorithms AIMech:NuSupportVectorRegression DRAFT
Aliases: Nu-SVR, ν-SVR, Nu-SV regression
A supervised regression algorithm that jointly fits a regularized kernel expansion and a nonnegative constant epsilon-insensitive tube half-width, using nu to penalize the width relative to linearly penalized residual excesses.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The original symmetric constant-width nu-SVR in Scholkopf, Smola, Williamson and Bartlett (2000) is the selected identity. It differs from fixed-epsilon SVR parameterization, the paper’s supplied parametric-insensitivity extension, later learned-insensitivity/twin models, supervised nu-SVC and one-class support estimation. Claims & Grounded Evidence

7 Claims
Mechanism #learned-tube-objective
For n>=1 input/real-response pairs and a positive-semidefinite kernel with feature map Phi, fit f(x)=w.Phi(x)+b by minimizing (1/2)||w||^2+C*(nu*epsilon+sum_i(xi_i+xi_star_i)/n), with C>0, nu>=0, epsilon>=0 and nonnegative slacks. The constraints are f(x_i)-y_i<=epsilon+xi_i and y_i-f(x_i)<=epsilon+xi_star_i. Thus the symmetric, input-independent tube half-width is optimized jointly with the fit; C and the kernel still require specification.
  • supports Source: New Support Vector Algorithms · Locator: Section 2, PDF pp4-6 / printed pp1210-1212, Equations 2.1-2.15
Mechanism #dual-and-recovery
Write beta_i=alpha_star_i-alpha_i. The dual maximizes sum_i beta_i*y_i-(1/2)*beta^T*K*beta, subject to sum_i beta_i=0, 0<=alpha_i,alpha_star_i<=C/n and sum_i(alpha_i+alpha_star_i)<=C*nu. Prediction is sum_i beta_i*k(x_i,x)+b. A free alpha_i gives f(x_i)-y_i=epsilon; a free alpha_star_i gives y_i-f(x_i)=epsilon. These equations can recover b and epsilon when adequate free coefficients exist. Otherwise the original KKT inequalities must be respected; neither an interior coefficient nor unique b, epsilon or dual coefficients is guaranteed.
  • supports Source: New Support Vector Algorithms · Locator: Section 2, PDF pp5-6 / printed pp1211-1212, Equations 2.6-2.15 and following KKT discussion; exact certificates in research/discovery/nu-svm-boundaries.py
Scope #conditional-nu-counts
At an exact optimum with epsilon>0, the fraction of training observations strictly outside the tube is at most nu, and the fraction with a nonzero expansion coefficient is at least nu. The two sides separately obey the corresponding nu/2 bounds relative to the entire sample, not relative to each side. These are inequalities, not exact finite counts or calibrated test error. For four zero inputs with responses -1,-1,1,1, C=1 and nu=1/2, an exact optimum has b=0, epsilon=1 and four free coefficients of magnitude1/8: every point is a support vector and none is outside. The paper’s asymptotic equality additionally uses iid data, conditional-density regularity and function-class arguments; it is not asserted here unconditionally.
  • supports Source: New Support Vector Algorithms · Locator: Section 2 Proposition 1 and discussion, PDF pp7-8 / printed pp1213-1214; finite certificate in research/discovery/nu-svm-boundaries.py
Scope #fixed-epsilon-correspondence
Fixing epsilon at a value learned by nu-SVR, while retaining the same C, gives an epsilon-SVR problem sharing that fitted primal optimum. This conditional correspondence does not make arbitrary independently selected epsilon and nu interchangeable. The original nu-SVR estimates one constant tube half-width; the asymmetric quantile losses and supplied heteroscedastic basis functions described in this paper change the objective or constraints. Later learned parametric-insensitive methods remain pending separate full-method assessment and are not covered by this source claim.
  • supports Source: New Support Vector Algorithms · Locator: Section 2 Proposition 2, PDF pp8-9 / printed pp1214-1215; Sections 3 and 5, PDF pp10 and12-14 / printed pp1216 and1218-1220
Limitation #degenerate-width-and-endpoints
A positive nu need not produce a positive epsilon. With all inputs and responses zero, zero coefficients, b=0 and epsilon=0 attain the exact minimum, so the positive-width support-count lower bound cannot be applied. At nu=0 an unpenalized sufficiently wide tube permits a constant fit. For nu>1, epsilon=0 reduces the loss to absolute residuals. At nu=1, epsilon and b can be nonunique: with two zero inputs, responses -1 and1 and C=1, (b,epsilon)=(0,0),(1/2,0),(0,1) all attain objective1. The fitted function or accepted tube can therefore vary between optimal solutions.
  • supports Source: New Support Vector Algorithms · Locator: Section 2, PDF pp6-8 / printed pp1212-1214, endpoint discussion and Equations 2.1-2.15; exact certificates in research/discovery/nu-svm-boundaries.py
Limitation #robustness-and-parameter-limits
The linear penalty outside the tube yields resistance to local response changes that keep those observations strictly outside on the same side; an existing optimum remains valid after matching slack changes. This is not resistance to arbitrary input leverage or unlimited contamination. The paper’s proposed asymptotically optimal nu calculation assumes a specified additive-noise scale family and convergence of the regression estimator, and is justified in a location-estimation model. The authors explicitly identify these restrictions; it is not a universal automatic nu selector.
  • supports Source: New Support Vector Algorithms · Locator: Section 3 Proposition 3, PDF pp9-10 / printed pp1215-1216; Section 4 and its final caveats, PDF pp10-12 / printed pp1216-1218
Limitation #evaluation-scope
The authors use LOQO for selected noisy-sinc experiments and a Boston-housing regression study. Sinc risk measures integrated absolute deviation from the noiseless target; the housing study reports response MSE over 100 random splits of 506 observations into 481 training and 25 test observations. Table 3 reports sweeps over nu and fixed epsilon, with other settings held fixed. The text compares nu-SVR with the best fixed-epsilon test result after evaluation; this is not nested parameter validation or proof of universal superiority. The supplied-noise-shape experiment studies a separate parametric-insensitivity extension. No numerical performance advantage is curated.
  • supports Source: New Support Vector Algorithms · Locator: Section 8.1, PDF pp24-34 / printed pp1230-1240, Figures 3-10 and Tables 2-3; discussion PDF p36 / printed p1242
