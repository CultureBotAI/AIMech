GSAT averaging-in strategy

Algorithms AIMech:GSATAveragingIn DRAFT
A GSAT restart-initialization strategy that reuses agreements between earlier complete assignments, randomizes their differing positions, and periodically resets to a fresh random start.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is Strategy II, Averaging in, in the 1993 GSAT extensions paper. The word averaging denotes Boolean agreement preservation and random filling of disagreements, not arithmetic averaging into fractional truth values. It reuses selected earlier near-solutions in a sequence of GSAT attempts; it is not a full genetic algorithm with population selection, crossover and mutation, although the authors note related features. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Within a reset block, start the first GSAT attempt from a random complete assignment and retain the best assignment encountered, defined by the fewest unsatisfied clauses. Form the second attempt’s initial assignment by averaging the first initial assignment with that first best assignment. For attempt i from 3 through RESET-TRIES, average the best assignments from attempts i−1 and i−2 to obtain its initial assignment, run the GSAT inner loop, and retain its best assignment. Averaging preserves a bit when both inputs agree and randomly assigns a truth value when they disagree. After the reset block, begin again from a fresh random assignment. The source motivates periodic resets because reused starts and best assignments can become similar; no diversity guarantee follows from the operation alone.
  • supports Source: Domain-Independent Extensions to GSAT: Solving Large Structured Satisfiability Problems · Locator: Section 3.2, PDF4, Strategy II pseudocode, definition of bitwise average and periodic reset explanation; Section 2/Figure 1, PDF1–2, base GSAT attempt

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The first two attempts use a different recurrence from later attempts: initial2 combines initial1 with best1, while initiali for i>=3 combines the two preceding best assignments. Substituting terminal states for best states, or using the same pair rule from the first attempt onward, changes the specification.
  • The pseudocode explicitly performs the first two attempts before its i=3 loop. An implementation must declare RESET-TRIES and budget boundary conventions, including a budget too small to complete that prefix, rather than silently infer arbitrary one-attempt behavior.
  • The inspected source says discrepant positions receive random truth values but does not fully specify joint random-number coupling or tied-best-assignment retention. No particular implementation of those boundaries is certified here.
  • Assignment reuse can concentrate starts and does not prove faster convergence, eventual SAT success or an UNSAT result on failure. The reported reset settings are empirical choices, not universal constants; no numerical benchmark claim is curated.
