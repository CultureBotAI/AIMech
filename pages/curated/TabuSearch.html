Aliases: TS
A memory-guided optimization framework that steers a search trajectory by making selected moves temporarily inadmissible from search history, allowing non-improving moves and, where specified, aspiration-based overrides.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Maintain the current solution, the best solution found, a neighborhood of applicable moves and memory-based tabu conditions on moves or their attributes. In the simple cost-minimizing version, choose the best admissible candidate under the configured evaluator even when every admissible move worsens the current objective, move there, and update the best-so-far record only on improvement. Update tabu memory so recent reversals or selected attributes remain restricted for a declared tenure, then expire restrictions according to that rule. Attribute-based restrictions can exclude unvisited solutions that share an attribute with a recent move. In the aspiration extension, a move satisfying the declared aspiration criterion can be admissible despite tabu status. Candidate sampling or approximate evaluation may replace full-neighborhood minimization. Intermediate-term memory can intensify search around features of good solutions, while longer-term frequency information can diversify it toward underexplored features; these are framework components whose concrete rules must be specified.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Finite recency memory is not a proof of cycle freedom or eventual global optimality. Attribute-based restrictions can exclude useful unvisited moves, and aspiration overrides must be designed with their effect on cycling in mind. The Part II finiteness construction was not inspected or generalized here.
  • The simple pseudocode stops when no admissible move exists, while the subsequent prose describes releasing oldest restrictions to restore a move. These are distinct handling choices; the conceptual record does not silently present them as one fully specified executable algorithm.
  • Tabu tenure, attributes, aspiration thresholds, candidate lists, move connectivity, initialization and stopping affect the search. Choosing the best currently admissible move does not imply monotonic objective improvement or a globally optimal final solution.
  • The source also discusses probabilistic, strategic-oscillation and mixed-integer extensions. This record does not instantiate all variants or curate performance comparisons. Memory-based search need not be a reinforcement-learning task.
