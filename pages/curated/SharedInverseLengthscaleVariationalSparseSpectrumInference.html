Shared inverse-lengthscale variational sparse-spectrum inference

Algorithms AIMech:SharedInverseLengthscaleVariationalSparseSpectrumInference DRAFT
The Tan–Ong–Nott–Jasra sparse-spectrum regression inference procedure that alternates a Gaussian nonconjugate variational proposal for a shared inverse-lengthscale vector with full Gaussian coefficient and half-Cauchy-derived scale-coordinate updates.

This descriptive local name denotes Tan et al. Algorithm 1 for a shared Gaussian inverse-lengthscale vector, full Gaussian coefficients and half-Cauchy-derived scale factors. It is a specified spectral inference procedure beyond generic NCVMP, not Gal–Turner independent spectral factors, the fixed-hyperparameter SSGP model, uncertain-test-input prediction, or the separately curated query-local neighborhood wrapper. Claims & Grounded Evidence

7 Claims
Scope #hierarchical-spectral-model
For n scalar responses y_i at fixed inputs x_i in R^d and m fixed draws s_r from N(0,I_d), define t_ir=s_r elementwise x_i and the 2m-column z_i(lambda) containing all cos(t_ir^T*lambda) followed by all sin(t_ir^T*lambda). The n by 2m matrix Z has rows z_i^T. Use y|alpha,lambda,gamma~N(Z*alpha,gamma^2*I_n), alpha|sigma~N(0,(sigma^2/m)*I_2m), lambda~N(mu0,Sigma0) with positive-definite Sigma0, and independent positive half-Cauchy scales sigma,gamma with known positive scale parameters A_sigma,A_gamma. Each half-Cauchy density is 2*A/(pi*(A^2+s^2)) for s>0. The signed vector lambda is shared by every spectral feature; its magnitude is an inverse-lengthscale under the displayed kernel convention. The spectral draws are held fixed during this inference.
  • supports Source: Variational inference for sparse spectrum Gaussian process regression · Locator: Section 2, Equations 1 and 4–5, PDF pp. 3–4; Section 5 spectral-draw conditioning, PDF p. 7
Mechanism #variational-family-objective
Use q(alpha)q(lambda)q(sigma)q(gamma), with full Gaussian factors q(alpha)=N(mu_alpha,Sigma_alpha) and q(lambda)=N(mu_lambda,Sigma_lambda). The scale factors have their unrestricted coordinate-optimal forms stated below. Target L=E_q[log p(y,alpha,lambda,sigma,gamma)]-E_q[log q], a lower bound on log p(y) when the posterior is proper, the evidence positive and finite, and the expectations well-defined. Within-block covariances are retained, while dependence between coefficients, shared inverse-lengthscales and scales is omitted. This approximation does not separately factorize each transformed spectral frequency.
  • supports Source: Variational inference for sparse spectrum Gaussian process regression · Locator: Section 3, Equations 6–11, PDF pp. 4–5; Appendix B, PDF pp. 19–20
Mechanism #shared-feature-moments
At current q(lambda)=N(mu,Sigma), define c(t)=exp(-t^T*Sigma*t/2)*cos(t^T*mu) and s(t)=exp(-t^T*Sigma*t/2)*sin(t^T*mu). These give the feature first moments. For any two feature arguments a,b, E[cos(a^T*lambda)cos(b^T*lambda)]=(c(a-b)+c(a+b))/2, E[sin(a^T*lambda)sin(b^T*lambda)]=(c(a-b)-c(a+b))/2 and E[cos(a^T*lambda)sin(b^T*lambda)]=(s(a+b)+s(b-a))/2. Consequently F=E[Z] and K=E[Z^T*Z]=sum_i E[z_i*z_i^T] are analytic. Different spectral features generally have nonzero covariance because they depend on the same uncertain vector; K cannot generally be replaced by F^T*F.
  • supports Source: Variational inference for sparse spectrum Gaussian process regression · Locator: Appendix A, Lemma 1, Equations 17–20 and matrix moment blocks, PDF pp. 18–19
Mechanism #coefficient-coordinate
With current F=E[Z], K=E[Z^T*Z], u=E[gamma^-2] and v=E[sigma^-2], the exact Gaussian coefficient coordinate is Sigma_alpha=(u*K+m*v*I_2m)^-1 and mu_alpha=u*Sigma_alpha*F^T*y, provided these moments are finite and positive. K is positive semidefinite, so m*v>0 makes the displayed precision positive definite. The full covariance retains correlations between the finite coefficients. This is optimal holding the other variational factors fixed, not exact integration of the true coupled coefficient and inverse-lengthscale posterior.
  • supports Source: Variational inference for sparse spectrum Gaussian process regression · Locator: Section 3.1 and Algorithm 1 steps 3–4, PDF pp. 5–6; Appendix B, PDF p. 19
Mechanism #scale-coordinates
Set M=mu_alpha*mu_alpha^T+Sigma_alpha, C_sigma=(m/2)*tr(M) and C_gamma=(y^T*y-2*y^T*F*mu_alpha+tr(M*K))/2. The coordinate densities are q(sigma) proportional to sigma^(-2m)*exp(-C_sigma/sigma^2)/(A_sigma^2+sigma^2) and q(gamma) proportional to gamma^(-n)*exp(-C_gamma/gamma^2)/(A_gamma^2+gamma^2), on positive scales. For C,A>0, define J_k(C,A)=integral_0^infinity r^(-k)*exp(-C/r^2)/(A^2+r^2) dr, finite for k>-1. Their inverse-square expectations are J_(2m+2)(C_sigma,A_sigma)/J_(2m)(C_sigma,A_sigma) and J_(n+2)(C_gamma,A_gamma)/J_n(C_gamma,A_gamma). Substitution x=1/r relates J_k to the paper’s H(k-2,C,A^2); normalizer evaluation uses one-dimensional integration. Gaussian feature moments being analytic does not make every scale normalization an elementary closed-form computation.
  • supports Source: Variational inference for sparse spectrum Gaussian process regression · Locator: Section 3.1 scale densities and H integral, PDF p. 5; Algorithm 1 steps 5–6, PDF p. 6; Appendix B, PDF pp. 19–20
Mechanism #lambda-natural-proposal
Holding the other factors fixed, let S=E_q[log p(y,alpha,lambda,sigma,gamma)] as a function of the current lambda-factor mean mu and covariance Sigma. Define g=grad_mu S and the symmetric covariance derivative G by dS=g^T*dmu+tr(G^T*dSigma). Evaluate both derivatives at the same old mu,Sigma. The unit NCVMP proposal is Sigma_new=(-2*G)^-1 and mu_new=mu+Sigma_new*g, valid as a Gaussian factor only if -2*G is positive definite. In the source Algorithm 1 cycle, this lambda proposal precedes recomputing feature moments, the Gaussian coefficient coordinate, and the two scale coordinates. Reevaluate the moments after accepted changes. The lambda proposal is generally a fixed-point/natural-gradient step, not an exact Gaussian-family coordinate maximizer; no bound-ascent or convergence guarantee follows.
  • supports Source: Variational inference for sparse spectrum Gaussian process regression · Locator: Section 3, Equations 8–10 and Algorithm 1, PDF pp. 5–6; Section 4 convergence discussion, PDF p. 7; Appendix C, PDF p. 20
  • supports Source: Fully Simplified Multivariate Normal Updates in Non-Conjugate Variational Message Passing · Locator: Section 4, Theorem 1 and Equation 7, PDF pp. 5–7 / printed pp. 1355–1357; Appendix A, PDF pp. 13–15 / printed pp. 1363–1365
Mechanism #predictive-moments
For a fixed query x_star, form the same 2m-column z_star(lambda), f_star=E[z_star] and R_star=E[z_star*z_star^T]. Under the independent variational factors, the future noisy-response mean is mu_alpha^T*f_star and its variance, when finite, is E[gamma^2]+tr(Sigma_alpha*R_star)+mu_alpha^T*(R_star-f_star*f_star^T)*mu_alpha. The latent-function variance omits E[gamma^2]. These are moments of the variational predictive mixture, which is generally not Gaussian, and do not establish calibrated intervals or an exact full-kernel GP posterior. For the proper C_gamma,A_gamma>0 scale density, E[gamma^2]=J_(n-2)/J_n is finite only for n>1; its n=1 tail yields a logarithmically divergent second moment.
  • supports Source: Variational inference for sparse spectrum Gaussian process regression · Locator: Section 5, Equation 15 and predictive-moment derivation, PDF pp. 7–8; scale density in Section 3.1, PDF p. 5

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This is the base shared-inverse-lengthscale spectral inference procedure. Mean-field restrictions can underrepresent coefficient/spectral dependence, and initialization and local stationary structure can affect the result. A finite mixture over uncertain inverse-lengthscales and scales is not automatically one Gaussian process with the original squared-exponential kernel. No general posterior-accuracy, runtime or model-selection superiority is asserted.
  • Proper Gaussian factors, finite current expectations and normalizable scale factors are required. The n=1 scale density can be proper while its second moment diverges. The paper’s particular scale initialization has positive C_gamma only for n>2 with positive response variance and positive C_sigma only for m>1 with positive response variance; it is not a universal initializer for all positive n,m.
  • The source’s Algorithm 1 covariance/mean lines are interpreted as one Gaussian natural-parameter proposal with both derivatives evaluated at the old mean and covariance, as derived by the general NCVMP formula. Appendix C has an inconsistent printed covariance evaluation point. Recomputing the mean derivative after changing only the covariance would be a different update. The saved scalar F1–F4 check supports this convention; no executable author implementation is certified.
  • Adaptive Algorithm 2 permits natural steps above one and introduces positive-definiteness and objective checks. Its printed step-size initialization/index/reset control flow remains ambiguous, so it is not asserted as a separately verified controller here. The simplified Equation 12 objective applies after the scale-coordinate updates; it is not automatically valid for arbitrary trial lambda changes with stale scale factors. A stopping tolerance is not a convergence proof.
  • The source calls lambda a lengthscale although its displayed squared-exponential exponent uses inverse-lengthscale magnitudes. Its spectral-density covariance prose conflicts with the subsequent feature reparameterization. Componentwise sign changes at fixed finite spectral draws do not generally leave the finite model unchanged. The predictive display’s inner/outer feature transpose is resolved by the preceding squared-response expectation and the declared column-vector dimensions; this record uses the outer moment R_star.
  • All 21 preprint pages and all 19 pages of the supporting Wand paper were read. Journal metadata does not establish inspection of the complete Tan journal PDF. Direct access to the author’s publication page later succeeded, but the inspected sparse-spectrum entry and home-page links yielded no code link; this is not an exhaustive code-absence conclusion. No author code, model fitting loop, training or benchmark was run. Supplied-parameter arithmetic checks feature moments, scale normalization, a Gaussian coordinate, one scalar derivative example and predictive moments; DRAFT remains.
