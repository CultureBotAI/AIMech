Extended Guided Local Search

Algorithms AIMech:ExtendedGuidedLocalSearch DRAFT
Aliases: EGLS
The Tsang–Mills–Ford extension of Guided Local Search that combines cost-scaled penalty weighting, aspiration for a new best original-objective solution despite an augmented-objective penalty, and probabilistic random neighborhood moves.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope EGLS here denotes the combined coefficient, aspiration and randomness extension in CSM371. GLS+A and GLS+R are separate ablations, not exact aliases of the combined algorithm. The method modifies the GLS search framework; it is not FLS activation-bit bookkeeping, simulated annealing, or a generic name for any later GLS extension. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Retain GLS feature counters, augmented objective h=g+lambda*sum(p_i*I_i), and penalty increments for present features tied for maximal cost/(1+p_i) utility. Scale lambda=a*C, where C estimates a characteristic cost contribution; the TSP illustration uses the first local-minimum tour cost divided by the number of cities. Add the report’s literal aspiration condition: accept an examined neighbor with g(s_next)<g(best_so_far) even when h(s_next)>h(s_current). This bypasses an augmented-cost rejection; it does not erase feature counters. Add randomization so that, with configured probability P_r per move, a randomly selected neighbor is accepted regardless of its objective or penalty quality. The combined EGLS uses all three changes. Its reported recommendations a=1 and P_r=.2 are experimental defaults, not universal constants or a proof of parameter independence.
  • supports Source: Extending Guided Local Search – towards a metaheuristic algorithm with no parameters to tune · Locator: Sections2–6, PDF pp.2–5, GLS pseudocode, penalty coefficient, strict aspiration test and randomness; discussion/recommendations Sections7–8, pp.6–8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The report title does not establish a parameter-free algorithm. Feature definitions, neighborhood and local solver, cost estimate C, coefficient a, randomization probability P_r, initialization and stopping remain choices; the proposed defaults were evaluated only in the reported problem settings.
  • The literal aspiration inequality is strict in both original improvement and augmented deterioration. It does not specify equality handling when h(s_next)=h(s_current); the underlying local solver and concrete implementation must declare that boundary and the order of random versus ordinary/aspiration moves.
  • Scaling by a typical cost contribution is problem-dependent. The TSP first-tour average is not a universal normalization for arbitrary objectives, especially zero, negative or mixed-scale terms.
  • Random moves can worsen either objective and need not improve exploration on a particular step. Aspiration protects access to an examined new original best, not global optimality or monotonic improvement of the entire trajectory. No comparative performance claim is curated.
