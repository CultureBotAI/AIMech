Barker acceptance rule

Mathematical Methods AIMech:BarkerAcceptanceRule DRAFT
An acceptance-probability rule for reversible Monte Carlo proposals that uses R/(1+R), where R is the target-density ratio times the reverse-to-forward proposal ratio.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The record covers Hastings’s s_ij=1 form, which reduces to Barker’s 1965 rule for symmetric proposals. It is a scalar decision rule used inside a sampler, not a complete choice of proposal, a logistic-regression model, or the Metropolis min(1,R) rule. The original 1965 plasma paper was located but its full text has not been inspected for this record. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a current state x and proposed state y with positive finite denominator, compute R=pi(y)q(x|y)/(pi(x)q(y|x)) and accept with alpha=R/(1+R). This follows algebraically by choosing s_ij=1 in Hastings Equation (6). With a symmetric proposal alpha=pi(y)/(pi(x)+pi(y)). Pairing the rule with the proposal and retaining x on rejection yields detailed balance. At equal positive target weights and symmetric q, acceptance is one half rather than the Metropolis value one.
  • supports Source: Monte Carlo sampling methods using Markov chains and their applications · Locator: Section 2.2, Equation (6) and s_ij^(B)=1, with symmetric-proposal alpha_ij^(B), printed pp. 99–100 / PDF pp. 3–4

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The asymmetric-proposal formula is an explicit algebraic restatement of Hastings’s general construction, not a claim that the inspected source is Barker’s original paper.
  • The rule alone supplies neither a proposal distribution nor an ergodic or rapidly mixing chain. Support, boundary cases and numerical evaluation still require specification.
  • The same ratio generally gives different acceptance probabilities under Barker and Metropolis rules. A lower or higher acceptance probability alone does not prove a universal computational-efficiency ordering.
  • No claim is made about newer methods called Barker proposals or gradient-based Barker samplers; those can use different proposal mechanisms and need separate identity review.
