Relevant component analysis

Algorithms AIMech:RelevantComponentAnalysis DRAFT
Aliases: RCA
A partially supervised linear metric-learning procedure that estimates within-group covariance from positive equivalence constraints and whitens the representation using that covariance.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope RCA forms chunklets from observations known to share a class without requiring their class names. Different chunklets need not represent different classes. It estimates variation within chunklets, unlike whitening by total covariance, and it does not optimize NCA’s stochastic neighbor reward. The maintained LDA record is a Gaussian classifier, not RCA or the optional constraints-based Fisher projection. This record describes batch RCA; online adaptation and optional dimensionality-reduction procedures are qualified extensions. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method
Take the transitive closure of positive same-class constraints to obtain chunklets C_j with means m_j. Let N be the total number of observations in the chunklets, as clarified in Section 5, and compute Chat=(1/N)sum_j sum_(x in C_j)(x-m_j)(x-m_j)^T. When Chat is positive definite on the represented space, use the symmetric whitening transform W=Chat^(-1/2) on the original feature vectors. Equivalently, compare vectors with squared distance (x-y)^T Chat^(-1)(x-y). Large within-chunklet variance directions receive less weight. The method uses positive constraints without requiring explicit class labels or negative constraints. The paper permits an optional reduction before whitening and describes a constraints-based Fisher discriminant projection; nonsingularity must hold in the retained space.

Limitation #source-convention-check
Curator calculation: two chunklets {(0,0),(1,0),(2,0)} and {(10,0),(11,0),(12,0)} have within-chunklet covariance diag(2/3,0), of rank 1. The count sum_j(|C_j|-1)=4 exceeds the ambient dimension 2 while the covariance is singular. This disproves treating the sample-size count as a general exact rank or invertibility certificate. The exact calculation is saved in research/discovery/metric-source-boundaries.py.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The inverse and inverse square root require a nonsingular covariance on the represented space. Few, singleton or geometrically redundant chunklets may give a singular estimate. This record does not invent a universal pseudoinverse or regularization fallback.
  • Algorithm 2 calls sum_j(|C_j|-1) the covariance rank. In general it is a sample-size upper bound, not the actual rank; collinearity can leave the covariance singular even when that count exceeds the feature dimension. The preceding prose’s rank-greater-than-dimension condition is not a feasible matrix-rank requirement. The source heuristic is not copied as an invertibility guarantee.
  • Whitening can amplify low-variance noise, and erroneous equivalence constraints can contaminate entire chunklets through transitive closure. Small within-group variance is not itself evidence of relevance to a downstream task.
  • The paper’s likelihood and information-optimality results have stated modeling assumptions; this record claims neither universal classification improvement nor unconditional statistical optimality. Optional cFLD, online RCA and their implementation details require separate scope assessments. No model was trained.
