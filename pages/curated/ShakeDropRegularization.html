ShakeDrop regularization

A residual-network regularization procedure that uses a Bernoulli switch to alternate ordinary residual computation with separately specified forward and prescribed backward multipliers, and uses an expected forward coefficient for deterministic inference.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Scope #gated-residual-perturbation
ShakeDrop is a stochastic regularization procedure for residual networks. Within an identity-skip block, a Bernoulli gate selects either the ordinary residual computation or a perturbed residual contribution. The construction can operate on one learned residual branch plus its skip path; it does not require the two learned residual branches used by the compared Shake-Shake construction. It is a training and inference procedure, not a complete network architecture or a stand-alone parameter optimizer.
  • supports Source: ShakeDrop Regularization for Deep Residual Learning · Locator: Figure 1 and Section II, PDF p. 2; Section III-A, Equations (6)–(8), PDF p. 3; Section III-B.3, PDF pp. 3–4.
Mechanism #distinct-forward-backward-coefficients
For a residual block x+F(x), use forward residual multiplier c_f=b_l+(1-b_l)*alpha and prescribed backward residual multiplier c_b=b_l+(1-b_l)*beta. The same Bernoulli gate b_l is used in both passes. When b_l=1, both multipliers equal one; when b_l=0, they are alpha and beta respectively. The source uses mutually independent alpha and beta, including a configured constant-zero alpha case. The backward expression prescribes derivative scaling through the residual path; it is not another forward evaluation or a claim that beta is the derivative of the realized forward multiplier.
  • supports Source: ShakeDrop Regularization for Deep Residual Learning · Locator: Figure 1(d), PDF p. 2; Section III-A, Equations (6)–(8), PDF p. 3; Section IV-A, PDF p. 5.
Mechanism #depth-dependent-switching
Set P(b_l=1)=p_l using the displayed depth schedule p_l=1-(l/L)*(1-p_L), where l indexes the residual blocks from the input, L is their total number, and p_L is the configured probability at the last block. For 0<=p_L<1, later blocks have a greater probability of entering the perturbed case. This is a schedule across network depth, not an annealing schedule over training iterations. The paper varies p_L with architecture and experimental setting rather than fixing one universal probability.
  • supports Source: ShakeDrop Regularization for Deep Residual Learning · Locator: Section II, Equation (5), and Section III-A, PDF p. 3; Sections V-B and V-E, PDF pp. 8–9; Appendix A, PDF pp. 9–10.
Mechanism #sampling-and-host-settings
The 2019 author copy compares batch, image, channel and element-level sharing of alpha and beta and uses element-level coefficients in its principal experiments. It uses alpha=0 with beta uniform on [0,1] for the tested original architectures, and alpha uniform on [-1,1] with beta uniform on [0,1] for the tested modified residual branches ending with batch normalization. These are experimentally selected configurations. The original 2018 preprint instead selects image-level sharing for memory efficiency while reporting a lower pixel-level error in its preliminary comparison; that earlier default is retained as version context.
  • supports Source: ShakeDrop Regularization for Deep Residual Learning · Locator: Sections IV-A and IV-B, Tables 2–3, PDF p. 5; Section V-A and Table 5 caption, PDF p. 7; Appendix A, PDF pp. 9–10.
  • supports Source: ShakeDrop regularization · Locator: Section 4.1, Table 2 and the following sharing-level discussion, PDF p. 5; Appendix A, PDF p. 10.
Mechanism #deterministic-test-rule
At inference, replace the random forward multiplier by E[b_l+(1-b_l)*alpha]. For p_l<1 this expectation is p_l+(1-p_l)*E[alpha | b_l=0]; independent gate and coefficient draws with zero-mean alpha reduce it to p_l. This includes the stated constant-zero alpha and symmetric uniform alpha configurations. When p_l=1, the coefficient is one directly. This defines a deterministic local inference rule, not the exact expected output of the complete nonlinear network.
  • supports Source: ShakeDrop Regularization for Deep Residual Learning · Locator: Figure 1(d), PDF p. 2; Section III-A, Equation (6) and coefficient ranges, PDF p. 3; Appendix A inference use, PDF p. 10. The conditional-expectation expansion is a direct consequence of the displayed Bernoulli mixture, with its assumptions stated here.
Scope #distinction-and-special-cases
Setting alpha=beta=0 reduces the displayed residual forward and backward operation to the Bernoulli RandomDrop or stochastic-depth operation, with its expected gate coefficient at inference. Setting alpha=beta=1 removes this perturbation. Those parameter boundaries do not identify the full methods as aliases. In particular, b_l=0 with alpha=0 and beta>0 removes the residual value in the forward pass while still permitting a nonzero prescribed residual derivative, unlike dropping that path in both passes.
  • supports Source: ShakeDrop Regularization for Deep Residual Learning · Locator: Section II, Equation (4), and Section III-A, Equations (6)–(8), PDF p. 3; Section III-C, PDF p. 4; Table 2 cases A and B, PDF p. 5; Section IV-C final paragraph, PDF pp. 6–7.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Independent forward and backward multipliers generally do not give the chain-rule derivative of the realized forward computation or an unbiased gradient of its expected loss. The prescribed rule is intentional. The source ratio beta/alpha requires a nonzero denominator and does not by itself imply an unbounded absolute backward multiplier; alpha=0 is an explicit configuration. With negative alpha and positive beta, the sign comparison concerns the residual contribution relative to the derivative of that same perturbed forward pass, not necessarily the gradient of an unperturbed network.
  • Averaging local coefficients does not commute with subsequent nonlinearities, losses or training-dependent batch-normalization state. The inference rule is not an exact full-network ensemble average or a calibrated uncertainty estimate. Simplifying its coefficient to p_l when p_l<1 requires E[alpha | b_l=0]=0; this is satisfied, for example, by independent gate and coefficient draws with zero-mean alpha.
  • The fail-safe and local-minimum explanations and the conceptual trajectories in Figure 2 are heuristic. They establish no general convergence, nondivergence or superiority guarantee. Tables 2–3 and the later experiments show that coefficient ranges, sharing, depth, architecture and p_L matter. Valid Bernoulli probabilities and compatible residual/skip shapes are prerequisites of the displayed construction.
  • Source versions use different defaults. The original preprint selects image-level sharing for memory efficiency and emphasizes batch-normalization-ending branches; the later author copy uses element-level sharing and also studies constant-zero alpha on original architectures. Their Type A and Type B names for separate-versus-summed residual perturbation are reversed: the original preprint describes A before and B after the residual sum, whereas the later Equations (10)–(11) define A after and B before. Those labels and experimental settings are not silently unified.
  • The same gate is used across the forward and backward rules. Resampling it between passes would define a different procedure. A zero backward multiplier suppresses the local residual-path loss-gradient contribution, not necessarily every parameter or state change under momentum, weight decay, other paths or running statistics. A forward-zero branch with a nonzero prescribed backward contribution does not justify skipping all residual computation. No implementation-level audit of every sharing or parallelism convention is asserted.
  • The principal evidence is the specifically retained 2019 author-hosted accepted-proof-layout copy with local pages 1–11 and placeholder publication dates. Its DOI and bibliographic journal pages identify the article, but this record does not assert byte identity with a final publisher PDF or arXiv v3. The original 2018 v1 preprint is separately cited as version context. File-build timestamps and current acquisition times are not publication dates.
  • The evidence concerns specified residual CNNs, datasets and training settings. Architecture changes, controls and training schedules differ across versions. No new numerical benefit, universal stability, causal-ablation result, speedup guarantee or benchmark reproduction is claimed. The source texts and selected formula/table visuals were read, but no author implementation, model training or later library behavior was inspected or executed.
