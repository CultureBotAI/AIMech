Reverse-submartingale confidence sequences for convex divergences and functionals

Mathematical Methods AIMech:ReverseSubmartingaleConvexDivergenceConfidenceSequences DRAFT
Aliases: Reverse-Submartingale Convex Functional Confidence Sequences, Exchangeable-Filtration Reverse-Stitched Divergence Confidence Sequences, Partially Ordered Reverse-Submartingale Two-Sample Divergence Confidence Sequences, Time-Uniform Plug-In Convex Divergence Estimation
A sequential nonparametric estimation and inference methodology (Manole & Ramdas 2023, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 69(7):4641-4658, arXiv:2103.09267) that constructs time-uniform confidence sequences for convex functionals Phi(P) of a single distribution and convex divergences Psi(P, Q) between two distributions — including integral probability metrics (Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance, kernel Maximum Mean Discrepancy, positive-definite V-statistics), optimal transport costs (Wasserstein and Gaussian-smoothed Wasserstein distances), phi-divergences (Kullback-Leibler divergence, Total Variation, and Gaussian-smoothed divergences), classification generalization risks (Rademacher complexity), and general-norm multivariate means — by exploiting the property that empirical measures P_t and Q_s are measure-valued reverse martingales with respect to the exchangeable filtrations (E_t^X)_{t>=1} and (E_s^Y)_{s>=1}, so plug-in estimators Phi(P_t) and Psi(P_t, Q_s) form reverse submartingales (or partially ordered reverse submartingales on N^2) to which Ville's reverse maximal inequality and Christofides-Serfling's partially ordered maximal inequality are stitched across geometric sample-size epochs while affine variational minorants provide matching upper confidence sequences.

Mechanism #exchangeable-filtration-reverse-submartingale-loo-and-partially-ordered-ville-inequality
For i.i.d. observations X_1, X_2, ... ~ P and Y_1, Y_2, ... ~ Q, the empirical measures P_t = (1/t) sum_{i=1}^t delta_{X_i} and Q_s = (1/s) sum_{j=1}^s delta_{Y_j} are measure-valued reverse martingales with respect to the exchangeable sigma-fields E_t^X and E_s^Y satisfying P_t(A) = E[P_{t-1}(A) | E_t^X] = E[I(X_1 in A) | E_t^X] almost surely (Section 3.1, Appendix B.1 Lemma 27), so for any convex functional Phi and convex two-sample functional Psi — or any permutation-invariant sequence satisfying the leave-one-out inequality (1/(t+1)) sum_{i=1}^{t+1} f_t(X_{-i}) >= f_{t+1}(X_1, ..., X_{t+1}) (Proposition 5), including integral probability metrics, phi-divergences, optimal transport costs (Lemma 1), and positive-definite V-statistics V_t and sqrt(V_t) (Proposition 16, whereas U-statistics form reverse martingales for arbitrary symmetric kernels) — the plug-in process (Phi(P_t))_{t>=1} is a reverse submartingale wrt (E_t^X)_{t>=1} and (Psi(P_t, Q_s))_{t,s>=1} is a partially ordered reverse submartingale wrt E_{ts} = E_t^X vee E_s^Y (Definition 1, Theorem 4) satisfying Ville's reverse maximal inequality P(exists t >= t_0 : R_t >= u) <= E[R_{t_0}] / u (Theorem 2) and Christofides & Serfling's partially ordered maximal inequality (Proposition 3, Equations 14–15).

Mechanism #reverse-cramer-chernoff-geometric-epoch-stitching-variational-minorant-and-stopping-time-bias
Exponentiating the centered processes N_t = Phi(P_t) - Phi(P) and M_{ts} = Psi(P_t, Q_s) - Psi(P, Q) yields one-sample and two-sample reverse Cramér-Chernoff tail bounds P(exists t >= t_0 : N_t >= u) <= exp(-psi_{t_0}^*(u)) and P(exists t >= t_0, s >= s_0 : M_{ts} >= u) <= e * exp(-psi_{t_0 s_0}^*(u)) where psi_t^*(u) = sup_{lambda>=0}{lambda u - psi_t(lambda)} (Proposition 6, Equation 19), which when stitched across geometric epochs [2^j, 2^{j+1}) and [2^j, 2^{j+1}) x [2^k, 2^{k+1}) with bar{t} = ceil(t/2), bar{s} = ceil(s/2) and stitching functions ell, g produce time-uniform lower confidence sequences P(exists t >= 1 : Phi(P_t) >= Phi(P) + gamma_t) <= delta and P(exists t, s >= 1 : Psi(P_t, Q_s) >= Psi(P, Q) + gamma_{ts}) <= delta matching the offline concentration rate up to iterated-logarithm log ell(log_2 t) factors without knowing finite-sample variance (Theorem 7, Corollary 8, Equations 20–21); conversely, when Phi(mu) = sup_{f in F} int f dmu and Psi(mu, nu) = sup_{(f,g) in G} (int f dmu + int g dnu) attain their suprema at f_Phi and (f_Psi, g_Psi), N_t and M_{ts} are minorized by mean-zero sample-average reverse martingales R_t = int f_Phi d(P_t - P) and R_t^X + R_s^Y (Proposition 9, Equations 22–23), proving nonnegative stopping-time upward bias E[Phi(P_tau)] >= Phi(P) and E[Psi(P_tau, Q_sigma)] >= Psi(P, Q) for all stopping times (Corollary 10) and yielding matching upper confidence sequences (Corollary 11, Appendix C.3 Proposition 29) and two-sided confidence sequences valid uniformly over all stopping times in N^2 (Proposition 12), including sequential Kolmogorov-Smirnov (Corollaries 13–14) and kernel MMD (Corollary 15).

Mechanism #finite-support-phi-divergences-optimal-transport-and-gaussian-smoothed-parametric-rates
For discrete distributions on k >= 2 support points, reverse-stitching yields nonasymptotic confidence sequences for Kullback-Leibler divergence KL(P_t || P) via the exact polynomial moment generating function upper bound G_{k,t}(lambda) of Guo & Richardson (2021) (Proposition 18, Equation 35) and for Total Variation ||P_t - P||_TV with boundary sqrt((k/(4t)) max(1, 4/pi - 2/t)) + 2 sqrt((1/t)(log ell(log_2 t) + log(1/delta))) (Corollary 19); for continuous distributions on R^d where unsmoothed empirical phi-divergences are degenerate (||P_t - P||_TV = 1) and unsmoothed alpha-Holder optimal transport suffers O((t wedge s)^{-alpha/d}) curse-of-dimensionality bias (Corollary 17, Equations 33–34), convolving P_t and P with a Gaussian kernel K_sigma of bandwidth sigma > 0 preserves functional convexity and restores parametric O(sqrt((log log t)/t)) confidence sequences in arbitrary dimension d for Gaussian-smoothed Total Variation ||P_t - P||_TV^sigma under tau^2-sub-Gaussian tails (Proposition 20(i), Equation 36), Gaussian-smoothed 1-Wasserstein distance W_1^sigma(P_t, P) under Talagrand T_1(tau^2) transportation inequalities (Proposition 20(ii), Equation 37, Appendix D.5 Lemma 31), and plug-in Gaussian-smoothed differential entropy h(P * K_sigma) on [-1, 1]^d (Corollary 21).

Mechanism #rademacher-generalization-norm-estimation-upper-lil-and-forward-supermartingale-failure
Beyond divergences, reverse-submartingale stitching establishes (1) stopping-time-valid uniform classification risk bounds sup_{f in F} |R_t(f) - R(f)| <= R_{floor(t/2)}(F) + 2 sqrt((2/t)(log ell(log_2 t) + log(1/delta))) and the leave-one-out reverse-submartingale property of empirical Rademacher complexity R_hat_t(F) wrt E_t^X yielding a time-uniform lower bound on R_{floor(t/2)}(F) (Section 4.6, Corollary 22, Appendix D.6), (2) general-norm multivariate mean confidence sequences ||mu_t - mu|| via gamma-covering numbers N_gamma of the dual unit sphere S_*^{d-1} scaling at O(sqrt((log log t + d)/t)) (Section 4.7, Corollary 23, Equations 38–40, Appendix D.7), and (3) a sharp one-sided upper Law of the Iterated Logarithm limsup_{t->infty} sqrt(t / (2 sigma^2 log log t)) D(P_t || P) <= 1 almost surely whenever E[D(P_t || P)] = o(sqrt((log log t)/t)) (Section 4.8, Corollary 24, Equation 41, Appendix D.8); in contrast, Appendix E (Lemma 32, Lemma 33, Theorem 34) proves that forward canonical-filtration supermartingales L_t(lambda) = exp(lambda t D(P_t || P) - t psi(lambda)) fail to yield vanishing-width confidence sequences because E[D(delta_{X_1} || P)] > 0 (e.g., equal to Shannon entropy H(P) > 0 for discrete KL divergence), forcing psi(lambda) to decay only linearly as lambda -> 0+ and leaving a non-vanishing asymptotic width floor.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Because plug-in estimators of convex functionals Phi(P_t) are upward biased (E[Phi(P_t)] is nonincreasing in t), every lower confidence sequence in Theorem 7 and Corollary 8 requires an explicit upper bound on the finite-sample expected bias E[Phi(P_{ceil(t/2)}) - Phi(P)] at half the sample size; for unsmoothed optimal transport in dimension d >= 3, this expected bias scales as O((t wedge s)^{-alpha/d}) (Corollary 17) with leading constants that are often not known in closed form.
  • While Gaussian convolution smoothing removes the curse of dimensionality from the O(t^{-1/2}) sample-size exponent for smoothed Total Variation and smoothed 1-Wasserstein confidence sequences (Proposition 20), the multiplicative prefactors c_d and C_d grow exponentially with dimension d when the smoothing bandwidth sigma is fixed (Section 4.5, p. 4651).
  • Upper confidence sequences for Phi(P) and Psi(P, Q) cannot be obtained by applying Ville's maximal inequality directly to -Phi(P_t) (which is a reverse supermartingale and lacks an infinite-horizon reverse maximal inequality), and instead require a variational supremum representation whose supremum is attained at an integrable witness function f_Phi or (f_Psi, g_Psi) with controlled tail behavior (Proposition 9, Corollary 11, Proposition 29); similarly, while Corollary 22(ii) provides a time-uniform lower bound on population Rademacher complexity R_{floor(t/2)}(F) via empirical Rademacher complexity R_hat_t(F), deriving a time-uniform upper confidence sequence for R_t(F) to yield a fully empirical stopping-time generalization bound remains an open problem (Section 4.6, p. 4652).
