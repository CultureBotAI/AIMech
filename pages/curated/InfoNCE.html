InfoNCE

Mathematical Methods AIMech:InfoNCE DRAFT
Aliases: InfoNCE loss
A contrastive log-softmax objective that identifies a joint positive among sampled candidates and, under independent marginal-negative sampling, gives an expectation-level lower bound on mutual information.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope InfoNCE is the loss introduced in contrastive predictive coding, separable from the CPC encoder/context/prediction procedure. It does not by itself prescribe cosine similarity, temperature, augmentation pairs, symmetric losses or an autoregressive architecture. Named configurations such as SimCLR NT-Xent and DIM’s finite-masking implementation require their own sampling and numerical qualifications. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Given a context c, draw one positive x_+ jointly with c and N−1 negative candidates independently from the marginal p(x), with N>=2. For real scores s(x,c), define ell=−log[exp(s(x_+,c))/sum_(j=1)^N exp(s(x_j,c))], including the positive in the denominator, and minimize L_NCE=E[ell]. For the ideal sampling scheme, log(N)−L_NCE<=I(X;C); since ell>=0, the bound is at most log(N) and may itself be negative. An expressive optimum scores the conditional-to-marginal density ratio up to a context-dependent multiplicative constant in exp(s). The categorical objective remains definable with other candidate constructions, but its stated mutual-information interpretation then requires a separate argument. Poole et al. provide an independent rigorous derivation of the expected bound beyond the approximate step in the original CPC appendix.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The lower bound holds for an expectation under its specified joint and independent marginal sampling. A realized finite-batch number is not certified to be below true mutual information.
  • The log(N) ceiling limits estimation of high mutual information at a fixed candidate count. Optimizing a finite, restricted critic need not recover the density ratio or true information.
  • False semantic negatives, conditional negative proposals and correlated in-batch candidates can change the learned representation and invalidate an unqualified use of the ideal sampling proof. Semantic usefulness is not guaranteed by the loss.
  • Finite masking values contribute nonzero denominator mass and are not algebraically identical to removing candidates. No numerical implementation or training experiment was run for this loss record.
