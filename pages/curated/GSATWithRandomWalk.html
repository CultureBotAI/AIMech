GSAT with random walk

Algorithms AIMech:GSATWithRandomWalk DRAFT
Aliases: GWSAT
A GSAT variant that mixes a uniformly chosen flip from the distinct variables currently occurring in unsatisfied clauses with a globally best GSAT flip, using a fixed probability to choose the focused random move.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the mixed random-walk extension of GSAT described by Selman and Kautz and clarified in the later noise paper. Its focused move samples a duplicate-free union of eligible variables, not a clause followed by one of its variables. The latter distribution underlies the separately described WSAT family. It is also distinct from unrestricted GSAT random noise and from a pure clause-first walk for 2-SAT. 2 Claims
Mechanism #method
Maintain a complete assignment of the variables in the input CNF formula. Stop successfully when it satisfies the formula. Otherwise, with fixed probability p, select uniformly from the distinct variables appearing in at least one currently unsatisfied clause and flip the chosen variable. With probability 1−p, evaluate all single-variable flips and choose a largest increase in the total number of satisfied clauses, breaking tied best choices randomly as in GSAT. The random and greedy branches can make non-improving moves. The extension can use the GSAT outer random-initialization, per-attempt flip budget and restart/attempt budget. The 1994 paper explicitly explains the duplicate-free variable list, which gives equal probabilities to eligible variables even when their clause occurrence counts differ.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The focused random branch and the global greedy branch use different candidate sets. Replacing the former by random-clause-then-random-variable sampling changes the transition distribution, even at p=1. Shared behavior at p=0 does not make the full variant identical to basic GSAT.
  • Finite search failure is not a proof of unsatisfiability. The polynomial analysis cited for pure random walks on satisfiable 2-CNF does not establish a corresponding general-CNF bound for this mixture.
  • The noise parameter, CNF encoding, initialization and outer budgets affect behavior. No single best p, universal speedup or numerical benchmark claim is curated. Empty clauses or empty candidate sets require explicit handling outside the described nonempty-variable flip rule.
  • The record curates the move policy and its stated GSAT context, not an executed solver or a certification of every loop boundary. The original GSAT pre-flip-only pseudocode boundary remains an implementation choice to declare.
