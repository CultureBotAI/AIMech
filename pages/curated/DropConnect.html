DropConnect

Layers & Modules AIMech:DropConnect DRAFT
A stochastic neural-network layer that masks individual weights and biases during training, so each example is processed through a randomly selected subset of the layer’s connections.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the original fully connected DropConnect layer and its training/inference conventions. Dynamic connection masking differs from dropping activation units, removing whole residual branches, masking spatial blocks and permanently pruning a weight matrix. The surrounding feature extractor and classifier are not part of the layer identity. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For each training example, draw independent Bernoulli keep masks for the layer’s weights and biases with a chosen retention probability p. Multiply the parameters elementwise by this mask, compute the affine preactivation and apply the nonlinear activation. Backpropagate through the masked connections, using the same mask for the weight gradients and for gradients passed to earlier layers. The original inference procedure approximates each preactivation by a Gaussian matched to the masked weighted sum’s mean and variance: p times the ordinary weighted sum, and p(1−p) times the sum of squared weight-input products, including the constant input used for bias. Draw samples from these Gaussians, apply the activation to each sample and average the resulting activations before passing them to the next layer. The paper’s sample count and p=0.5 experiments are configuration choices.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The original layer uses per-example masks, including biases. Shared minibatch masks, unmasked biases, inverted training rescaling and later structured weight-masking variants are not silently substituted for this source version.
  • Gaussian moment matching is an approximation to the finite Bernoulli sum; averaging sampled nonlinear activations is also approximate. Passing an averaged activation through a nonlinear downstream network is not exact averaging of full network predictions. Applying the activation to the mean generally differs from averaging activations.
  • The equal-mask paragraph uses 1/|M| while also describing 2^|M| masks. Exact averaging requires normalized mask probabilities; the record follows the weighted expectation in Equation4 rather than propagating that inconsistent displayed factor. No full proof audit of the source’s generalization bound is claimed.
  • Dense connection-mask storage and sampling can be costly; dynamic sparsity alone does not guarantee faster execution. Fully connected-layer evidence does not establish arbitrary convolutional/recurrent implementations, universal generalization improvement or a calibrated Bayesian posterior. No benchmark result or author code execution is asserted.
