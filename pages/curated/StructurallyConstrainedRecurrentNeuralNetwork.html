Structurally constrained recurrent neural network

Architectures AIMech:StructurallyConstrainedRecurrentNeuralNetwork DRAFT
Aliases: SCRN, SCRNN
A recurrent sequence architecture coupling a nonlinear recurrent hidden state with a separate linear context state whose recurrence is constrained to a scalar or diagonal decay, with context-to-hidden connections and no hidden-to-context feedback.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #linear-context-and-fast-state
For the fixed-decay paper formulation, s_t=(1-alpha)*B*x_t+alpha*s_(t-1), with 0<alpha<1; its nonlinear state is h_t=sigma(P*s_t+A*x_t+R*h_(t-1)), and output probabilities are obtained from softmax(U*h_t+V*s_t). The context update has no activation nonlinearity and no feedback from h. The fast-state recurrent matrix R is learned without imposing the same diagonal decay constraint. These are the paper equations; the pinned implementation uses a lagged context input to h.
  • supports Source: Learning Longer Memory in Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 2.2, Equations 4–6, p. 4
  • supports Source: Author SCRNNs implementation: mfactory.lua · Locator: mfactory.lua lines 150–221, especially 191–199
Scope #persistent-structure-and-adaptive-variant
The zero hidden-to-context block and diagonal context recurrence are structural restrictions, rather than a dense matrix initialized near identity and then freely trained. The paper also proposes Q=diag(sigmoid(beta_j)) in place of alpha*I, with s_t=(I-Q)*B*x_t+Q*s_(t-1). In ideal real arithmetic finite beta gives per-unit decays in (0,1); these are learned parameters shared over time, not input-dependent LSTM-style gates. The fixed and adaptive context forms remain configurations of this architecture.
  • supports Source: Learning Longer Memory in Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 2.2, alternative interpretation and adaptive context, Equations 7–8, pp. 4–5
Mechanism #trace-memory-and-boundaries
For fixed scalar alpha, the linear context expands to alpha^t*s_0+(1-alpha)*sum from j=1 to t of alpha^(t-j)*B*x_j. Its direct carry derivative over k steps is alpha^k*I, which tends to zero for 0<alpha<1; slower forgetting does not mean permanent gradient preservation. This is a recency-weighted summary and is not generally invariant to permuting tokens. With uniformly bounded embedded inputs the scalar-decay context stays bounded by the larger of the initial-state and input norm bounds, but this does not bound the fast-state Jacobian or establish stability of the whole trained system.
  • supports Source: Learning Longer Memory in Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 2.2, Equation 4 and exponential trace discussion, p. 4; finite memory limitation in conclusion, pp. 7–8; curator expansion
Scope #current-versus-lagged-context
Equation 5 feeds current s_t to the fast state, but mfactory.lua feeds prev_hid_slow even though its nearby comment describes current c_t. A simultaneous transition using the paper Equation 7 block [R,P;0,alpha*I] also feeds the previous context. Substituting Equation 4 into Equation 5 instead gives the effective recurrent coupling alpha*P and effective input matrix A+(1-alpha)*P*B. These formulations can be reparameterized for nonzero alpha, but are not identical with unchanged raw parameters, initialization or optimization. The record preserves the paper and pinned-code conventions separately.
  • supports Source: Learning Longer Memory in Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Equations 4–7 and alternative interpretation, p. 4
  • supports Source: Author SCRNNs implementation: mfactory.lua · Locator: mfactory.lua comment lines 164–166 versus graph lines 191–199; curator substitution
Mechanism #pinned-fixed-context-implementation
In the inspected fresh code setup, util.initSCRNN initializes the slow recurrent matrix to (1-semb_scale)*I with zero off-diagonal entries. The builder scales the slow input embedding by semb_scale, and SCRNN.zeroSomeGradients zeroes the slow recurrent weight gradient before the parameter update. The shown SGD or momentum update, starting with the zero momentum buffer created by SCRNN, therefore preserves this slow matrix under that path. This static reading does not certify arbitrary loaded optimizer state, external mutation, library behavior or an implementation of the paper adaptive-Q variant.

  • supports Source: Author SCRNNs implementation: util.lua · Locator: util.lua orthInit and initSCRNN
  • supports Source: Author SCRNNs implementation: scrnn.lua · Locator: scrnn.lua lines 120–134 and 320–338

Limitation #evaluation-and-finite-memory
The paper evaluates language modeling on Penn Treebank and Text8 under selected hidden/context sizes, output parameterizations, backpropagation horizons and learning-rate choices. Text8 performance uses the reported development split, and context-weight learning helps some capacity settings more than others. The authors explicitly acknowledge limits of finite-capacity recurrent memory. These comparisons do not establish universal superiority to LSTM, arbitrary-length exact storage, or equivalence between the paper and historical code training runs. No numerical benchmark or independent reproduction is asserted.
  • supports Source: Learning Longer Memory in Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 3 and Tables 1–4, pp. 5–7; conclusion, pp. 7–8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Paper current-context equations and pinned previous-context code are kept distinct. Their parameter transformation does not imply identical initialization, optimization or empirical results.
  • The slow-context structural constraint does not constrain the learned fast recurrent matrix in the same way. A decaying trace is not an unlimited exact memory or unconditional gradient guarantee.
  • The paper prints a sigmoid expression with a positive exponential sign while calling it sigmoid; the author graph uses nn.Sigmoid. This record names the operation without importing a theorem from that printed scalar formula.
  • The adaptive per-unit decay is a paper variant; the inspected fixed-context code path is not presented as its implementation. Static preservation reasoning assumes the stated fresh initialization and update path.
  • All nine paper pages and six selected author files were read completely; only lines 230–285 of rnn.lua were read. No full repository/dependency audit, Torch execution, training or benchmark replication was performed.
  • The source evaluations use specific capacities, splits and training choices. No current best performance, unconditional LSTM advantage or independent scientific approval is asserted.
