Hill climbing

Algorithms AIMech:HillClimbing DRAFT
Aliases: Hill-climbing search, Greedy local search
A family of local-improvement search procedures that replaces a current solution with a strictly better neighbor and stops when no improving neighbor exists under the specified move set.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The record covers the strict-improvement hill-climbing family: Glover selects some improving move, while the inspected AIMA figure selects the best-valued successor. Steepest ascent/descent is a particular selection rule, not an exact alias for every family member. Hill climbing does not denote every local-search metaheuristic, continuous gradient descent, greedy global best-first search, or the full ParamILS framework. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Choose an initial solution, an objective and applicable neighborhood moves. In the minimizing formulation, select a move s with c(s(x))<c(x), replace x by s(x), and repeat. If no such move exists in the specified neighborhood, stop at the current solution. The source permits selecting any improving move. The inspected AIMA maximizing variant instead selects a highest-valued successor and stops if its value is no greater than the current value. Thus best-improvement and other improving-move choices share the strict-improvement principle but need not follow the same trajectory. A complete neighborhood check is needed to certify the stated local stopping condition; failure to find an improvement in a sample is not that certificate.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A local optimum need not be globally optimal. Strict improvement cannot traverse equal-valued plateaus or take an initially worsening escape step; sideways moves, random restarts and worsening acceptance change the specified baseline.
  • Curator deduction: on a finite state space with a fixed objective and strict improvements, accepted states cannot repeat. This does not supply a polynomial bound, finite termination in arbitrary continuous spaces, or global optimality.
  • Move definition, neighborhood coverage, initialization and selection/tie rules affect results. AIMA Figure 4.2 does not explicitly handle an empty successor set; a concrete implementation must declare that boundary. No numerical performance claim is curated.
