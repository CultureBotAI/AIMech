Flattened slice sampling

Algorithms AIMech:FlattenedSliceSampling DRAFT
A finite categorical conditional-update algorithm that splits excess probability from a maximum-probability state into additional bars and samples a directed move to the preceding bar intersecting a random height, while retaining the given state order.

Mechanism #ordered-bar-flattening
Use a fixed ordered list of finite states with nonnegative conditional probabilities pi summing to one. In the regular case max(pi)<1/2 and pi(k)>0, choose the first maximum a in that order. Let h=pi(a), let s be the second largest probability, and let b be the cyclic predecessor of a. Shorten the original a bar to height s and append an additional a-labelled bar after each original bar j other than a and b, with height f*pi(j), where f=(h-s)/(1-h-pi(b)). The denominator is positive and 0<=f<1 in this case; tied maxima give f=0. FSS retains the original ordering. For the current symbol, choose one of its bars in proportion to its height, draw a uniform height within that bar, and move left with wraparound to the next bar intersecting the height. The destination bar label is the new state.

Mechanism #invariant-conditional-and-joint-target
The total height of all bars labelled a remains s+f*(1-h-pi(b))=h; other symbols retain their probability mass. At each fixed height, moving to the preceding active bar cyclically permutes the active bars and preserves their uniform distribution. Integrating over height therefore preserves pi after projecting back to symbols. Using such a kernel for a valid full conditional while leaving other coordinates unchanged preserves the joint target. Fixed mixtures and fixed sequential compositions of these updates also preserve that target. This is an invariance statement, not a guarantee of independent draws, reversibility or convergence from every initial state.

Scope #dominant-mass-and-zero-state-conventions
Algorithm 8 handles pi(k)=0 first by assigning the outgoing row pi. This is a convention for a zero-probability value of an otherwise defined conditional; it does not define a conditional law for a conditioning context with zero total mass. If h=max(pi)>=1/2 and k has positive mass, use the common kernel that moves every nonmaximum state to a and assigns P(a,a)=(2h-1)/h and P(a,j)=pi(j)/h for j!=a. At h=1/2 the maximum has zero self probability; at h=1 it is absorbing. This kernel is reversible and has overall self probability 2h-1. Its uniqueness is restricted to positive-support rows. Zero-probability labels remain part of the given ordering: for ordered pi=(0,3/7,2/7,2/7), FSS has self probability 1/6 at the 3/7 state, whereas deleting the first zero label changes that probability to zero. Both kernels preserve their respective target law.

Scope #finite-kernel-comparisons
Curator exact rational calculations compare Algorithm 8 flow accumulation, explicit geometric bar integration and a separately written interpretation of Algorithm 9. Across 4,450 ordered finite target vectors, the 8,900 paired FSS and ZFSS kernels agree between these constructions on 48,580 rows and preserve their targets. The cases include ties, zeros, half-mass boundaries and point masses. FSS and ZFSS differ for 297 targets in this suite, where FSS retains avoidable self mass. For Figure 10 target (1/10,1/5,1/5,1/20,9/20), the FSS self probability at the last state is 1/9 and its stationary overall self probability is 1/20. The printed FSS matrix agrees with the calculations. These bounded checks do not prove general numerical stability or a runtime advantage.

Limitation #periodicity-and-composed-scan-limits
FSS does not always minimize self transitions and is generally nonreversible. Removing self transitions does not establish ergodicity. For m equally probable states, FSS is a deterministic predecessor cycle with period m. For two independent uniform three-state coordinates, a full fixed scan maps (a,b) to (a-1 mod 3,b-1 mod 3), giving three closed classes indexed by a-b mod 3. The uniform joint target remains invariant, but a chain started in one class does not explore the other classes. No universal asymptotic-variance, finite-run accuracy or wall-clock improvement follows from a lower self-transition probability.

Limitation #printed-sampler-and-example-caveats
Algorithm 8 defines the maintained transition probabilities. The exact comparison with Algorithm 9 interprets its zero-current branch as one inverse-CDF traversal; the printed outer loop line there reuses an index that is immediately reset and is not claimed as a verified literal implementation. The printed closed-uniform endpoint guarantee also needs qualification: with pi=(1/4,1/4,1/2), current state 3 and r=1, its dominant-mass branch returns state 3 although P(3,3)=0. This endpoint has zero probability under an ideal continuous uniform law. Figure 10 also contains a nonstationary printed ZFSS comparison matrix; that matrix is retained as a source caveat, not evidence against the verified FSS matrix or a claimed author-issued correction.
