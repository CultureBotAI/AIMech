Competitive learning

Learning Paradigms AIMech:CompetitiveLearning DRAFT
An unsupervised learning paradigm in which units or modules compete to represent presented input patterns and adapt their representations according to that competition. Winner-only adaptation and posterior-weighted soft adaptation are distinct realizations; the paradigm does not prescribe one universal update law or network architecture.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This source-scoped learning paradigm keeps normalized hard-winner adaptation and Gaussian posterior-weighted soft adaptation distinct. It is not one universal architecture, k-means, a self-organizing map, PBIL search, or exact-cache incremental EM. Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #competition-and-allocation
The inspected hard realization organizes units into inhibitory clusters: each cluster selects the unit with the largest input, sets that winner active and the other units inactive, and adapts only the winner. Nowlan contrasts a hard assignment with a soft realization in which normalized component responses share responsibility for a presented input. Hard exclusivity and soft sharing are alternative ways to allocate adaptation, not requirements that every competitive learner satisfy simultaneously.
  • supports Source: Feature Discovery by Competitive Learning · Locator: “Competitive Learning,” PDF pp. 15–17 / printed pp. 162–164; Figure 2.
  • supports Source: Maximum Likelihood Competitive Learning · Locator: Section 2, PDF pp. 4–5 / printed pp. 577–578, Equations 3–6 and the following response-normalization paragraph.
Mechanism #normalized-binary-winner-update
For the reprint’s binary input x with n=sum_i x_i>0, each winning unit redistributes a fraction g of its unit-total input weight equally over active input lines. In vector form its update is w_new=(1-g)w+g*x/n; losing units keep their weights. With nonnegative w summing to one and 0<=g<=1, this update preserves nonnegativity and unit total. These conditions describe the selected normalized-weight realization, not all hard competitive learning.
  • supports Source: Feature Discovery by Competitive Learning · Locator: PDF p. 17 / printed p. 164, binary inputs, weight constraint, displayed learning rule and footnote 2; algebraic restatement of that rule.
Scope #categorical-features-and-layering
A winner-take-all cluster with M units supplies an M-valued categorical response to an input. The reprint’s construction can combine responses from multiple clusters and use a layer’s active-unit pattern as the input to a higher competitive layer. This makes learned competitive features available for hierarchical classification, without requiring a deep architecture or guaranteeing uniquely meaningful features, balanced use of units or successful separation of every input distribution.
  • supports Source: Feature Discovery by Competitive Learning · Locator: Figure 2 and caption, PDF p. 16 / printed p. 163; feature interpretation, PDF pp. 19–20 / printed pp. 166–167; Figure 18 and conclusion, PDF pp. 42–43 / printed pp. 189–190.
Mechanism #gaussian-soft-responsibilities
In Nowlan’s equal-prior, common-variance spherical Gaussian example, each unit represents a center mu_j and computes a component response phi_j(x). Soft competition uses r_j(x)=phi_j(x)/sum_i phi_i(x), so all positive-response components can contribute to adaptation. The corresponding hard approximation selects the largest component response; under those equal-prior and common-variance assumptions this is a nearest-center assignment. The normalized responsibilities refer to this mixture model, not to a universal soft-competition formula.
  • supports Source: Maximum Likelihood Competitive Learning · Locator: Section 2, PDF pp. 3–5 / printed pp. 576–578, Equations 1–6, the equal-prior/common-variance assumption and the unnumbered posterior formula.
Mechanism #responsibility-weighted-center-fit
For the fixed positive-variance Gaussian mean problem, the soft stationary relation is mu_j=sum_k r_j(x_k)*x_k / sum_k r_j(x_k), when the denominator is positive. The hard counterpart averages only the assigned cases. Nowlan describes iterative adaptations of these relations for radial-basis centers and then fits a separate supervised output layer for classification. Because responsibilities depend on current parameters, the stationary relation alone specifies neither a unique online update schedule nor a solved global maximum-likelihood estimate.
  • supports Source: Maximum Likelihood Competitive Learning · Locator: Section 2, PDF p. 4 / printed p. 577, Equations 5–6; iterative versions and Section 3 training sequence, PDF p. 5 / printed p. 578.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The normalized binary-input rule requires a nonempty input, a declared winner or tie convention, initialized unit-total weights and an appropriate gain. Zero input leaves x/n undefined; gains outside [0,1] need not preserve the simplex, and endpoint gains do not preserve strict positivity in general. Input normalization, gain schedules and competition rules are realization-specific, not fixed for the entire paradigm.
  • The inspected reprint contains a scaling discrepancy in its equilibrium discussion. Its zero-expected-drift equation on PDF p. 21 / printed p. 168 implies, for positive learning gain, constant active count n and positive winning probability, w_i=P(input i active | unit wins)/n. The next page prints n times that conditional probability. The preceding equation and unit-total constraint support division by n; this is a discrepancy in the inspected 1986 reprint, not an asserted author erratum or a claim about uninspected 1985 journal bytes.
  • Unit-total nonnegative weights have fixed L1 norm, not fixed Euclidean norm. The reprint qualifies its geometric analogy, and maximum weighted input is not universally equivalent to nearest Euclidean center under that constraint. Winner-only adaptation can leave units unused; neither equal occupancy, distinct semantic features nor a unique stable partition follows in general. Zero expected weight drift also does not itself prove convergence of continually updated random weights.
  • The soft weighted-mean condition assumes a valid mixture and positive variance and total responsibility. It is a fixed-point or stationary relation because its responsibilities depend on the means. Computing responsibilities under old parameters and maximizing a fixed-responsibility surrogate can give an EM mean step; an arbitrary online gradient step, cumulative average or decayed statistic update is not automatically that procedure. No global maximum, monotone likelihood for arbitrary finite steps or unique online schedule is established here.
  • Nowlan’s Equation 1 prints a prefactor 1/(K*sigma_j) with covariance sigma_j^2 I. A normalized d-dimensional spherical Gaussian requires a factor proportional to sigma_j^(-d); the printed form needs a dimension/variance-dependent interpretation of K outside one dimension. A common fixed factor cancels in the equal-prior, common-variance responsibilities used here, so this caveat does not invalidate that restricted comparison. Maximizing log likelihood calls for ascent, or descent of its negative, despite the source’s “gradient descent” wording; no general literal variance-update implementation is certified.
  • The maximum component term in Nowlan’s Equation 4 is a hard approximation to the mixture sum, not generally an equal normalized density. Unequal priors or covariances change assignments and responsibilities. Hard centroid fitting, marginal-likelihood EM and annealed or stochastic competitive procedures therefore are not unconditional synonyms, and finite experimental comparisons do not establish universal superiority or convergence.
  • This paradigm is not identical to Lloyd k-means, a self-organizing map, PBIL or exact-cache incremental EM. A self-organizing map additionally adapts a topological neighborhood; PBIL updates a search distribution using objective-selected candidates; exact-cache incremental EM has its own fixed-data replacement schedule. In Nowlan’s application a separately trained output layer uses class labels, so an unsupervised center-fitting component does not make the complete classifier unsupervised.
  • Primary evidence uses the inspected 1986 Rumelhart–Zipser chapter reprint and official NIPS 1989 Nowlan proceedings PDF. The reprint identifies its 1985 journal origin, but journal-byte equality was not checked; reading used selected OCR and formula visuals, not the unread middle experiment pages. The official Nowlan year 1989 and historical citation year 1990 are bibliographic conventions, not two algorithms. Yuille’s 1994 note supplies contextual EM/annealing discussion only. The unavailable 1991 Nowlan thesis, author implementations and benchmark reproduction were not inspected in this research; general biological or semantic-feature guarantees are not inferred.
