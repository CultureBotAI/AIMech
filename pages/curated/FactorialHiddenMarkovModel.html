Factorial hidden Markov model

Model Families AIMech:FactorialHiddenMarkovModel DRAFT
Aliases: FHMM, Factorial HMM
A directed latent sequence-model family in which several independently evolving hidden Markov chains jointly determine the observation distribution at each time.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record covers the independent-chain factorial HMM developed by Ghahramani and Jordan, with a shared observation distribution. It is not a mixture selecting one HMM, a globally normalized product of complete HMM likelihoods, an input-conditioned HMM, or the variational algorithm used to fit the model. Each combination of chain states is one joint state; factorial refers to its distributed component representation. The later coupled-chain and input-conditioned extensions in Section 5 are outside this narrow definition. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
With independent initial states and chain-specific transitions, the joint distribution factors into the product of chain priors, the product over times and chains of p(s_t^m|s_(t-1)^m), and one shared p(y_t|s_t^1,...,s_t^M) per time. The paper’s worked emission is Gaussian with mean sum_m W_m s_t^m for one-hot states and common covariance C. Although chains are independent a priori, observations generally couple their posterior paths through the shared emission. For M chains each having K states, the joint latent state has K^M configurations. Exact forward–backward can exploit the transition factorization by contracting one chain dimension at a time, with O(T M K^(M+1)) transition work under the paper’s fixed-observation-dimension setting; this remains exponential in M. EM uses posterior state, temporal-pair and cross-chain moments. In the additive Gaussian model its weight update is a linear-system solution with a pseudoinverse because chain contributions have redundant mean offsets. Exact inference, Gibbs sampling and variational approximations are separate procedures, not defining alternatives for model identity.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Independent latent dynamics do not imply posterior independence after shared observations. Special emission structures may decouple chains; for the Gaussian case a sufficient condition is cross-chain orthogonality in the C^-1 metric. Note 3’s ordinary orthogonality with a merely diagonal covariance is not sufficient in general.
  • The Gaussian additive mean is a worked parameterization, not the only possible factorial-HMM emission. Its closed-form parameter updates do not automatically transfer to nonlinear or discrete emissions.
  • Factorized transitions reduce exact computation relative to a naive unconstrained joint-state HMM but do not remove exponential state growth. A compact parameterization is not a universal scalability or accuracy guarantee.
  • Approximate E-steps may improve a variational bound without monotonically improving the true likelihood. Additive offsets are not individually identifiable without conventions; the paper uses a pseudoinverse.
  • Section 5’s coupled-transition and input-conditioned extension displays contain indexing/factorization ambiguities. They are not adopted as the defining joint distribution here. No experimental performance, author implementation or general convergence guarantee is certified.
