PhaseLift Convex Semidefinite Programming Matrix-Lifting for Phase Retrieval

Algorithms AIMech:PhaseLiftConvexSemidefiniteProgrammingPhaseRetrieval DRAFT
Aliases: PhaseLift, Convex Semidefinite Relaxation for Phase Retrieval, Candes-Strohmer-Voroninski Trace-Norm Matrix Lifting for Quadratic Equations
A convex semidefinite programming (SDP) relaxation and dual-certificate recovery methodology (introduced by Emmanuel J. Candès, Thomas Strohmer, and Vladislav Voroninski in 2013) for reconstructing a real or complex signal $\boldsymbol{x}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^n$ or $\mathbb{C}^n$ (up to a global phase $e^{i\phi}$) from quadratic intensity measurements $b_k = |\langle \boldsymbol{a}_k, \boldsymbol{x}_0\rangle|^2 + w_k$ ($k=1,\dots,m$). By lifting the rank-1 outer product $\boldsymbol{X}_0 = \boldsymbol{x}_0\boldsymbol{x}_0^*$ into the positive semidefinite cone $\boldsymbol{X} \succeq \boldsymbol{0}$, the quadratic measurements become linear trace functionals $\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{X})_k = \mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{a}_k\boldsymbol{a}_k^* \boldsymbol{X})$, and PhaseLift replaces combinatorial rank minimization with the trace-norm SDP $\min_{\boldsymbol{X}\succeq\boldsymbol{0}} \mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{X})$ subject to $\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{X}) = \boldsymbol{b}$ in the noiseless case (Equation 1.8) or $\|\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{X}) - \boldsymbol{b}\|_1 \le \varepsilon$ in the noisy case (Equation 1.9). Using concentration of rank-2 $\ell_1$-isometry functionals $\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^m |\langle \boldsymbol{a}_k\boldsymbol{a}_k^*, \boldsymbol{H}\rangle|$ on the tangent space $T = \{\boldsymbol{x}_0\boldsymbol{y}^* + \boldsymbol{y}\boldsymbol{x}_0^*\}$ and its orthogonal complement $T^\perp$ (Lemmas 3.1–3.3) together with an inexact dual certificate $\boldsymbol{Y} = \mathcal{A}^*(\boldsymbol{\lambda})$ obeying $\|\boldsymbol{Y}_T\|_F \le 1/5$ and $\boldsymbol{Y}_{T^\perp} \preceq -c\boldsymbol{I}_{T^\perp}$ constructed from fourth-moment expectations $\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{X}) = \mathbb{E}[(\boldsymbol{z}^*\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{z})\boldsymbol{z}\boldsymbol{z}^*] = \boldsymbol{X} + \mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{X})\boldsymbol{I}$ (complex) or $2\boldsymbol{X} + \mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{X})\boldsymbol{I}$ (real) (Section 3.2), Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 prove exact recovery $\widehat{\boldsymbol{X}} = \boldsymbol{x}_0\boldsymbol{x}_0^*$ and stable noisy recovery $\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{x}} - e^{i\phi}\boldsymbol{x}_0\|_2 \le C_0\min(\|\boldsymbol{x}_0\|_2, \frac{\varepsilon}{m\|\boldsymbol{x}_0\|_2})$ from $m \ge c_0 n\log n$ Gaussian or unitary-frame measurements without any initialization.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Resolved against all 442 pinned AIO concepts and 690 maintained AIMech concepts. Distinct from `AIMech:WirtingerFlowPhaseRetrievalAndSpectralInitialization` (Candès, Li, & Soltanolkotabi 2015) and `AIMech:TruncatedWirtingerFlowAndTruncatedSpectralInitializationForQuadraticSystems` (Chen & Candès 2017), which operate directly in the nonconvex vector space $\mathbb{C}^n$ via spectral initialization and Wirtinger gradient descent rather than convex semidefinite matrix lifting $\boldsymbol{X} = \boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{x}^* \succeq \boldsymbol{0}$.

3 Claims
Mechanism #claim-rank-two-l1-isometry-concentration-for-symmetric-hermitian-matrices
For any unit-Frobenius-norm Hermitian rank-2 matrix $\boldsymbol{X} = \lambda_1\boldsymbol{u}_1\boldsymbol{u}_1^* + \lambda_2\boldsymbol{u}_2\boldsymbol{u}_2^*$ ($\lambda_1^2 + \lambda_2^2 = 1$), Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2 prove that under uniform spherical or Gaussian sensing vectors $\boldsymbol{a}_k$, the expected $\ell_1$ measurement energy $\mathbb{E}|\langle\boldsymbol{a}_k\boldsymbol{a}_k^*, \boldsymbol{X}\rangle|$ satisfies the exact closed-form bounds $\min_{t\in[0,1]} f_{\mathbb{R}}(t) \approx 0.941 \le n\mathbb{E}|\langle\boldsymbol{a}_k\boldsymbol{a}_k^*, \boldsymbol{X}\rangle| \le \sqrt{2}$ in the real case and $2(\sqrt{2}-1) \approx 0.8284 \le n\mathbb{E}|\langle\boldsymbol{a}_k\boldsymbol{a}_k^*, \boldsymbol{X}\rangle| \le 1$ in the complex case, yielding uniform $\ell_1$-isometry over all rank-at-most-2 perturbations $\boldsymbol{H}$ once $m \ge c_0 n$ (Section 3.1, Lemmas 3.1–3.3).
  • supports Source: PhaseLift: Exact and Stable Signal Recovery from Magnitude Measurements via Convex Programming · Locator: Section 3.1 (Lemmas 3.1, 3.2, and 3.3, pp. 14-18)
Mechanism #claim-inexact-dual-certificate-from-fourth-moment-operator-inversion
Using the fourth-moment operator $\mathcal{S}(\boldsymbol{X}) = \mathbb{E}[(\boldsymbol{z}^*\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{z})\boldsymbol{z}\boldsymbol{z}^*]$, which evaluates to $\boldsymbol{X} + \mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{X})\boldsymbol{I}$ with inverse $\mathcal{S}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{X}) = \boldsymbol{X} - \frac{1}{n+1}\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{X})\boldsymbol{I}$ in the complex case and $2\boldsymbol{X} + \mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{X})\boldsymbol{I}$ with inverse $\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{X} - \frac{1}{n+2}\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{X})\boldsymbol{I})$ in the real case, Section 3.2 constructs the truncated dual certificate $\boldsymbol{Y} = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^m [n(n+1)|z_{k,1}|^2\mathbb{I}_{\{|z_{k,1}|\le \sqrt{2\beta\log n}\}} - (n+1)]\boldsymbol{z}_k\boldsymbol{z}_k^*$ satisfying $\|\boldsymbol{Y}_T\|_F \le 1/5$ and $\boldsymbol{Y}_{T^\perp} \preceq -\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{I}_{T^\perp}$ with probability at least $1 - O(n^{-2})$ for $m \ge c_0 n\log n$ (Section 3.2, Lemmas 3.4–3.6).
  • supports Source: PhaseLift: Exact and Stable Signal Recovery from Magnitude Measurements via Convex Programming · Locator: Section 3.2 (Eqs. 3.11-3.17, Lemmas 3.4, 3.5, and 3.6, pp. 18-23)
Application #claim-exact-and-noise-stable-phaselift-recovery-via-trace-minimization
Combining the rank-2 $\ell_1$ covariance lower bound on $T$ with the dual certificate negativity on $T^\perp$, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 prove that for any real or complex signal $\boldsymbol{x}_0$ and noisy quadratic observations $b_k = |\langle\boldsymbol{a}_k,\boldsymbol{x}_0\rangle|^2 + w_k$ with $\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_1 \le \varepsilon$, the solution $\widehat{\boldsymbol{X}}$ of the convex SDP $\min_{\boldsymbol{X}\succeq\boldsymbol{0}}\mathrm{Tr}(\boldsymbol{X})$ subject to $\|\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{X}) - \boldsymbol{b}\|_1 \le \varepsilon$ satisfies $\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{X}} - \boldsymbol{x}_0\boldsymbol{x}_0^*\|_F \le C_0 \varepsilon / m$, and its leading eigenvector $\widehat{\boldsymbol{x}}$ scaled by $\sqrt{\lambda_1(\widehat{\boldsymbol{X}})}$ obeys $\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{x}} - e^{i\phi}\boldsymbol{x}_0\|_2 \le C_0\min(\|\boldsymbol{x}_0\|_2, \frac{\varepsilon}{m\|\boldsymbol{x}_0\|_2})$ (Section 1.3, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2; Section 3.3, Proof of Theorem 1.2).
  • supports Source: PhaseLift: Exact and Stable Signal Recovery from Magnitude Measurements via Convex Programming · Locator: Section 1.3 (Theorems 1.1-1.2, pp. 5-7), Section 3.3 (pp. 23-25)

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Lifting an n-dimensional vector x_0 to an n x n positive semidefinite matrix X = x_0 x_0^* squares the ambient variable dimension from O(n) to O(n^2), incurring O(n^3) to O(n^4.5) interior-point / first-order SDP computational cost.
  • In the original fourth-moment dual-certificate analysis of Candes, Strohmer, & Voroninski (2013), truncation of the fourth-order Gaussian tails at sqrt(2 beta log n) introduces a logarithmic factor m >= c_0 n log n (subsequently removed to O(n) by Candes & Li 2014 via bowling-scheme certificates).
