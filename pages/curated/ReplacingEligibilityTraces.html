Replacing eligibility traces

Mathematical Methods AIMech:ReplacingEligibilityTraces DRAFT
An eligibility-trace update that replaces a visited state’s eligibility by one while geometrically attenuating other state traces, preserving recent credit eligibility without adding repeated-visit multiplicity.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This source-scoped credit-memory component replaces visited state/selected feature traces rather than accumulating repeated-visit counts. It is distinct from a complete TD/Sarsa controller, the original Watkins hard-cut forward/delayed construction, neural MCQ-L and Peng–Williams two-error control. Existing owner264 retains general/true-online and other-variant residuals. Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #state-replacing-recurrence
For a visited state s_t, replace its eligibility by e_(t+1)(s_t)=1. For every other state s, set e_(t+1)(s)=gamma*lambda*e_t(s), with gamma and lambda in [0,1]. This differs from the accumulating rule e_(t+1)(s)=gamma*lambda*e_t(s)+1{s=s_t}: another visit replaces the current state trace rather than adding to its previous value.
  • supports Source: Reinforcement learning with replacing eligibility traces · Locator: Section 1, author PDF pp. 2–3, displayed accumulating and replacing trace recurrences and parameter domains.
Mechanism #trace-as-td-credit-component
In the paper’s undiscounted absorbing-state prediction setting, the replacing trace enters the TD update as Delta V_t(s)=alpha_t(s)*[r_(t+1)+V_t(s_(t+1))-V_t(s_t)]*e_(t+1)(s), with terminal value zero. The trace determines which earlier states remain eligible for this learning signal; it is a component of the prediction update rather than a complete policy or control algorithm.
  • supports Source: Reinforcement learning with replacing eligibility traces · Locator: Section 2.1, author PDF pp. 3–4, absorbing Markov prediction setting, Equation 1 and online/offline/batch distinctions.
Scope #bounded-recency-with-endpoints
For traces initialized in [0,1] and q=gamma*lambda in [0,1], the replacing recurrence preserves each trace in [0,1]. After k consecutive inactive updates, a trace equals q^k times its earlier value. Thus inactive traces vanish after one update when q=0 and do not decay when q=1. These are direct consequences of reset-to-one and multiplication by q; repeated visits do not build the accumulating rule’s visit-multiplicity sum.
  • supports Source: Reinforcement learning with replacing eligibility traces · Locator: Section 1, author PDF pp. 2–3, replacing recurrence; boundedness and endpoint statements are elementary consequences of that displayed rule under the stated initialization.
Scope #qualified-first-visit-correspondence
In the undiscounted absorbing prediction setting with gamma=lambda=1, repeated-batch replacing TD converges to the first-visit Monte Carlo sample estimate for sampled states with N(s)>0 under the paper’s fixed step size 0<alpha<1/N, where N is the number of training trials. Separately, offline replacing TD(1), applied after each completed trial with alpha_t(s) equal to the reciprocal number of first visits to s up through t, gives the first-visit sample-average update. These are the paper’s specified batch-limit and offline correspondences, not arbitrary finite-iteration online equalities.
  • supports Source: Reinforcement learning with replacing eligibility traces · Locator: Sections 2.1 and 2.4–2.5, author PDF pp. 3–4 and 7–8, Theorem 1 and its limiting calculation, and Theorem 2 with the first-visit-count step-size schedule.
Mechanism #binary-feature-control-realization
The reported CMAC control realization maintains traces for action-specific binary features. After decaying old traces, it sets active features for the chosen action to one and clears the corresponding active features for other actions; the resulting trace vector weights a later scalar TD update. This chosen control convention is distinct from the alternative discussed in the paper, which replaces a state-action pair’s trace only when that same pair recurs.
  • supports Source: Reinforcement learning with replacing eligibility traces · Locator: Section 5, author PDF pp. 19–23, especially Figure 9 on p. 20, active-feature action traces on pp. 20–22 and the explicitly described alternative on p. 22.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This source-scoped recurrence is not the entire eligibility-trace family, a TD or Sarsa controller, MCQ-L, Peng–Williams Q(lambda), or a true-online trace algorithm. The original accumulating recursion already has a separate maintained TD-family context. General eligibility-family organization and other trace variants remain outside this record’s scope; the source’s earlier historical citations do not establish independently verified first-use priority.
  • The [0,1] trace bound requires the stated initialization and gamma*lambda domain. A bounded trace does not bound arbitrary rewards, TD errors, step sizes or parameter changes, and does not itself prove stable or convergent learning. The endpoint gamma*lambda=1 retains inactive traces rather than forgetting them; no continuing undiscounted-return existence result follows from it.
  • The first-visit correspondences retain the absorbing-process, completed-trial and step-size conditions stated in the claim. The batch result is a convergence limit, not equality after an arbitrary finite number of sweeps, and alpha=0 is excluded. The paper’s MSE comparisons are conditional and non-strict, with equality cases; they are not a universal superiority claim. No complete appendix-proof or external convergence-theorem certification is asserted.
  • The selected chosen-action feature replacement with clearing of other actions is one control realization, not a universal definition of replacing traces. The source explicitly leaves comparisons with state-action-only replacement open. Tabular and binary-feature results are not a general nonlinear-network replacement prescription, and stationary prediction results do not automatically apply while the control policy changes.
  • Figure 9 initializes all traces in step 1, but its end-of-trial branch returns to step 2 without an explicit all-trace reset. The note that the time index continues across trials does not settle that omission. This description preserves the printed recurrence and selected ongoing control steps without silently adding a reset or claiming a defect in unseen author software.
  • Appendix B states that position is clipped within [-1.2,0.5] while also describing termination when the next position exceeds 0.5. A literal test after that clipping cannot terminate by this condition. Its displayed minus-g force together with negative g also requires a sign/convention clarification. These ambiguities limit a literal reading of that experimental environment; they do not invalidate the trace recurrence or establish how the experiments were implemented.
  • The inspected evidence is a 36-page author-hosted prepublication layout associated with the 1996 journal article; local PDF page locators are used instead of inferred journal pagination. OCR was needed for prose, with selected equations and pseudocode checked visually. No author implementation, benchmark or full original-proof reproduction was performed for this evidence assessment; the reported random-walk and CMAC experiments do not establish universal performance or an optimal trace setting.
