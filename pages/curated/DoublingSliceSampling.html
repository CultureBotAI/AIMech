Doubling slice sampling

Algorithms AIMech:DoublingSliceSampling DRAFT
A univariate slice-sampling algorithm that expands a randomly placed initial interval by randomly directed doublings and uses shrinkage with a retrospective acceptability test to preserve the target.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record specifies Neal’s Figures 4–6, including the extra acceptance test for general slices. It differs from stepping out by geometric interval expansion and the need to reject some in-slice candidates. Its binary interval construction is unrelated to NUTS trajectory-tree doubling. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Draw a slice height and randomly place an interval of width w around x0. While at least one endpoint is inside the slice and fewer than p doublings have occurred, choose left or right with probability one half and extend that side by the current interval width, even if the chosen endpoint was already outside the slice. Propose uniformly from the resulting interval and shrink rejected sides relative to x0. An in-slice x1 must also pass Figure 6: repeatedly bisect the original doubled interval toward x1, track whether x0 and x1 have entered different halves, and after such separation reject if both endpoints of a resulting interval lie outside the slice. Continue back to the original width, with the source’s round-off guard. This excludes candidates from which interval construction would have stopped earlier and preserves the required transition symmetry.
  • supports Source: Slice Sampling · Locator: Sections 4.1–4.3, Figures 4–6 and detailed-balance argument, PDF/printed pp. 10–16

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • For a general multimodal density, being inside the slice is insufficient for acceptance. Omitting Figure 6 can invalidate the update.
  • Stopping expansion on one side merely because that side is already outside the slice is not the random doubling procedure used in the proof.
  • The test may be omitted under the source’s single-interval slice/unimodality conditions, not as a blanket optimization.
  • The width and maximum doubling count remain parameters; the interval length cap is 2^p w, and finding an interval is not proof of rapid mixing across disconnected slice components.
  • The doubling interval must remain available for the retrospective test even as the proposal interval shrinks. Numerical boundary handling and round-off remain implementation concerns.
