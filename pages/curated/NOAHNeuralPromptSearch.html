NOAH (Neural Prompt Search)

Algorithms AIMech:NOAHNeuralPromptSearch DRAFT
Aliases: Neural Prompt Search, NOAH One-Shot Neural Prompt Architecture Search, NOAH Prompt Module Search
A one-shot neural architecture search (NAS) parameter-efficient tuning methodology for large vision models that embeds Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT-Deep), Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), and Adapter modules into each Transformer block of a weight-entangled supernet and conducts parameter-constrained evolutionary search over per-module application depths and per-layer token lengths or bottleneck ranks for each downstream dataset.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Introduced by Yuanhan Zhang, Kaiyang Zhou, and Ziwei Liu (2022, arXiv:2206.04673; published in IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence, vol. 47, no. 7, pp. 5268-5280). Distinct from UniPELT (Mao et al., ACL 2022; AIMech Round 693), which combines fixed-dimension Adapter, Prefix-Tuning, and LoRA submodules via continuous sigmoid gates in language models, and distinct from individual hand-crafted prompt modules (VPT, Adapter, LoRA). Both inherited (retraining-free) and retrained NOAH subnet deployment modes are covered within this record.

4 Claims
Mechanism #noah-unified-prompt-supernet-mechanism
NOAH embeds three complementary prompt modules into each of the 12 blocks of a frozen Vision Transformer supernet: VPT-Deep at the block input x = [CLS, P, E] with m learnable tokens P (Eq. 4), LoRA residuals alongside query and key projections Q = x W_q + s x W^down_q W^up_q and K = x W_k + s x W^down_k W^up_k (Eq. 3), and a bottleneck Adapter h' = phi(h W_down) W_up after the normalized MLP output (Eq. 2), training the supernet via uniform subnet sampling over module depths in {3, 6, 9, 12} and embedding dimensions in {1, 5, 10} or {5, 10, 50, 100} with AutoFormer weight entanglement so prefix tokens and low-rank dimensions are maximally shared across subnets.

Mechanism #noah-evolutionary-search-and-synergy-mechanism
After supernet training, NOAH performs evolutionary search (crossover of module trajectories between top-k parent subnets and mutation of active depth in {0, 3, 6, 9, 12} and layer dimensions with probability 0.2) under a parameter budget cap (0.64M parameters on VTAB-1k), discovering complementary architectures where Adapter and LoRA concentrate in deep layers (dimension > 4) while VPT spans all depths, and allowing inherited subnets to be deployed directly without retraining (74.1% vs. 75.5% retrained average on VTAB-1k).
  • supports Source: Neural Prompt Search · Locator: Section 2.2 Evolutionary Search & Figure 2, p. 4; Section 3 Implementation Details, p. 5; Section 3.4 Architecture of Subnet & With vs Without Retraining, Figure 5 & Table 3, pp. 7-8
Application #noah-vtab-fewshot-domain-gen-application
Evaluated on ViT-B/16 across the 19-dataset VTAB-1k benchmark (achieving 75.5% average top-1 accuracy with 0.43M average parameters, outperforming Full tuning 68.9%, VPT 72.0%, Adapter 73.9%, and LoRA 74.5%), 5-dataset few-shot visual recognition (Food101, OxfordFlowers102, StanfordCars, OxfordPets, FGVCAircraft), and domain generalization from 16-shot ImageNet to ImageNetV2 (+6.8%), ImageNet-Sketch (+4.8%), ImageNet-A (+5.0%), and ImageNet-R (+5.2%).
  • supports Source: Neural Prompt Search · Locator: Section 3.1-3.3, Table 1, Figure 3, Figure 4 & Table 2, pp. 5-7
Limitation #noah-supernet-cost-and-extremely-low-shot-limitation
NOAH requires 500 epochs of supernet training plus 5 epochs of evolutionary search (and 100 epochs of subnet retraining for peak performance), increasing development cost over training a single prompt module, and in extreme 1-shot or 2-shot regimes or specialized datasets such as EuroSAT it performs only on par with or slightly behind standalone LoRA.
  • supports Source: Neural Prompt Search · Locator: Section 3 Implementation Details, p. 5; Section 3.1 Observation 2 & Section 3.2 Results, p. 6; Section 5 Discussion, Limitation and Future Work, p. 9

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Training the weight-entangled supernet (500 epochs) and running evolutionary search before optional subnet retraining (100 epochs) increases upfront computational development cost compared to a single fixed prompt module.
  • In extreme low-shot regimes (1 or 2 labeled shots per class), NOAH performs similarly to standalone LoRA and Adapter rather than showing a clear margin, requiring more labeled examples (e.g., 8-16 shots) to realize its full search advantage.
  • On certain Specialized remote-sensing datasets such as EuroSAT, NOAH slightly trails standalone LoRA or VPT.
