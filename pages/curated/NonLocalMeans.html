Non-local means

Algorithms AIMech:NonLocalMeans DRAFT
A classical image-denoising algorithm that estimates each pixel by a normalized average of observed pixel values, assigning exponential similarity weights from Gaussian-weighted distances between their surrounding observed patches. The candidate pixels may span the image or a chosen finite search window.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #nonlocal-pixel-estimate
Given an observed noisy image v, estimate each pixel i as a weighted sum of observed pixel values v(j). Candidate pixels can range over the whole image, so repeated structures away from i can contribute. The method obtains its weights from the observed image rather than requiring a separately trained encoder, value embedding or residual neural block.
  • supports Source: A non-local algorithm for image denoising · Locator: Section 1, PDF p. 1, continuous NL-means formula and explanation; Section 3, PDF p. 3, discrete NL[v](i) formula.
Mechanism #patch-geometry-distance
Compare fixed-size square neighborhoods centered on i and j using their vectors of observed gray values. Their squared difference is weighted spatially by a Gaussian kernel with positive scale a. This patch comparison includes surrounding configuration rather than only the two central intensities; visually different neighborhoods can receive different weights even when their center values agree.
  • supports Source: A non-local algorithm for image denoising · Locator: Section 3 and Figure 1, PDF p. 3, Gaussian-weighted Euclidean patch distance and final paragraph.
Mechanism #normalized-exponential-weights
For h>0, form the unnormalized similarity k(i,j)=exp(−d_a(i,j)^2/h^2), where d_a is the Gaussian-weighted patch distance, and set w(i,j)=k(i,j)/sum_j k(i,j) over the candidate set. The normalizer sums the same kernels used in the numerator. Increasing h reduces the decay with patch distance. This coherent normalization follows the correctly indexed continuous formula and the explicitly required unit-sum weights; the discrete denominator’s printed index discrepancy is retained in the limitations.
  • supports Source: A non-local algorithm for image denoising · Locator: Section 1, PDF p. 1, continuous formula and C(x); Section 3, PDF p. 3, w(i,j), Z(i), stated weight constraints and h paragraph.
Mechanism #adaptive-convex-average
For a finite nonempty candidate set, finite observed values and h>0, the normalized nonnegative weights produce a convex average of candidate pixel values. The result lies between their minimum and maximum and preserves a constant image. These are direct consequences of normalization, not unbiasedness guarantees. The coefficients depend on the observed patches, so the complete image-to-image denoiser is generally nonlinear despite its weighted-sum output.
  • supports Source: A non-local algorithm for image denoising · Locator: Section 3, PDF p. 3, NL[v](i), nonnegative unit-sum weights and image-dependent similarities; curator consequences of these displayed formulas.
Scope #finite-search-and-direct-work
The source restricts the candidate search for practical computation: its displayed experiments use a 21x21 search window and 7x7 similarity patches. It states approximately 49x441xN^2 direct work for an N-by-N image. More generally, direct patch comparison scales with the number of output pixels, candidate pixels per query and patch samples; searching every image pixel is quadratic in image pixel count at fixed patch size. The particular windows and parameters are experimental choices, not the definition of every non-local means implementation.
  • supports Source: A non-local algorithm for image denoising · Locator: Section 5, PDF p. 4, first two paragraphs, search and similarity windows, work expression and filtering parameter; curator direct-comparison scaling.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The discrete Z(i) display in Section 3 repeats v(N_i)−v(N_i), whereas its numerator compares N_i with N_j. Taken literally, that denominator would not normalize the displayed kernels for nonidentical patches. The method here uses the sum of the numerator kernels, consistent with the explicit unit-sum requirement and correctly indexed continuous normalizer on PDF p. 1. This is a documented printed-source discrepancy, not a claimed published author erratum or an audited implementation correction.
  • The stated expected noisy patch-distance offset of 2sigma^2 requires normalized spatial weights, distinct corresponding image pixels and independent zero-mean noise of equal variance. Ordinary overlap between different patches correlates some summands but does not itself invalidate that expectation. The self-comparison i=j has distance zero; padding or boundary rules that repeat indices need separate treatment. Expected distance ordering does not imply the same ordering in a particular noisy image, unbiased exponential weights or unbiased restored pixels.
  • Section 4 considers center-excluded neighboring values as conditional-expectation covariates and assumes a strictly stationary mixing process, with complete hypotheses and proof referred to an unread external source. Those statements do not establish unconditional consistency of a fixed-bandwidth finite-image implementation whose practical patches include the center. Equality of noisy and clean conditional means needs zero conditional mean of center noise given the surrounding observations; the stated signal-independent, spatially independent, zero-mean Gaussian noise model is sufficient. Squared-error optimality of the exact conditional mean additionally requires finite second moments and does not make every finite kernel estimate optimal.
  • Bandwidth, patch size, search set, self-weight convention and image boundaries affect smoothing, bias, noise sensitivity and computation. The exponential formula requires h>0; h=0 is not a defined direct substitution. Finite exact arithmetic gives a positive normalizer with an included self-patch, but numerical underflow and other implementation choices need care. The source’s fixed experimental settings do not specify an automatic noise estimator, a universally optimal parameter choice or a guarantee for all noise distributions.
  • This identity uses the original 2005 pixelwise Gaussian-spatially weighted formulation. The later IPOL presentation includes RGB distances, subtraction and clipping of a noise-distance offset, self-weight replacement prose and patchwise overlapping-estimate aggregation; these are not silently incorporated into the original definition. Shared patch similarity does not make the method identical to BM3D transform-domain collaborative filtering, a denoising autoencoder or a learned non-local neural block.
  • The finite image demonstrations and reported errors do not establish universal reconstruction quality, benchmark superiority or a general runtime advantage. Evidence uses the exact six-page university-mirrored CVPR 2005 paper and its local page locators. Historical inspection included the later IPOL manuscript as context, but did not include author software, the external consistency proof, the full journal expansion or benchmark reproduction. Bibliographic publication year, PDF build time and actual source access remain distinct.
