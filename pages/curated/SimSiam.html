SimSiam

Algorithms AIMech:SimSiam DRAFT
A self-supervised representation-learning procedure that trains a shared encoder and predictor to match the encoder output of another augmented view using symmetric negative cosine similarity with stopped gradients on each target output.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is Chen and He’s SimSiam procedure, not generic Siamese-network architecture or a checkpoint. The two views share encoder weights directly; the target output is detached only for its directional loss. There is no separately averaged target encoder or explicit negative-pair term. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Generate two augmented views v1 and v2 of each image. A shared encoder f, including backbone and projection MLP, produces z1=f(v1) and z2=f(v2); a shared prediction MLP h produces p1=h(z1) and p2=h(z2). For nonzero vectors let D(p,z)=−(p/||p||)·(z/||z||). Average [D(p1,stopgrad(z2))+D(p2,stopgrad(z1))]/2 over images and update f and h by gradients. Each view’s encoder output is constant on its target side but receives gradients through its prediction side in the opposite term. The original procedure uses no momentum-averaged encoder, negative dictionary or clustering assignment step.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Stop-gradient changes the optimization dynamics but does not remove constant representations from the numerical loss’s minimizers. Section 5.3 explicitly treats noncollapse as empirical and says the alternating-optimization hypothesis does not explain its prevention.
  • The predictor is trained; removing it or fixing random predictor weights is not the defined procedure. A symmetric target stop does not mean a branch never receives gradients.
  • Batch normalization, augmentation, architecture, optimizer and scheduling affect the reported results. Absence of explicit negative pairs does not imply independent per-example learning when batch statistics are used.
  • The record covers the original symmetric cosine formulation. Asymmetric, cross-entropy and multistep-alternation experiments are not silently made equivalent. Nonzero-norm handling is required; no universal performance or convergence guarantee is asserted.
