Metropolis-adjusted Langevin algorithm

Algorithms AIMech:MetropolisAdjustedLangevinAlgorithm DRAFT
Aliases: MALA
A Metropolis–Hastings sampler that proposes a Gaussian step with drift proportional to the target log-density gradient and corrects the asymmetric proposal with an accept/reject test.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record covers the fixed-step, identity-diffusion formulation of Roberts and Tweedie. It differs from ULA by its Metropolis correction, from random-walk Metropolis by its gradient-directed mean, and from multi-step Hamiltonian trajectories. Preconditioned and manifold variants are not silently included in the displayed formula. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method
For a positive differentiable target pi on Euclidean space, choose h>0 and propose y from q(y|x)=Normal(x+(h/2)grad log pi(x),hI). Accept with min(1,pi(y)q(x|y)/(pi(x)q(y|x))) and otherwise keep x. Evaluate the reverse Gaussian using its mean at y; the state-dependent gradient generally makes q asymmetric, so its ratio cannot be dropped. This correction makes the transition reversible with respect to the intended target under the stated support conditions, despite the discretized proposal.
  • supports Source: Exponential convergence of Langevin distributions and their discrete approximations · Locator: Section 1.4.2, proposal and Equations (9)–(11), printed pp. 345–346 / PDF pp. 6–7; Section 4 introductory discussion, printed p. 355 / PDF p. 16
Limitation #mala-convergence-boundaries
The paper separates conditions for geometric convergence from counterexamples and non-geometric cases. Its rejection and tail analysis shows that correcting the invariant target does not universally supply fast convergence.
  • supports Source: Exponential convergence of Langevin distributions and their discrete approximations · Locator: Sections 4.1–4.3, printed pp. 355–359 / PDF pp. 16–20

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The full target gradient and both proposal densities must be correctly evaluated. Unknown common target normalization cancels, but the reverse proposal factor does not.
  • Target invariance and correction of proposal discretization do not certify rapid mixing, independent draws or accuracy of a finite run.
  • The source gives tail-dependent failures of geometric ergodicity; adding a Metropolis step is not a universal guarantee of exponential convergence.
  • Large steps, problematic geometry and numerical gradient or density errors can make practical sampling ineffective. This record asserts no universal step size or acceptance target.
  • Truncated drift, stochastic gradients, variable metrics and constrained-state extensions need separate definitions and validation.
