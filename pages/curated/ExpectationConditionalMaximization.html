Aliases: ECM Algorithm, ECM
An extension of expectation maximization that replaces a joint maximization of the expected complete-data log likelihood with a sequence of constrained maximizations, holding the E-step expectation fixed throughout that sequence.
Definition Evidence
  • supports Source: Maximum likelihood estimation via the ECM algorithm: A general framework · Locator: Section 3, Equations 3.1-3.3 and Definition 1, printed p.272 / PDF p.6
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The Meng-Rubin ECM framework allows general constraint functions; coordinate-block maximization is a special case. It is distinct from posterior Gibbs sampling, ECME steps on actual observed likelihood, and parameter-expanded EM. Ordinary EM is recovered by one unconstrained maximization. Multicycle ECM refreshes the expectation within an outer iteration and is identified here as a related schedule variant, not the fixed-Q definition. Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #fixed-expectation-conditional-maxima
At theta_t compute Q(theta|theta_t), the conditional expectation of complete-data log likelihood given the observed data under theta_t. Starting at theta_t, CM step s maximizes that same Q subject to g_s(theta)=g_s(theta_previous_substep). The constraint preserves feasibility of the previous substep. Use the final CM result as theta_(t+1), then compute the next E step. The constraints can fix a parameter block, its complement, or a more general function; the CM subproblems need not have closed-form solutions.
  • supports Source: Maximum likelihood estimation via the ECM algorithm: A general framework · Locator: Sections 2-3, pp.269-273 / PDF pp.3-7, especially Equations 3.1-3.3
Mechanism #ascent-and-space-filling
Exact CM steps make the fixed Q nondecreasing, so the full ECM iteration is a generalized EM step and makes observed likelihood nondecreasing. This compares the final iterate with the E-step anchor; it does not require observed likelihood to increase between every pair of intermediate CM points. Stationary-limit results additionally require the stated regularity and parameter-space coverage conditions. In the differentiable full-rank formulation, space filling requires that the available constraint-tangent directions collectively span the parameter directions.
  • supports Source: Maximum likelihood estimation via the ECM algorithm: A general framework · Locator: Sections 3-4, Equations 3.4-3.7, Theorems 1-3 and Corollary 1, pp.272-275 / PDF pp.6-9; curator Gaussian counterexample to intermediate observed-likelihood monotonicity
Scope #moments-and-refresh-schedule
Incomplete normal-data examples use old conditional first and second moments throughout the CM sequence, while regression coefficients and covariance parameters change in their specified order. Missing values replaced only by conditional means discard required uncertainty terms. Multicycle ECM refreshes expectations before selected CM steps; its outer update need not increase one single original Q, although each valid refreshed cycle has its own EM ascent relation.
  • supports Source: Maximum likelihood estimation via the ECM algorithm: A general framework · Locator: Section 2 Example 1, pp.269-270 / PDF pp.3-4; Section 5, pp.276-277 / PDF pp.10-11

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Bounded monotone likelihood values do not alone establish parameter convergence, stationarity, a local maximum or global optimality. The source stationary-limit theorems impose regularity, interior and space-filling conditions, with additional uniqueness or alternative technical conditions on CM updates.
  • Optimizing only a restricted set of directions can stop at a point with nonzero gradient in an excluded direction. Arbitrary approximate Newton or other numerical steps require their own ascent checks.
  • The framework is deterministic likelihood or specified posterior-mode optimization, not posterior simulation. The incomplete-data examples assume an appropriate ignorable missingness formulation.
  • CM simplicity, number of iterations and total computation are different considerations. The source does not supply a universal speed ordering among EM, ECM and multicycle ECM. Finite curator examples check scheduling and direction-coverage boundaries; no historical fit or author software was reproduced.
