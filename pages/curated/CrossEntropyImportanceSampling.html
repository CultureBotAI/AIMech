Cross-entropy rare-event importance sampling

Algorithms AIMech:CrossEntropyImportanceSampling DRAFT
A rare-event probability estimation algorithm that fits a parametric importance-sampling proposal by cross-entropy minimization toward the nominal distribution conditioned on the event. Its multilevel construction uses likelihood-ratio-weighted pilot fits at adaptive score thresholds, then a new sample from the fitted proposal to estimate the fixed nominal event probability.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #fixed-nominal-event-and-ideal-proposal
For a fixed nominal density or mass function f(x;u), score S and target gamma, the estimand is ell=P_u(S(X)>=gamma). An admissible proposal is positive almost everywhere that the event-weighted nominal density is positive. When ell>0, the ideal proposal g*(x)=I{S(x)>=gamma} f(x;u)/ell gives a constant likelihood-ratio contribution ell, but depends on the unknown probability. This ideal motivates proposal fitting rather than supplying a directly available zero-variance solution.

Mechanism #forward-kl-weighted-proposal-fit
Where the objectives are defined, minimizing KL(g* || f(.;v)) is equivalent to maximizing E_u[I{S(X)>=gamma} log f(X;v)]. With pilot draws from f(.;w), its empirical counterpart maximizes the sum of I{S(X_i)>=gamma} [f(X_i;u)/f(X_i;w)] log f(X_i;v). The nominal parameter u remains fixed while the proposal changes. This is the indicated KL direction and an event- and likelihood-ratio-weighted fit, not an unweighted elite fit or a generic variance-minimization objective.
  • supports Source: A Tutorial on the Cross-Entropy Method · Locator: Section 2.1, PDF pp. 10–12 / printed pp. 28–30, Equations 11–17 and 21–22; Remark 2.2, PDF p. 14 / printed p. 32, Equations 27–29.
Mechanism #multilevel-pilot-thresholds
Starting from v_0=u, draw a pilot sample under the preceding fitted proposal and sort its scores increasingly. The stated finite-sample level is min(gamma, S_(ceil((1-rho)N))) for a usable sample size and 0<rho<1. Fit the event-weighted likelihood using that same pilot sample at the current level, retain the original nominal density in its likelihood ratios, and repeat while the capped level is below gamma. These pilot levels temporarily replace the target event for fitting; they do not change the final estimand.
  • supports Source: A Tutorial on the Cross-Entropy Method · Locator: Section 2.1, PDF pp. 11–13 / printed pp. 29–31, Equations 18–22 and Algorithm 2.1 Steps 1–4; Algorithm 1.1, PDF p. 5 / printed p. 23.
Mechanism #exponential-mean-fit-example
For the source example of independent exponential coordinates parametrized by positive means, each fitted mean is the corresponding event- and likelihood-ratio-weighted coordinate average. This formula requires positive total usable weight and a result in the admissible positive-mean domain. It is an example-specific closed form; a different exponential family or parametrization requires its own sufficient statistics and likelihood fit.
  • supports Source: A Tutorial on the Cross-Entropy Method · Locator: Example 2.1, PDF p. 13 / printed p. 31, Equation 24, with Algorithm 1.1 Step 3 on PDF p. 5 / printed p. 23.
Mechanism #fresh-final-likelihood-ratio-estimate
After adaptation ends at T, the source generates a new sample from f(.;v_T) and estimates ell by the ordinary average M^-1 sum_j I{S(Y_j)>=gamma} f(Y_j;u)/f(Y_j;v_T). This is not division by the sum of importance weights. Conditional on the learned proposal, a fixed positive M of conditionally independent draws with the stated proposal law and valid event support gives conditional expectation ell, and hence unconditional expectation ell. This qualification does not require the fit to be an exact KL optimum.

Scope #probability-estimation-versus-optimization
The selected static formulation estimates a fixed nominal event probability after proposal adaptation. The tutorial separately defines cross-entropy optimization by repeatedly redefining the associated search problem and setting the fitting weights W to 1. Shared parametric fitting does not identify the rare-event estimator with that optimizer, its FACE controller, self-normalized importance sampling or a classifier cross-entropy loss.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Proposal support is required on every event used in a change of measure, including lower pilot thresholds, not only the final target event. The ideal conditional density requires ell>0. Valid support and an unbiased final mean do not imply finite variance, low relative error or guaranteed variance reduction: a finite second moment of the event likelihood ratio is an additional condition. Restricted-family KL fitting is not generally the same as minimizing that second moment.
  • The elementary final-estimator argument requires new draws with their stated conditional proposal law. Reusing pilot observations with a denominator fitted from those same observations need not satisfy it, even when all fitted proposals have full support. Adaptive final-sample stopping also needs separate analysis. This does not claim that all reuse or adaptive importance-sampling schemes are invalid; it limits the particular unadjusted averaging argument.
  • The weighted likelihood must have a suitable maximizer in the chosen admissible domain; zero usable weights, singular fits, support collapse and numerical likelihood ratios need explicit policies. The source calls a maximized weighted log likelihood convex on PDF p. 11, but a zero-gradient condition is not a generic maximum certificate. In regular natural exponential-family coordinates the usual log likelihood is concave; other parametrizations change curvature. Its following blanket raw-coordinate mean statement on PDF p. 13 must be read through the relevant sufficient statistics and parametrization, rather than transferred from the independent-exponential example to every family.
  • The empirical quantile convention, ties, finite pilot sizes and fitted-family restrictions can affect the trajectory. Inclusive score thresholds need not select exactly rho N occurrences. The sample levels need not improve monotonically or reach gamma in a finite run, and no universal fit, variance or convergence guarantee is inferred. The exact-expectation deterministic counterpart is separate from this finite-sample procedure; noisy scores and stochastic-process extensions require additional evidence and assumptions.
  • This is the static rare-event construction, not a generic identity for every importance sampler, annealed importance-sampling path estimator, EDA or information-geometric optimizer. Some fitting substeps can overlap without making the complete procedures equivalent. The source’s problem-specific timings and error estimates do not establish universal speedups, sample efficiency or confidence guarantees for arbitrary applications.
  • The selected source is the retained 49-page published 2005 tutorial; the separate 47-page 2003 manuscript and earliest papers are not silently treated as equivalent or newly read. Source acquisition, PDF build dates, publication year and later selective readings are distinct. The research inspected the defining static method and selected context, not all application pages or all cited proofs. Author code, calculator-based validation and benchmark reproduction were not part of this evidence. Historical owner 244 and the dated identity assessment are research context, not a statement about uninspected future repository states.
