Riondato–Kornaropoulos fixed-sample betweenness approximation

Algorithms AIMech:RiondatoKornaropoulosBetweennessSampling DRAFT
Aliases: RK betweenness approximation, Riondato–Kornaropoulos betweenness sampling
A fixed-sample graph betweenness approximation algorithm that chooses a sample count using a VC-dimension bound for shortest-path incidence ranges, then averages internal-vertex indicators from independent uniform endpoint-pair and conditional shortest-path draws.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Scope #domain-and-estimand
The maintained core applies to finite simple static directed or undirected graphs with n>=3 and strictly positive edge weights, including unit weights. Draw an ordered pair of distinct vertices uniformly from all n(n-1) pairs, then a shortest path uniformly among that pair’s shortest paths. Unreachable pairs contribute an empty path and zero indicators. The output averages indicators for internal vertices only and targets shortest-path betweenness normalized by n(n-1). At a fixed sample count chosen independently of the main sample stream, each estimate is unbiased for that target.
  • supports Source: Fast approximation of betweenness centrality through sampling · Locator: Section 3.1, PDF pp. 5–6; Section 5.1, pp. 15–19
Mechanism #vc-based-fixed-count
Let D_hat>=3 be a valid integer upper bound on the maximum number of vertices in any shortest path, fixed before the main sample stream. Corollary 1 supplies d=floor(log2(D_hat-2))+1 as a VC-dimension upper bound for the internal-vertex path-incidence ranges. For tolerance epsilon and failure budget delta in (0,1), Equation 3 prescribes r=ceil((c/epsilon^2)(d+ln(1/delta))), using a constant c sufficient for the cited uniform-convergence theorem. Under its assumptions, the resulting iid sample gives simultaneous all-vertex additive error at most epsilon with probability at least 1-delta. The author manuscript calls c approximately 0.5 an estimate; this record does not certify that experimental value as a universal sufficient constant.
  • supports Source: Fast approximation of betweenness centrality through sampling · Locator: Theorem 1 and following constant discussion, PDF p. 8; Corollary 1, p. 9; Section 5.1, Equation 3, p. 15
Mechanism #count-weighted-path-sampling
For each sampled pair (s,t), a shortest-path search computes distances, predecessor sets and exact shortest-path counts sigma_sx. Starting at t, choose predecessor z of the current vertex x with probability sigma_sz/sigma_sx and continue to s. The factors telescope to probability 1/sigma_st for each complete shortest path. All equal-distance predecessor contributions must be retained; choosing predecessors uniformly can bias the complete-path law. Add 1/r to each internal vertex’s score and perform exactly r pair draws, including unreachable pairs.
  • supports Source: Fast approximation of betweenness centrality through sampling · Locator: Section 5.1, PDF pp. 16–17 and Algorithm 1; curator weighted-path calculations in research/discovery/rk-path-boundaries.py
Limitation #diameter-and-transcription-boundaries
Vertex diameter counts vertices on shortest paths, not total edge weight or the minimum number of hops. A largest weakly connected component’s vertex count is a safe graph-dependent upper bound for the maintained positive-weight domain. If the actual vertex diameter is at most two, all internal-vertex scores are zero; log(VD-2) must not be evaluated blindly. Equation 3 includes the +1 in the VC bound, whereas Algorithm 1 line 5 omits it. Integer rounding is required. The undirected unique-shortest-path bound VC<=3 must not be extended to directed graphs. Source reuse of a diameter-estimation draw as a main sample needs a separate dependence argument; it is outside this record’s independent-main-stream scope.
  • supports Source: Fast approximation of betweenness centrality through sampling · Locator: Definition 1, PDF p. 6; Corollary 1 and Lemma 2, pp. 9–10; directed counterexample, pp. 11–12; Equation 3, p. 15; Algorithm 1 and diameter approximation, pp. 17–18
Limitation #finite-law-and-normalization-checks
Curator enumeration compares count-weighted backtracking with an independent simple-path oracle on all 729 directed three-vertex graphs whose arcs are absent or have weight 1 or 2, covering 4374 ordered-pair cases. A separate unequal-predecessor example gives three paths probability 1/3 each, versus 1/4,1/4,1/2 under uniform predecessor choice. A directed three-vertex chain plus an isolated vertex has empty-path probability 3/4 and central-vertex expectation 1/12; conditioning away unreachable pairs changes it to 1/3. Arithmetic with D_hat=4, epsilon=0.05, delta=0.1 and the experimental c=0.5 gives ceilings 861 from Equation 3 and 661 from the omitted-+1 transcription. These finite checks certify neither the constant nor general confidence coverage.
  • supports Source: Fast approximation of betweenness centrality through sampling · Locator: Section 5.1, PDF pp. 15–19; explicit curator calculations in research/discovery/rk-path-boundaries.py
Limitation #identity-and-evaluation-scope
The fixed VC-based sample count distinguishes this algorithm from KADABRA’s adaptive stopping and ABRA’s exact pair-fraction estimators; its complete centrality estimator is also distinct from a conditional path-sampling subroutine. The inspected manuscript’s historical C/igraph implementation and five-run experiments use c=0.5, delta=0.1 and selected directed, undirected and synthetic networks. They do not establish a universal speedup, validate every parameter setting or certify current software. Relative top-k output, edge betweenness, bounded-distance and walk variants require separate identity and evidence assessment.
  • supports Source: Fast approximation of betweenness centrality through sampling · Locator: Sections 5.1–5.2, 6 and 7, PDF pp. 15–35; named-neighbor distinction assessed against the maintained KADABRA and ABRA records

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The sample-size guarantee is conditional on a sufficient theorem constant, a valid vertex-diameter upper bound and iid draws independent of its selection. No explicit universal validity of c=0.5 is asserted.
  • Strictly positive weights, simple static graphs and exact shortest-path counts are the maintained scope. Zero-weight cycles, multigraph conventions, finite-precision counting and dynamic changes need separate analysis.
  • The all-ordered-pair denominator includes unreachable pairs. A single collapsed empty path must carry the aggregate unreachable-pair mass; dropping it or assigning only one pair’s mass changes the distribution.
  • The source contains small-diameter and equation-versus-pseudocode boundaries. This record follows the scoped mathematical procedure, not literal unqualified execution of Algorithm 1.
  • Only selected source displays were visually inspected; remaining figures, tables and proof displays are text-only. No general proof certification, author-code execution, benchmark replication or publisher-byte equivalence.
  • Top-k, edge, bounded-distance and walk variants, and the diameter-sample reuse optimization remain outside this identity. DRAFT remains after author self-review.
