Oja Learning Rule

Algorithms AIMech:OjaLearningRule DRAFT
Aliases: Oja’s Rule
A single-output linear-neuron learning rule that combines a Hebbian input-output weight increment with an output-squared weight decay, obtained as a first-order approximation to Euclidean-normalized Hebbian learning.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The original Oja rule updates one linear unit and targets a leading second-moment direction under its stated assumptions. It is distinct from exact normalized Hebbian learning, generic Hebbian learning, supervised perceptron fitting, multi-output Sanger learning, nonlinear Hebbian ICA and FastICA fixed-point updates. Biological motivation does not establish experimental biological implementation. Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #local-update
For current input x, weights w, scalar output y=w^T x and positive gain eta, the truncated rule is w_new=w+eta*y*(x-y*w). Each synaptic increment uses its input, its weight and the common scalar output. The negative eta*y^2*w term regulates Hebbian growth. This drops second- and higher-order gain terms from explicit normalization of w+eta*y*x, and therefore does not preserve unit norm exactly at a finite step.
  • supports Source: A Simplified Neuron Model as a Principal Component Analyzer · Locator: Equations 1-6, printed pp. 267-269 / PDF pp. 1-3; Section 4, Equations 11-15, pp. 271-272 / PDF pp. 5-6; curator finite-norm example
Mechanism #mean-dynamics
When the current input is independent of current weights, averaging the truncated increment per unit gain gives Cw-(w^T Cw)w, where C=E[xx^T]. The associated mean differential equation has signed top unit eigenvectors as attracting equilibria when C has a positive simple largest eigenvalue and initialization has a nonzero component along its eigenvector. In eigenvector coordinates, ratios to the leading coordinate decay according to the eigenvalue gap. This is a mean-ODE result with explicit conditions.
  • supports Source: A Simplified Neuron Model as a Principal Component Analyzer · Locator: Section 3, Equations 7-8 and theorem, printed pp. 269-270 / PDF pp. 3-4; curator positive-eigenvalue qualification
Scope #centering-and-scope
For zero-mean inputs, C is the covariance, and the leading learned response corresponds to the largest-variance principal component. Without centering, C includes the outer product of the mean and the rule targets maximum quadratic mean instead. Mean subtraction is not part of the displayed update. The paper analyzes a single neuron and does not derive a full multi-component PCA network or independent-component separator.
  • supports Source: A Simplified Neuron Model as a Principal Component Analyzer · Locator: Section 3, printed p. 270 / PDF p. 4; Section 5, p. 272 / PDF p. 6
Limitation #stochastic-convergence-scope
The source invokes stochastic-approximation results with suitable input regularity and a decreasing gain, such as a gain proportional to inverse time; it does not spell out a complete general stochastic convergence theorem. A small fixed gain is discussed as an approximation to mean dynamics, not a guarantee of exact almost-sure convergence. Zero or mean-ODE orthogonal starts and repeated leading eigenvalues require separate treatment. Large discrete gains can increase weight magnitude instead of controlling it.
  • supports Source: A Simplified Neuron Model as a Principal Component Analyzer · Locator: Section 3, printed pp. 269-270 / PDF pp. 3-4; curator stationary-direction, finite-norm and large-gain examples

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The PCA interpretation requires centered finite-second-moment inputs and the relevant stationary/input assumptions. Correlation across time, changing distributions and dependence between current input and weights need a justified analysis.
  • The first-order discrete rule is not exact normalization. Step size and initialization matter; the mean-ODE theorem does not establish global stability of every finite-gain implementation.
  • With a repeated largest eigenvalue there is no unique principal direction, and sign is intrinsically ambiguous. A single unit yields one direction rather than an ordered orthogonal basis.
  • All seven source pages were read. Finite arithmetic and analytic mean-flow checks were performed; no neuron training, historical simulation or biological experiment was reproduced.
