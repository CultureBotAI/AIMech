Information-geometric optimization

Algorithms AIMech:InformationGeometricOptimization DRAFT
A stochastic black-box optimization framework that adapts a parametrized sampling distribution using objective ranks and Fisher natural gradients, defining a continuous flow with reference-frozen quantile utilities and finite algorithms that approximate that flow by ranked sampling and Euler updates.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope IGO specifies quantile/rank utility, reference-frozen natural-gradient flow and a parameterized sampled update across distribution families. It is not an exact alias of generic NES, xNES, SNES, full maintained CMA-ES, CEM or PBIL. The scoped rank-mu/no-path Gaussian, normalized-weight ML and Bernoulli/no-mutation correspondences do not equate the maintained whole methods. Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #distribution-relative-rank-utility
For minimization of a black-box objective f over a search space X, choose a parametrized family of sampling distributions P_theta and a non-increasing selection function w on [0,1]. Define the lower and upper probability levels of x using, respectively, objective values strictly below and at or below f(x) under P_theta. When the two levels coincide, the utility W_theta(x) is w at that level. For a positive-probability tie, it is the average of w over the whole interval between the two levels. Averaging w over this interval is generally different from evaluating w at its midpoint. This distribution-relative rank utility replaces raw objective magnitudes as the preference signal.
  • supports Source: Information-Geometric Optimization Algorithms: A Unifying Picture via Invariance Principles · Locator: Section 2.2.1, Definition 3 and Equations 4–6, PDF/printed pp. 9–10.
Mechanism #reference-frozen-natural-gradient-flow
At a current parameter theta_t, freeze the reference utility W_theta_t while differentiating its expectation under a variable P_theta. The IGO flow follows natural-gradient ascent of that expectation, evaluated at theta_t. With a differentiable sampling family and a usable nonsingular Fisher information matrix I, its velocity is I(theta_t)^(-1) times the expectation under P_theta_t of W_theta_t(x) times the score gradient of log P_theta(x), evaluated at theta_t. Differentiation acts on the distribution parameters, not on the black-box objective. Differentiating the utility and its averaging distribution together would instead differentiate the constant integral of w and lose the intended preference update.
  • supports Source: Information-Geometric Optimization Algorithms: A Unifying Picture via Invariance Principles · Locator: Section 2.1.2, PDF/printed p. 7; Section 2.2.2, Definition 4 and Equations 7–13, pp. 10–11; fixed-reference explanation below Equation 7.
Mechanism #sampled-euler-update-and-ties
The finite IGO algorithm draws N independent samples from the current distribution, evaluates f and ranks samples from best to worst for minimization. Without ties, a sample with zero-based rank r receives weight (1/N) w((r + 1/2)/N). Tied samples receive the average of these weights over their possible rank positions. The parameter update adds delta_t times the sum of each weight multiplied by its natural score gradient at the current parameter. The factor 1/N is already in each weight. This is a sampled Euler approximation, requiring a chosen distribution family, weighting scheme, coordinate parametrization, sample size and step size; it is not an exact finite-time solution of the flow.
  • supports Source: Information-Geometric Optimization Algorithms: A Unifying Picture via Invariance Principles · Locator: Section 2.2.3, Definition 5 and Equations 14, 16–18, PDF/printed pp. 11–13; tie convention after Equation 14. Footnote 2, Equation 15, gives a separate integral-weight alternative.
Mechanism #qualified-invariance-properties
Strictly increasing transformations of objective values preserve the population probability levels, sampled rankings and ties, so they preserve the IGO flow and the finite rule with corresponding samples. A smooth invertible reparametrization preserves the continuous trajectory in distribution space. Finite additive updates generally agree only to first order in delta_t, with local differences of order delta_t squared under the stated regularity assumptions; affine parameter changes preserve the finite rule exactly. A one-to-one search-space transformation also requires preservation of the distribution family, a locally diffeomorphic induced parameter correspondence and transformed initialization. Its finite-update equivariance has the same second-order qualification unless the induced parameter map is affine.
  • supports Source: Information-Geometric Optimization Algorithms: A Unifying Picture via Invariance Principles · Locator: Section 2.2.4, PDF/printed p. 13; Appendix C.1, Propositions 17–19 and their finite-step qualifications, pp. 43–44.
Scope #population-limit-and-quantile-improvement
At a fixed parameter, independent sampling, square-integrable score components and a bounded non-increasing selection function support almost-sure convergence of the rank-weighted empirical update to the exact-population Euler update as N tends to infinity, with the stated tie averaging. This limit alone does not remove the time-discretization error. For the ideal continuous IGO flow with truncation weights w(u)=1 when u<=q and 0 otherwise, where 0<q<1, the source establishes non-increase of the q-quantile of f under its regularity assumptions. Strict improvement additionally requires no objective plateaus and a nonstationary trajectory. These are not finite-population monotonicity, finite-budget accuracy or global-optimum guarantees.
  • supports Source: Information-Geometric Optimization Algorithms: A Unifying Picture via Invariance Principles · Locator: Section 3.1, Theorem 6, PDF/printed p. 14; Appendix D.2, Proposition 27 and proof, pp. 52–54, with its monotonicity-wording inconsistency retained below; Section 3.2, Proposition 7, p. 15, and Appendix D.3, pp. 54–55.
Scope #scoped-algorithm-correspondences
The framework overlaps rank-shaped natural evolution strategies; it does not equate the rank utility with raw expected fitness. For Gaussian distributions in mean/covariance coordinates, the IGO update matches the displayed rank-mu CMA update when mean and covariance learning rates are equal and evolution-path and step-size-control effects are disabled. For product Bernoulli distributions in probability coordinates, a weight of one on the best sample and zero on the others recovers the stated PBIL attraction update without probability mutation. The separate IGO-ML construction maximizes a weighted likelihood objective; for an exponential family in expectation coordinates and sample weights summing to one, it coincides with the corresponding IGO and smoothed cross-entropy updates when their required maximizers exist. These special cases do not make the whole framework an exact alias of full CMA-ES, mutated PBIL, generic NES or cross-entropy updates in arbitrary coordinates.
  • supports Source: Information-Geometric Optimization Algorithms: A Unifying Picture via Invariance Principles · Locator: NES discussion following Definition 4, PDF/printed p. 11; Section 4, Definition 11, Equations 30–34 and Theorem 12, pp. 20–22; Section 5.1, Proposition 14 and best-sample/no-mutation case, pp. 23–24; Section 5.2.1, Proposition 15 and footnote 5, p. 25.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The source is the official 2017 JMLR article. Supporting research read native text on pages 1–33, 39–45 and 52–55 and selected visuals; root reading has its own narrower ledger. This is not a full 65-page proof audit. Earlier versions, author implementations and benchmark results were not reproduced.
  • The core score/Fisher formulas require a suitable regular sampling family and legitimate differentiation under the integral. An empirical Fisher estimate can be singular: at least as many distinct samples as parameter dimensions is necessary, not sufficient, for invertibility. Arbitrarily large finite steps can leave the parameter domain or poorly approximate the flow; no automatic regularization or boundary repair is supplied.
  • The rank utility depends on the current distribution and the selected weight function. A constant weight provides no population preference signal, although its finite-sample estimate can fluctuate. Finite ties average weights over compatible ranks. The integral-weight alternative in footnote 2 is not silently substituted for midpoint weights; they need not agree at finite sample size.
  • Definition 3 on page 9 uses a non-increasing weight for minimization. Proposition 27 on page 52 prints non-decreasing, while its proof on pages 53–54 uses non-increasing inequalities. The record follows the defining minimization convention and retains this source inconsistency; it does not claim an author-issued erratum or certification of all proofs.
  • The introductory discussion on page 8 uses minimize/descent wording beside a plus-gradient update. Definitions 3–5 specify ascent of the transformed rank utility. The overview on page 17 also needs the detailed finite-step invariance qualifications of Propositions 18–19 on pages 43–44; nonlinear coordinate changes are not asserted to leave arbitrary finite updates identical.
  • Strictly increasing objective transformations preserve order and ties; a merely non-decreasing transformation can create new ties. Search-space transformations must preserve the distribution family. Sampling consistency is a fixed-parameter limit and supplies no explicit finite population threshold, high-probability trajectory bound, finite-sample monotonicity or global convergence guarantee.
  • IGO-ML is a separately defined likelihood-maximization construction, not an automatic alias of the generic sampled Euler rule. Its required maximizer must be well defined and tractable. Expectation coordinates, mean/covariance coordinates and other smoothed cross-entropy parametrizations need not produce the same finite update. No distinct IGO-ML, rank-mu CMA or PBIL variant record is created by this framework description.
  • Existing NES, CMA-ES, cross-entropy optimization and PBIL records remain distinct. The special-case correspondences do not import complete evolution-path adaptation, probability-vector mutation, all raw-fitness NES rules or every cross-entropy variant. No historical-priority, general superiority, diversity-optimality or benchmark claim is made.
