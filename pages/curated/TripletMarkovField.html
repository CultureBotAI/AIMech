Triplet Markov field

Model Families AIMech:TripletMarkovField DRAFT
Aliases: TMF
A latent-variable spatial-model family that introduces an auxiliary field so the complete hidden, auxiliary and observed triplet has a normalized Gibbs factorization on a specified neighborhood graph, while its hidden–observed projection need not remain Markov on that graph.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The inspected construction uses finite target labels X_s and auxiliary states U_s with observed Y_s, and imposes spatial Markov structure on T_s=(X_s,U_s,Y_s). Triplet refers to three processes at each site, not three-site clique size. A pair field embeds through a constant or redundant auxiliary coordinate; general triplet fields can have projections outside that pair-field family. This is distinct from a triplet Markov chain, a neural hidden layer or an emitted-symbol memory mechanism. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Choose clique energies over T=(X,U,Y) on a finite neighborhood graph and normalize p(x,u,y) proportional to exp(-sum_c phi_c(x_c,u_c,y_c)). At observed y, the augmented hidden field V_s=(X_s,U_s) has a Gibbs posterior on that graph, even when the marginalized target posterior p(x|y) does not. Sample or otherwise infer that augmented posterior, then sum p(X_s=x,U_s=u|y) over u at fixed x to obtain the target-site posterior; its maximizer is the sitewise marginal-posterior-mode estimate. The paper illustrates a Markov auxiliary field with local conditionally independent X and Y, and experiments with a more restricted Markov V field and independent Gaussian site observations. Neither p(x,u) nor p(x,y) is required to be Markov in the general joint-triplet definition. The proposed parameter-estimation use of ICE averages a chosen complete-data estimator under the current augmented posterior, exactly when that expectation is available or approximately from posterior samples.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The augmented joint law must be normalizable and the observed evidence positive. A finite-site Gibbs factorization does not itself make marginal likelihoods, posterior probabilities or sampling computationally easy.
  • Marginalizing U can induce additional interactions; an implementation cannot assume p(X|Y) retains the original local graph. Maximizing over U before choosing X can disagree with summing over U, and sitewise marginal decoding differs from global configuration MAP.
  • The experiments restrict the (X,U) prior to a Potts-style field and use sitewise Gaussian observations. Those restrictions are examples, not defining requirements of every triplet field. Non-Gaussian and broader graphical extensions in the conclusion are follow-up work.
  • ICE is conditional expectation of a complete-data estimator, whereas EM optimizes an expected complete log likelihood. The interchange needed to identify them is not automatic; likelihood monotonicity and global convergence are not guaranteed merely by the ICE name. Gibbs/Metropolis feasibility does not establish exact independent finite samples.
  • The least-squares spatial estimator used in the example is referenced rather than fully derived here. Empty empirical classes, constrained parameter values, initialization and sampling convergence need explicit handling in an implementation.
  • The synthetic examples and one radar-image grouping compare particular fitted or chosen baselines. They do not establish the paper’s broad suggestion of universal superiority, and no numerical performance claim or author-code reproduction is curated.
