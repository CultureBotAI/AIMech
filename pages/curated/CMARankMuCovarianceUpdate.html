CMA rank-mu covariance update

Mathematical Methods AIMech:CMARankMuCovarianceUpdate DRAFT
A covariance-valued update component introduced in the 2003 CMA-ES construction that forms an equal-weight second moment of selected mutation steps about the old sampling mean and blends it with an evolution-path outer product and the previous search covariance.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record isolates the original 2003 equal-weight selected-step second-moment covariance update and its path blend. The maintained 2001 CMA-ES record explicitly excludes this extension; IGO and xNES retain qualified rank-mu correspondences rather than this defining finite component. It is not the whole optimizer, a modern weighted or active covariance rule, or an IPOP restart policy. Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #selected-old-mean-second-moment
For integer search dimension n>=1, current mean m, positive global scale sigma and covariance C=B*D^2*B^T, write each selected offspring step as y_i=(x_i-m)/sigma=B*D*z_i. With an integer mu>=1 selected offspring, the original equal-weight construction forms Z=(1/mu)*sum_i(y_i*y_i^T). The steps use the old sampling mean and scale. Z is their uncentered second moment, not their covariance about the newly selected mean and not the outer product of their average step.
  • supports Source: Reducing the Time Complexity of the Derandomized Evolution Strategy with Covariance Matrix Adaptation (CMA-ES) · Locator: Section 2, PDF p. 4 / printed p. 3, Equations 1–4; Section 3, PDF p. 6 / printed p. 5, Equation 10. The centering distinction is a direct algebraic reading of these formulas.
Mechanism #path-and-population-covariance-blend
The source retains the evolution path p_new=(1-c_c)*p+sqrt(c_c*(2-c_c))*sqrt(mu)*mean_i(y_i), then updates the search covariance by C_new=(1-c_cov)*C+c_cov*(alpha*p_new*p_new^T+(1-alpha)*Z). Here alpha is the source alpha_cov in [0,1]. The path outer product represents accumulated mean-step information, while Z uses individual selected steps from the current generation. This is a covariance-update component, not a complete initialization, sampling, selection or termination algorithm.
  • supports Source: Reducing the Time Complexity of the Derandomized Evolution Strategy with Covariance Matrix Adaptation (CMA-ES) · Locator: Section 2, PDF p. 4 / printed p. 3, Equations 5–6; Section 3, PDF p. 6 / printed p. 5, Equations 10–11.
Scope #blend-endpoints-and-host-boundary
At alpha=1 the blend recovers the rank-one covariance rule reviewed in the source; alpha=0 uses Z as the sole added moment, and alpha=1/mu is the hybrid configuration studied there. The paper labels these Orig-CMA, New-CMA and Hybr-CMA. These are configurations of the construction, not asserted exact aliases for its component identity. The modification leaves the host global step-size adaptation unchanged, so its scalar exponential update is outside the defining Eq. 10–11 component.
  • supports Source: Reducing the Time Complexity of the Derandomized Evolution Strategy with Covariance Matrix Adaptation (CMA-ES) · Locator: Section 3, PDF pp. 5–6 / printed pp. 4–5, unchanged global step-size statement and text following Equation 11; Section 6 opening paragraph, PDF p. 8 / printed p. 7, variant names.
Scope #rank-and-positive-definite-domain
Z is positive semidefinite and has rank at most min(mu,n). Equality requires the selected vectors to have the corresponding span; the source’s equality statement is interpreted with this generic full-span qualification. For positive-definite C, alpha in [0,1] and 0<=c_cov<1, the blend remains positive definite. At c_cov=1, the positively weighted path and selected steps must together span the space to ensure positive definiteness. These are exact-arithmetic consequences of the outer-product blend, not numerical-conditioning guarantees.
  • supports Source: Reducing the Time Complexity of the Derandomized Evolution Strategy with Covariance Matrix Adaptation (CMA-ES) · Locator: Section 3, PDF p. 6 / printed p. 5, Equation 10 and its rank statement, followed by Equation 11. The rank bound and positive-definiteness conditions are explicit curator linear-algebra consequences.
Scope #neutral-selection-and-path-initialization-scope
Under the paper’s neutral-selection or unchanged selected-law idealization, independent zero-mean selected Gaussian steps with covariance C give E[Z]=C. This is not an unbiasedness claim under arbitrary fitness selection. The analogous path second moment additionally depends on its distribution: with frozen C, p_0=0 and 0<c_c<=1, the first path has E[p_1*p_1^T]=c_c*(2-c_c)*C. Consequently the first covariance expectation is [1-c_cov*alpha*(1-c_c)^2]*C. This bounded curator derivation distinguishes zero initialization from a stationary path idealization; it is not a theorem about the full adaptive run.
  • supports Source: Reducing the Time Complexity of the Derandomized Evolution Strategy with Covariance Matrix Adaptation (CMA-ES) · Locator: Section 2, Equation 5, PDF p. 4 / printed p. 3, and zero-path initialization on PDF p. 5 / printed p. 4; Section 3, Equations 13–14 on PDF p. 6 / printed p. 5 and the expectation paragraph continuing on PDF p. 7 / printed p. 6. The first-step expectations are curator algebra under the stated frozen-law assumptions.
Scope #empirically-chosen-covariance-rate
The source couples the new covariance mechanism to an empirical learning-rate prescription: Equation 12 blends the original rate 2/(n+sqrt(2))^2 with min(1,(2*mu-1)/((n+2)^2+mu)), using alpha and 1-alpha. Section 4 obtains this expression by trial and error on ellipsoidal tests and chooses conservative rates below the measured optima. Its experimental choice lambda=4*mu is not a universal requirement of the covariance formula, and the prescription is not asserted optimal for arbitrary objectives.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This record is restricted to the 2003 equal-weight selected-step moment and nonnegative path/moment blend. Arbitrary unequal or negative weights, active covariance updates, later safeguards, restart schemes, limited-memory variants and modern implementations are not established by this source scope. The alpha=1 endpoint recovers the source’s reviewed host rule without making the new component identical to the whole maintained 2001 CMA-ES algorithm.
  • The source’s rank=min(mu,n) wording requires a generic full-span qualification. Degenerate supplied steps or finite precision can reduce rank. Positive-semidefinite arithmetic and a retained positive old-covariance coefficient do not guarantee good conditioning, stable eigendecompositions or an admissible numerical implementation. The boundary c_cov=1 requires adequate positive span; the ordinary positive-definite domain cannot be assumed there.
  • The neutral-model expectation argument on PDF pp. 6–7 / printed pp. 5–6 does not establish conditional martingale behavior or stationarity throughout an adaptive optimization run. The zero-path first-step calculation is curator hand algebra under fixed C and independent neutral steps, not an author-issued erratum or a claim to have disproved the separate Hansen 1998 argument cited by the paper. That thesis proof was not acquired or audited here.
  • The new term uses old-mean normalized individual steps. Replacing it by covariance around the selected mean, by an outer product of the selected mean step or by a generic elite maximum-likelihood covariance changes the rule. Connections to IGO or natural-gradient search require their own distribution, utility and finite-update assumptions; neither a first-order resemblance nor a shared Gaussian family makes these methods exact aliases.
  • Equation 12 is an empirically selected rate, not a universal optimum. Reported time-complexity results count generations in the stated parallel experiments; they do not establish linear dense-matrix arithmetic, a universal speedup or a convergence guarantee. No numerical performance result or benchmark replication is imported.
  • Section 7, PDF pp. 18–19 / printed pp. 17–18, reports excessive shrinkage of overall variance and difficulties with global step-size control for large populations and rates. Smaller covariance rates and covariance renormalization are discussed as possible responses, not silently added to this component as mandatory safeguards. Positive-definite covariance alone does not prevent these problems.
