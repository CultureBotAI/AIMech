RNN-RBM

Architectures AIMech:RecurrentNeuralNetworkRestrictedBoltzmannMachine DRAFT
Aliases: Recurrent neural network restricted Boltzmann machine
A neural sequence architecture in which a deterministic recurrent network with its own hidden state supplies parameters of a conditional RBM that models each next visible vector.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the RNN-RBM architecture introduced by Boulanger-Lewandowski, Bengio and Vincent, with recurrent hidden units distinct from the RBM’s stochastic hidden variables. Their displayed single-layer construction changes visible and hidden biases while sharing the RBM interaction weights across time. The broader parameter-emitting formulation and particular pretraining strategies are not all required features of that specialization. RTRBM ties recurrent state computation to RBM hidden probabilities; untying those parameters is a defining distinction here. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
In the displayed binary model, let r_t=sigmoid(F v_t+B r_(t-1)+c) be the deterministic recurrent state. Set visible bias a_t=a+A r_(t-1) and hidden bias d_t=d+D r_(t-1). With hidden-by-visible weights W, define p(v_t,h_t|r_(t-1)) proportional to exp(a_t^T v_t+d_t^T h_t+h_t^T W v_t), and marginalize binary h_t for the frame distribution. The visible-sequence probability is the product of these conditional frame distributions; the current frame updates r_t only after its probability has been evaluated from r_(t-1). Recurrent and RBM hidden widths may differ, and W is distinct from recurrent input weights F. Train using conditional RBM likelihood statistics, approximated in the paper by data-started finite contrastive divergence, and backpropagate bias-gradient signals through A, D and the recurrence. The reverse state signal includes A^T and D^T times next-frame bias gradients, plus B^T times the next recurrent preactivation gradient. Generation alternates conditional frame sampling with the deterministic state update.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Equation (1) prints negative terms in the joint exponent that conflict with the positive-bias sigmoid conditionals (2)–(3) and free energy (4). Equations (14)–(15) also print negative hidden-bias signs. This record follows the mutually consistent conditionals and free energy, disclosing the conflicts rather than asserting author-code behavior.
  • Under the column-vector convention of Equation (11), reverse state propagation requires transposed recurrent and bias-projection matrices. Printed Equation (19) omits those transposes; unequal recurrent and RBM hidden widths make the dimensional issue explicit. Original finite calculations check the stated convention and exact small-model gradients, not the authors’ implementation.
  • Fully observed frames determine the recurrent state, but conditional RBM normalization and equilibrium sampling remain generally expensive. Finite CD and finite Gibbs generation are approximate; exact state inference is not exact likelihood learning.
  • The worked model emits two bias vectors, not every RBM weight. A more general recurrent parameter-emission scheme is mentioned separately. Gaussian outputs, different recurrent cells and pretraining choices require their own stated conventions.
  • RNN-RBM reduces to the paper’s RTRBM parameterization under matching hidden widths and appropriate weight/bias tying; this does not make the independently parameterized family identical to RTRBM. No global optimization, long-range memory or empirical ranking guarantee is asserted.
