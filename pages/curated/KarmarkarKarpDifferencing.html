Karmarkar–Karp differencing

Algorithms AIMech:KarmarkarKarpDifferencing DRAFT
Aliases: Karmarkar–Karp heuristic
A two-way number-partitioning heuristic that repeatedly replaces the two largest remaining nonnegative residuals by their difference, then reconstructs a partition whose absolute sum difference is the final residual.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #largest-pair-reduction
For a finite nonempty multiset of nonnegative weights, repeatedly remove two largest residuals a>=b and insert a-b until one remains. The final value is the achieved absolute difference between the two subset sums. Residuals include combinations formed at earlier steps, so this is not simply assigning the next original weight to the currently lighter bin. Equal-valued input items retain distinct identities.
  • supports Source: The Differencing Method of Set Partitioning · Locator: Section2.1 AlgorithmA and worked example, PDF/printed p.2
  • supports Source: A Complete Anytime Algorithm for Number Partitioning · Locator: Section2.6 and Figure1, manuscript PDF/printed pp.8–10
Mechanism #relative-orientation-reconstruction
A residual represents a partial signed sum of original weights. Combining a and b by a-b preserves the orientation of one partial partition and reverses the other. Recording the combination tree permits signs to be propagated back to all original item indices; the positive and negative groups reconstruct a partition with the reported residue. A later reversal can flip an entire partial partition, so a differencing step does not irrevocably name each raw item’s final bin.
  • supports Source: The Differencing Method of Set Partitioning · Locator: Section2.1 AlgorithmA and worked example, PDF/printed p.2
  • supports Source: A Complete Anytime Algorithm for Number Partitioning · Locator: Section2.6 and Figure1, manuscript PDF/printed pp.8–10
Scope #heap-and-arithmetic-cost
A max-priority queue performs n-1 pair reductions with O(n log n) comparison/arithmetic operations. Retaining one binary combination node per reduction uses O(n) items, and a traversal reconstructs the partition in O(n) operations. These are unit-cost operation and item-count statements, not bounds independent of numeric bit length, precision or implementation overhead.
  • supports Source: A Complete Anytime Algorithm for Number Partitioning · Locator: Section2.6, manuscript PDF/printed pp.8–10; curator explicit combination-tree construction
Limitation #heuristic-not-certificate
The heuristic is not an exact optimizer. On weights8,7,6,5,4, its residual sequence is(6,5,4,1), (4,1,1), (3,1), then2, while subsets{8,7} and{6,5,4} have equal sum15 and residue0. Thus the achieved residue is an upper bound on the minimum residue, not a lower bound or proof of optimality. The procedure does not enforce equal subset cardinalities.
  • supports Source: A Complete Anytime Algorithm for Number Partitioning · Locator: Section2.6 and Figure1, manuscript PDF/printed pp.8–10
  • supports Source: A Complete Anytime Algorithm for Number Partitioning · Locator: Section3 and Figure4, manuscript PDF/printed pp.12–13; curator exhaustive sign enumeration
Scope #distinct-randomized-theorem-algorithm
AlgorithmA is distinct from the original report’s adjacent-pair AlgorithmB and the later interval-based random differencing procedure with compaction. The report’s high-probability asymptotic analysis uses that randomized procedure and assumptions on independent input values with a Lipschitz density. Its opening linear-time and vanishing-residue claims are not adopted as unconditional guarantees for the bare largest-pair heuristic. The k-way cyclic-differencing construction is also outside this two-way record.
  • supports Source: The Differencing Method of Set Partitioning · Locator: Sections1–2.4, PDF/printed pp.1–16, especially AlgorithmB p.3, random differencing/compaction pp.4–6 and Theorems1–5 pp.6–7; Section3 pp.16–30
Scope #reported-empirical-scope
Korf’s Figure2 compares average residues from KK and sorted greedy partitioning on sampled uniformly distributed ten-digit integers. The reported average advantage depends on that input distribution and precision; it does not establish that KK beats greedy on every instance. CKK’s later improvements arise from additional search, not an optimality guarantee of this heuristic. No historical runtime or stochastic experiment was replicated here.
  • supports Source: A Complete Anytime Algorithm for Number Partitioning · Locator: Section2.6 and Figure2, manuscript PDF/printed pp.9–11; Section3 pp.12–13

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Scope is two-way unconstrained partitioning of a finite nonempty nonnegative multiset. Multiway, cardinality-balanced, randomized-compaction and complete-search variants have distinct identities.
  • The returned partition is feasible but can be suboptimal. Ties can change reconstructed item membership even when the residue is unchanged.
  • Unit-cost complexity assumes exact comparisons and arithmetic; floating-point cancellation and very large numeric representations require separate implementation analysis.
  • The Berkeley archive metadata and Korf’s1982 report citation differ. The inspected versions are identified explicitly, without inferring publication priority.
  • All30 original-report pages and all31 Korf manuscript pages were read. Small constructed calculations were run; author implementations and the final1998 journal full text were not inspected.
