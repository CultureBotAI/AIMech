Guided Genetic Algorithm

Algorithms AIMech:GuidedGeneticAlgorithm DRAFT
Aliases: GGA
The Lau–Tsang genetic-search framework that uses shared adaptive solution-feature penalties both to modify fitness and to construct per-chromosome gene weights that bias crossover and mutation.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record identifies the named Lau–Tsang GLS/genetic hybrid, not every algorithm described informally as guided genetic search. Later template-guided, fuzzy-guided or distributed algorithms are not asserted equivalent from their names. Generic GA supplies population evolution and GLS supplies feature penalties; GGA adds penalty-derived per-gene templates and specialized variation. It does not replace the population by the probability vector used in PBIL. LPGA is a problem-specific precursor, not an exact alias. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Maintain a population and a shared set of nonnegative feature-penalty counters, initially zero. Define each feature by an exhibited variable assignment or group of assignments and an application-specific undesirability cost. When the best current population member’s fitness remains unchanged for a configured number of generations, increment all maximal-utility present-feature counters, with utility cost/(1+counter). Use the augmented objective f+lambda*sum(counter*indicator) for the minimizing formulation and recompute each chromosome’s template: a gene weight sums counters for its exhibited features that involve that gene. Thus weights depend on the chromosome under shared penalties; offspring templates are recalculated, not inherited. At a crossover position with positive total parental weight, choose parent1’s value with probability weight2/(weight1+weight2), favoring the lighter gene. For mutation with positive total template weight, sample a gene in proportion to its weight and choose an objective-best domain value, with random tie handling in the displayed operator. The manuscript decrements a changed gene’s weight only if positive and allows repeated gene selection. Population replacement, mutation/crossover rates, stagnation threshold and stopping are configurable; the application uses elitist parent-plus-offspring replacement. These statements describe the documented framework and positive-weight operators, not a complete executable specification for all boundaries.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The source initializes counters and template weights to zero but its displayed crossover and roulette rules divide by total weight or draw from an empty integer range when that total is zero. Neither inspected method supplies the fallback. A uniform fallback or pseudocount is an implementation decision requiring separate evidence; none is asserted here.
  • The displayed mutation comparisons maximize g, whereas the article identifies the illustrated application as minimizing augmented cost and explicitly notes descent in footnote4. The record preserves this inconsistency and states objective-best selection with an explicit minimizing convention, rather than claiming the printed pseudocode is directly executable unchanged.
  • The stagnation trigger is a heuristic, not a certificate that every possible improving gene change or population transition has been exhausted. The thesis’s future-work discussion states this limitation. Penalty updates need not escape all local traps or reach a global optimum.
  • The application’s elitist replacement, finite mutation count, tie multiplicity and zero-weight handling do not define a unique universal implementation. Feature encoding, scaling and penalty semantics affect results; the reported benchmark superiority is not curated as a general guarantee.
