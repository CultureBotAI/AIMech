Momentum contrast

Algorithms AIMech:MomentumContrast DRAFT
Aliases: MoCo
A contrastive representation-learning mechanism that maintains a queue of encoded negative keys and produces new keys with an encoder updated by an exponential moving average of the query encoder.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is original MoCo from the 2020 paper, with its image instance-discrimination realization. The momentum acts on encoder parameters, not on per-example memory-bank embeddings. It is distinct from MoCo v2/v3 and from large-batch end-to-end contrastive training. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Initialize key-encoder parameters from the query encoder and maintain a finite FIFO queue of K≥1 encoded keys. For each image draw two augmented views, form normalized query q and current positive key k+, and use loss −log[exp(q·k+/tau)/(exp(q·k+/tau)+sum_(k in queue)exp(q·k/tau))] with tau>0. Stop gradients through the key outputs and queue; update the query encoder from the minibatch loss. In paper Algorithm 1, next update key parameters as theta_k←m theta_k+(1−m)theta_q, m in [0,1), enqueue the keys already computed for this batch, and remove the oldest batch to maintain the queue. The queue decouples dictionary size from current minibatch size; the momentum encoder reduces changes between key representations created at different iterations.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Queued keys were encoded at different parameter states. A slow update aims to improve consistency; it does not make historical keys identical to recomputed current-encoder outputs.
  • The image realization treats different images as negatives. This is instance discrimination, not verification that all negative pairs belong to different semantic classes.
  • The paper’s distributed ResNet implementation shuffles the key minibatch across GPUs and restores correspondence after encoding to reduce batch-normalization leakage. Other normalization choices need their own reproduction checks.
  • The displayed algorithm abstracts queue initialization and requires a valid finite dictionary. Temperature, queue length, momentum and augmentation are tuning choices; no single setting is universally optimal.
  • This record specifies the original paper’s update order and scope. It does not import later MoCo variants, claim independent samples, or reproduce benchmark training.
