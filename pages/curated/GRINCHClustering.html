GRINCH clustering

Algorithms AIMech:GRINCHClustering DRAFT

An incremental hierarchical clustering algorithm that uses set-wise linkage scores to revise a binary cluster tree through local rotations, nonlocal subtree grafting and restructuring.

6 Claims
Mechanism #incremental-tree-revision
GRINCH builds a binary cluster hierarchy incrementally under a set-wise linkage function. It places a new observation near a nearest leaf, applies local rotations, and attempts nonlocal grafts followed by restructuring. Earlier groupings can therefore change as observations arrive.
  • supports Source: Scalable Hierarchical Clustering with Tree Grafting · Locator: Sections2–3, PDF pp.2–8; Algorithms1–3
  • supports Source: GRINCH author implementation: src/scala/grinch/core/Grinch.scala · Locator: Grinch.insert, rotate and graft
Scope #model-based-separation
The paper defines separation using a latent graph: for a common set, a partner producing a connected union must score higher than one producing a disconnected union. Under that assumption, Theorem1 states that the intended exact construction contains the graph connected components as a tree-consistent partition for any arrival order. This is a conditional result about cluster recovery, not identical trees across orders or an unconditional guarantee for approximate implementations.
  • supports Source: Scalable Hierarchical Clustering with Tree Grafting · Locator: Definition1, PDF p.3; Theorem1, p.7; AppendixA, pp.19–23
Mechanism #grafting-and-tie-boundary
A graft searches outside the current subtree and requires a candidate pair to prefer one another over their current siblings. Candidate ancestors are considered when a sibling is preferred. The printed Algorithm1 uses strict inequalities that leave certain tied non-sibling states unchanged; the inspected Scala implementation treats equality as rejection and advances a candidate. Its behavior is not inferred from a literal transcription of that pseudocode.
  • supports Source: Scalable Hierarchical Clustering with Tree Grafting · Locator: Section3.2, Equation2 and Algorithm1, PDF pp.6–7
  • supports Source: GRINCH author implementation: src/scala/grinch/core/Grinch.scala · Locator: Grinch.graft, i_like_you/you_like_me and candidate-parent updates
Scope #restructuring-and-source-scope
The intended restructuring operation compares a subtree with siblings of its ancestors below a specified common ancestor, swapping when that improves the linkage. Strong connectivity requires every descendant subtree of a connected node to be connected; this differs from merely containing a complete cluster. The inspected Scala routine differs from Algorithm2: it scores with the original z as curr advances, does not use its computed r_ancs set to restrict candidates, and its node swap changes pointers without updating cached summaries. These static differences preclude assuming exact paper-to-code equivalence.
  • supports Source: Scalable Hierarchical Clustering with Tree Grafting · Locator: Section3.3, Algorithm2, PDF pp.6–8; Definitions3–4 and Lemma4, pp.19,22–23
  • supports Source: GRINCH author implementation: src/scala/grinch/core/Grinch.scala · Locator: Grinch.restructure
  • supports Source: GRINCH author implementation: src/scala/grinch/core/GrinchNode.scala · Locator: GrinchNode.swap, score and updatedFromChildren
Scope #approximation-and-control-flow
The paper considers capped operations, single-elimination grafting, reuse of an insertion-time neighbor search, and approximate navigable-small-world search. Such approximations do not automatically inherit the exact separation theorem. The pinned Scala caps use subtree point counts, whereas the paper describes height caps. Its graft_attempts variable stays zero and its positive-beam exit predicate is graft_attempts < graft_beam, causing exit after a completed outer iteration with the default positive beam. This is a static control-flow observation, not a measured clustering failure.
  • supports Source: Scalable Hierarchical Clustering with Tree Grafting · Locator: Section4.2, PDF pp.9–11
  • supports Source: GRINCH author implementation: src/scala/grinch/core/Grinch.scala · Locator: Grinch.graft, rotate, restructure and graft_cknn_search
  • supports Source: GRINCH author implementation: src/scala/grinch/core/Config.scala · Locator: GrinchConfigOpts and Config, graft_beam and size-cap defaults
Scope #implementation-and-evaluation-boundaries
The paper distinguishes dendrogram purity from pairwise F1 after flat-cluster extraction and evaluates both fixed and learned linkage functions. Its approximations and data-order experiments are empirical comparisons, not universal quality or speed rankings. The pinned author repository provides Scala implementations and experiment scripts, but that later source snapshot is not assumed to reproduce every historical result. The inspected average-link implementation averages negative squared Euclidean distances over stored points; its name alone does not establish an average of unsquared distances.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The exact recovery statement assumes model-based separation and intended exact operations. Arbitrary linkage scores, approximate searches and caps do not establish those conditions.
  • The printed Algorithm3 does not show an update to p in its final loop; Algorithm1 also leaves certain ties without progress. The record describes the intended method and preserves these source limitations.
  • Equation1 and Algorithm3 use different node roles for rotation. The insertion implementation ascends a candidate sibling before attaching the new leaf; variable names are not silently identified across descriptions.
  • The finite tied-score example is a standalone tree state, not an established insertion history or author execution. It does not refute the intended theorem or quantify clustering quality.
  • The pinned Scala source has explicit control-flow and restructuring differences. No full reachable counterexample or correctness certification of that implementation is claimed.
  • The 23-page arXiv version and complete cited files were read. The separate Python implementation, NSW and Ent internals, other revisions, datasets and historical benchmark runs were not audited or executed.
  • The record remains DRAFT. No model fitting, paid service, data upload or benchmark replication was performed.
