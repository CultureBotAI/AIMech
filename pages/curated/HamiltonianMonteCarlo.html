Aliases: HMC, Hybrid Monte Carlo
A Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm for differentiable continuous targets that augments positions with momenta, proposes a state through reversible volume-preserving numerical Hamiltonian dynamics, and applies a Metropolis correction for the Hamiltonian change.
Definition Evidence
  • supports Source: The No-U-Turn Sampler: Adaptively Setting Path Lengths in Hamiltonian Monte Carlo · Locator: Section 2 and Algorithm 1, printed pp. 1594–1596 / PDF pp. 2–4
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The record describes conventional Euclidean HMC with Gaussian momenta, leapfrog integration and an end-of-trajectory Metropolis test. The source’s unit-mass baseline is explicit; fixed positive-definite mass matrices are documented in the author technical chapter. HMC is not deterministic optimization, an uncorrected molecular-dynamics simulation, or NUTS’s adaptive tree-selection kernel. The original 1987 publication’s metadata was verified, but its full text was inaccessible in this session. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method
For target pi(theta), use potential U=−log pi up to a constant and Gaussian momentum r with kinetic energy K=r·r/2 in the unit-mass baseline. Refresh r independently at each iteration. Apply L leapfrog steps: half a momentum update using the log-density gradient, a full position update, and the remaining half momentum update. Form the reversible proposal with momentum reversal and accept with min(1,exp(H_start−H_proposed)); on rejection retain the current position. The integrator preserves volume and reversibility mathematically, while the Metropolis correction accounts for numerical integration error in the invariant target. The final momentum sign can be omitted when only positions are retained and momenta are fully refreshed before reuse.
  • supports Source: The No-U-Turn Sampler: Adaptively Setting Path Lengths in Hamiltonian Monte Carlo · Locator: Section 2 and Algorithm 1, printed pp. 1594–1596 / PDF pp. 2–4
Scope #author-method-and-limits
Neal’s author-posted technical chapter defines fixed Gaussian mass matrices, the full-refresh leapfrog/Metropolis algorithm, its invariant-distribution argument, and periodicity failures of ergodicity. It is a methodological review and implementation description, not the original 1987 invention paper.
  • supports Source: MCMC using Hamiltonian dynamics · Locator: Sections 2.1, 2.3 and 3.1–3.2, Equations (2.5)–(2.8), (2.28)–(2.30), (3.6), Figure 2 and Ergodicity of HMC, PDF/printed pp. 3–4, 8, 10–15

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The baseline needs evaluable log density and gradients on continuous coordinates. Discrete variables or constraints require additional handling; the stated algorithm does not directly solve those representation problems.
  • Step size, trajectory length and coordinate scaling materially affect behavior. Too-large steps can cause integration failure or rejection, and too-short or resonant trajectories can explore poorly.
  • Target invariance is not universal ergodicity or finite-sample convergence. Neal explicitly gives periodic trajectories that can cause nonergodicity.
  • The correction does not remove Monte Carlo error or make floating-point dynamics exactly reversible in every implementation. Rejected positions remain part of the chain.
  • No universal dimension-scaling law, tuned parameter value, benchmark superiority or current library default is asserted.
