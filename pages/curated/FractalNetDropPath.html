FractalNet DropPath

Algorithms AIMech:FractalNetDropPath DRAFT
A structured regularization procedure for fractal networks that trains shared parameters using locally selected nonempty subsets of join inputs or a single globally selected column, with elementwise averaging over the active join inputs.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Scope #fractal-host-and-procedure
The procedure operates on the parallel paths of a fractal network whose join layers combine compatible feature tensors. In the demonstrated construction, every join input comes from a preceding learned layer; none is designated as an identity pass-through or residual correction. The curated identity is the path-selection regularizer, not the entire FractalNet architecture or a particular trained column.
  • supports Source: FractalNet: Ultra-Deep Neural Networks without Residuals · Locator: Section 3, Equations (1)–(2), join definition and residual-network comparison, PDF p. 4; Section 3.1, PDF pp. 4–5.
Mechanism #local-nonempty-join-selection
In local mode, a join randomly drops its input operands using a fixed drop-probability setting while ensuring that at least one input survives. The selected objects are join inputs rather than independent samples from the set of all end-to-end paths. The surviving set must remain nonempty for the active-input mean to be defined; the paper does not specify the exact enforcement law.
  • supports Source: FractalNet: Ultra-Deep Neural Networks without Residuals · Locator: Section 3.1, local sampling bullet, PDF p. 4; Figure 2 caption and active-input rescaling paragraph, PDF p. 5.
Mechanism #global-single-column-selection
In global mode, select one column as the single active path through the entire network. This mode trains columns of different depths as usable stand-alone subnetworks. A network-wide column choice couples the active computation across its blocks; it is not a separate independent drop decision for every block. The source does not specify a probability distribution over the selected columns.
  • supports Source: FractalNet: Ultra-Deep Neural Networks without Residuals · Locator: Section 3.1, global sampling bullet, PDF p. 4; Figure 2 and the global-path discussion, PDF p. 5.
Mechanism #mean-over-active-inputs
Each evaluated join takes the elementwise mean of only its active inputs. For a nonempty surviving set S of compatible tensors h_i, this is J=(1/|S|)*sum_{i in S} h_i. The denominator is the realized number of active inputs, not a fixed keep probability. With one surviving input, the join returns that input.
  • supports Source: FractalNet: Ultra-Deep Neural Networks without Residuals · Locator: Section 3, elementwise join definition, PDF p. 4; Section 3.1, rescaling paragraph, PDF p. 5. The displayed finite-sum expression directly restates the active-input mean.
Mechanism #reported-minibatch-mixture
The reported experiments use a mixture with 50% local and 50% global sampling, drawing a new subnetwork for each minibatch. The local drop setting is 15% across the network, and ordinary dropout is also used in the stated regularized training recipe. These are reported configuration choices. The alternating local/global drawings in Figure 2 do not require strict deterministic alternation of the sampled modes.
  • supports Source: FractalNet: Ultra-Deep Neural Networks without Residuals · Locator: Section 3.1, mixture and minibatch paragraphs and Figure 2, PDF p. 5; Section 4.1, local drop rate, PDF p. 6.
Scope #full-graph-and-extracted-column-use
The trained fractal graph can be evaluated as a full network or used to extract a single trained column for stand-alone prediction. The source evaluates both choices and discusses a speed–accuracy trade-off across columns of different depths. This is a choice of prediction graph after shared training, not a claim that every column has equal quality or that the full prediction is the exact average of all sampled subnetworks.
  • supports Source: FractalNet: Ultra-Deep Neural Networks without Residuals · Locator: Section 3.1, final global-path discussion, PDF p. 5; Table 1, PDF p. 6; Table 4 and Section 4.2, PDF p. 7; Section 4.3, PDF p. 8.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The inspected paper does not specify how an empty local mask is prevented, whether by resampling, repair or another dependent rule. Independent drop draws at a nontrivial probability can remove all inputs, so independence of the final nonempty mask and exact repaired marginal probabilities must not be inferred. No particular implementation fallback is asserted.
  • The probability distribution over globally selected columns is not specified. The 50/50 mode mixture and 15% local drop setting describe reported experiments rather than universal constants. A constrained local join mask is not uniform sampling of all possible end-to-end paths, and the illustrative figure does not determine the full sampling law.
  • A mean over active features does not generally make subsequent nonlinear predictions or losses equal to an ensemble expectation. A hand illustration can use nonnegative join operands 0 and 2, uniform singleton selection, and a later affine map z−1 followed by ReLU: the mean output is 1/2, whereas applying that map to the full join mean gives 0. This is an illustrative permitted feature composition, not the paper's specified mask distribution, a training experiment or a contradiction of its claims.
  • Suppressing a path's current loss-gradient contribution does not necessarily freeze every parameter or state associated with it. Momentum, weight decay, running statistics or use elsewhere can still matter. No author-code audit of mask repair, batching, computation skipping or batch-normalization handling in extracted columns has been performed.
  • The reported regularized recipe also uses ordinary dropout, and the experiments vary architecture, augmentation and training conditions. No new numerical effect size, isolated causal attribution to one mode, universal speedup or benchmark reproduction is asserted. The student–teacher and deep-supervision explanation is presented as a hypothesis, not a convergence or generalization theorem.
  • This record follows the retained 2017 arXiv v4 paper, which identifies itself as an ICLR conference publication. Earlier preprints and the alternate author-hosted PDF whose download failed are not assumed byte-equivalent. The procedure does not require a residual identity skip, and the unqualified name DropPath is not assigned as an exact alias covering stochastic depth, NASNet Scheduled DropPath or later library functions.
  • The source mentions more general possible layer types, but its detailed experiments use specified convolutional hosts. Compatible join shapes and nonempty active input sets remain prerequisites. Reading all 11 retained text pages and selected visuals does not certify every implementation detail, training result or architecture outside that evidence scope.
