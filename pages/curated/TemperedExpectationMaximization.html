Aliases: Tempered EM
An entropy-tempered generalization of expectation maximization for finite latent-state models that alternates powered latent-joint responsibilities with weighted complete-log-likelihood parameter updates at a positive inverse temperature.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For observations x with multiplicities n_x, finite latent states z, model parameters eta and a positive inverse temperature beta, minimize F_beta(q,eta)=sum_x n_x*sum_z q_xz*[log q_xz-beta*log p_eta(x,z)]. For fixed eta, on positive joint support use q_xz=p_eta(x,z)^beta/sum_j p_eta(x,j)^beta. For fixed q and beta>0, minimizing F_beta over eta is equivalent to maximizing the expected complete log likelihood; in PLSA this gives the usual normalized expected-count M-step with the tempered responsibilities. At beta=1 the E-step is the ordinary posterior. With 0<beta<1 it is flatter on its positive support. Hofmann’s inverse-annealing schedule first runs early-stopped EM at beta=1, then reduces beta multiplicatively, continues iterations while held-out performance improves, and stops decreasing beta when further improvements cease. At fixed beta, exact block updates optimize that temperature’s free energy; changing beta changes the objective.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Responsibilities require a positive finite normalizer for each observed x. At positive beta, zero latent-joint entries remain zero; the beta-to-zero limit is uniform only over the positive support. beta=0 makes the displayed parameter objective independent of eta and is not included as an ordinary M-step.
  • This record concerns finite latent-state sums. Raising a continuous latent density to a power below one need not preserve integrability; that extension requires its own conditions.
  • Exact block updates concern fixed-beta free energy. They do not guarantee monotonic ordinary likelihood for beta different from one, global optimality, posterior calibration or held-out improvement. Changing beta does not optimize one unchanged objective.
  • The inverse-annealing schedule decreases beta after an ordinary-EM start and uses held-out decisions. It is not the same schedule as conventional deterministic-annealing cooling, and it is not a stochastic sampling transition with an invariant target. Schedule thresholds and multiplicative factors require an implementation choice.
  • The source’s perplexity and retrieval comparisons concern selected historical corpora and settings. No automatic prevention of overfitting, speedup, universal benefit or author-code reproduction is claimed.
