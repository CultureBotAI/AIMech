Support vector data description

Aliases: SVDD, Support vector domain description, L1-loss SVDD
A one-class support-estimation algorithm that learns a sphere in an input or kernel feature space by minimizing squared radius plus linearly penalized nonnegative distance-constraint violations, then accepts a query when its feature lies within or on the learned sphere.

Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record covers the target-only linear-slack method called support vector domain description in 1999 and support vector data description in 2004, with later qualifications to its optimization. L1 refers to the slack loss, not an L1 norm on the sphere center. The negative-example extension and squared-hinge-loss variant change the optimization and are not exact aliases. Sphere support estimation does not include SVC graph-component labeling or the general origin-separating one-class hyperplane formulation. Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #convex-formulation
For N target observations and C>0, use squared radius t>=0, center a and slacks xi_i>=0 to minimize t+C*sum_i xi_i subject to ||Phi(x_i)-a||²<=t+xi_i. This is a convex reformulation of the radius-variable problem and satisfies a strict-feasibility condition supporting strong duality. The objective penalizes violation magnitudes, not an exact count of rejected examples. The optimal center is unique, but the radius and slacks can have multiple optima.

Mechanism #dual-and-small-penalty
For C>1/N, the usual dual maximizes sum_i alpha_i*K_ii-alpha^T*K*alpha subject to sum_i alpha_i=1 and 0<=alpha_i<=C, and a=sum_i alpha_i*Phi(x_i). For 0<C<1/N this equality-simplex dual is infeasible even though the primal is feasible: its optimum has t=0 and the feature-space sample mean as center. At C=1/N the same zero-radius solution is optimal, but additional positive-radius optima may exist. Dividing the original radius stationarity equation by R silently omits R=0. For C>1 the method reduces to the minimum sphere enclosing all training features.
  • supports Source: A Revisit to Support Vector Data Description · Locator: Sections 2.3 and 3.2, PDF pp.5-11, Theorems 4 and 6, Equations 9-26; Appendices B and D, pp.16-17
Mechanism #radius-and-prediction
For C>1/N and an optimal dual solution, squared distances d_i² to the center bound an optimal t by max_{alpha_i<C} d_i² <= t <= min_{alpha_i>0} d_i². A free coefficient 0<alpha_i<C fixes the radius; otherwise choosing the interval midpoint is an implementation convention. Bounded coefficients need not be strictly outside the sphere. Accept a query when K(x,x)-2*sum_i alpha_i*K(x_i,x)+alpha^T*K*alpha<=t. For observations {-2,-1,1,2} with a linear kernel and C=1/2, a=0 and every t in [1,4] achieves objective 4; this illustrates different acceptance regions at the same optimum.

Mechanism #gaussian-and-boundary-scope
A Gaussian kernel exp(-||x-y||²/s²) gives a weighted kernel-score acceptance region without explicitly constructing features. It can produce a non-spherical region in input space. The narrow-width equal-weight limit assumes distinct training inputs; the exact constant-kernel limit is degenerate and does not select a unique coefficient vector. Comparisons with one-class hyperplane support estimation require the stated kernel/normalization conditions rather than general method identity. The boundary estimate is not by itself a normalized density model or a component-labeling rule.

Limitation #support-count-and-error
The papers use support-vector fractions to guide target-error and kernel-width choices. The 2004 account distinguishes essential from nonessential vectors and gives a leave-one-out bound/estimate; the raw support fraction is not universally equal to observed leave-one-out rejection or a calibrated future error rate. With two identical training features and C=2, a valid two-support-vector representation has radius zero, while either leave-one-out fit accepts the omitted duplicate. For the nonnegative equality-simplex coefficients the fraction at the upper bound is at most 1/(N*C); bounded coefficients can be on the boundary, and this count bound cannot be transferred unchanged to signed negative-example coefficients.

Limitation #empirical-and-extension-boundaries
The published tests depend on target/negative sampling, feature dimension, preprocessing, kernel width and target-rejection settings. The 2004 paper reports failures when a pump training set covers too little operating range and cautions that overlapping negative examples can worsen its separate negative-example extension. Its reverse distance constraints are not the convex target-only formulation above. These studies do not establish universal performance superiority or immunity to distribution changes.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Kernel, preprocessing, regularization and decision-threshold conventions affect the acceptance region. The method does not guarantee a calibrated future outlier probability, discovery of all abnormal cases, or optimal cluster labels.
  • The later mathematical reassessment is an author-hosted technical manuscript with conflicting author-index year labels. Exact URL and hash preserve inspected evidence; journal publication and version equivalence are not asserted.
  • Exact finite calculations check mathematical edge cases. No historical implementation, training run or benchmark was executed.
  • All 9, 22 and 21 pages of the three cited PDFs were visually read. Equations with degenerate-radius assumptions, categorical multiplier descriptions and unqualified support-count interpretations are explicitly qualified. No new numerical benchmark result is asserted.
