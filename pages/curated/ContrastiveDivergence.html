Contrastive divergence

Algorithms AIMech:ContrastiveDivergence DRAFT
Aliases: CD, CD-k
A parameter-learning algorithm for energy-based or product-of-experts models that replaces the equilibrium negative-phase expectation by configurations obtained from a short model-invariant Markov chain restarted at observed data, without differentiating through the chain distribution.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the customary contrastive-divergence update family, including CD-1 and CD-k, rather than only the formal difference-of-KL-divergences objective that motivated it. The number of transition steps is an algorithm parameter, not a separate architecture. It differs from persistent-chain learning, CD-PercLoss prediction search and pairwise representation-learning losses. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method
For each observed example or minibatch, initialize a Markov chain at the data and perform k transitions with the model parameters held fixed. For an RBM, alternate sampled hidden and visible blocks, then compute hidden conditional expectations at the resulting visible configurations for the negative statistics. Update weights by a learning rate times observed-data minus k-step reconstructed visible-hidden statistics; update biases by the corresponding first-moment differences. More generally, contrast derivatives of the unnormalized log model at the data and chain endpoints, treating the endpoints as fixed for the update. The motivating objective is C_k(theta)=KL(q_data||p_theta)-KL(q_data T_theta^k||p_theta). Its exact derivative also contains the parameter dependence of the k-step distribution; the customary CD update omits that contribution. With a fixed ergodic kernel and sufficiently long chains, the negative expectation approaches the equilibrium expectation; a short-chain update does not assume that limit has been reached.

Limitation #nonconservative-update
For the noiseless binary-RBM CD-1 update, the inspected source gives a one-visible/one-hidden counterexample with hidden bias fixed at zero and data visible=0. At weight 1 and visible bias 0, the cross-partial difference is e*(e-1)*(e+3)/(8*(e+1)^3), which is nonzero. This rules out a universal scalar potential for that update field. Its constructed cycling regularizer and separate fixed-point results have narrower assumptions than a general convergence claim.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A short-chain CD estimate is generally biased for the likelihood gradient and is not generally the exact negative gradient of the formal contrastive-divergence objective. A small objective or accurate reconstruction can coexist with poor global modeling when the chain mixes slowly.
  • The binary-RBM CD-1 field can have unequal cross partial derivatives, so it is not universally the gradient of any scalar objective. The inspected convergence paper also constructs cycling with a particular nonconvex regularizer; this is not a claim that ordinary L2-regularized CD always cycles. Existence of a regularized fixed point does not establish convergence to it.
  • Exact Gibbs transitions require sampled states. Conditional expectations can replace final sufficient statistics by Rao–Blackwellization, but feeding probability-valued states into later nonlinear transition conditionals defines a different approximation. Hinton Section 8 explicitly uses such a probability-valued visible reconstruction in its digit experiment; that variant is not silently equated to exact binary Gibbs CD.
  • The original simulations do not prove universal likelihood improvement, unbiased learning or global convergence. Their digit classification experiments also disclose test-set model selection; no reported ranking is adopted as a general property.
  • The algorithm is not tied to an RBM architecture, and the formal objective is not merely a difference of mean log likelihoods: distribution-entropy terms matter. No author implementation, training run or complete convergence theorem is certified.
