Distributed Principal Eigenspace Estimation by Subspace Projection Averaging

Algorithms AIMech:DistributedPrincipalEigenspaceEstimationBySubspaceProjectionAveraging DRAFT
Aliases: Distributed PCA by Subspace Projection Averaging, Fan-Wang-Wang-Zhu Distributed Principal Eigenspace Estimator, One-Shot Distributed Principal Eigenspace Estimation, Least-Squares Subspace Consensus Distributed PCA, High-Order Davis-Kahan Distributed Eigenspace Perturbation
A one-shot divide-and-conquer distributed principal component analysis algorithm and second-order subspace perturbation theory (introduced by Jianqing Fan, Dong Wang, Kaizheng Wang, and Ziwei Zhu in 2019). When $N = m n$ independent $d$-dimensional observations are partitioned across $m$ machines of local sample size $n$, each machine $\ell \in [m]$ computes the top-$K$ orthonormal eigenvector matrix $\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)} \in \mathcal{O}_{d \times K}$ of its local sample covariance matrix $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{(\ell)}$ and transmits $\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)}$ to a central server with $O(m K d)$ communication cost; the central server forms the averaged projection matrix $\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}} = m^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^m \widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)}\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)T}$ and outputs its top-$K$ eigenvectors $\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_K \in \mathcal{O}_{d \times K}$ (Algorithm 1, Equations 3.1–3.2), which solves the least-squares subspace consensus problem $\arg\min_{\mathbf{U} \in \mathcal{O}_{d \times K}}\sum_{\ell=1}^m \rho^2(\mathbf{U}, \widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)})$ where $\rho(\mathbf{U}, \mathbf{V}) = \|\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^T - \mathbf{V}\mathbf{V}^T\|_F$ (Lemmas 5 and 6). Using a bias–variance subspace decomposition $\rho(\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_K, \mathbf{V}_K) \le \rho(\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_K, \mathbf{V}_K^*) + \rho(\mathbf{V}_K^*, \mathbf{V}_K)$ where $\mathbf{V}_K^*$ is the top-$K$ eigenspace of $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^* = \mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)}\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)T}]$ (Lemma 1), the framework proves that (1) the variance term scales as $O(\kappa\sqrt{K r / N})$ in sub-exponential $\psi_1$-norm where $r = \operatorname{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma})/\lambda_1$ is the effective rank and $\kappa = \lambda_1/\Delta$ (Theorem 1); (2) under reflection-symmetric innovation distributions $\mathbf{Z} = \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-1/2}\mathbf{V}^T\mathbf{X} \stackrel{d}{=} (\mathbf{I}_d - 2\mathbf{e}_j\mathbf{e}_j^T)\mathbf{Z}$ (including Gaussian and elliptical distributions), $\mathbf{V}^T\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^*\mathbf{V}$ is diagonal so the bias $\rho(\mathbf{V}_K^*, \mathbf{V}_K) = 0$ vanishes identically (Definition 4.1, Theorem 2); and (3) for general asymmetric sub-Gaussian distributions, a deterministic first-order projection expansion $\widehat{\mathbf{U}}\widehat{\mathbf{U}}^T - \mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^T \approx f(\mathbf{E}\mathbf{U})\mathbf{U}^T + \mathbf{U}f(\mathbf{E}\mathbf{U})^T$ with quadratic residual $\le 24\sqrt{K}\varepsilon^2$ (Lemma 2) cancels the first-order zero-mean noise in expectation to yield $\rho(\mathbf{V}_K^*, \mathbf{V}_K) \lesssim \kappa^2\sqrt{K}r/n$ (Theorem 3), matching the centralized full-sample PCA rate $O(\kappa\sqrt{K r / N})$ whenever $m \lesssim n / (\kappa^2 r)$ (Theorem 4).

Distinct from `AIMech:EntrywiseFirstOrderEigenvectorApproximationAndLeaveOneOutPerturbationAnalysisForLowExpectedRankRandomMatrices` (Abbe, Fan, Wang, & Zhong 2020, which analyzes entrywise $\ell_\infty$ eigenvector perturbations of a single random matrix via leave-one-out row decoupling rather than distributed subspace projection averaging $\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}} = m^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^m \widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)}\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)T}$ across $m$ machines), from `AIMech:LInfinityEigenvectorPerturbationBoundAndRobustFactorCovarianceEstimation` (Fan, Wang, & Zhong 2018, which bounds $\ell_\infty$ eigenvector perturbations and estimates heavy-tailed factor covariances on a single machine), and from `AIMech:PrincipalOrthogonalComplementThresholdingForApproximateFactorCovarianceEstimation` (Fan, Liao, & Mincheva 2013, which thresholds centralized PCA residuals).

Mechanism #claim-subspace-projection-averaging-consensus-and-variance-bound
Given $m$ local top-$K$ eigenvector matrices $\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)} \in \mathcal{O}_{d \times K}$ ($\ell=1,\dots,m$), Lemma 6 proves that the top-$K$ eigenvector matrix $\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_K$ of the averaged projection matrix $\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}} = m^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^m \widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)}\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)T}$ minimizes $\sum_{\ell=1}^m \rho^2(\mathbf{U}, \widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)}) = 2mK - 2m\operatorname{Tr}(\mathbf{U}^T\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\mathbf{U})$ over $\mathbf{U} \in \mathcal{O}_{d \times K}$, and under sub-Gaussian observations with effective rank $r = \operatorname{Tr}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma})/\lambda_1$ and $\kappa = \lambda_1/\Delta$ ($\Delta = \lambda_K - \lambda_{K+1}$), Theorem 1 proves that the sample variance component relative to the population mean projection eigenspace $\mathbf{V}_K^*$ of $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^* = \mathbb{E}[\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)}\widehat{\mathbf{V}}_K^{(\ell)T}]$ satisfies $\|\rho(\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_K, \mathbf{V}_K^*)\|_{\psi_1} \le C_1\kappa\sqrt{K r / N}$ where $N = mn$ (Sections 3 and 4.1, Lemmas 1, 5, 6, and Theorem 1).

Mechanism #claim-symmetric-innovation-exact-unbiasedness-and-asymmetric-bias-rate
When the standardized innovation $\mathbf{Z} = \boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-1/2}\mathbf{V}^T\mathbf{X}$ satisfies reflection symmetry $\mathbf{Z} \stackrel{d}{=} (\mathbf{I}_d - 2\mathbf{e}_j\mathbf{e}_j^T)\mathbf{Z}$ for all $j \in [d]$ (Definition 4.1), Theorem 2 proves that $\mathbf{V}^T\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^*\mathbf{V}$ is diagonal so $\rho(\mathbf{V}_K^*, \mathbf{V}_K) = 0$ whenever $\|\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^* - \mathbf{V}_K\mathbf{V}_K^T\|_2 < 1/2$; for general asymmetric sub-Gaussian distributions, Lemma 2 establishes the deterministic linear projection expansion $\|\widehat{\mathbf{U}}\widehat{\mathbf{U}}^T - \mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^T - (f(\mathbf{E}\mathbf{U})\mathbf{U}^T + \mathbf{U}f(\mathbf{E}\mathbf{U})^T)\|_F \le 24\sqrt{K}(\|\mathbf{E}\|_2/\Delta)^2$ where $f(\mathbf{A}) = \sum_{j=K+1}^d \mathbf{u}_j\mathbf{u}_j^T\mathbf{A}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_1 - \lambda_j\mathbf{I}_K)^{-1}$, whose linear term has zero expectation under $\mathbb{E}\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{0}$, proving $\rho(\mathbf{V}_K^*, \mathbf{V}_K) \le C_2\kappa^2\sqrt{K}r/n$ (Theorem 3) and $\|\rho(\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_K, \mathbf{V}_K)\|_{\psi_1} \lesssim \sqrt{K}(\kappa\sqrt{r/N} + \kappa^2 r/n)$ (Theorem 4).

Scope #claim-phase-transition-impossibility-and-heterogeneous-factor-extensions
In a spiked covariance model $\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = \lambda\mathbf{v}_1\mathbf{v}_1^T + \mathbf{I}_d$, Theorem 5 proves a sharp phase transition: if the local sample size falls below the Baik–Ben Arous–Péché threshold $n \le (d-1)/(3\lambda)$, then $\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_1 \perp \mathbf{V}_1) \ge 1 - d^{-1}$ regardless of how large the number of machines $m$ is; above the threshold, Theorems 6 and 7 extend distributed subspace projection averaging to heterogeneous local covariances $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{(\ell)}$ sharing a common top-$K$ eigenspace and to high-dimensional approximate factor models $\mathbf{X}_i^{(\ell)} = \mathbf{B}\mathbf{f}_i^{(\ell)} + \mathbf{u}_i^{(\ell)}$ achieving rate $O((\sqrt{1/N} + 1/n)(1 + \sqrt{d}/\psi_K) + \sqrt{d}/(\sqrt{N}\psi_K^2))$ (Sections 4.2 and 5, Theorems 5, 6, and 7).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • For general asymmetric sub-Gaussian distributions, matching the centralized full-sample PCA rate O(kappa * sqrt(K * r / N)) requires the machine-count scaling constraint m <= O(n / (kappa^2 * r)) so that the O(kappa^2 * sqrt(K) * r / n) second-order bias does not dominate the O(kappa * sqrt(K * r / N)) variance (Theorem 4); if the data are partitioned into too many small local shards (n <= (d-1)/(3*lambda)), Theorem 5 shows that local sample eigenvectors become asymptotically orthogonal to the true spike and one-shot projection averaging fails regardless of m.
  • Because Algorithm 1 averages projection matrices V_hat_K^{(ell)} V_hat_K^{(ell)T} rather than raw sample covariance matrices, it requires a positive eigengap Delta = lambda_K - lambda_{K+1} > 0 separating the K-th and (K+1)-th population eigenvalues on each local machine; if lambda_K = lambda_{K+1}, the K-dimensional principal subspace is not uniquely defined.
  • Primary-source typographical audit of Fan, Wang, Wang, & Zhu (2019, arXiv:1702.06488v4) uncovered five groups of minor notation and typographical errors that are corrected in this record: (1) Section 1 p. 4 line 5 writes 'V_tilde_K = (v_tilde_1^{(ell)}, ..., v_tilde_K^{(ell)})' with a stray machine superscript '(ell)' on the central aggregated eigenvectors; (2) Section 2 p. 5 line 9 writes 'be the effected rank of Sigma' ('effected' instead of 'effective') and line 13 spells 'top K engenspace Col(V_hat_K)' ('engenspace' instead of 'eigenspace'); (3) Section 3 p. 7 line 2 writes 'We specify the distributed in Algorithm 1' (missing 'estimator' or 'procedure') and p. 8 line 11 writes 'Then the central server collect all the eigenvalues' ('collect' instead of 'collects'); (4) Section 4.1 p. 9 line 17 writes 'so does rho(V_tilde_K, V_K^*)' and p. 10 line 9 writes '{V_hat_K^{(ell)} V_hat_K^{(ell)T}}_{ell=1}^K' with upper index 'K' instead of the number of machines 'm'; and (5) Section 4.2 p. 13 line 9 writes 'controls it tail' ('it' instead of 'its'), Section 6.1 p. 21 line 12 writes 'is proportion to' ('proportion' instead of 'proportional'), and Section 8.2.2 Lemma 6 p. 41 line 2 spells 'eigen-deconposition'.
