Manifold Mixup

Algorithms AIMech:ManifoldMixup DRAFT
A neural-network training procedure that interpolates paired examples’ representations at a selected network layer and interpolates their labels with the same coefficient, then trains the entire network on the mixed representation-target pairs.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the end-to-end hidden-representation extension of input MixUp, not an alias for MixUp’s input-only case, interpolation of fixed extracted features, or a separate trained model. It uses observed labels, rather than ICT’s mixed teacher predictions on unlabeled inputs. Its name does not guarantee that interpolated representations lie on a true data manifold. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Select a layer k from an eligible set S, which may include the input layer. Forward a minibatch and its randomly shuffled copy to layer k. Draw lambda from Beta(a,a), a>0, and mix both the paired layer representations and the paired one-hot targets using lambda and 1−lambda. Continue the forward pass from the mixed representation and compute the prediction loss against the mixed soft target. Backpropagate through the whole graph, including the network before the mixing layer, so the representations themselves can adapt. The reported implementation choice uses one layer and one coefficient per minibatch. With only the input layer eligible, S={0}, the objective reduces to original input MixUp; allowing hidden layers is the distinguishing extension. The objective averages over examples, mixing coefficients and eligible layers.
  • supports Source: Manifold Mixup: Better Representations by Interpolating Hidden States · Locator: Section 2 and Equation (1), PDF p. 3; theoretical scope in Sections 3.1–3.2, PDF pp. 4–5

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Representations being mixed must be compatible at the selected layer. The eligible layers and mixing distribution remain choices.
  • The source’s flattening theorem is framed under idealized function-class, dimensionality and minimization conditions; it does not guarantee the same geometry for every finite trained network.
  • Interpolated labels impose an inductive bias and need not equal the true conditional targets between examples.
  • The paper explicitly distinguishes single-step adversarial results from full adversarial robustness. No empirical robustness or accuracy claim is imported.
