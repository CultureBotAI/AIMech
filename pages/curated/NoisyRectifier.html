Noisy rectifier

Mathematical Methods AIMech:NoisyRectifier DRAFT
A stochastic activation that adds zero-mean noise to a preactivation and then applies the positive-part rectifier, producing h=max(0,a+z).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record covers the noisy rectifier described in Bengio et al. Section 2.1, with standard logistic noise as the analytically characterized special case. It is a stochastic activation, distinct from deterministic ReLU or softplus, input-only corruption, weight-noise dense layers and multiplicative dropout. The paper credits earlier rectifier work; this record does not claim that the 2013 paper invented noisy rectification. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a preactivation a, sample zero-mean additive noise z and output max(0,a+z). With the source’s standard logistic density sigma(z)*(1-sigma(z)), the probability of a positive output is sigma(a), and the conditional mean output is log(1+exp(a)). These identities are specific to that logistic-noise choice, not arbitrary zero-mean noise. Holding a sampled noise value fixed, the derivative with respect to a is 1 on the positive side and 0 on the negative side, with a nondifferentiable boundary at a+z=0. Noise can occasionally turn an otherwise inactive unit on or an active unit off. The source also suggests adjusting a unit bias when a moving activity rate falls below a chosen target; that heuristic is additional to the forward activation rule.
  • supports Source: Estimating or Propagating Gradients Through Stochastic Neurons for Conditional Computation · Locator: Section 2.1, noisy-rectifier definition and Proposition 1 with proof, PDF/printed pp.3–4

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Equality of the logistic-noise mean to softplus does not make a sampled noisy rectifier a deterministic softplus unit. Passing the mean through a nonlinear downstream loss does not generally preserve the expected loss.
  • The standard logistic activation and mean formulas change with the noise distribution or scale. Gaussian noise is mentioned as a practical alternative, but it does not inherit the displayed logistic identities.
  • Occasional activation permits gradient flow for some samples but does not guarantee that inactive units recover promptly, that every derivative-through-expectation interchange is valid, or that conditional computation saves wall-clock time.
  • No empirical speed, accuracy or sparsity advantage is asserted. The original authors’ implementation and later noisy-activation variants were not audited.
