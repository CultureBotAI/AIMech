Restricted Eigenvalue Properties for Correlated Gaussian Designs

Mathematical Methods AIMech:RestrictedEigenvaluePropertiesForCorrelatedGaussianDesigns DRAFT
Aliases: Restricted Eigenvalue Properties for Correlated Gaussian Designs (Raskutti, Wainwright, & Yu 2010), Gordon-Slepian Restricted Eigenvalue Lower Bound for Correlated Gaussian Random Designs, Raskutti-Wainwright-Yu Restricted Eigenvalue Transfer Theorem for Correlated Gaussian Ensembles, Restricted Nullspace and Restricted Eigenvalue Guarantee for Correlated Gaussian Designs
Restricted Eigenvalue Properties for Correlated Gaussian Designs is a high-dimensional random matrix and sparse recovery analysis framework introduced by Raskutti, Wainwright, and Yu (2010, Journal of Machine Learning Research 11:2241-2259) that establishes high-probability uniform lower bounds on the empirical quadratic form ||X v||_2 / sqrt(n) of n x p design matrices X with independent rows X_i ~ N(0, Sigma) having arbitrary correlation structure Sigma. Using Gordon's Gaussian comparison inequality on the doubly-indexed min-max Gaussian process Y_{u, v} = u^T W Sigma^{1/2} v over S^{n-1} x V(r) (Equations 5-6, Lemma 1), Lipschitz concentration of Gaussian measure in Frobenius norm (Lemma 2), and geometric peeling over dyadic l_1 shells (Section 4.4, Lemma 3), Theorem 1 (Equation 3) proves that with probability at least 1 - c' exp(-c n), ||X v||_2 / sqrt(n) >= (1/4) ||Sigma^{1/2} v||_2 - 9 rho(Sigma) sqrt((log p)/n) ||v||_1 simultaneously for all v in R^p, where rho^2(Sigma) := max_{j=1,...,p} Sigma_{jj}. Consequently, Corollary 1 (Equation 4) proves that whenever the population covariance Sigma satisfies the Restricted Eigenvalue (RE) condition of order k with parameters (alpha, gamma) on the cone C(S; alpha) := {theta in R^p : ||theta_{S^c}||_1 <= alpha ||theta_S||_1} (|S| = k) and n > c'' rho^2(Sigma) (1 + alpha)^2 k (log p) / gamma^2, the sample covariance matrix Sigma_hat = X^T X / n satisfies the RE condition of order k with parameters (alpha, gamma / 8) (and hence the Restricted Nullspace property when alpha >= 1) with probability at least 1 - c' exp(-c n), improving entrywise l_infinity coherence bounds from n = Omega(k^2 log p) to the minimax-optimal linear scaling n = Omega(k log p) while encompassing strongly correlated Toeplitz, spiked-identity, and rank-degenerate covariance structures that violate the Restricted Isometry Property (RIP).

Mechanism #uniform-empirical-l2-lower-bound-and-restricted-eigenvalue-transfer-theorem
For any n x p Gaussian random design matrix X with independent rows X_i ~ N(0, Sigma) and maximal diagonal variance rho^2(Sigma) := max_{j=1,...,p} Sigma_{jj}, Theorem 1 (Section 3.1, p. 2246, Equation 3) proves that there exist universal positive constants c, c' such that with probability at least 1 - c' exp(-c n), ||X v||_2 / sqrt(n) >= (1/4) ||Sigma^{1/2} v||_2 - 9 rho(Sigma) sqrt((log p)/n) ||v||_1 simultaneously for all v in R^p. Consequently, Corollary 1 (p. 2246, Equation 4) shows that if the population covariance Sigma satisfies the Restricted Eigenvalue (RE) condition of order k with parameters (alpha, gamma) on the cone C(S; alpha) := {theta in R^p : ||theta_{S^c}||_1 <= alpha ||theta_S||_1} (meaning ||Sigma^{1/2} theta||_2 >= gamma ||theta||_2 for all theta in cup_{|S|=k} C(S; alpha)) and the sample size satisfies n > c'' rho^2(Sigma) (1 + alpha)^2 k (log p) / gamma^2, then because ||theta||_1 <= (1 + alpha) sqrt(k) ||theta||_2 on C(S; alpha), the empirical design X satisfies ||X theta||_2 / sqrt(n) >= (gamma / 8) ||theta||_2 for all theta in cup_{|S|=k} C(S; alpha) (the RE condition of order k with parameters (alpha, gamma / 8), and the Restricted Nullspace property C(S; 1) cap null(X) = {0} when alpha >= 1) with probability at least 1 - c' exp(-c n), reducing the sample complexity required by entrywise l_infinity bounds ||X^T X / n - Sigma||_infty = O(sqrt((log p)/n)) (Section 3.2, pp. 2247-2248) from n = Omega(k^2 log p) to the minimax-optimal linear scaling n = Omega(k log p).

Scope #separation-from-restricted-isometry-and-rank-degenerate-covariance-structures
Section 3.3 (pp. 2248-2250, Examples 1-3) proves that the Restricted Eigenvalue condition on Sigma is strictly weaker than both the Restricted Isometry Property (RIP) and full-rank positive definiteness: (1) for Toeplitz covariance matrices Sigma_{ij} = a^{|i-j|} with a in [0, 1) (Example 1) and spiked identity covariance matrices Sigma = (1 - a) I_{p x p} + a 1 1^T with a in (0, 1) (Example 2), every k x k principal submatrix Sigma_{SS} of the spiked covariance has minimum eigenvalue lambda_min(Sigma_{SS}) = 1 - a and maximum eigenvalue lambda_max(Sigma_{SS}) = 1 + a(k - 1), so the empirical submatrix condition number lambda_max(Sigma_hat_{SS}) / lambda_min(Sigma_hat_{SS}) >= (1/4) (1 + a(k - 1)) / (1 - a) diverges as k -> infinity (violating the RIP bounded-condition-number requirement with high probability), whereas Sigma has bounded minimum eigenvalue 1 - a > 0 and rho^2(Sigma) = 1 so Corollary 1 guarantees RE of order k for n = Omega(k log p); and (2) Example 3 (p. 2250) shows that setting Sigma_deg = Sigma_hat = X^T X / n for an initial sample of size n < p yields a population covariance matrix with p - n zero eigenvalues (a (p - n)-dimensional nullspace) that nonetheless satisfies the RE condition of order k, so resampled designs X_tilde_i ~ N(0, Sigma_deg) satisfy RE of order k for n = Omega(k log p).

Mechanism #gordon-slepian-minmax-comparison-lipschitz-concentration-and-dyadic-l1-peeling
Writing X = W Sigma^{1/2} with standard normal W in R^{n x p} and rescaling by ||Sigma^{1/2} v||_2 = 1 onto V(r) := {v in R^p : ||Sigma^{1/2} v||_2 = 1, ||v||_1 <= r}, Section 4.1 (p. 2251, Equation 5) expresses M(r, X) := 1 - inf_{v in V(r)} ||X v||_2 / sqrt(n) = 1 + (1/sqrt(n)) sup_{v in V(r)} inf_{u in S^{n-1}} u^T W Sigma^{1/2} v and controls it in three steps: (1) Lemma 1 (Section 4.2, pp. 2251-2253) compares Y_{u, v} = u^T W v_tilde (v_tilde = Sigma^{1/2} v in S^{p-1}) against the separable Gaussian process Z_{u, v} = g^T u + h^T Sigma^{1/2} v by verifying Equation (6), E[(Y_{u, v} - Y_{u', v'})^2] = |||u v_tilde^T - u' (v_tilde')^T|||_F^2 = ||u - u'||_2^2 + ||v_tilde - v_tilde'||_2^2 - 2(1 - u^T u')(1 - v_tilde^T v_tilde') <= E[(Z_{u, v} - Z_{u', v'})^2] with equality when v = v', yielding via Gordon's inequality E[M(r, X)] <= t(r) := 1/4 + 3 rho(Sigma) sqrt((log p)/n) r for n >= 19; (2) Lemma 2 (Section 4.3, pp. 2253-2254) proves that h(W) := M(r, X) - 1 is (1/sqrt(n))-Lipschitz in W under the Frobenius norm because |u^T (W - W') v_tilde| <= |||u v_tilde^T|||_F |||W - W'|||_F = |||W - W'|||_F, giving P[M(r, X) >= (3/2) t(r)] <= 2 exp(-n t^2(r) / 8); and (3) Section 4.4 and Lemma 3 (pp. 2254-2256, Appendix A) partition V = {v : ||Sigma^{1/2} v||_2 = 1} into dyadic shells A_m = {v in A : 2^{m-1} mu <= g(h(v)) <= 2^m mu} with g(r) = (3/2) t(r) >= mu = 3/8 and sum the tail probabilities sum_{m=1}^infty 2 exp(-c n 2^{2m} mu^2) <= 2 exp(-4 c n mu^2) / (1 - exp(-4 c n mu^2)) to establish Theorem 1 uniformly over all r > 0.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Gaussian Design Distribution Requirement: Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 rely on the rows of X being drawn from a multivariate Gaussian distribution N(0, Sigma) so that Gordon's Gaussian comparison inequality (Lemma 1) and Gaussian concentration of Lipschitz functions (Lemma 2) apply directly; as noted in Section 5 (p. 2255), extending sharp n = Omega(k log p) Restricted Eigenvalue guarantees to general non-Gaussian or heavy-tailed correlated designs requires different small-ball or multiplier-process arguments.
  • Conservative Universal Constants: The prefactor 1/4 in Theorem 1 (and gamma / 8 in Corollary 1) together with the l_1 penalty multiplier 9 rho(Sigma) are chosen for analytical transparency of the three-step Gordon comparison and dyadic peeling proof rather than sharp numerical constants.
  • Primary-Source Typographical Notes in Raskutti, Wainwright, & Yu (2010, JMLR 11:2241-2259): Verified four self-correcting typographical details during full-text inspection: (1) Section 2.1 (p. 2244, Equation 2) prints the basis pursuit objective as beta_hat in arg min_{beta_hat in R^p} ||beta||_1 with a hat on the minimization variable beta_hat under arg min; (2) Section 3.1 (p. 2247, Remark (a)) prints the Restricted Nullspace condition as C(S, 1) cap (X) = {0}, omitting the word null before (X) (i.e., C(S; 1) cap null(X) = {0}); (3) Section 4.2 (p. 2251, bottom display) writes E[M(r, X)] = 1 + E[sup_{v in V(r)} inf_{u in S^{n-1}} Y_{u, v}] <= 1 + E[sup_{v in V(r)} inf_{u in S^{n-1}} Z_{u, v}], omitting the factor 1/sqrt(n) before both expectations (which is present in Equation 5 on p. 2251 and restored on p. 2253 when dividing by sqrt(n)); and (4) Section 4.4 (pp. 2254-2255) vs. Appendix A (p. 2256, Lemma 3) defines the bad event T on p. 2254 with f(v; X) >= g(||v||_1) whereas Lemma 3 (p. 2256) bounds E := {exists v in A s.t. f(v; X) >= 2 g(h(v))} with factor 2 g(h(v)) = 3 t(||v||_1), which matches the top of p. 2255 writing 1 - ||X v||_2 / sqrt(n) <= 3 t(||v||_1) = 3/4 + 9 rho(Sigma) sqrt((log p)/n) ||v||_1 and Theorem 1's constants 1 - 3/4 = 1/4 and 9.
  • All 19 pages of Raskutti, Wainwright, & Yu (2010, JMLR 11:2241-2259) were read.
