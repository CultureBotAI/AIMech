Supervised Principal Components

Algorithms AIMech:SupervisedPrincipalComponents DRAFT
Aliases: Supervised Principal Components Regression
A prediction procedure that screens features by their marginal association with an outcome, computes principal components of the retained feature matrix, and uses those components in an outcome regression.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the Bair-Hastie-Paul-Tibshirani screening/PCA/regression procedure. It is distinct from ordinary principal-component regression, sparse PCA, PPCA, partial least squares, supervised gene shaving, and other objectives also called supervised PCA. The ambiguous acronym SPCA is searched but not asserted as an alias. Unlabeled observations are optional in a proposed extension, not required by the base procedure. Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #screen-then-project
For centered training feature columns x_j and response y, the linear-response version scores each nonzero column by s_j=x_j^T y/||x_j|| and retains those with |s_j| greater than a threshold. It computes an SVD of the retained matrix and regresses the outcome on its first or first few left singular vectors. The threshold and component count are tuning choices. Outcome-dependent selection makes the entire fitting procedure nonlinear in the training responses, even though the fitted linear-response predictor is linear in new features.
  • supports Source: Prediction by Supervised Principal Components · Locator: Section 2.1, Equations 1-7, pp. 120-121 / PDF pp. 2-3; Section 6.1, p. 125 / PDF p. 7
Mechanism #new-sample-prediction
With retained training SVD X_theta=U D V^T, use weights V D^(-1) for nonzero retained singular values to project new observations centered by the training feature means. For one unit-norm component u, the training linear fit is ybar+(u^T y)u; the same fitted outcome coefficient multiplies the new projected score. The new observation does not need an outcome to obtain its prediction. Generalized-response versions replace the marginal score and outcome regression appropriately, including score-statistic screening and proportional-hazards regression for survival data.
  • supports Source: Prediction by Supervised Principal Components · Locator: Section 2.1, Equations 4-8 and test-vector instructions, pp. 120-122 / PDF pp. 2-4; curator nonzero-singular-value qualification
Limitation #screening-and-consistency-limits
The consistency analysis assumes a specified Gaussian latent-factor structure, identifiable separated signal directions, suitable relevant-dimension and signal-to-noise scaling, and adequate recovery of the relevant coordinates. With multiple factors, cancellation can make a relevant feature marginally uncorrelated with the outcome. The paper does not prove that cross-validation selects the correct feature set and notes substantial variability in small samples. Its detailed analysis also distinguishes noise-corrected latent regression coefficients from attenuated uncorrected component coefficients at fixed nonzero noise.
  • supports Source: Prediction by Supervised Principal Components · Locator: Section 9, Theorems 1-2 and Equations 36-45, pp. 130-134 / PDF pp. 12-16; Section 11, p. 135 / PDF p. 17; curator jointly-informative zero-marginal-covariance example
Scope #extensions-and-comparisons
An optional unlabeled-data extension selects features using labeled observations and their outcomes, then estimates components from those selected columns in labeled plus unlabeled observations. This differs from repeatedly selecting all features by correlation with the current component, which loses the original outcome supervision. The procedure does not guarantee better prediction than ridge, partial least squares or other regression methods. The historical microarray examples do not establish clinical validity.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The retained feature set may be empty, columns may have zero norm, and singular values may vanish. A usable implementation must handle these cases; inverses and component formulas do not establish full rank automatically.
  • Marginally unassociated but jointly predictive variables can be missed; marginal associations can also select conditionally irrelevant variables. Feature scaling, threshold selection, component count and the response model affect the result.
  • The fitted latent components need not identify causal biological processes. Survival and classification extensions have their own outcome assumptions; the source acknowledges incomplete theoretical justification for its Cox setting.
  • All 19 primary pages were read. Finite orthogonal-vector calculations check selection, projection and a marginal-screening failure. Historical microarray results and software were not reproduced.
