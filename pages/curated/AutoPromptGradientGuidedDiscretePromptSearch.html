AutoPrompt Gradient-Guided Discrete Prompt Search

Algorithms AIMech:AutoPromptGradientGuidedDiscretePromptSearch DRAFT
Aliases: AutoPrompt, AutoPrompt Gradient-Guided Prompt Generation, Gradient-Guided Discrete Trigger Prompt Search
An automated discrete prompt construction and knowledge-probing algorithm for pretrained language models that maps task inputs x_inp into a cloze template containing a prediction token [MASK] and shared discrete trigger tokens x_trig, automatically selects class-associated label token sets V_y via a logistic classifier over [MASK] contextual representations scored against output word embeddings w_out, and iteratively updates each trigger token x_trig^(j) by evaluating the top-k vocabulary candidates V_cand = top-k_{w in V} [w_in^T grad_{x_trig^(j)} log p(y|x_prompt)] under a first-order gradient approximation of the marginalized label log-likelihood p(y|x_prompt) = sum_{w in V_y} p([MASK]=w|x_prompt) while keeping all language model weights frozen.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from continuous soft prompt methods such as Prompt Tuning (AIMech:PromptTuning), Prefix-Tuning (AIMech:PrefixTuning), and WARP (AIMech:WARPWordLevelAdversarialReProgramming), which optimize continuous vectors in embedding space, and from manual cloze prompting (LAMA, Petroni et al., 2019) or corpus-mined/paraphrased prompting (LPAQA, Jiang et al., 2020), which do not perform gradient-guided discrete trigger token search.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #r718-claim-gradient-trigger-search
AutoPrompt initializes shared trigger tokens [T] to [MASK] and at each step approximates the log-likelihood change of swapping the j-th trigger token x_trig^(j) with vocabulary token w in V via the inner product w_in^T grad_{x_trig^(j)} log p(y|x_prompt), evaluating the top-k candidates V_cand with k forward passes to retain the trigger replacement that maximizes marginalized label likelihood.
  • supports Source: AutoPrompt: Eliciting Knowledge from Language Models with Automatically Generated Prompts · Locator: Section 2.1-2.2, Equations 1-2, p. 3
Mechanism #r718-claim-automated-label-token-selection
For classification tasks whose labels do not directly map to single vocabulary words, AutoPrompt trains a logistic classifier p(y|h^(i)) proportional to exp(h^(i) * y + beta_y) on the [MASK] contextualized representation h^(i) and substitutes the MLM output word embeddings w_out to select the top-k scoring words V_y = top-k_{w in V} [exp(w_out * y + beta_y)] as class label tokens.
  • supports Source: AutoPrompt: Eliciting Knowledge from Language Models with Automatically Generated Prompts · Locator: Section 2.3, Equations 3-5, pp. 3-4
Application #r718-claim-probing-and-low-data-application
Without fine-tuning any model weights, AutoPrompt achieves 91.4% test accuracy with RoBERTa-large on SST-2 sentiment analysis, outperforms finetuned BERT and RoBERTa on low-data SICK-E natural language inference, and reaches 43.34% Precision@1 on the LAMA fact retrieval benchmark (exceeding manual LAMA prompts at 31.10% and LPAQA at 34.10%).
  • supports Source: AutoPrompt: Eliciting Knowledge from Language Models with Automatically Generated Prompts · Locator: Sections 3-6, Tables 1, 2, 4, and 5, Figure 2, pp. 4-9
Limitation #r718-claim-ungrammatical-imbalance-limitation
Discrete prompts discovered by greedy search over vocabulary tokens lack human interpretability and grammaticality, struggle on tasks such as QQP and RTE (performing near chance) or under severe class imbalance where prompts amplify majority-class prior probability, and drop sharply on counterfactual relation extraction sentences where objects are synthetically perturbed (e.g., BERT P@1 dropping from 90.73% to 56.43%).
  • supports Source: AutoPrompt: Eliciting Knowledge from Language Models with Automatically Generated Prompts · Locator: Section 6 & Section 7, Table 5, pp. 8-9

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Generated discrete trigger sequences are ungrammatical and lack human interpretability, and greedy discrete search over the vocabulary can be brittle or fail to exceed chance accuracy on tasks such as QQP and RTE.
  • Requires labeled training data to guide the gradient search and is sensitive to severe class imbalance unless training batches are rebalanced; on relation extraction, performance drops substantially when factual context entities are counterfactually perturbed.
