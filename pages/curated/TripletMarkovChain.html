Triplet Markov chain

Model Families AIMech:TripletMarkovChain DRAFT
Aliases: TMC
A latent-variable sequence-model family that introduces an auxiliary process so that the joint hidden, auxiliary and observed process is first-order Markov, while the hidden–observed pair obtained by marginalizing the auxiliary process need not be Markov.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The inspected construction uses finite hidden X and auxiliary U states with an observed Y process, and models the Markov triplet T_t=(X_t,U_t,Y_t). The auxiliary variable is a stochastic latent coordinate, not the emitted-symbol stack or queue used by an auxiliary-memory HMM. Pairwise Markov chains embed through a constant or redundant auxiliary coordinate; general triplet models allow hidden–observed pair laws outside that subclass. Independent factorial hidden chains and particular evidential models impose additional structure and are not synonyms of the generic triplet family. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Specify a normalized initial law and first-order transition kernels for T_t=(X_t,U_t,Y_t). The observable/target joint law p(x,y) is obtained by summing the complete triplet law over auxiliary paths u. For observed y, treat V_t=(X_t,U_t) as the augmented hidden state and apply the pairwise-chain forward–backward recursions with observed kernels p(v_(t+1),y_(t+1)|v_t,y_t). The product of forward and backward messages gives p(v_t,y); sum over u_t at fixed x_t and divide by p(y) to obtain p(x_t|y). Maximizing this marginalized posterior gives the per-position marginal-posterior-mode estimate. Neither X nor (X,Y), nor even the marginal pair (X,U), is required to be Markov in the generic family. A simple source example uses a Markov auxiliary U and conditionally independent (X_t,Y_t) pairs given U_t; its hidden–observed pair can still fail to be Markov. The experiments use more restricted subclasses, including a Markov (X,U) process with conditionally independent observations given that pair.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Inference cost depends on the enlarged state cardinality |X|*|U| and the transition structure. Finite auxiliary-state forward–backward tractability is not a constant-cost claim as those spaces grow.
  • Summing over U is essential: maximizing over U first can change the chosen X state. Statewise marginal decoding, joint augmented-path decoding and marginal MAP of the whole X path are different objectives.
  • The finite forward/backward update range is t=1,...,T-1 with the stated initial/terminal messages; the manuscript prints a range including zero without defining the corresponding message. Posterior formulas require nonzero observation evidence.
  • The conditional-symmetry hypothesis in Proposition2.1 is part of that result. It is not adopted as an unconditional characterization of all possible Markov projections.
  • The evidential/Dempster–Shafer construction in Section2.2 is a particular application. Its proportional factors require a consistent normalization treatment before use as conditional transition rows; the generic model record does not certify those displayed factors or the evidential theorem.
  • Despite the title of Section3.2, the displayed image segmentations use supervised parameter estimates. Remark3.2 reports that attempted unsupervised EM on those noisy images did not give good results. The source’s synthetic successes do not establish universal superiority or global convergence.
