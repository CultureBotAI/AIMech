Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy

Algorithms AIMech:CovarianceMatrixAdaptationEvolutionStrategy DRAFT
Aliases: CMA-ES
An evolution strategy for continuous optimization that samples a multivariate normal search distribution and adapts its mean, covariance and global step size from ranked offspring and accumulated selected-step paths.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record describes CMA-ES through the 2001 weighted-recombination algorithm and its author-supplied errata. That algorithm’s covariance update is based on a rank-one evolution-path outer product; later rank-mu, active covariance, restart and limited-memory variants are not silently imported. The search distribution is not a predictive model of objective values. Derandomized adaptation does not make offspring sampling deterministic. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Initialize a search mean, positive global step size, identity covariance and zero evolution paths. Sample a generation of Gaussian offspring with covariance equal to the global step size squared times the covariance matrix. Rank offspring by the minimization objective and recombine the selected best offspring with positive normalized weights to set the next mean. Accumulate a normalized selected mean displacement into a covariance evolution path. In the inspected 2001 procedure, blend the old covariance with the outer product of that path. Accumulate a separate path with covariance scaling removed and adjust the global step size exponentially according to its length relative to the expected length of a standard normal vector. Obtain the covariance axes and scales by eigendecomposition for subsequent sampling and repeat under the chosen stopping criterion. No derivatives of the objective are required.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This is a source-specific account of the 2001 algorithm, not an exact implementation contract for all contemporary CMA-ES software. Its positive recombination weights, rank-one covariance update and cumulative step-size rule are distinguished from later extensions. The author errata qualify the default weight formula when the selected-parent count exceeds half the offspring population and revise a suggested ordering of cumulation parameters.
  • Initial mean and step size matter. Weak, noisy or selection-irrelevant feedback and numerical conditioning can impair covariance adaptation or collapse short search directions. Full covariance storage grows quadratically with dimension; eigendecomposition and expensive function evaluations add computational costs.
  • Constraint handling and termination require explicit choices. The paper discusses resampling feasible offspring but cautions about boundary optima. Ranking and distribution adaptation do not guarantee a global optimum, monotonic improvement of every generation, or a calibrated inverse-Hessian estimate. No benchmark speedup or author-code execution is asserted.
