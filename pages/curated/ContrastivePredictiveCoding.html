Contrastive predictive coding

Algorithms AIMech:ContrastivePredictiveCoding DRAFT
Aliases: CPC
A representation-learning procedure that encodes observations, summarizes preceding representations as context, and jointly trains these components to distinguish future representations from sampled alternatives using InfoNCE.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope CPC denotes the van den Oord et al. procedure, with configurable encoders and ordered context. It uses InfoNCE but is not identical to that loss. It differs from DIM local-to-global feature matching and from SimCLR augmentation-pair learning. An autoregressive context module is part of the original procedure; a GRU and raw-input reconstruction are not requirements. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Compute z_t=g_enc(x_t) and c_t=g_ar(z_<=t). For each selected future offset k use the positive score f_k(x,c_t)=exp(z(x)^T W_k c_t). Form a candidate set containing one true future observation from the context-conditional distribution and N−1 negatives, ideally drawn independently from the observation marginal. Minimize the expected negative log of the positive score divided by the sum of all N scores, including the positive, over selected prediction horizons. Train the encoder, context module and prediction parameters jointly. With the ideal sampling scheme and a sufficiently expressive optimally fitted score, the optimal score is proportional to the conditional-to-marginal density ratio, with a context-dependent factor cancelling in the categorical probabilities. The expected loss yields the InfoNCE lower bound I(X_future;C)>=log(N)−L_NCE under its sampling assumptions. This is an expectation-level statement, not a guarantee for one minibatch. Learned local representations or context summaries can be used downstream.

  • supports Source: On Variational Bounds of Mutual Information · Locator: Section 2.3, PDF p. 4, Equations (8)–(10) and footnote 2: rigorous expected InfoNCE bound and log-number-of-samples ceiling

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The bound is at most log(N) and depends on the negative proposal. Same-speaker, same-sequence or otherwise conditional negatives used in particular experiments do not automatically satisfy the ideal independent marginal sampling assumptions.
  • The original appendix uses an approximate substitution in its derivation. The population bound is supported here by Poole et al.’s separate proof, not by promoting that approximation to an exact equality.
  • Finite critics, finite samples and incomplete optimization do not guarantee density-ratio recovery or an accurate numerical mutual-information estimate. Future-predictive information can include nuisance factors.
  • Only declared primary passages were inspected. No CPC implementation, training run or benchmark was reproduced.
