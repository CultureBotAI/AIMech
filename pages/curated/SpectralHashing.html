Spectral hashing

Algorithms AIMech:SpectralHashing DRAFT
An unsupervised binary-code learning algorithm that approximates a spectral similarity-preservation objective using a PCA-aligned rectangular data model and zero-thresholded single-coordinate analytic modes.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the original Weiss–Torralba–Fergus spectral hashing algorithm. Its graph-based relaxation motivates the method, while the efficient out-of-sample algorithm uses a fitted rectangular distribution instead of eigendecomposing the complete empirical graph for every query. It differs from neural semantic hashing, random-projection locality-sensitive hashing, Laplacian Eigenmaps and the later multidimensional spectral hashing formulation. The ambiguous abbreviation SH is not claimed as an exclusive alias. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
The motivation minimizes affinity-weighted squared distances between binary codes, subject to balanced bits and pairwise decorrelation; dropping the binary restriction yields low nonconstant graph-Laplacian modes. The practical algorithm first computes PCA coordinates and fits an interval [a_j,b_j] along each retained nondegenerate axis. Under its uniform-rectangle approximation, enumerate nonconstant single-axis modes with positive integer frequency m, represented in shifted coordinates by cos(m*pi*(x_j-a_j)/(b_j-a_j)). Rank these modes by their analytic eigenvalue order, equivalently increasing squared frequency (m/(b_j-a_j))^2 for a common positive bandwidth, and select the required number of bits. Exclude the constant mode and multidimensional outer-product modes. Threshold each selected function at zero to form binary codes. A new point is projected into the same PCA coordinates and evaluated by these selected functions; hash-table retrieval then compares codes within a chosen Hamming radius.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The uniform rectangular model is an approximation to the data distribution. The resulting code is not the exact optimum of the discrete balanced-partition problem or necessarily the thresholded eigenvectors of the finite empirical affinity graph.
  • Equation (2) prints Y^T Y=I while retaining unscaled plus/minus-one entries. Equation (1) instead implies Y^T Y=nI for n observations; an identity normalization requires rescaled real-valued relaxed coordinates. The record preserves this distinction rather than copying the incompatible constraints.
  • Equation (4) gives the interval width but omits the origin shift unless its x already denotes a shifted coordinate. The mechanism states that shift explicitly. Degenerate axes, zero-threshold ties and points outside fitted intervals require explicit numerical conventions.
  • Continuous orthogonality does not guarantee empirical balance, decorrelation or independence after thresholding. Excluding outer-product modes avoids one explicit source of deterministic redundancy but does not ensure independent bits; even first and third thresholded modes on one uniform axis are correlated.
  • The number of bits need not be smaller than the input dimension. Compact storage and approximate-neighbor retrieval are not a guarantee of dimension reduction, exact semantic similarity, universal retrieval accuracy or collision resistance for cryptography. No benchmark ranking, general spectral-convergence theorem or author-code behavior is certified.
