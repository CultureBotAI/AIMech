HAC-ES network clustering

Algorithms AIMech:HACES DRAFT
Aliases: HAC-ES
A hierarchical network-clustering variant that guides agglomeration by the sum of cross-group edge density and Jaccard overlap of the groups’ external neighboring vertices.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #combined-merge-guide
The guide adds the cross-group edge density to a shared-neighbor term. That term counts outside vertices connected to both groups and divides by the number connected to either group. Vertices in the two candidate groups are excluded, and each outside vertex contributes a Boolean presence indicator rather than its number of incident edges.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Different merging scores, PDF p. 4, Equations 9–10 and explanation of the summation
Scope #ranking-and-probability-boundary
The unweighted sum can change candidate-pair rankings relative to edge density alone and can exceed one. A constructed pair with edge density one half and complete external-neighbor overlap scores three halves, exceeding a pair with density one and no overlap. The combined score is therefore a merge heuristic, not a Bernoulli edge probability.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Different merging scores, PDF p. 4, Equations 9–10; curator-derived candidate comparison in research/discovery/haces-boundaries.py
Limitation #empty-neighborhood-boundary
If neither candidate group has an external neighbor, the printed shared-neighbor expression has zero numerator and denominator. The paper does not state a numerical convention for this case. Candidate restrictions or code might handle it, but no such behavior was inspected or assumed here.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Different merging scores, PDF p. 4, Equation 10; curator-derived empty-union boundary in research/discovery/haces-boundaries.py
Scope #hierarchical-link-estimation
The method belongs to the paper’s hierarchical link-prediction comparison, where densities at tree joins estimate missing links. The shared-neighbor merge heuristic and those subsequent density estimates play different roles. Improved agreement of local scores with common neighbors does not itself establish biological identity or universal predictive improvement.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Link prediction and Empirical evaluation, PDF p. 5; Table 2 and Figure 1, p. 6
Scope #evaluation-scope
The study compares this named variant with HAC-ML and other methods on biological interaction networks using held-out-edge prediction. Positive test pairs are balanced by an equal number of sampled holes, and maximum F scores summarize ranked predictions. This selection changes the negative prevalence relative to deployment. The reported comparisons support a setting-specific empirical assessment, not universal superiority or calibrated real-world link probabilities.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Performance Evaluation and Link prediction, PDF pp. 4–5; Table 2 and Figure 1, p. 6

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The external-neighbor term is a Jaccard ratio over vertices, not over the current cluster labels or a sum of all shared-neighbor paths. It is distinct from ROCK’s link-count normalization.
  • The printed sum supplies no fitted mixing weight. Its two components have different information content despite each lying between zero and one when defined.
  • This record covers a named 2011 score variant in Park and Bader’s network-clustering study, not a fitted interactome or the entire family of agglomerative clustering methods.
  • The paper defines each alternative by replacing the merge guide. It does not separately specify every candidate-restriction, tie, stopping and terminal-collapse convention for each variant. The detailed HAC-ML implementation and its complexity are not automatically asserted for this variant.
  • The experiments use undirected, unweighted interaction graphs with iterative removal of isolated and degree-one vertices. Missing observed edges can include unobserved biological interactions; sampled holes are not established biological negatives.
  • No author implementation, data or supplementary archive was audited or executed. Finite constructed checks assess local scores and algebraic distinctions, not complete clustering runs, benchmark replication or global optimality. DRAFT remains.
