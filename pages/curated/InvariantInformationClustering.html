Invariant information clustering

Algorithms AIMech:InvariantInformationClustering DRAFT
Aliases: IIC
A neural clustering procedure that maximizes mutual information between discrete cluster-assignment variables for paired unlabeled samples, estimating their joint distribution from the network’s soft assignment probabilities.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Invariant information clustering (IIC) refers to Ji et al.’s paired-data discrete-information objective and neural learning procedure. It directly learns cluster assignments and differs from continuous embedding mutual-information estimators, k-means pseudo-label training and generic biclustering. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For n paired samples (x_i,x′_i), apply the same network ending in a C-class softmax to obtain probability vectors a_i and b_i. Define discrete labels z and z′ as conditionally independent given the input pair, with these probabilities. Form the batch joint estimate P=(1/n) sum_i a_i b_i^T and, for symmetric pair sampling, replace it by (P+P^T)/2. Obtain marginals r_c=sum_d P_cd and s_d=sum_c P_cd. Minimize −sum_(c,d) P_cd log[P_cd/(r_c s_d)], differentiating through both network outputs and their induced joint/marginals. Use the continuous zero-mass convention or a documented numerical stabilization. For images, pairs are generated by content-preserving transformations. An auxiliary overclustering head can learn more clusters while sharing the backbone with the main head; the reported training alternates the heads. For segmentation, align transformed prediction grids back to the original coordinates and average mutual information separately over local spatial displacements between patch assignments.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The information quantity is between modeled discrete assignment variables after averaging paired samples, not between the continuous probability vectors themselves. Finite-batch estimation and conditional modeling assumptions affect the objective.
  • Constant assignments give zero mutual information, whereas a feasible balanced deterministic correspondence can give a larger value. This distinguishes the objective from pure agreement but does not prove arbitrary optimization escapes stationary collapsed states or recovers semantic classes.
  • The entropy term favors cluster utilization, so chosen cluster count, class imbalance, distractors and augmentation semantics matter. High mutual information can preserve a nuisance factor shared by paired inputs; it is not proof of correct categories.
  • The pinned author loss computes marginals before epsilon clamping; the paper’s compact Figure 4 clamps the joint first. These stabilized computations approximate the ideal normalized probability formula. The optional entropy coefficient differs from pure mutual information when it is not one.
  • Geometric alignment is required for segmentation; same raw pixel coordinates need not denote corresponding content after a transform. Learned cluster indices have arbitrary order: evaluation maps them to reference classes using labels, and the paper’s semi-supervised modes must remain distinct from unlabeled training. No benchmark result was reproduced.
