Hidden Markov decision tree

Model Families AIMech:HiddenMarkovDecisionTree DRAFT
Aliases: HMDT
A conditional latent sequence-model family that extends a probabilistic hierarchical decision tree by coupling each decision level to the same level at the previous time.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The Jordan–Ghahramani–Saul model uses probabilistic choices down a hierarchy and Markov persistence within each level. It is not a hard deterministic classification tree, an independent-chain factorial HMM, a tree-shaped index, or a particular variational inference algorithm. Current higher-level decisions influence lower-level decisions; independent factorial chains arise only under additional restrictions. The model conditions on an external input sequence. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For level m and time t, its latent decision z_t^m is distributed conditional on the current input x_t, all current ancestor decisions z_t^1,...,z_t^(m-1), and its own preceding decision z_(t-1)^m. Initial decisions condition on the input and current ancestors without a preceding-time state. Multiplying these normalized conditional factors and one observation factor p(y_t|x_t,z_t^1,...,z_t^M) per time gives a normalized directed model conditional on inputs. A latent path through the hierarchy selects an output expert. Exact inference may enumerate the joint states but grows exponentially with hierarchy depth. The paper explores three distinct approximations: a product of chain-path distributions that preserves temporal dependence within each level; a product over times of tree-structured distributions that preserves current ancestor dependence; and a point-mass path approximation. Its point-mass procedure optimizes one level’s path by a Viterbi subroutine while holding the other paths fixed. These are inference choices for the family, not alternative definitions of its identity.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Same-level temporal connections and current-ancestor dependencies are defining restrictions of the inspected construction. They do not cover arbitrary cross-time connections or all models whose names contain hidden Markov tree.
  • The probabilistic hierarchy sums over decision paths; it is not necessarily a hard classification tree. Input conditioning and an output expert do not imply a causal interpretation of the graph.
  • Independent latent chains, a single HMM and an ordinary hierarchical mixture arise under specific restrictions, not as unconditional aliases of this family.
  • Forest-of-chains, forest-of-trees and point-mass inference discard different posterior dependencies. A coordinate Viterbi step finds the best path for one fixed-context chain, not a guaranteed global joint MAP path.
  • The paper motivates hierarchical time scales but its synthetic runs did not always recover the intended hierarchy. No universal coarse-to-fine organization, general convergence guarantee, benchmark advantage or author-code reproduction is asserted. Detailed generic variational updates remain a separate source lead.
