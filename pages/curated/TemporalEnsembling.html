Temporal ensembling

Algorithms AIMech:TemporalEnsembling DRAFT
A semi-supervised classifier-training procedure that regularizes a current stochastic prediction toward a bias-corrected exponential average of earlier predictions for the same indexed training example, alongside labeled cross-entropy.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the prediction-ensemble algorithm in Laine and Aila, distinguished from the same paper’s Pi-model. Its dataset-indexed stored targets and epoch-based refresh differ from Mean Teacher’s per-step weight averaging. The name does not imply an ensemble of separately trained classifiers at inference. This record describes a training algorithm, not a trained checkpoint or fixed network architecture. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Maintain zero-initialized N-by-C arrays Z and target z_tilde for N indexed examples and C classes. During epoch t, obtain one stochastic prediction z_i for each example when its minibatch is processed. Minimize labeled cross-entropy divided by the full minibatch size plus w(t)/(C|B|) sum over all i in B of ||z_i−z_tilde_i||², holding the stored targets constant during differentiation. After predictions have been used for that epoch, update each accumulated prediction Z_i←alpha Z_i+(1−alpha)z_i and set the next target to Z_i/(1−alpha^t), with fixed 0≤alpha<1 and t starting at 1. Set the first epoch’s consistency weight to zero and ramp it up. Algorithm 2 displays the target update after the epoch; its caption permits per-example updates within a minibatch after the old target has been used. Predictions gathered within an epoch can come from different current parameter states. The exponential average is over example predictions, not model parameters.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Per-example prediction memory scales with the number of indexed examples and classes; targets depend on preserving example identity across visits.
  • The stated bias correction assumes zero initialization and a fixed alpha; arbitrary momentum schedules or missing-example update schedules need their own accounting.
  • Old predictions can remain incorrect or become stale, and input perturbations must preserve the intended class; no unconditional improvement is implied by averaging.
  • The record covers the original classifier procedure, not an online-streaming extension, a claim of doubled speed, or reported benchmark accuracy.
