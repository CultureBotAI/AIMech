Logarithmic opinion pool

Aliases: Log opinion pool, Logarithmic pooling
A probability-aggregation method that forms a normalized weighted geometric mean of expert distributions over the same outcomes, using nonnegative exponent weights that sum to one.

Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record uses the normalized-weight convention inspected in Heskes and Hinton. It combines probability assessments, possibly conditional on an input, rather than spatially pooling neural activations. It is distinct from a linear opinion pool, a unit-exponent product learned cooperatively, and a particular algorithm for estimating the exponent weights. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For each input x, aggregate expert probabilities as pbar(y|x)=product_m p_m(y|x)^w_m/Z(x), with w_m>=0 and sum_m w_m=1. Omit zero-weight factors, and require 0<Z(x)<infinity. On a common support where the divergences are finite, pbar minimizes sum_m w_m KL(r||p_m) over normalized r, with the candidate r in the first KL argument. For a target distribution q, KL(q||pbar)=sum_m w_m KL(q||p_m)-sum_m w_m KL(pbar||p_m); the subtracted term is -log Z at a fixed input, or its expectation over inputs. Thus the pool is no worse in that KL direction than the weighted average of experts, not necessarily the best expert. A common likelihood update commutes with pooling when the same fixed normalized weights are retained and normalization exists. For binary logistic probabilities this rule averages logits; for equal-variance Gaussian experts it averages means while preserving that shared variance.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • An outcome assigned zero probability by any positively weighted expert receives zero pooled probability. Disjoint positively weighted supports can yield Z=0. Zero-weight experts are excluded from this veto; finite-divergence identities require compatible support.
  • The weighted-average KL bound is not a guarantee of improvement over every expert, calibrated uncertainty or empirical predictive superiority. It depends on nonnegative weights summing to one, and does not transfer unchanged to arbitrary unit-exponent products.
  • Pooling does not specify how expert models or their weights are trained. Heskes’s quadratic weight-selection objective is an approximation obtained by neglecting KL asymmetry terms for nearby distributions; it is not the exact general weight-optimization objective. Finite-data divergence estimates may be biased by fitting and overfitting.
  • The Heskes display following Equation (3) prints ambiguity A as positive log Z, conflicting with its nonnegative weighted-KL definition and the preceding prose. The consistent identity is A=-log Z. The normalization constraint there also integrates over x where conditional normalization requires y; these display slips are not adopted.
  • The variance example on printed p.270 reverses the precision ratio for the displayed KL argument order. The record relies on the probability-pool definition and the equal-variance example, not that reversed formula. No general weight-selection implementation or empirical result is certified.
