Interpolated variational information bound

Mathematical Methods AIMech:InterpolatedVariationalInformationBound DRAFT
Aliases: I-alpha bound, Nonlinearly interpolated mutual information lower bound
A family of multi-sample mutual-information lower bounds that interpolates the critic’s empirical partition estimate with a positive variational normalizer inside a ratio, connecting NWJ and InfoNCE parameterizations.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is Poole et al.’s I_alpha family, not Rényi alpha-information, a convex average of final scalar bounds, or an ordinary temperature parameter. Its endpoints connect existing parameterizations; the interior normalizer and residual product expectation define the distinct construction. The paper’s proposed optimal critic is explicitly a conjecture. No normalized identity match was found across all 442 pinned AIO labels/scoped synonyms and 208 preceding native IDs/names/aliases. Mechanism #method
Draw X_1:K independently from p(x), with Y conditional on X_1 for the joint term. Let m(y;X_1:K)=K^−1 sum_i exp(f(X_i,y)), b_alpha=alpha*m+(1−alpha)*q(y), and r=exp(f(X_1,y))/b_alpha, for q(y)>0 and alpha in [0,1]. Define I_alpha=1+E_p(x_1:K)p(y|x_1)[log r]−E_p(x_1:K)p(y)[r]. The second expectation uses Y independent of all K inputs. Applying NWJ to the augmented variables gives I_alpha<=I(X_1;Y). At alpha=0 this is NWJ after the critic transformation T=1+f−log q; at alpha=1 exchangeability makes the product expectation equal one and yields InfoNCE. For interior alpha the product term must be retained. A leave-one-out minibatch implementation can hold out an additional paired sample’s Y to estimate that independent product term, leaving K other inputs in the normalizer.
  • supports Source: On Variational Bounds of Mutual Information · Locator: Sections 2.3–2.4, PDF pp. 4–5, Equations (8)–(11), independence construction, endpoint discussion and conjecture
Limitation #ceiling-qualification
A curator derivation from Equation (11) gives the conservative ceiling 1+log(K/alpha) for alpha>0: r<=K/alpha and the product expectation is nonnegative. At alpha=1 the cancellation gives the sharper InfoNCE ceiling log K. The paper’s page-5 prose states log(K/alpha) for the general family, whereas the pinned notebook’s plotting code includes the additive one. A deterministic finite-domain calculation saved in the discovery ledger yields I_alpha approximately 2.08113 for K=2 and alpha=0.5, exceeding log(4) approximately 1.38629 while remaining below the true information. Therefore the stronger printed ceiling is not adopted. This is explicit curator analysis of the displayed formula, not an author-issued erratum.
  • supports Source: On Variational Bounds of Mutual Information · Locator: Equation (11), PDF p. 4, and stated ceiling at the start of p. 5; formula and prose distinguished

The main Equation (11) uses a ratio without a logarithm in the product expectation, but supplementary Table 2 prints a logarithm in that term. The pinned notebook implements the ratio term; its interpolation docstring reverses endpoint labels even though log_interpolate weights the batch normalizer by alpha. The notebook also uses different score orientations in its two critic classes. ⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The lower bound is an expectation with specified independence; a finite minibatch is not a guaranteed lower bound. The held-out product sample must be independent of every input used in its normalizer.
  • The positive q is a variational normalizer; choosing it as an estimated marginal is one configuration. The paper conjectures f=log p(y|x), q=p(y) as an optimum rather than proving it generally.
  • The main formula, supplemental table and notebook are not textually consistent: the supplemental I_alpha row places a log in the product term, and a notebook docstring reverses the alpha-logit endpoint labels. This record follows main Equation (11), the explicit mixture definition and the independently checked ceiling qualification.
  • The notebook mixes score-matrix orientations across critic implementations. Only declared cells were read; no TensorFlow code, training or benchmark was executed. The finite-domain arithmetic check evaluates the population formula by enumeration and is not a trained-estimator experiment.
