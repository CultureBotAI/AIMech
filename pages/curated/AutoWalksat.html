Auto-Walksat

Algorithms AIMech:AutoWalksat DRAFT
A preprocessing-and-run algorithm that estimates a fixed noise setting for a stochastic SAT solver by probing a terminal-score mean-to-standard-deviation ratio, searching the proxy with bracketing and parabolic interpolation, and using an empirical post-minimum adjustment.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Auto-Walksat denotes the Patterson–Kautz 2001 fixed-noise SAT preprocessing controller, distinct from its supplied Walksat-SKC solver and later online-adaptation variants. Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Scope #fixed-noise-preprocessing-controller
Auto-Walksat is a preprocessing controller for a CNF formula and a supplied stochastic SAT algorithm with a noise parameter. It probes settings, estimates a setting from a proxy statistic, and then runs the solver with that selected setting; the defining experiments instantiate Walksat-SKC.
  • supports Source: Auto-Walksat: A Self-Tuning Implementation of Walksat · Locator: PDF pp. 1–4, Sections 1–3 and Figures 1–2
Mechanism #terminal-unsuccessful-score-ratio
For Walksat-SKC the objective is the number of unsatisfied clauses. The stated invariant ratio is the mean divided by the standard deviation of objective values at the ends of several unsuccessful attempts made with different random seeds.
  • supports Source: Auto-Walksat: A Self-Tuning Implementation of Walksat · Locator: PDF p. 3, Section 2
  • context Source: Walksat in the 2004 SAT Competition · Locator: PDF p. 2, AutoWalksat paragraph; later statistic wording is contextual and does not substitute for the 2001 definition
Mechanism #short-repeated-probes
Figure 2 estimates the ratio at a fixed noise setting with repeated short attempts, collecting objective statistics after 2,000 flips plus one flip for every atom of the formula. It states a mean-confidence-interval stopping test whose exact displayed numerical convention is unresolved.
  • supports Source: Auto-Walksat: A Self-Tuning Implementation of Walksat · Locator: PDF p. 4, Figure 2, Probe definition
Mechanism #bracketing-and-parabolic-search
The described controller brackets noise over the displayed 0–100 scale, uses a parabolic interpolation proposal when the parabola is well formed, and otherwise takes a golden-section step toward the larger side before probing again and shrinking the bracket.
  • supports Source: Auto-Walksat: A Self-Tuning Implementation of Walksat · Locator: PDF pp. 3–4, Section 3 and Figure 2
Scope #post-minimum-heuristic-and-failure
The discussion and conclusion describe increasing the ratio-minimizing noise by ten percent before the final solver run as an empirical heuristic. The same paper reports that its logistics example instead calls for decreasing noise and says how to choose the direction is unresolved.
  • supports Source: Auto-Walksat: A Self-Tuning Implementation of Walksat · Locator: PDF pp. 7–9, Section 5, Figure 6 and Section 6

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The terminal statistic is defined over unsuccessful attempts, while the probe pseudocode does not specify treatment of successful attempts, a minimum retained count, a standard-deviation estimator, or what to do when the retained set is empty or has zero spread. These are source specification gaps, not observed implementation failures.
  • Figure 2 visually prints 0.05 with superscript 3 and no corresponding footnote visible on that page. Its exact exponent/footnote meaning, interval width convention, replication rule and statistical construction are unresolved. Even a precise mean-only interval does not establish precision of mean divided by a near-zero standard deviation.
  • Figure 2 leaves endpoint values initially undefined, gives only a general bracket reassignment instruction, and does not fully specify how the last proposal n relates to a retained best point. The prose’s one-percent refinement criterion does not identify its exact comparison quantity or zero boundary. Finite noisy probes and this description do not prove a global ratio minimum or an optimal solver noise setting.
  • The prose’s ten-percent increase is absent from Figure 2. The logistics example explicitly needs the opposite direction. Its 39–69 to 9–39 numbers illustrate a 30-percentage-point decrease for that example, without establishing a general relative/additive offset or clipping convention. The original does not give an automatic rule for choosing the direction.
  • The supplied algorithm must expose the relevant noise parameter and objective observations. The detailed evaluated move rule is Walksat-SKC; tuning one parameter need not fix instances difficult for that solver. This is preprocessing followed by a chosen fixed setting, not a specification of generic within-run adaptation. A finite failed SAT search is not an UNSAT certificate.
  • Illustrated curves, four DIMACS problem experiments, short tuning overhead and logistics results are bounded observations. They do not establish universal speedup, calibrated success probability, optimality for every formula/variant, or a theorem that the invariant ratio predicts performance. No benchmark or author implementation was run here.
  • This assessment uses the nine-page May 2001 author preprint layout, with publication identity corroborated separately. The 2004 owner-URL description uses different shorthand for the statistic and is context only. Publisher-byte equivalence, later code behavior, the original cited invariants theory and full Brent-method assumptions were not verified, so their details are not silently imported.
