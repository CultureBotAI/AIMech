Soft Q-learning

Algorithms AIMech:SoftQLearning DRAFT
The 2017 maximum-entropy continuous-control learning algorithm that fits neural soft action values from replayed transitions and trains an amortized Stein sampler toward the corresponding Boltzmann action policy.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The selected identity is the 2017 Haarnoja source-defined control algorithm combining entropy-regularized soft action values and an amortized Stein action sampler. It is distinct from 2018 SoftActorCritic, ordinary maximum-backup QLearning, DQN and deterministic DDPG. Original SVGD particle inference is a reusable mechanism, not an alias of the complete control algorithm. Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Scope #entropy-objective
The 2017 method learns continuous-action policies under a reward-plus-conditional-policy-entropy formulation with a positive entropy coefficient alpha. Entropy enters future soft-value targets rather than serving only as Boltzmann exploration of an ordinary Q table; the source separately qualifies its discounted state-weighting convention.
  • supports Source: Reinforcement Learning with Deep Energy-Based Policies · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.2, PDF pp. 2–3, Equations 2–7
  • supports Source: Reinforcement Learning with Deep Energy-Based Policies — supplementary material · Locator: Appendix A, PDF p. 11, discounted-objective discussion and Equations 15–16
Mechanism #energy-policy
For a finite positive action normalizer, the target policy is proportional to exp(Q(s,a)/alpha), with V(s)=alpha*log(integral exp(Q(s,a)/alpha) da). The neural scalar action value acts as negative energy. This describes the target policy family; a finite learned sampler need not represent it exactly.
  • supports Source: Reinforcement Learning with Deep Energy-Based Policies · Locator: Section 2.2, PDF p. 3, Equations 3–6
Scope #soft-backup
The idealized soft backup replaces the hard next-action maximum by the log-partition soft value: Q_new(s,a)=r(s,a)+gamma*E[V(s_next)]. The source gives an exact fixed-point result under boundedness and finite-partition assumptions with discount below one. This operator result is separate from the practical neural, replay and finite-sampling approximations.
  • supports Source: Reinforcement Learning with Deep Energy-Based Policies · Locator: Section 3.1, PDF pp. 3–4, Theorem 3 and Equations 8–9
  • supports Source: Reinforcement Learning with Deep Energy-Based Policies — supplementary material · Locator: Appendix A.2, PDF pp. 12–13, exact soft-backup argument
Mechanism #approximate-value-fit
The practical procedure estimates soft next-state values by taking the logarithm of a sample mean of importance weights exp(Q_target(s_next,a)/alpha)/q(a), scaled by alpha, for proposal actions with suitable support and density. Replayed transition minibatches then fit neural Q values by squared soft Bellman residuals using delayed target parameters.
  • supports Source: Reinforcement Learning with Deep Energy-Based Policies · Locator: Section 3.2, PDF p. 4, Equations 10–11; Algorithm 1, PDF p. 5
  • supports Source: Reinforcement Learning with Deep Energy-Based Policies — supplementary material · Locator: Appendix C.2, PDF p. 14, proposal-density discussion and Equation 33
Mechanism #amortized-stein-sampler
A state-conditioned sampler transforms random noise into actions. Its update projects a kernelized Stein transport direction through the sampler Jacobian, using particle averages of an action-value-gradient attraction term and a kernel-gradient repulsion term. Main Equation 13 weights the repulsion by alpha. The projected quantity is treated as an update direction, with the source sign and scaling conventions retained explicitly.
  • supports Source: Reinforcement Learning with Deep Energy-Based Policies · Locator: Section 3.3, PDF p. 5, Equations 12–14
  • supports Source: Reinforcement Learning with Deep Energy-Based Policies — supplementary material · Locator: Appendix C.1, PDF pp. 13–14, empirical particle projection
  • context Source: softqlearning SQL implementation, softqlearning/algorithms/sql.py · Locator: Lines 247–268 at the pinned 2019 commit; later negative-surrogate sign convention is context only, not the original method version
Scope #replay-alternation
Algorithm 1 alternates environment interaction, replay-buffer minibatch action-value fitting and sampler fitting, and periodically copies current parameters into target parameters. It specifies a reusable off-policy learning procedure rather than a single trained controller or an exact sampling oracle.
  • supports Source: Reinforcement Learning with Deep Energy-Based Policies · Locator: Section 3.4 and Algorithm 1, PDF p. 5

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The exact soft-backup result requires a suitable bounded function class, discount below one and finite positive action integrals. It does not certify convergence of finite replay estimates, a nonconvex neural Q approximator or an imperfect sampler.
  • The partition sample mean and its logarithm have different bias properties. The log estimate is generally Jensen-biased; constant importance weights require the exact normalized proposal for the same Q being integrated, not merely an approximate or delayed sampler.
  • The single-inverse density expression in Appendix C.2 needs global one-to-one support or an appropriate sum over preimages. Equal dimension and a nonsingular local Jacobian alone are insufficient; the source’s suggested switch away from uniform proposals is not an injectivity certificate.
  • The Stein projection is an update direction rather than a generally exact ordinary parameter gradient of KL. Main Equation 13 retains alpha on repulsion, while Appendix C.1 writes coefficient one; the sign and temperature convention must be explicit. The pinned 2019 author code minimizes a negative surrogate, but is later context and is not established as the original experimental version.
  • Appendix A qualifies discounted state weighting and has a future-sum index mismatch in its opening display; Appendix B Equation 32 displays a descent sign rather than the literal derivative of its squared loss. These source-local issues do not justify importing an unqualified objective or gradient-equivalence theorem.
  • Finite action partitions, proposal support and action-density assumptions are constitutive; differential entropy depends on the chosen action measure. A zero empirical residual or a nonsingular Jacobian at sampled points does not establish the required global properties.
  • The selected evidence is the official 2017 main paper and supplementary artifact. Their finite simulations do not establish universal exploration, multimodal recovery or transfer performance. ArXiv version metadata and later author code are qualified context; no training reproduction, complete implementation audit or later SAC/compositional extension is included.
