Aliases: SAGE
A generative model family for categorical text data that forms word probabilities by normalizing a background log-frequency vector plus sparse component deviations, allowing multiple generative facets to combine additively in log space.

Identity Notes & Concept Scope SAGE here denotes Eisenstein, Ahmed and Xing’s sparse additive generative construction, not Sage mathematical software or graph-neural aggregation. Its classification, topic and multifaceted uses share this parameterization; it is not synonymous with all naive Bayes models, sparse dictionary learning, sparse topic proportions or an arithmetic mixture of component word distributions. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a component k, set p(w=i|k) proportional to exp(m_i+eta_ki), normalizing over the vocabulary. Multiple selected facets contribute a sum of their deviation vectors before this normalization; no additional token-level switch is needed to choose which facet generates the word. This does not remove latent topic assignments used by a topic-model variant. The paper places sparsity-inducing priors on deviations, using zero-mean Gaussian components with latent variances: an exponential variance prior gives a Laplace marginal, while an alternative improper Jeffreys prior gives a different shrinkage construction. It combines MAP estimates of deviations with variational treatment of other latent quantities. For a single component and fixed expected precisions, the gradient is observed or expected counts minus total count times word probabilities minus the precision-weighted deviations; its Hessian is a rank-one term minus a diagonal term, enabling the reported efficient Newton update.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Sparsity concerns log deviations. Finite logits still give positive word probabilities, and even zero-deviation words can change absolute probability through normalization; their relative odds remain those of the background when their deviations agree.
  • Additive log facets yield multiplicative unnormalized effects, not an arithmetic probability mixture. The model family does not require every document to have a single observed class or prohibit latent variables.
  • The Jeffreys-prior paragraph on PDF p.4 prints expected inverse variance equal to eta squared. Its preceding Gamma moment and b=eta squared/(a-1) instead give 1/eta squared for nonzero eta, with a>1 and positive scale. The printed final identity is not adopted; zero deviations require a limiting/numerical treatment. The improper prior is not represented as a normalized marginal distribution.
  • The paper warns that warm-starting tightly coupled deviation/variance updates can lock in early sparsity patterns. Neither approximate inference nor Newton updates establish global convergence or calibrated posterior uncertainty.
  • The single-component rank-one Hessian argument is not asserted unchanged for sums over multiple facet combinations. No author implementation, universal predictive advantage or numerical benchmark result is certified.

 related_match SAGE is explicitly used to construct probabilistic topic models, but its family also includes observed-class and multifaceted generative text models.
