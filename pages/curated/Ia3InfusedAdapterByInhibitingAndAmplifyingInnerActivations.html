Infused Adapter by Inhibiting and Amplifying Inner Activations ((IA)^3)

Algorithms AIMech:Ia3InfusedAdapterByInhibitingAndAmplifyingInnerActivations DRAFT
Aliases: (IA)^3, IA3 Adapter, Infused Adapter by Inhibiting and Amplifying Inner Activations
Infused Adapter by Inhibiting and Amplifying Inner Activations ((IA)^3) is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) method for Transformer architectures that introduces three learned element-wise rescaling vectors per Transformer block—l_k in R^{d_k} on attention keys, l_v in R^{d_v} on attention values, and l_ff in R^{d_ff} on intermediate point-wise feed-forward activations—while keeping all pretrained model weights frozen. Specifically, (IA)^3 computes scaled dot-product attention as softmax((Q (l_k odot K^T)) / sqrt(d_k)) (l_v odot V) and feed-forward hidden states as (l_ff odot gamma(W_1 x)) W_2, where odot denotes broadcasted element-wise multiplication and all scaling vectors are initialized to ones (1) so the initial model function is unchanged. After fine-tuning, l_k, l_v, and l_ff are absorbed into the rows/columns of W_K, W_V, and W_1 (or W_2), incurring zero additional inference latency or memory overhead.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) and Compacter (Karimi Mahabadi et al., 2021), which add low-rank matrix updates Delta W to weight matrices using O(r d) parameters per layer rather than element-wise rescaling inner activations with only d_k + d_v + d_ff parameters per block (~0.01% of total parameters). Also distinct from VeRA (Kopiczko et al., 2024), which scales frozen random projection matrices B and A rather than directly rescaling attention key/value and FFN hidden activations.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #r657-claim-weight-absorption-and-mixed-task-batching
Because element-wise multiplication by a static vector l is associative with preceding linear projections (l odot (W x) = (diag(l) W) x), (IA)^3's learned vectors l_k, l_v, and l_ff can be permanently folded into the frozen weight matrices W_K, W_V, and W_1 for single-task deployment with zero inference cost, or kept separate to enable mixed-task inference batching where each example i in a batch applies its own tiny task vector l^{(i)} to the shared frozen backbone activations.
  • supports Source: Few-Shot Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning is Better and Cheaper than In-Context Learning · Locator: Section 2.2 (Desiderata for Few-Shot PEFT), p. 3; Section 3.2, p. 4-5
Application #r657-claim-parameter-efficiency-and-t-few-recipe
On T0-3B and T0-11B, (IA)^3 updates only L (d_k + d_v + d_ff) parameters (~0.01% to 0.02% of model weights, 10x to 40x fewer than LoRA and Adapter) and, when combined with length-normalized and unlikelihood auxiliary losses in the T-Few recipe, outperforms full fine-tuning, 9 other PEFT methods, and 175B GPT-3 in-context learning on RAFT and held-out T0 tasks at over 1,000x lower inference FLOPs than few-shot ICL.
  • supports Source: Few-Shot Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning is Better and Cheaper than In-Context Learning · Locator: Section 3.2-3.5, Figures 1 & 3, Table 1, p. 1, 5-8; Appendix D, Table 4, p. 17-18
Limitation #r657-claim-diagonal-expressivity-ceiling-limitation
Because (IA)^3 restricts each adapted projection to a rank-preserving diagonal rescaling diag(l) W rather than an arbitrary rank-r update W + B A, it cannot rotate feature subspaces or increase the rank of frozen projection matrices and relies on strong pretrained multi-task representations (such as T0) or auxiliary pretraining of the (IA)^3 vectors.
  • supports Source: Few-Shot Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning is Better and Cheaper than In-Context Learning · Locator: Section 3.2-3.4, p. 4-6; Appendix E (Ablation Studies), p. 19-20

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Restricts weight updates to diagonal row/column rescalings diag(l) W, which cannot mix coordinates across dimensions or add new rank directions when adapting to tasks far outside the pretrained backbone's feature space.
