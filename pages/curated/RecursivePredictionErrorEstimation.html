Recursive prediction-error estimation

Algorithms AIMech:RecursivePredictionErrorEstimation DRAFT
Aliases: Recursive prediction error method
A recursive parameter-estimation method that incrementally adjusts a parameterized predictor using prediction errors and search directions based on its parameter sensitivities.

6 Claims
Mechanism #prediction-error-update
For the squared prediction-error criterion, let epsilon_t=y_t-yhat_t and let psi_t denote the recursive predictor-sensitivity approximation. The displayed Gauss–Newton form updates R_t=(1-gamma_t)R_(t-1)+gamma_t*psi_t*psi_t^T and theta_t=theta_(t-1)+gamma_t*R_t^(-1)*psi_t*epsilon_t, with positive-definite curvature handling as needed. The positive update sign corresponds to minus the residual-loss gradient because epsilon=y-yhat. R approximates curvature; it is not the exact Hessian of every nonlinear objective. Other search directions belong to the broader prediction-error method family.
  • supports Source: Recursive prediction error parameter estimator for non-linear models · Locator: Section 3, Equations 18–25, PDF p. 5 / printed p. 572
Mechanism #residual-dependent-sensitivity
For a fixed parameter theta and predictor theta^T*phi_t(theta) built from past residuals, the total sensitivity is psi_t=phi_t-sum_i[theta^T*(partial phi_t/partial epsilon_(t-i))]*psi_(t-i). The online algorithm uses previous parameter estimates and stored residuals/sensitivities. This is a recursive approximation based on the fixed-parameter derivative, not exact differentiation through the entire sequence of changing parameter updates. Treating phi as parameter-independent generally changes the direction.
  • supports Source: Recursive prediction error parameter estimator for non-linear models · Locator: Sections 2–3, Equations 8–12 and 24–25, PDF pp. 3–5 / printed pp. 570–572
Mechanism #linear-noise-filter
With a nonlinear process predictor and a constant-coefficient linear lagged-error polynomial C(q^-1)=1+sum_i c_i*q^-i, the source writes the sensitivity recursion as filtering the current regressor through 1/C. The leading coefficient is 1. Predictor/filter stability concerns residual reconstruction and differs from stability of the physical system dynamics. Merely making the noise terms linear in past errors does not guarantee a stable filter.
  • supports Source: Recursive prediction error parameter estimator for non-linear models · Locator: Sections 2–4.1, Equations 15,26–30, PDF pp. 4,6 / printed pp. 571,573
Mechanism #inverse-curvature-form
An algebraically equivalent inverse update uses P_t=[P_(t-1)-P_(t-1)*psi_t*psi_t^T*P_(t-1)/(lambda_t+psi_t^T*P_(t-1)*psi_t)]/lambda_t and theta_t=theta_(t-1)+P_t*psi_t*epsilon_t, where P_t=gamma_t*R_t^(-1) and lambda_t=gamma_(t-1)*(1-gamma_t)/gamma_t. The equivalence requires well-defined inverses and nonzero denominators; the gain and forgetting factors are linked rather than arbitrary independent choices. Exact two-dimensional calculations check this identity for a specified positive-definite common state.
  • supports Source: Recursive prediction error parameter estimator for non-linear models · Locator: Section 3, Equations 19–23, PDF p. 5 / printed p. 572; exact curator calculations in research/discovery/recursive-estimation-boundaries.py
Limitation #conditional-convergence
The ODE-based convergence discussion assumes a stable predictor domain, admissible parameter restrictions, suitable smoothness and data/moment limits, decreasing gains, and positive-definite curvature. It concerns local behavior under those conditions, not unconditional global minimization. For general nonlinear noise the stable domain can depend on unknown input/output statistics, preventing straightforward enforcement. The source’s recommendation to use simpler noise models is not a universal convergence proof.
  • supports Source: Recursive prediction error parameter estimator for non-linear models · Locator: Section 4.1, conditions (a)–(g) and Equations 32–41, PDF pp. 7–8 / printed pp. 574–575; Section 4.4, PDF p. 15 / printed p. 582
Application #fixed-structure-identification-use
The paper applies recursive prediction-error estimation to a simulated closed-loop polynomial system and a measured pilot liquid-level system. Model structures are determined beforehand. For the liquid-level example, recursive and offline estimators use the same selected NARMAX structure. These examples establish a documented parameter-identification use; they are not held-out accuracy, causal recovery or universal superiority evidence.
  • supports Source: Recursive prediction error parameter estimator for non-linear models · Locator: Section 6, Examples 1–2, Tables 1–3 and Figures 1–6, PDF pp. 17–26 / printed pp. 584–593

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The inspected mechanism evidence is the complete 1989 polynomial-NARMAX treatment. Earlier recursive-identification sources and broader filtered, vector, nonquadratic, rational and output-affine variants remain follow-up scopes; no priority claim is made.
  • Model structure, initialization, gain or forgetting schedule, numerical conditioning and predictor stability require separate choices. A recursive parameter estimator does not select a correct model structure, establish physical causality or guarantee out-of-sample accuracy.
  • The source defines m-invertibility by mean-square residual-initialization forgetting. Almost-sure or exponential forgetting is not inferred solely from that definition. Its nonlinear noise examples and convergence extensions require their stated assumptions; no unrestricted theorem is adopted.
  • All 27 rendered PDF pages were read, including the experiments and references. Exact examples are curator derivations, not replications of the source experiments. No model training, empirical benchmark or paid service was run. DRAFT status and author self-review do not imply independent scientific approval.
