Univariate marginal distribution algorithm

Algorithms AIMech:UnivariateMarginalDistributionAlgorithm DRAFT

The original unsmoothed binary population optimizer that selects a nonempty set of evaluated bit strings, replaces each coordinate probability by its frequency in that selected set, and generates the next population from the product of those Bernoulli marginals before repeating selection and refitting.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #selected-binary-frequency-refit
For bit strings x in {0,1}^n, an iteration selects M candidates from an evaluated population of N candidates, with positive integers 1<=M<=N. The next probability p_i of bit i being one is its empirical frequency in that selected set, counting the selected occurrences. Each zero probability is 1-p_i. The algorithm directly refits these univariate marginals rather than preserving pairwise parental chromosomes or estimating a full joint table. The nonempty-selection condition is necessary for the frequency estimate.
  • supports Source: From recombination of genes to the estimation of distributions I. Binary parameters · Locator: Section 3, PDF pp. 3–4, Equation 7 and UMDA Step 1.
  • supports Source: Evolutionary Algorithms: From Recombination to Search Distributions · Locator: Section 3, internal/PDF p. 10, UMDA Step 1 and the n versus 2n parameter discussion.
Mechanism #product-bernoulli-resampling
The next population consists of N newly generated candidates from the product of the fitted binary marginals. Within a generated string, coordinate i is Bernoulli(p_i), independently of the other coordinates under the sampling law. Thus the fitted law discards dependencies not determined by univariate frequencies. For example, the selected sets {00,11} and {01,10} both give p_1=p_2=1/2 and hence the same uniform law over all four strings. This direct consequence of the product rule does not assert exact empirical independence in every finite sampled population.
  • supports Source: From recombination of genes to the estimation of distributions I. Binary parameters · Locator: Section 3, PDF pp. 3–4, product form and UMDA Step 2. The two selected-set example is a direct deduction from the displayed rule.
  • supports Source: Evolutionary Algorithms: From Recombination to Search Distributions · Locator: Section 3, internal/PDF pp. 9–10, Equation 13 and UMDA Step 2.
Scope #selection-and-stopping-remain-explicit
The original skeleton begins with a randomly generated population, repeats selection, marginal estimation and resampling, and stops according to a termination criterion. It leaves the selection schedule and stopping details open. The later chapter discusses proportionate, truncation and tournament selection as different policies. The generic skeleton does not impose one elite fraction, a universal tie convention or a certified global-optimum stopping test; these choices must be specified for a particular run.
  • supports Source: From recombination of genes to the estimation of distributions I. Binary parameters · Locator: Section 3, PDF p. 4, UMDA Steps 0–3.
  • supports Source: Evolutionary Algorithms: From Recombination to Search Distributions · Locator: Section 3, internal/PDF p. 10, UMDA steps; Section 3.2, internal/PDF p. 13, selection alternatives.
Scope #unsmoothed-update-versus-sumda
Original UMDA replaces the coordinate probability by the selected frequency r_i. The separately described smoothed update is p_i,new=p_i+lambda*(r_i-p_i), which recovers that replacement when lambda=1. For 0<lambda<1 it retains part of the preceding parameter value. This distinction identifies the original unsmoothed mechanism; it does not make SUMDA, all full PBIL implementations or every modern UMDA variant exact aliases of this record.
  • supports Source: From recombination of genes to the estimation of distributions I. Binary parameters · Locator: Section 3, PDF p. 4, UMDA; Section 4, PDF p. 6, Equation 14 and SUMDA.
  • supports Source: Evolutionary Algorithms: From Recombination to Search Distributions · Locator: Section 4, internal/PDF p. 16, Equations 38–39 and lambda=1 boundary.
Mechanism #ideal-proportionate-selection-recurrence
For an exact product-Bernoulli population law, nonnegative fitness f and positive mean W=E[f(X)], ideal proportionate selection gives p_i,new=p_i*E[f(1,X_-i)]/W, where the expectation is over the remaining coordinates under the product law. Equivalently, p_i,new-p_i=p_i*(1-p_i)*(partial W/partial p_i)/W. The later chapter states this result for infinite populations. It is not the exact update of each realized finite sampled generation, not the formula for arbitrary truncation or tournament selection, and not a guarantee of reaching a global optimum.
  • supports Source: From recombination of genes to the estimation of distributions I. Binary parameters · Locator: Section 3, PDF p. 4, Theorem 3 and Equations 8–10.
  • supports Source: Evolutionary Algorithms: From Recombination to Search Distributions · Locator: Section 2, internal/PDF p. 3, nonnegative fitness convention; Section 3, internal/PDF pp. 10–11, Theorem 5, Equations 15–17 and proof.
Limitation #finite-population-support-loss
Without mutation, probability margins or another support-restoring operation, a selected coordinate frequency of zero or one is absorbing for subsequent product sampling and refitting. More specifically, if a coordinate has probability p strictly between zero and one and N candidates are independently sampled, the event that every candidate has zero at that coordinate has probability (1-p)^N>0. Every nonempty selection from that population then sets the fitted coordinate probability to zero. This is a possible fixation event, not a quantified overall failure probability or a claim that every run fails.
  • supports Source: From recombination of genes to the estimation of distributions I. Binary parameters · Locator: Section 3, PDF p. 4, UMDA selection and resampling steps; absorption and the displayed event probability are direct deductions under independent sampling.
  • supports Source: Evolutionary Algorithms: From Recombination to Search Distributions · Locator: Section 8, internal/PDF pp. 32–34, finite-population fixation discussion.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This record covers the original unsmoothed binary procedure. Probability margins, mutation, smoothing, restarts, nonbinary distribution families and later runtime-analysis variants change or supplement that procedure. The skeleton does not completely specify selection multiplicities, tie handling, initialization probabilities or termination.
  • A product sampling law does not guarantee independence of the empirical coordinates in every finite sample, or preserve dependencies in selected candidates. More expressive conditional models are separate constructions; the original article expressly leaves CDA incomplete.
  • The proportionate-selection equations require nonnegative fitness and positive mean fitness. They cannot be applied unchanged to arbitrary signed objectives, zero mean fitness, truncation selection or a particular finite sampled generation. The chapter’s continuous expected-fitness interpretation does not certify global optimization: on a two-bit landscape with f(00)=f(11)=2 and f(01)=f(10)=1, p_1=p_2=1/2 is stationary under the ideal recurrence despite a larger attainable mean fitness at a maximizer. That stationary law still samples optimal strings with positive probability.
  • The 1996 SUMDA fixed-selected-frequency result assumes r(t)=c; it is not a general adaptive-convergence theorem when selected frequencies change. The basic blend is cited from its update equation. The later chapter’s function-specific finite-population examples and conjectural sample-size discussion are not universal bounds.
  • Formula-level overlap does not establish full-method equivalence. Under matching selection and tie policies, an unsmoothed Bernoulli cross-entropy refit can produce the same next distribution. Full incremental PBIL with mutation, natural evolution strategies and information-geometric optimization have distinct maintained scopes. No benchmark, author implementation or optimizer run was used to establish superiority or a convergence rate.
  • The primary 1996 paper was read from an author-hosted ten-page copy using OCR and selected formula visuals; its file build date is separate from publication. The later 39-page author manuscript is indexed as 2000 but associated with the 2001 published chapter. Its reading PDF is an explicitly documented derivative of the retained original PostScript after removal of a trailing MIME delimiter following the document EOF. The source hashes and conversion failures preserve that distinction; publisher-byte equivalence is not asserted.
