No-U-Turn sampler (original slice formulation)

Algorithms AIMech:OriginalNoUTurnSampler DRAFT
The original 2014 slice-based NUTS algorithm, which adapts a Hamiltonian trajectory’s length through randomly directed binary-tree doubling and selects a valid next state while enforcing subtree U-turn and reversibility conditions.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record is restricted to Hoffman and Gelman’s 2014 slice formulation, principally efficient Algorithm 3. Algorithm 2 is its simpler uniform-selection derivation. The paper’s acronym NUTS is context, not an asserted exact alias for every later algorithm with that name. Dual-averaging step-size warmup is separable from the fixed-step-size NUTS transition. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method
Refresh standard Gaussian momentum and draw a slice variable below the current joint density. Build a balanced trajectory tree by randomly extending forward or backward through 1, 2, 4 and further powers of two leapfrog steps. Check both endpoint dot products for the balanced subtrees; stop if either turns negative or the slice-relative joint log density falls below the allowed energy-error threshold. States outside the slice cannot be next samples. A stopping condition inside the newly added half-tree excludes that half-tree from candidate selection; a U-turn only at the completed whole-tree boundary permits its valid candidates. Efficient Algorithm 3 samples a representative within each new subtree in proportion to valid-state counts, then updates the retained candidate with probability min(1,n_new/n_old) when that new subtree is valid. Its transition preserves the uniform distribution over the valid candidate set; it is not simply uniform selection from the final whole tree.

Limitation #nuts-domain
The paper limits direct use to differentiable continuous coordinates, discusses transformations and separate treatment of discrete variables, and identifies mass-matrix changes as extensions rather than the unit-mass formulation itself.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Stopping one forward trajectory at its first U-turn without the paper’s balanced-tree construction and candidate exclusions is not the defined valid NUTS kernel.
  • The source mechanism uses identity mass and its corresponding Euclidean dot-product criterion. Nonidentity or position-dependent metrics require an appropriate formulation; no generalized metric rule is inferred here.
  • Eliminating a preset leapfrog-step count does not eliminate step size, numerical-divergence handling, model geometry, convergence assessment or implementation resource limits.
  • The efficient top-level candidate transition and uniform selection within a subtree are different operations. Zero valid-candidate counts require guarded implementation; no literal zero-over-zero probability is asserted.
  • This is not a specification of later multinomial NUTS or current Stan defaults. No author implementation was executed or performance result reproduced.
