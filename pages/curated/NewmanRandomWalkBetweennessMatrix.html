Newman random-walk betweenness matrix computation

Algorithms AIMech:NewmanRandomWalkBetweennessMatrix DRAFT
Aliases: Newman matrix algorithm for random-walk betweenness
An exact network-analysis algorithm that computes random-walk betweenness from an embedded inverse of a grounded graph Laplacian, explicitly enumerates source–target pairs, and averages their incident-current magnitudes with a specified endpoint convention.

Nine complete AIO contexts and six full native neighbors were assessed. The identity is the four-step matrix and explicit-pair procedure in Newman2003, not only the centrality measure. Its unit-resistance scope, endpoint convention and pair enumeration distinguish it from the later sorted-row algorithm, stochastic pair sampling, shortest-path dependency accumulation and PageRank. It is not a random-walk simulation or a learned predictor.

6 Claims
Scope #domain-and-grounded-inverse
The maintained domain is a simple connected undirected graph with n>=3 and unit edge resistances. Form L=D-A, remove one corresponding row and column, invert the reduced matrix, and embed it in T by restoring a zero row and column. For source s and target t, p_i=T_is-T_it gives potentials up to the chosen ground. The original source treats separate connected components separately; this record does not impose a whole-disconnected-graph normalization.
  • supports Source: A measure of betweenness centrality based on random walks · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.2, Equations 3–8 and four-step prescription, PDF/printed pp. 5–9
Mechanism #pair-enumeration-and-endpoints
For each unordered pair s<t, compute I_i(st)=one half the sum over adjacent j of |T_is-T_it-T_js+T_jt| for internal vertices i. Set I_s=I_t=1 using the source’s adopted endpoint convention. Return b_i=2/[n(n-1)] times the sum of I_i(st) over unordered pairs. The source also permits endpoints zero; changing that choice does not itself change the denominator into the later endpoint-excluded Brandes–Fleischer normalization.
  • supports Source: A measure of betweenness centrality based on random walks · Locator: Section 2.1, Equations 9–11, PDF/printed pp. 6–7; Section 2.2 prescription, p. 9
Mechanism #absorbing-walk-interpretation
A walk moves uniformly to an adjacent vertex and stops on first reaching t. With transient matrix M_t=A_t D_t^(-1), the fundamental matrix (I-M_t)^(-1) gives expected transient visits. The method differences expected directed traversals of each edge before taking its absolute value and aggregating incident edges. It is not ordinary node occupancy or the expected absolute net count of an individual walk. In a unit complete four-vertex graph with source 0 and target 3, expected transitions 1-to-2 and 2-to-1 are each 1/4, so their difference is zero. Yet the first-hit paths 0-1-2-3 and 0-2-1-3 each have probability 1/27 and opposite nonzero net counts, proving the expectation of the absolute net count is at least 2/27. This is a curator example.
  • supports Source: A measure of betweenness centrality based on random walks · Locator: Section 2.2, Equations 12–13 and net-flow discussion, PDF/printed pp. 7–9; curator example in research/discovery/newman-randomwalk-boundaries.py
Scope #computation-and-identity
The original procedure forms one dense grounded inverse and explicitly evaluates pair currents. With ordinary dense inversion its arithmetic cost is O(n^3+mn^2), becoming O(n^3) on sparse graphs, and storage is O(n^2+m) with streamed pair accumulation. The source discusses the dense-matrix memory burden. This pair-enumeration algorithm is distinct from the later sorted-edge-row algorithm and from stochastic pair sampling; neither a random-walk interpretation nor a structural score makes it a simulation or a learned predictor.
  • supports Source: A measure of betweenness centrality based on random walks · Locator: Section 2.1, runtime paragraph and footnote 6, PDF/printed p. 7; Section 2.2 prescription, p. 9; storage with streamed pairs is explicit curator accounting
Limitation #normalization-comparison
For matched connected unit-resistance graphs with n>=3, let B_i be the later Brandes–Fleischer normalized endpoint-excluded score. Pair counting gives b_i=(n-2)B_i/n+2/n under Newman’s adopted endpoint-included convention. This is explicit curator algebra, not identical reported normalizations. On a unit three-vertex path, Newman’s scores are (2/3,1,2/3), while B is (0,1,0); on a unit triangle they are (7/9,7/9,7/9), while B is (1/3,1/3,1/3). Endpoint contribution must not be interpreted as evidence of internal bridging or causal importance.
  • supports Source: A measure of betweenness centrality based on random walks · Locator: Section 2.1, Equations 10–11, PDF/printed p. 7; curator pair-counting comparison in research/discovery/newman-randomwalk-boundaries.py
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Sections 3.1 and Definition 2, PDF pp. 4–5 / printed pp. 536–537
Limitation #evidence-and-application-limits
The inspected manuscript uses synthetic graph examples and contact, intermarriage and collaboration networks to illustrate the measure. These illustrations and descriptive comparisons do not validate causal transmission, political success or learned predictive accuracy. No historical runtime, numerical benchmark or author implementation was reproduced. The 2003 arXiv manuscript is the inspected method version; the later 2005 journal publication is bibliographic context, without an assertion of identical bytes.
  • supports Source: A measure of betweenness centrality based on random walks · Locator: Section 3, Figures 1–7 and Tables 1–2, PDF/printed pp. 2–14; Section 4, p. 14; arXiv version identification on p. 1

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The record restricts the algorithm to connected simple undirected unit-resistance graphs with n>=3. General conductance weighting, singleton components and whole-disconnected-graph normalization require explicit separate conventions.
  • Endpoint inclusion and the n(n-1)/2 unordered-pair denominator are part of the adopted output convention. The source’s optional endpoint exclusion does not imply the later (n-1)(n-2) normalization.
  • Expected directed edge counts are differenced before absolute values. Ordinary visits and the expected absolute signed count of a realized walk are different quantities; the finite example demonstrates that distinction.
  • The 15-page arXiv v1 manuscript, all 21 references, seven figures and two tables were read. No later journal-layout PDF, author software, corrected edition or raw historical data was inspected. The finite calculations are mechanism checks, not broad empirical or numerical-solver certification.
  • Graph prominence does not by itself establish causal influence or a validated AI predictor. DRAFT remains after author self-review.
