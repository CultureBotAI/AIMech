Pairwise Markov tree

Model Families AIMech:PairwiseMarkovTree DRAFT
Aliases: Arbre de Markov couple
A latent-variable tree-model family whose paired hidden and observed states factor into a root distribution and parent-to-child conditional kernels on a fixed rooted tree, without requiring the hidden marginal to be Markov on that tree.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Pairwise denotes Z_s=(X_s,Y_s), not generic pairwise edge potentials. The tree is the stochastic dependence topology across sites, rather than a decision-tree classifier, hierarchical mixture-of-experts gate structure, or hidden Markov decision tree’s within-time state hierarchy. An ordinary memoryless-emission hidden Markov tree is a constrained special case. A path is a degenerate tree, but the family permits branching dependencies absent from a sequential chain. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
On a finite fixed rooted tree with one parent for each nonroot site, specify a normalized root law p(z_r) and normalized kernels K_s(z_s|z_parent(s)); their product defines the joint paired distribution. At fixed complete y, the hidden posterior factors on the same rooted tree. Set beta_s(x_s)=1 at leaves and compute each internal beta_s(x_s) as the product over children t of sum_(x_t) K_t(x_t,y_t|x_s,y_s)*beta_t(x_t). The root posterior is proportional to p(x_r,y_r)*beta_r(x_r). Each child posterior transition at fixed parent is proportional to K_t*beta_t and normalized over x_t. Propagate the root marginal downward through these conditional kernels to obtain all hidden-site marginals. Classical independent-emission hidden Markov trees result from root pi(x_r)*b(y_r|x_r) and child kernels A(x_t|x_s)*b(y_t|x_t). General joint kernels can leave hidden siblings dependent given only their hidden parent, so the hidden marginal need not itself be a Markov tree.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The definition uses a fixed finite rooted tree; it is not a graph-structure learning algorithm, a loopy-field model or a decision-making policy. Exact tree recursions require finite hidden states or separately tractable integrals.
  • Posterior formulas require nonzero observation evidence and valid conditional support. Numerical scaling can be needed on large trees; the factorization alone does not address parameter estimation, missing observations or arbitrary topology changes.
  • Independence of the child hidden-state transition from the parent observation is sufficient for a hidden Markov-tree marginal. Proposition 3.2 asserts necessity under the additional hypotheses that each internal node has at least two children with identical conditional laws given its paired parent; that converse is not adopted without those hypotheses.
  • Correlated observations can coexist with a Markov hidden marginal. Non-Markov projection is permitted, not required, and memoryless site emissions are not necessary for every hidden-Markov special case.
  • The four-page source proves structural and inference results; it does not report a comparative empirical evaluation or establish universal segmentation superiority. Later parameter-estimation and image-processing papers remain separate evidence leads.
