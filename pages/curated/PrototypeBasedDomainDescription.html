Prototype-based domain description

Algorithms AIMech:PrototypeBasedDomainDescription DRAFT
Aliases: PDD radius-offset rule
A one-class decision rule that accepts a query when its metric distance to at least one prototype, plus that prototype's stored radius offset, is no greater than a common threshold.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope PDD is the radius-offset prediction rule, distinct from CPDD and FastCPDD algorithms for choosing its prototypes. Its dataset-derived construction stores each eligible reference point with its kth-neighbor distance measured against the original dataset. Agreement with infinity-norm NNDD on original reference observations does not make the rules identical on new queries. A qualified PDD alias avoids assigning every use of the acronym to this concept. Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #radius-offset-decision
Given metric d, positive threshold theta and prototype pairs (x_i,r_i), accept query y iff some i satisfies d(x_i,y)+r_i<=theta; reject otherwise. The score can be written as the minimum of these offset distances. Each eligible nonnegative radius contributes a metric ball with query radius theta-r_i. The general definition calls r_i real; interpreting r_i itself as a geometric radius requires nonnegativity, as provided by the dataset-derived construction. The rule is not a neighbor-label vote, local density ratio or calibrated probability.
  • supports Source: Prototype-based Domain Description for One-Class Classification · Locator: Section 2 and Definition 2.1, author p2 / PDF p3
Mechanism #dataset-derived-prototypes
For a finite dataset D and 1<=k<=|D|, compute each reference point's kth ordered neighbor distance in the full D, including itself at distance zero. Retain (x,r_x) only when r_x<=theta. These radii remain attached to selected prototypes; they are not recomputed after condensation. At k=1 all radii are zero and the full rule is a union of radius-theta balls. Equal-distance neighbor identities do not alter kth distance values, although selection ties can alter a subsequent condensed set.
  • supports Source: Prototype-based Domain Description for One-Class Classification · Locator: Definitions 2.2-2.3, author pp2-3 / PDF pp3-4; Section 4 prototype construction, author p5 / PDF p6
Limitation #reference-correspondence-not-global-identity
Theorem 2.4 gives agreement between the full derived PDD and infinity-norm NNDD on observations in D. Triangle inequality makes the derived PDD acceptance region a subset of the full NNDD region, but equality need not hold on new queries. For D={0,2}, k=2 and theta=2, both radii are 2: PDD accepts just its two prototype locations, whereas NNDD also accepts midpoint 1. This curator example rules out a global reading of the abstract's equivalence wording.
  • supports Source: Prototype-based Domain Description for One-Class Classification · Locator: Definition 2.3 and Theorem 2.4, author p3 / PDF p4; exact curator calculation in research/discovery/prototype-domain-boundaries.py
Limitation #consistent-subset-scope
A PDD-consistent subset preserves the full prototype rule's decisions at every original prototype location. For the eligible dataset-derived prototype set, all such locations are accepted, and preserving them also preserves all full-set NNDD decisions on D, including rejected observations omitted from the prototype set. Subsetting fixed prototypes can only shrink acceptance. Reference agreement therefore does not establish identical new-query decisions or universally improved generalization, nor does it require a minimum-cardinality subset.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The general decision rule and the nonnegative kth-distance construction have different scopes. Claims about reference equivalence here require the latter, a valid metric and self-included reference distances.
  • Printed kth-neighbor rank wording does not fully specify distance ties; this record uses the ordered-distance interpretation. Theorem 2.6 prints an acceptance sign in its rejection case; the defining rule determines the rejection.
  • Population examples relating parameters to statistical outlier tests do not supply distribution-free finite-sample error calibration. No universal threshold, density interpretation or numerical performance advantage is curated.
  • All 15 author-PDF pages were visually read. Exact finite calculations were executed; no historical implementation, trained model or benchmark was reproduced.
