Hidden–observed pair Markov random field

Model Families AIMech:HiddenObservedPairMarkovRandomField DRAFT
Aliases: Pairwise hidden–observed Markov field
A latent-variable spatial-model family in which each site contains a hidden label and an observation, and their joint paired field has a normalized Gibbs factorization on a specified neighborhood graph, without requiring either marginal field to be Markov on that graph.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the hidden–observed process-pair construction called pairwise Markov random fields by Pieczynski and Tebbache. Pairwise denotes Z_s=(X_s,Y_s), not a restriction to two-site clique potentials or the generic pairwise Markov property. The descriptive local label avoids those homonyms. The existing generic MarkovRandomField record does not specify this hidden–observed partition or its conditional-field construction. Classical hidden Markov fields with a Markov label prior and independent site emissions are a constrained special case; pairwise Markov chains and trees impose different graph topologies. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a finite site set and specified undirected neighborhood graph, assign clique energies phi_c(x_c,y_c) and define p(x,y)=Z^(-1)*exp(-sum_c phi_c(x_c,y_c)), with a finite positive partition function. At fixed complete y, the posterior p(x|y) retains clique factors on the same graph; fixing x likewise gives a local conditional observation field. Summing over one field can introduce dependencies beyond that graph, so neither p(x) nor p(y) is required to retain its Markov structure. A classical hidden Markov field p(x)*product_s b_(x_s)(y_s) is recovered by selecting label-clique and site-emission energies. This construction permits spatially correlated observation models without separately specifying a conditional likelihood whose configuration-dependent normalization could destroy locality of the label posterior. Local conditional probabilities support Gibbs sampling of hidden labels and approximation of sitewise posterior marginals; preserving the exact model factorization does not make finite sampling exact.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The definition presupposes a proper joint distribution. For the continuous Gaussian examples, each hidden configuration needs a positive-definite observation precision matrix and a finite joint partition function; positive single-site conditional variances alone do not ensure this.
  • The source’s Gaussian displays contain normalization, completed-square and variance inconsistencies. For a local energy alpha+beta*y+delta*y^2 with delta>0, integrating exp(-energy) gives sqrt(pi/delta)*exp(-alpha+beta^2/(4*delta)); its conditional mean is -beta/(2*delta) and variance is 1/(2*delta). Off-diagonal precision makes inverse diagonal precision differ from marginal variance. This record does not certify the printed Gaussian sampling implementation.
  • The first eight-neighbor example requires particular caution: under unit diagonal precision and -0.2 on each unordered neighbor edge, a 4-by-4 open grid has a negative constant-vector quadratic form. This is a curator calculation under explicit counting and boundary conventions, not a claim about uninspected author code.
  • Posterior Markov structure is not a guarantee of efficient exact inference on a loopy field, Gibbs mixing, parameter identifiability or a globally optimal segmentation. Per-site marginal modes and a joint maximum-probability configuration are different objectives.
  • Section 5 leaves observation-only parameter estimation through iterative conditional estimation as future work. The synthetic texture illustrations and inconsistent sampling-count caption do not support a universal performance claim.
  • The inspected PDF footer says Machine GRAPHICS & VISION 9(4), 2000, pp.705–718 and its proof header is dated February 13, 2001; the author bibliography and later citations say issue 3. The exact inspected bytes and this discrepancy are retained.
