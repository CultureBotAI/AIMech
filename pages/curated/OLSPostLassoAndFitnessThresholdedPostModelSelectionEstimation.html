Aliases: Gauss-Lasso and OLS Post-Fit Lasso High-Dimensional Sparse Regression, OLS Post-Model Selection with Sublinear Restricted Sparse Eigenvalue Sparsity Bounds, Data-Driven Fitness-Thresholded Lasso (fit-Lasso) and Post-Selection Least Squares
OLS post-Lasso and fitness-thresholded post-model selection estimation are a nonasymptotic two-step high-dimensional nonparametric regression methodology introduced by Belloni and Chernozhukov (2013, Bernoulli 19(2):521-547, arXiv:1001.0188v5, DOI:10.3150/11-BEJ410) that applies ordinary least squares (OLS) to the covariate support T_hat(t) := support(beta_hat(t)) selected by a first-step L1-penalized Lasso estimator beta_hat in argmin_beta {Q_hat(beta) + (lambda/n)||beta||_1} with data-driven noise-gradient quantile penalty lambda = c' sigma_hat Lambda(1 - alpha | X) (Equation 2.12). Benchmarking against the oracle risk minimization program min_{0<=k<=p^n} (c_k^2 + sigma^2 k / n) with oracle target beta_0, support T (|T| = s), and approximation error c_s^2 = E_n[(f_. - x_.' beta_0)^2] (Equations 2.2-2.4), Section 3.3 combines empirical presparsity (Lemma 2) with the sublinearity of restricted sparse eigenvalues phi(ceil(ell k)) <= ceil(ell) phi(k) (Lemma 3) to prove the sharp false-positive sparsity bound m_hat := |T_hat \ T| <= s [min_{m in M} phi(m ^ n)] L_n = O_P(s) (Theorem 3). For any generic first-step selector T_hat, Theorem 4 proves via Samorodnitsky-Talagrand empirical process control (Lemmas 4-5) that the post-selection OLS estimator beta_tilde in argmin_{beta_{T_hat^c}=0} Q_hat(beta) satisfies ||f_tilde - f||_{P_n,2} <= K_varepsilon sigma sqrt((m_hat log p + (m_hat + s) log(e mu(m_hat))) / n) + 3 c_s + sqrt((B_n)_+ ^ (C_n)_+) where B_n := Q_hat(beta_hat) - Q_hat(beta_0) and C_n := Q_hat(beta_{0 T_hat}) - Q_hat(beta_0), establishing that OLS post-Lasso (t = 0) and OLS post-fit Lasso (with data-driven goodness-of-fit threshold t_gamma := max{t >= 0 : Q_hat(beta_tilde^t) - Q_hat(beta_hat) <= gamma}, gamma <= 0, Equations 2.9 and 2.14) achieve the near-oracle rate O_P(sigma sqrt(s log p / n) + c_s) even when model selection misses true components (T not subseteq T_hat) and achieve strictly faster rates than Lasso (down to the exact oracle rate O_P(sigma sqrt(s/n) + c_s)) when T subseteq T_hat and m_hat = o_P(s) (Theorems 5-6, Corollaries 2-3).

Distinct from AIMech:DoubleDebiasedMachineLearningWithNeymanOrthogonalityAndCrossFitting (Chernozhukov et al. 2018) and AIMech:DebiasedLassoWithNodewisePrecisionMatrixRelaxation (van de Geer et al. 2014), which construct Neyman-orthogonal scores or one-step nodewise-precision-debiased estimators for asymptotically normal confidence intervals of low-dimensional target parameters, whereas the present concept analyzes unpenalized second-step OLS refitting on the support T_hat(t) selected by Lasso, thresholded Lasso (t-Lasso), or data-driven fitness-thresholded Lasso (fit-Lasso) for full nonparametric regression function estimation under the min{(B_n)_+, (C_n)_+} empirical-fit trade-off (Theorem 4) and sublinear restricted sparse eigenvalue sparsity bounds (Lemma 3, Theorem 3). Distinct from AIMech:SimultaneousNormalEquationPostSelectionConfidenceRegions (Kuchibhotla et al. 2020) and AIMech:UniformInSubmodelDeterministicLinearRegressionAndFunctionalDependenceBounds (Kuchibhotla et al. 2021), which provide uniform-over-all-submodels deterministic inequalities and post-selection confidence regions rather than two-step Lasso/fit-Lasso selection and OLS refitting.

Scope #fitness-thresholded-lasso-binary-search-and-post-thresholded-lasso-tradeoff
Sections 2.3, 2.5, and 5.2-5.3 (pp. 7-9 and 17-20, Theorems 6-7, Corollary 3, Remarks 5.2-5.3, Equations 2.8-2.10, 2.13-2.14, and 5.2-5.4) introduce the data-driven fitness-based threshold t_gamma := max{t >= 0 : Q_hat(beta_tilde^t) - Q_hat(beta_hat) <= gamma} for gamma <= 0 (specifically gamma = (Q_hat(beta_tilde^0) - Q_hat(beta_hat)) / 2 < 0, Equation 2.14), which is computable exactly by binary search over sort{|beta_hat_j| : j in T_hat} in at most ceil(log_2 |T_hat|) OLS solves (p. 9) and guarantees that OLS post-fit Lasso obeys Theorem 5's rate bound with m_hat replaced by the strictly smaller or equal false-positive count m_tilde := |T_tilde(t_gamma) \ T| <= m_hat (Theorem 6, Equation 5.2), outperforming OLS post-Lasso whenever m_tilde = o_P(m_hat) and T subseteq T_tilde wp -> 1 (Remark 5.2); by contrast, traditional hard thresholding at level t (OLS post-t Lasso, Theorem 7) incurs an explicit goodness-of-fit loss gamma_t := ||beta_hat_t - beta_hat||_{2,n} <= t sqrt(phi(m_t) m_t) whenever T not subseteq T_tilde(t), requiring t = O(sigma sqrt(log p / n)) (e.g., t = c_tilde lambda / n) to preserve the near-oracle rate (Equation 5.4).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Homoscedastic Gaussian Error Assumption (Condition M) and Restricted Eigenvalue / Restricted Sparse Eigenvalue Conditions: The nonasymptotic finite-sample bounds in Theorems 1-7 assume fixed (or conditioned) covariates with i.i.d. Gaussian errors epsilon_i ~ N(0, sigma^2) (Condition M, Equation 2.1), restricted eigenvalue kappa(c_bar) > 0 (Condition RE(c_bar)), and restricted sparse eigenvalue kappa_tilde(m_hat) > 0 (Condition RSE(m_hat)).
  • Sensitivity of Traditional Hard Thresholding (OLS Post-t Lasso) to Imperfect Support Selection: Unlike OLS post-Lasso and OLS post-fit Lasso, traditional hard thresholding at level t (Theorem 7) can suffer degraded convergence rates or inconsistency when true oracle coefficients are not well-separated from zero and t is chosen larger than O(sigma sqrt(log p / n)), incurring the extra fit loss gamma_t = ||beta_hat_t - beta_hat||_{2,n} <= t sqrt(phi(m_t) m_t) whenever T not subseteq T_tilde(t).
  • Primary-Source Typographical Notes in Belloni & Chernozhukov (2013, arXiv:1001.0188v5): Verified five self-correcting typographical slips in the primary manuscript: (1) Section 2.3 (p. 7, paragraph below Equation 2.10) prints 'thresholded Lassol' instead of 'thresholded Lasso,'; (2) Lemma 1 (p. 10, first display) prints '||delta||' in the denominators of the quadratic forms delta' E[x_tilde x_tilde'] delta instead of '||delta||^2' (cf. Condition RSE(m) on p. 9); (3) Theorem 4 (p. 15, display 2, bound on C_n) prints 'k_hat' inside log(s choose k_hat) + k_hat log(e mu(0)) without defining k_hat in the theorem statement (by Lemma 4(2) just below on p. 15, k_hat := |T \ T_hat| is the number of missed oracle components); (4) Remark 5.3 (p. 20, paragraph 2, line 6) refers to 'Lasso's rate of convergence given in Lemma 1' where it means 'Corollary 1' (p. 12); and (5) Proof of Theorem 1 (p. 21, display 2) drops the factor 'c_bar' before '||delta_T||_1' in the middle inequality of the chain for ||delta_{T^c}||_1 > 2 c_bar ||delta_T||_1, while the final inequality '<= (1/2) ||delta_{T^c}||_1 + (c/(c-1))(n/lambda) c_s^2' holds directly from c_bar ||delta_T||_1 < (1/2) ||delta_{T^c}||_1.
