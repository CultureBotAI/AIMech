Nearest-neighbor ratio data description

Algorithms AIMech:NearestNeighborRatioDataDescription DRAFT
Aliases: Nearest neighbor distance ratio data description, Nearest-neighbor data description (Tax-Duin)
A target-class data-description algorithm that scores a query by its distance to a nearest reference observation divided by that observation's nearest-other-reference spacing, and accepts queries whose ratio does not exceed a chosen threshold, where the denominator and neighbor-selection conventions are well defined.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the local distance-ratio detector described in Tax-Duin1998 Equation3 and Tax-Duin2000 Equations4-6. The 2000 paper studies a unit threshold and threshold sweeps; the 1998 paper uses a different, incompletely specified calibration convention. The core score is shared, but the complete experimental protocols are not asserted identical. The denominator concerns the selected reference point's own nearest other reference, not necessarily the query's second-nearest point. This method does not vote among class labels, average regression values, learn a supervised distance matrix, label density-connected components or optimize an enclosing sphere. Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #local-spacing-score
For a reference set with at least two observations, select i minimizing ||x-x_i||_2 and another reference j!=i minimizing ||x_i-x_j||_2. When d2=||x_i-x_j||_2 is positive, use rho(x)=||x-x_i||_2/d2. The 2000 default accepts rho<=1; increasing the threshold expands acceptance for a fixed reference set and neighbor-selection rule. The score uses Euclidean distances, not their squares, and the density motivation does not make rho a normalized probability or an outlier posterior.

Limitation #duplicates-ties-and-region
The denominator requires excluding the selected reference observation itself. Duplicate coordinates can still make d2=0, including an undefined 0/0 for a matching query; a singleton reference set has no other neighbor. The sources do not specify a general duplicate or nearest-tie policy. Ties can affect the decision: for references {-1/10,0,2} and query 1, choosing 0 gives ratio 10 while choosing 2 gives 1/2. The nearest-selected rule is also not generally the union of all reference-centered balls with radii equal to local spacing: query 9/10 in the same example is rejected at unit threshold although it lies inside the radius-2 ball centered at 2. These are exact curator calculations, not repairs attributed to the papers.

  • supports Source: Outlier Detection using Classifier Instability · Locator: Section 2.1, PDF pp.2-3, Equation 3
Limitation #coverage-and-calibration
With distinct reference coordinates and queries evaluated against the full reference set, each training point has ratio zero and is accepted at a nonnegative threshold. This does not guarantee acceptance of all future target observations, minimum-volume support estimation or a calibrated false-alarm rate. The 1998 calibration text says three standard deviations above the training-score mean, while its displayed Equation6 uses three times var. Its nonzero training-ratio histogram also requires a scoring convention beyond naive self-including resubstitution; the exact exclusion protocol is not established here. These settings are not silently substituted for the 2000 unit-threshold rule.

  • supports Source: Outlier Detection using Classifier Instability · Locator: Section 2.2 and experimental protocol, PDF pp.3-4, Equation 6 and Figure 1
Limitation #evidence-and-noise-scope
The rule depends on individual reference locations, feature scaling and threshold choice, and is sensitive to noise and sparse internal regions. The 2000 study compares synthetic uniform, Gaussian, heavy-tailed and embedded low-variance settings, with target-coverage versus outlier-acceptance tradeoffs. Its pump example uses 96 training objects with 64 autoregressive features and compares reduced representations and other detectors. The 1998 digit study reports poor nearest-neighbor results in many settings and includes training observations in its normal test population. Neither source establishes universal small-sample or high-dimensional superiority; no numerical performance claim or minimum-volume theorem is inferred from their summaries.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Duplicate, tie, reference-exclusion and threshold conventions affect reproducibility; unspecified policies remain explicit instead of being invented.
  • The 1998 and 2000 papers share a score but differ in calibration and evaluation. Source shorthand concerning density, coverage and minimum volume is not treated as a universal guarantee.
  • All thirteen cited PDF pages were visually read. The 2000 document is a scan; extraction was insufficient and all equations and figures were checked from renders. Exact finite calculations were executed, without historical code, model training or benchmark reproduction.
