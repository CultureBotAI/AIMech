Boltzmann chain

Model Families AIMech:BoltzmannChain DRAFT
A family of tied-weight undirected Boltzmann sequence models with hidden temporal variables, observed variables and chain-derived connectivity chosen to permit exact elimination.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The Saul–Jordan construction includes a strictly linear categorical chain and specified coupled-parallel or looped variants. It denotes an energy parameterization and temporal graph family, not a sampled Monte Carlo chain, an RBM without hidden-hidden connections, or a generic directed factorial HMM. The linear positive-potential case overlaps HMM distributions under the terminal conditions established by MacKay; different parameter names alone do not establish additional distributional expressiveness. More complex source architectures are not automatically covered by that linear equivalence. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method
In the linear categorical case, for fixed length L and inverse temperature beta, assign joint probability proportional to exp(beta[Pi_(s_1)+sum_(t=1)^(L-1) A_(s_t,s_(t+1))+sum_(t=1)^L B_(s_t,y_t)]), normalized over all hidden paths and observation sequences by Z_L. A and B are tied across time and need not themselves be log-normalized transition or emission probabilities. Observed likelihood is the ratio of a clamped and a free partition function. A log-likelihood derivative with respect to a tied interaction is beta times its expected count under the clamped distribution minus its expected count under the free distribution. For the selected tractable graphs, exact variable elimination computes these quantities: pruning sums out leaves, decimation sums over an intermediate state, and parallel interactions combine additively in the energy. The paper uses particular coupled chains at different time resolutions and selected loops that preserve an eliminable structure; tractability depends on intermediate state-space size, not merely the presence of temporal edges.

Scope #linear-hmm-boundary
For the strictly linear finite-energy model, a positive eigenvector of the transfer matrix allows interaction weights to be transformed into normalized HMM parameters. A remaining factor depends only on the final hidden state. Conditioning both models on the same fixed terminal state removes that factor from normalized relative probabilities; more complex architectures are outside this equivalence result.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The model is globally normalized at each sequence length. Separately normalizing each energy table is generally not an equivalent model transformation.
  • MacKay’s positive-matrix gauge transformation produces normalized HMM parameters plus a final-state factor. Equivalence requires the same constrained final state and normalization over that constrained sample space, or explicit handling of the terminal factor. It is not evidence that every unrestricted-terminal HMM and linear energy parameterization agree.
  • The inspected equivalence proof assumes strictly positive transfer entries from finite energies. Reducible or zero-support cases need separate conditions. It does not cover every coupled or looped graph.
  • Exact elimination can become exponential in graph width or state size. The source’s chosen chain/loop motifs do not establish efficient exact inference for arbitrary Boltzmann networks.
  • First-order hidden dynamics can retain long-lag statistical correlations. The source’s wording about absent long-term dependence is not interpreted as zero correlation at all nonadjacent times; extra direct long-range links are a different structural property.
  • The synthetic learning curves do not certify universal gains, real-data effectiveness or scalability. No author implementation, model fitting or benchmark was executed.
