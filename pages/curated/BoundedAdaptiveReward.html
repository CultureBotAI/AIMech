Bounded adaptive reward for sequence scoring

Mathematical Methods AIMech:BoundedAdaptiveReward DRAFT
A beam-dependent sequence rescoring rule that adds per-step rewards derived from the beam’s mean negative log token probability, accumulating them only up to a predicted output-length cap.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Yang et al.’s bounded adaptive-reward changes the reward rate with the decoding step, unlike fixed-r bounded word-reward. It is a beam-conditioned scoring method rather than a reinforcement-learning reward-training algorithm or a beam-independent probability model. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a b-item beam at step t, compute r_t=−(1/b)·sum_i log p(word_i), using the last generated token probability of each beam item in its own context. Add accumulated r_t values to the sequence log probability up to the smaller of the candidate length and predicted length. After the cap is passed, no additional reward accrues, so the adjusted score cannot increase with further nonpositive log-probability increments. The source then permits stopping when the best current adjusted score is below the best completed adjusted score.
  • supports Source: Breaking the Beam Search Curse: A Study of (Re-)Scoring Methods and Stopping Criteria for Neural Machine Translation · Locator: Section 4.2.2, Equations (10)–(11), and Section 5.2 final paragraph, printed pp. 3056–3057 / PDF pp. 3–4

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The reward sequence depends on the beam and model probabilities. Changing beam policy can change the score assigned to a completed hypothesis.
  • Removing a manually tuned constant reward rate does not remove the trained translation model, length predictor, beam size or other implementation choices.
  • The printed sum uses a predicted length as its upper limit without specifying a noninteger rounding policy. A discrete implementation must define the effective integer cap and EOS/token counting; the record does not assert an author-approved rounding convention.
  • Equation (10) assumes b available beam entries and defined token log probabilities. Empty or shrunken beams and zero-probability tokens require an explicit policy.
  • The stated stopping argument applies after reward saturation with a completed incumbent. It is limited to retained beam continuations, not global sequence optimality or guaranteed quality improvement.
