KADABRA balanced bidirectional shortest-path sampling

Algorithms AIMech:KADABRABalancedBidirectionalPathSampling DRAFT

Aliases: KADABRA balanced bidirectional BFS sampler, KADABRA shortest-path sampler
A shortest-path sampling algorithm that expands complete breadth-first-search frontiers from opposite endpoints according to frontier edge volume, then samples a connecting edge and its two path segments with shortest-path-count weights to obtain a uniform shortest path.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Scope #conditional-path-domain
For a finite simple unweighted directed or undirected graph and distinct specified endpoints s and t, the output is a uniformly sampled shortest s-to-t path when one exists, and an empty result otherwise. This conditional sampler does not itself select the endpoint distribution or estimate centrality. KADABRA’s enclosing estimator uses independent uniform ordered distinct-node pairs, which is an additional sampling layer.
  • supports Source: KADABRA is an ADaptive Algorithm for Betweenness via Random Approximation · Locator: Sections 2 and 4, PDF pp. 4–7; Appendix A notation, p. 15
Mechanism #balanced-frontier-expansion
Maintain source and target BFS layers, distances and exact shortest-path counts. At each step expand the entire current frontier with smaller incident-edge volume, breaking equal-volume ties by a fixed convention. In directed graphs the source search uses outgoing arcs and the target search uses incoming arcs. Collect all connecting edges encountered in the selected frontier before choosing a path. The balance criterion is edge volume, not merely the number of frontier vertices or the total sizes of the two search trees.
  • supports Source: KADABRA is an ADaptive Algorithm for Betweenness via Random Approximation · Locator: Section 4, PDF p. 7; explicit finite implementation interpretation in research/discovery/kadabra-path-boundaries.py
Mechanism #count-weighted-cut-and-backtracking
For a source-side meeting edge (v,w), assign weight sigma_sv times sigma_wt. Sample one meeting edge in proportion to this weight, then sample a shortest prefix from s to v and shortest suffix from w to t using path-count-proportional predecessor or successor choices. Their concatenation with the edge is a shortest s-to-t path. The product of cut-edge probability and prefix/suffix probabilities gives each complete shortest path probability 1/sigma_st. Reaching a vertex a second time at the same BFS distance must still contribute to its path count.
  • supports Source: KADABRA is an ADaptive Algorithm for Betweenness via Random Approximation · Locator: Section 4, PDF p. 7; path-count factorization checked in research/discovery/kadabra-path-boundaries.py. The probability product and equal-distance accumulation are explicit curator interpretation of the source’s counting requirement.
Limitation #finite-uniformity-boundaries
Exact curator enumeration checks every ordered distinct-endpoint pair in all 64 undirected and 4096 directed simple four-node graphs, including disconnected cases. A separate directed branching example has meeting-edge weights 2 and 1, giving three complete paths probability 1/3 each; uniform meeting-edge selection would instead give probabilities 1/4, 1/4 and 1/2. These finite cases illustrate why full-frontier processing and exact counts matter; they do not certify author software or all possible graph implementations.
  • supports Source: KADABRA is an ADaptive Algorithm for Betweenness via Random Approximation · Locator: Section 4, PDF p. 7; finite exhaustive and branching calculations in research/discovery/kadabra-path-boundaries.py
Limitation #complexity-and-model-scope
The sampler retains linear worst-case graph-traversal work; a selected 129-node path requires scanning 254 incident edges in the curator calculation. The source’s sublinear analyses concern specified configuration and rank-one inhomogeneous random-graph models with additional degree-distribution and supercriticality assumptions, not every graph or endpoint pair under every model. Its unit-cost arithmetic assumption also hides the bit cost of potentially exponential shortest-path counts. Weighted edges, approximate counts and a universal square-root runtime are outside this maintained scope.
  • supports Source: KADABRA is an ADaptive Algorithm for Betweenness via Random Approximation · Locator: Section 4 and Theorem 4, PDF pp. 7–8; Appendix E, pp. 20–24; long-path calculation in research/discovery/kadabra-path-boundaries.py
Limitation #separation-from-centrality-and-later-variants
The sampler is a subroutine distinct from KADABRA’s confidence budgets, bounded adaptive stopping and top-k output. Replacing an exact pair contribution by one sampled path indicator changes the estimator variance even when the mean target is unchanged. The 2016 historical experiments describe the sampler within a particular implementation and graph collection; they are not a general performance guarantee. Other balancing or parallelization variants are outside this record’s scope.
  • supports Source: KADABRA is an ADaptive Algorithm for Betweenness via Random Approximation · Locator: Abstract and Sections 2, 4 and 6, PDF pp. 1, 4–8 and 9–12; finite indicator-versus-fraction comparison in research/discovery/kadabra-path-boundaries.py. Other algorithms are not included in this maintained identity.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Exact simple unweighted graph paths and exact counts are assumed. Weighted shortest paths, multiedge conventions, finite-precision sampling and approximate count correction require separate analysis.
  • The conditional endpoint sampler does not by itself define a centrality estimator or its accuracy, confidence, stopping rule or endpoint population.
  • Uniformity requires processing the full chosen frontier and count-weighted meeting-edge and segment choices. A generic bidirectional search that returns its first meeting path is not equivalent.
  • Finite exact calculations and source interpretation are curator checks, not execution or verification of the authors’ implementation. Some random-graph proof displays were read only as extracted text.
  • No universal runtime improvement, current-software comparison or proof of every reported random-graph bound is asserted. DRAFT remains after author self-review.
