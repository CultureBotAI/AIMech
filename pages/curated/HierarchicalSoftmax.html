Hierarchical softmax

Layers & Modules AIMech:HierarchicalSoftmax DRAFT
A neural categorical-output component that assigns a probability to each vocabulary leaf by multiplying normalized branch probabilities along its path through a word tree.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record covers the hierarchical probability output component, including the binary sigmoid form described as hierarchical softmax by Mikolov and colleagues. Morin and Bengio’s parameter-sharing language model and Mnih and Hinton’s hierarchical log-bilinear model are complete architectures using this factorization, not aliases for the component. A Huffman tree, a WordNet-derived tree and a learned tree are construction choices with different scope. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
In a binary tree with one leaf per word, each internal node computes a branch probability from the input representation, commonly sigmoid of a node-vector dot product with optional bias. The opposite branch has its complement. Multiply the appropriate branch probabilities on the target word’s path to obtain its exact probability under this tree-parameterized distribution; probabilities over all leaves sum to one. The negative log probability is a sum of binary losses, so only nodes on the observed path contribute output-parameter gradients. For a sigmoid node logit and branch indicator y, that loss derivative is sigmoid(logit)-y. Computing one target probability and its gradient costs work proportional to its path length, in addition to representation computation. The source’s skip-gram variant uses internal-node output vectors and frequency-based Huffman codes. Multiple leaves assigned to one word require summing their disjoint path probabilities, as in the HLBL extension.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The tree parameterization defines a normalized distribution of its own. Calling it an approximation to full softmax does not assert equality to probabilities from a particular flat-softmax parameter set or provide a numerical approximation-error bound.
  • Logarithmic target cost requires suitable depth or weighted-average code length. A full binary tree alone can have linear worst-case depth. Even a Huffman code’s mean length need not be at most log2(vocabulary size): three equally frequent words have optimal mean length 5/3, exceeding log2(3). Thus the unqualified average-length sentence in Mikolov Section 2.1 is not adopted as a universal bound.
  • Computing every vocabulary probability requires at least linear output work. The short-path calculation does not by itself give a logarithmic-time exact top-k or most-probable-word search.
  • Shared internal nodes, tree construction and input representation constrain the fitted model. Multiple codes per word add paths and require their sum, rather than selecting an arbitrary single leaf or averaging leaf probabilities.
  • This component is not a whole language model, a hard decision tree, negative sampling, or Gumbel-Softmax sampling. No empirical speedup, embedding-quality ranking or author-code behavior is certified.
