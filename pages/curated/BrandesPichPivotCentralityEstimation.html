Brandes–Pich pivot-based centrality estimation

Algorithms AIMech:BrandesPichPivotCentralityEstimation DRAFT
Aliases: Brandes-Pich centrality estimation
A network-analysis procedure that estimates shortest-path closeness and betweenness from single-source computations at a selected subset of pivot vertices, aggregating distances or source dependencies and comparing the resulting relative centrality scores.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

7 Claims
Scope #domain-and-contributions
The explicit formulation here uses a connected simple undirected unweighted graph. A pivot contributes shortest-path distances for closeness, and one-sided dependencies for betweenness. A dependency sums, over targets, the fraction of shortest paths on which a vertex is internal; the source and target do not receive endpoint contributions. This is a structural statistic, not a learned prediction or causal measure.
  • supports Source: Centrality Estimation in Large Networks · Locator: Sections 2.1–3.2, PDF/printed pp. 3–8, Equations 1–5
Mechanism #relative-score-formulation
For a nonempty set P of distinct pivots, let D_P(v)=sum over p in P of d(p,v), and B_P(v)=sum over p in P of delta(p|v), with delta(v|v)=0. In the adopted sum-to-one comparison convention, closeness is [1/D_P(v)] divided by the sum of these reciprocals, and betweenness is B_P(v) divided by the sum of B_P over vertices. These are curator algebraic restatements of the paper’s contribution aggregation and experimental normalization: common positive extrapolation factors cancel. They do not assert that raw totals, unbiased estimators and normalized vectors are interchangeable.
  • supports Source: Centrality Estimation in Large Networks · Locator: Sections 3.1–3.2, PDF/printed pp. 7–8, Equations 4–5; Section 5.2, p. 13; curator algebra and finite checks in research/discovery/pivot-centrality-boundaries.py
Mechanism #pivot-selection
Random selects distinct pivots uniformly; RanDeg selects with probability proportional to degree. The distance-based strategies begin with a uniformly random first pivot, then choose an unselected vertex maximizing its minimum distance to previous pivots (MaxMin), maximizing its summed distance (MaxSum), or minimizing its summed distance (MinSum). Tie policies must be specified by an implementation. The manuscript’s Mixed strategy is not fixed here because its prose and Table 1 disagree about which sum-based strategy participates.
  • supports Source: Centrality Estimation in Large Networks · Locator: Section 4, PDF/printed pp. 9–10, Equations 6–7; Table 1, PDF p. 20
Scope #computation-and-exact-limit
Only k selected sources require single-source shortest-path work, rather than all n sources. Breadth-first distances and dependency accumulation on the stated unweighted domain give O(k(n+m)) traversal work, with pivot-selection overhead accounted for separately. Choosing every vertex recovers the relative exact centrality vectors whenever their denominators are defined. For a proper subset, neither the exact vector nor its ranking is guaranteed.
  • supports Source: Centrality Estimation in Large Networks · Locator: Sections 2.2–3, PDF/printed pp. 5–8; Section 4, pp. 9–10; Section 5.2, p. 13; finite all-pivot check in research/discovery/pivot-centrality-boundaries.py
Limitation #normalization-and-degeneracy
The relative-score convention requires positive denominators. Two or more distinct pivots make every sampled distance sum positive on a connected graph, whereas a single pivot has zero sampled distance at itself. A complete graph has zero internal-vertex betweenness, so its all-zero vector cannot be scaled to sum one. No undocumented fallback is inferred. On the constructed path 0–1–2–3, all pivots yield relative betweenness (0,1/2,1/2,0); pivots {0,2} yield (0,3/4,1/4,0).
  • supports Source: Centrality Estimation in Large Networks · Locator: Definitions and aggregation, Sections 2–3, PDF/printed pp. 3–8; sum-to-one comparison, Section 5.2, p. 13; explicit curator consequences in research/discovery/pivot-centrality-boundaries.py
Limitation #sampling-and-source-limits
The manuscript motivates sampling with an iid concentration display, while its experiments select distinct pivots. Random draws without replacement are not independent, and nonuniform or deterministic selection does not inherit an iid uniform-sampling guarantee. The maintained record therefore does not adopt that display as a certified accuracy bound. The printed n/(n-1) per-pivot factors and the prose describing a total-distance estimate also require distinguishing normalized mean quantities from raw totals; the selected relative-score formulation avoids equating those scales.
  • supports Source: Centrality Estimation in Large Networks · Locator: Section 3, PDF/printed pp. 7–8, Equations 3–5 and following prose; Section 4, p. 9; curator finite and scalar checks in research/discovery/pivot-centrality-boundaries.py
Limitation #evaluation-and-application-scope
The study compares six pivot strategies on three generated graph families and protein-interaction, needle-exchange and ticker-text networks, using repeated progressively enlarged pivot sets and exact centrality as the comparator. The score vectors are scaled to sum one. Prose describes Euclidean and inversion distances, while plots label the latter weighted inversion number. Observed-network sizes differ between Table 2 and figure labels, and the text-network betweenness experiment permits multiple edges despite the simple-graph core. No unified numeric benchmark denominator, unreported weighting rule, universal strategy superiority or biological-function prediction is asserted.
  • supports Source: Centrality Estimation in Large Networks · Locator: Sections 5–6, PDF/printed pp. 11–16; Tables 1–2, PDF pp. 20–21; Figures 1–9, PDF pp. 22–30

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The detailed maintained formulation is bounded to connected simple undirected unweighted graphs and relative score vectors. Other graph domains, raw-score normalization and implementation conventions need separate evidence.
  • The inspected source is the August 18, 2006 manuscript associated with the 2007 publication. No claim of exact agreement with an unseen journal-layout copy, author implementation or raw experimental data is made.
  • Mixed is unresolved: prose on page 10 names Random, MaxMin and MinSum, whereas Table 1 names MaxMin, MaxSum and Random. No one composition is asserted as the tested code.
  • The manuscript’s page 7 absolute-tail display lacks the factor needed for a general two-sided iid bound: a single equally likely 0/1 variable at deviation 1/2 has event probability one, greater than its printed exp(-1/2) bound. This is an elementary curator counterexample to the inspected display, not an audit of a later version or author code. No replacement finite-sample guarantee for this procedure is claimed.
  • Table and figure graph sizes and inversion-metric terminology differ in the inspected copy. The discovery report preserves both readings rather than inferring an undocumented preprocessing rule.
  • Finite exact examples are illustrations, not model training, benchmark replication, empirical confidence intervals or proof of unbiasedness for nonlinear normalized outputs. No author software, paid service or data upload was used. DRAFT remains after author self-review.
