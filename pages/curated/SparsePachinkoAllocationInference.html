Sparse pachinko allocation inference

Algorithms AIMech:SparsePachinkoAllocationInference DRAFT
Aliases: pruned PAM Gibbs sampling
An approximate pachinko-allocation fitting procedure that restricts token path resampling to currently active document topics and word-associated subtopics, interleaving full sampling with pruning and optionally growing the training corpus incrementally.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Scoped to the sparse PAM inference techniques in Li and McCallum's submitted 2008 manuscript, particularly Sections 3.2.2-3.2.3. This is an algorithm for the already represented PAM model, with an LDA specialization, rather than a new dense probability-model identity. Sparse count-block storage is an exact implementation choice; deleting candidate paths from a Gibbs conditional is an approximation. The source calls its settings sparse PAM/sPAM and sparse LDA/sLDA; these names are not asserted aliases of every later sparse-LDA algorithm. Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #restricted-path-updates
For a document, collect its currently used supertopics C and subtopics C-prime. Remove a token's old assignment, form the union of document subtopics and subtopics with positive corpus counts for that token's word, and restrict new paths to C times that union. Renormalize the usual collapsed path weights on this candidate set, sample an assignment and update counts and active sets. The source initializes document sets before token removal; precise membership maintenance must preserve a defined candidate set. Zero count-block storage alone does not remove the positive prior mass of unoccupied paths.

Limitation #reachability-versus-invariance
Pruned updates cannot introduce a supertopic absent from the document. Alternating with full sampling restores possible routes into omitted states, but does not by itself preserve the exact target distribution: the pruned kernel need not leave that target invariant. In a two-token, two-supertopic PAM special case, the target over assignments is (1/3,1/6,1/6,1/3); one full sweep followed by one pruned sweep changes it to (13/27,1/54,1/54,13/27). The reported average 98 percent retained conditional mass is a local empirical statistic, not a bound on the final posterior error. No acceptance correction is provided.
  • supports Source: Pachinko Allocation: Scalable Mixture Models of Topic Correlations · Locator: Section 3.2.2, printed/PDF pp.10-11, retained mass, trapping discussion and alternating sampling
Mechanism #incremental-initialization-and-adaptation
Sparse initialization fits a small document subset before gradually adding documents. A new document permits all supertopics; an already seen word permits subtopics with positive corpus word counts, while a previously unseen word permits all subtopics. The sparse-LDA experiment starts with 10000 documents and doubles the corpus every five iterations. Multiple chains are also used. Separate empirical-moment hyperparameter updates change the sampling target during fitting; the displayed Dirichlet formula divides by an aggregated concentration expression rather than following the usual alpha_j=mean_j*concentration identity, so that display is not adopted as a verified implementation recipe.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The complete schedule of full versus pruned updates and all active-set edge cases are not specified. Fixed valid hyperparameters permit analysis of the transition rule; corpus growth, empirical-moment adaptation and heuristic pruning do not jointly certify an exact stationary posterior sampler.
  • The displayed moment update also needs valid variance and logarithm domains. A uniform Beta example has moment-consistent alpha=(1,1), whereas the literal displayed aggregation gives (1/8,1/8). This is a qualification of the printed formula, not a claim about uninspected author code.
  • AP/SJMN retrieval and large Rexa experiments use particular topic counts, chain combinations, initialization and hardware. Non-significant tests between best retrieval results do not establish posterior equivalence. Dense-PAM human judgment, likelihood and classification experiments are separate from evidence about sparse inference.
  • The retrieval scorer averages plug-in word probabilities across chains before multiplying query terms and mixes them termwise with a smoothed document model. It is not automatically the average whole-query posterior predictive law. Finite transition checks do not reproduce the author implementation or benchmark.
