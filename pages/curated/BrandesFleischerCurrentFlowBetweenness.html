Brandes–Fleischer current-flow betweenness computation

Algorithms AIMech:BrandesFleischerCurrentFlowBetweenness DRAFT
Aliases: Brandes-Fleischer exact current-flow betweenness
An exact network-analysis algorithm that computes current-flow betweenness by forming grounded-Laplacian current-response rows, sorting each edge row, and aggregating rank-weighted contributions with endpoint correction instead of explicitly enumerating all source–sink pairs.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Scope #electrical-domain-and-estimand
The domain is a simple connected undirected graph with n>=3 and positive edge conductances. For ordered distinct source–sink pairs s,t, solve the weighted Laplacian equation Lp=e_s-e_t. Edge current is c(u,v)[p(u)-p(v)]. Vertex throughput is half the sum of absolute incident currents minus half the absolute supply, excluding source and sink. The maintained normalized betweenness is the sum of these throughputs over ordered pairs, divided by (n-1)(n-2). Edge orientation is a computational convention and does not make the graph directed.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Section 2, PDF pp. 2–3 / printed pp. 534–535; Section 3.1 and Definition 2, PDF pp. 4–5 / printed pp. 536–537
Mechanism #grounded-current-response
Ground one vertex, invert the reduced weighted Laplacian, and embed the inverse in a matrix C with a zero row and column at that vertex. For oriented edge e=(u,v), define the indexed row F_ej=c(e)[C_uj-C_vj]. Then its current for supply s,t is F_es-F_et. This row formula follows the source’s Laplacian and Ohm-law construction while avoiding its inconsistent incidence-matrix dimensions. Choice of ground changes absolute potentials but not current differences.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Section 2, Equation 1, PDF p. 3 / printed p. 535; Section 4, current-response construction, PDF p. 7 / printed p. 539
Mechanism #sorted-row-aggregation
Fix vertex indices i=1,...,n and initialize a(v)=0. For each oriented edge e=(u,v), assign each vertex j its distinct rank r_e(j) in a nonincreasing ordering of F_ej, retaining the original vertex indices. Add (j-r_e(j))F_ej to a(u) and (n+1-j-r_e(j))F_ej to a(v) for every j. Return 2[a(v_i)-(i-1)]/[(n-1)(n-2)]. The subtraction removes sink endpoint contributions. Equal row values can occupy distinct consecutive positions in any consistent tie order; the aggregate is unchanged. This is the source’s raw accumulation and final correction, not the differently scaled intermediate prose expression.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Section 4, proof of Theorem 2 and Algorithm 1 lines 1.1–1.2, PDF pp. 7–8 / printed pp. 539–540; curator finite tie and orientation checks in research/discovery/current-flow-boundaries.py
Scope #computation-and-storage
After one reduced-matrix inversion, m rows of length n are formed and sorted, giving the source’s O(I(n-1)+mn log n) arithmetic/comparison bound, where I is the inversion cost. A dense C, graph storage and one current row require O(n^2+m) storage. The source discusses retaining selected inverse columns under a vertex ordering to reduce storage. Its rough condition-number estimate and conjugate-gradient cost do not provide an unconditional exact finite-precision runtime or tolerance guarantee.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Section 4, Theorem 2 and matrix/storage discussion, PDF pp. 7–9 / printed pp. 539–541; explicit dense-storage accounting is curator interpretation
Limitation #scope-and-comparison
Current-flow throughput can include routes beyond shortest paths; the unit triangle has normalized current-flow betweenness 1/3 at each vertex, while internal-vertex shortest-path betweenness is zero. On a unit four-vertex path the matched normalized scores are (0,2/3,2/3,0). These are curator examples. Algorithm 1 computes exact scores in its mathematical model; the chapter’s sampled-pair approximation and closeness procedures are different algorithms. Structural prominence and the electrical analogy do not establish causal influence, biological function or a learned prediction.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This record specifies Algorithm 1 for connected simple undirected positive-conductance networks with n>=3 and endpoint-excluded normalization. Disconnected components, singleton cases, signed weights, endpoint inclusion and edge centrality require explicit separate conventions.
  • The inspected Definition 1 writes a cycle sum of currents as zero, whereas its Ohm law and weighted Laplacian require signed potential drops x/c to sum to zero. The weighted-triangle curator example has nonzero cycle-current sum and zero cycle-potential sum. The literal unweighted cycle-current equation is not adopted for general conductances.
  • The inspected incidence matrix is declared n-by-m but used in BC where edge-by-vertex current rows require transposition or a different matrix convention. The maintained indexed row formula is explicit. Algorithm 1 uses raw accumulation before endpoint subtraction, unlike an inconsistently normalized intermediate prose expression.
  • The chapter’s separate closeness definition uses an n-1 numerator that disappears in its proof and Algorithm 2. This record makes no blanket normalization identity with information centrality and does not incorporate Algorithm 2 into its identity.
  • Exact mathematical accumulation is distinguished from numerical linear solves. No implementation-specific tolerance, conditioning guarantee, benchmark replication, paid service or model training was used. Finite rational examples do not certify every graph, theorem or historical program. DRAFT remains after author self-review.
