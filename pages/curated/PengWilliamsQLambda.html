Peng and Williams Q(lambda)

Algorithms AIMech:PengWilliamsQLambda DRAFT
The 1996 tabular incremental Q(lambda) algorithm that combines a direct one-step Q-learning update for the current state-action pair with a second greedy-state prediction error propagated through decayed accumulating activity traces, without automatically cutting traces on exploratory actions.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #tabular-values-traces-and-behavior
The inspected baseline maintains a table Q(x,a) and an accumulating activity trace Tr(x,a), both initialized to zero in Figure 1. At each step it chooses an action using the current exploration policy, executes that action and observes the immediate reward and next state. Its state-value estimate is V(x)=max_a Q(x,a).
  • supports Source: Incremental Multi-Step Q-Learning · Locator: Sections 3–4, PDF pp. 3–5 / printed pp. 285–287; Figure 1 initialization and steps A–C.
Mechanism #two-preupdate-errors
Using the same pre-update Q and V values for a transition (x,a,r,y), compute e_prime = r + gamma*V(y) - Q(x,a) and e = r + gamma*V(y) - V(x). The current-pair error e_prime and older-credit error e generally differ when the current action is not greedy; algebraically e_prime-e = V(x)-Q(x,a).
  • supports Source: Incremental Multi-Step Q-Learning · Locator: Section 4, PDF p. 4 / printed p. 286, Equations 6–7; Figure 1 steps D–E, PDF p. 5 / printed p. 287.
Mechanism #decay-old-credit-before-direct-update
First multiply every existing trace by gamma*lambda and add alpha*Tr(x,a)*e, using the decayed trace, to its Q entry. Next add alpha*e_prime separately to the current state-action pair. Only after both value updates increment the current pair’s trace by one. A revisited current pair can therefore receive both its older-trace contribution and the direct current-pair update.
  • supports Source: Incremental Multi-Step Q-Learning · Locator: Figure 1 steps F–H, PDF p. 5 / printed p. 287.
  • supports Source: Incremental Multi-Step Q-Learning · Locator: Author manuscript p. 3, Figure 1 steps f–h. These trace/value update steps agree with the 1996 core; the earlier figure’s action-selection wording is separately qualified in the version limitation.
Mechanism #retained-traces-through-exploration
The displayed core does not automatically zero traces after a nongreedy action. Older credit is driven by reward plus the discounted next-state maximum minus the current-state maximum, while rewards still come from the trajectory actually followed. This differs from selected-next-action MCQ-L and from a scheme that cuts prior traces whenever exploration chooses a nongreedy action.
  • supports Source: Incremental Multi-Step Q-Learning · Locator: Section 4, PDF pp. 4–5 / printed pp. 286–287, Equations 6–8, exploration discussion and Figure 1.
Scope #zero-product-one-step-boundary
Whenever gamma*lambda=0, including lambda=0 or the permitted gamma=0 boundary, every old-trace contribution in Figure 1 vanishes. Only the direct one-step Q-learning update remains for the current pair; at gamma=0 this is alpha*(r-Q(x,a)). Equality of this update does not by itself supply a convergence theorem without the relevant sampling and learning-rate assumptions.
  • supports Source: Incremental Multi-Step Q-Learning · Locator: Section 2, PDF p. 2 / printed p. 284, discount domain; Sections 3–4, PDF pp. 3–5 / printed pp. 285–287, Equation 4 and Figure 1.
Scope #trace-work-and-pruning-scope
Incremental value propagation avoids requiring a complete trajectory replay before each update, but the set of active state-action traces can grow to the entire table. The article discusses retaining only sufficiently significant traces when gamma*lambda<1. Such pruning is an approximation requiring its own threshold and conventions, not a proof that the exact update has constant per-step cost.
  • supports Source: Incremental Multi-Step Q-Learning · Locator: Section 4, PDF pp. 5–6 / printed pp. 287–288, activity-trace work and pruning discussion; Section 5, PDF p. 7 / printed p. 289, incremental versus replay comparison.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • With active multi-step credit (gamma*lambda>0), exploratory rewards need not evaluate the policy implicit in the greedy maxima. The article therefore does not guarantee convergence to Q-star under arbitrary continuing exploration. Its suggestions to reduce lambda or Boltzmann temperature are not a universal convergence proof. The zero-product boundary is separately described and is not covered by a blanket positive-lambda warning.
  • Equation 8 includes an additional summation caused by changing value estimates. The small-learning-rate discussion does not establish exact forward/backward equality for arbitrary online updates, a uniform finite-step residual bound or convergence of every nonlinear extension.
  • The source discount domain is 0<=gamma<1, with trace parameter lambda in [0,1]. The finite recurrence remains meaningful at lambda=1, but that endpoint is not justified by literally substituting lambda=1 into the displayed (1-lambda)-weighted infinite mixture. Return convergence and any limiting interpretation require their own conditions. Repeated visits add new trace mass, so attenuation of old credit is not monotonic decay of the total accumulated trace.
  • The selected primary algorithm is the tabular Figure 1 baseline. The paper discusses connectionist implementation, and Rummery’s report presents a separate neural extension with its own timing. Neither mention establishes that all neural approximators share the tabular update guarantees or that the two papers are interchangeable specifications.
  • Figure 1 is a continuing do-forever loop and does not specify every episodic terminal bootstrap, trace reset or final-update convention. Sparse pruning, replacing traces, clipping and finite-precision choices alter or approximate the literal recurrence and require explicit choices. No complete runnable implementation is reconstructed here.
  • The operational definition remains the 1996 journal Figure 1 method. The inspected 1994 author manuscript supports the same two-error and trace-update order, but its figure’s greedy-action line conflicts with adjoining exploration prose; the local V definition prints arg max despite an earlier max definition, and Equation 12 lacks the visible joining plus before its changing-value sum. The 1996 presentation explicitly uses an exploration policy, max and the plus sign. These are source-display observations, not a formal erratum. The 1994 cart-pole experiment uses separate current-pair and past-pair learning rates and resets traces at each trial; those experimental choices do not replace the single-rate core or specify every episodic convention.
  • The reported task comparisons and claims about faster learning or the necessity of multi-step propagation are not universal results over environments, representations or computational budgets. No author software, benchmark run or original convergence proof was executed or certified by this evidence assessment.
