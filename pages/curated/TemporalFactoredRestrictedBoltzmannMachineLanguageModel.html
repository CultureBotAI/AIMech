Temporal factored restricted Boltzmann machine language model

Architectures AIMech:TemporalFactoredRestrictedBoltzmannMachineLanguageModel DRAFT
Aliases: Temporal FRBM
A sequential extension of the factored RBM language model that directs information from the preceding binary hidden state into the current hidden bias, propagating context beyond the explicit word window.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the temporal FRBM in Mnih and Hinton’s 2007 language-model paper. It extends the factored categorical-word construction with shared hidden-to-hidden weights A. It is distinct from the static FRBM, the broader temporal RBM with additional visible-history connections, and deterministic recurrent language models. Its approximate filtering algorithm is not the exact latent-state model. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Apply shared FRBM parameters to successive overlapping word windows. Connect hidden state h_tau to h_(tau+1) so that the current hidden bias becomes b_h+A h_tau; conditional on that earlier state, the current word and hidden variables retain an FRBM joint with its own normalizer. Exact prediction must account for uncertainty in previous hidden states. The proposed online approximation instead substitutes their inferred probabilities p_tau, computes p_(tau+1)=sigmoid(b_h+A p_tau+sum_i W_i^T r_word_i) after observing the current window, and predicts the next word using the FRBM word distribution with the correspondingly shifted bias. Learning uses these approximate filtered probabilities, applies contrastive-divergence updates to individual model instances, and combines them. The temporal-weight update contrasts current hidden probabilities and their reconstruction, multiplied by the preceding filtered vector; it does not propagate an exact likelihood gradient through the full latent history.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Replacing an uncertain earlier hidden state by its mean inside a sigmoid or a context-dependent normalized word distribution is generally not exact marginalization. Observing a new word can also change the posterior over previous hidden states.
  • The cited temporal-RBM source explicitly ignores future evidence for online learning and notes that changes in its parameter-dependent approximate posterior can decrease the variational bound. Approximate filtering plus finite CD therefore does not guarantee monotonic likelihood or bound improvement.
  • The recurrent path permits dependence beyond the explicit context window, but does not guarantee faithful long-range memory, tractable exact filtering or a particular effective context length.
  • This construction has directed temporal links and undirected current word–hidden interactions. It is not equivalent to a single unconditioned RBM, a pure directed Bayesian network parameterization, or a deep belief network merely because latent states are present.
  • The static FRBM marginal’s printed bias-sign conflict remains qualified when its conditional prediction is reused. No implementation audit, empirical superiority or exact sequence-likelihood result is asserted.
