LargeVis

Algorithms AIMech:LargeVis DRAFT
A dimensionality-reduction and graph-visualization algorithm that constructs an approximate weighted nearest-neighbor graph and learns low-dimensional coordinates with sampled attractive-edge and repulsive-pair likelihood terms.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope LargeVis combines a specific approximate-neighbor construction with a probabilistic layout objective. It is distinct from t-SNE’s normalized KL objective, UMAP’s locally scaled graph weights, LINE graph representations, and generic nearest-neighbor search. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method-scope
Initialize approximate neighbors with random projection trees and refine candidate neighbors through neighbors-of-neighbors exploration. Assign locally normalized Gaussian weights with a perplexity-controlled bandwidth and symmetrize them. Model edge likelihood using a decreasing function of embedded distance, sample positive edges in proportion to their weights, draw negative endpoints with degree-to-the-three-quarters weighting, and optimize the sampled log objective using asynchronous stochastic gradients.
  • supports Source: Visualizing Large-scale and High-dimensional Data · Locator: Sections 3.1–3.2, Algorithm 1 and equations (1)–(6), PDF pp. 3–5; Section 4.2 for the graph/visualization distinction

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Graph construction is approximate; the reported near-perfect neighbor recovery on selected datasets is not a guarantee for arbitrary input.
  • The sampled objective in equation (6) replaces the all-negative-pairs expression in equation (5); it is not asserted to be an unbiased evaluation of every original negative term.
  • The linear layout-cost statement assumes fixed embedding dimension and negative-sample count with a number of updates proportional to points; it does not establish linear cost for all graph construction or convergence.
  • Perplexity, graph quality, negative distribution, optimizer and chosen low-dimensional probability function affect results. No universal geometric fidelity or benchmark superiority is asserted.
