WalkSAT

Algorithms AIMech:WalkSAT DRAFT
Aliases: WalkSAT/SKC, SKC WalkSAT
A clause-focused SAT local-search algorithm, in its classical SKC formulation, that first selects an unsatisfied clause, prioritizes a variable with zero breakcount, and otherwise mixes a random variable flip with a minimum-breakcount flip within that clause.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record curates the classical SKC variable-selection rule under the commonly used WalkSAT name. It does not equate every later WalkSAT heuristic, the earlier underspecified WSAT family description, pure clause-first random walk, or global GSAT-with-walk selection with that exact rule. The 1994 source gives the clause-first lineage; the authors’ 2004 solver description explicitly supplies the zero-break priority and breakcount-based choice. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Maintain a complete truth assignment and stop successfully when all clauses are satisfied. Otherwise randomly choose an unsatisfied nonempty clause. For each candidate variable in that clause, its breakcount is the number of currently satisfied clauses that would become unsatisfied if it were flipped. If any candidate has breakcount zero, choose one such candidate at random. Only when there is no zero-break candidate, choose a random variable from the selected clause with probability p; with probability 1−p choose a candidate of minimum breakcount, breaking ties randomly. Flip the chosen variable and repeat under the application’s stopping budget. Breakcount omits how many previously unsatisfied clauses become satisfied, so it need not rank candidates like GSAT’s global net-gain score. Random restarts are an optional outer strategy; the described 2004 competition configuration uses one long run.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The zero-break branch precedes the noise coin, including at p=1. That setting is therefore not generally identical to a pure random-clause/random-variable walk. Removing the priority changes the specified SKC heuristic.
  • Candidate selection is restricted to the chosen clause and can miss the globally best net-gain flip. Minimizing breakcount is different from minimizing breakcount minus makecount; replacing one by the other changes the heuristic.
  • A finite unsuccessful run does not prove UNSAT, and the pure 2-CNF random-walk guarantee is not automatically a guarantee for this SKC policy on general formulas. The noise setting and restart strategy are not universal constants.
  • The move rule needs a nonempty selected clause and a declared formula representation. Empty clauses require separate handling; duplicate clauses, literal normalization and weighted extensions can change counts or sampling and are not silently normalized here.
  • AutoWalksat, Rnovelty, Rnovelty+ and later software additions are distinct procedures or configurations, not exact aliases of this SKC record. No author implementation or numerical solver benchmark was run.
