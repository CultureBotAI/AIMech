Independence Metropolis–Hastings sampling

Algorithms AIMech:IndependenceMetropolisHastings DRAFT
Aliases: Independence Metropolis sampler, IMH
A Metropolis–Hastings sampler whose proposal distribution is fixed independently of the current state, with proposal-density ratios still included in acceptance.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Independence describes the proposal q(y), not independence of the retained Markov-chain states. This specializes Metropolis–Hastings and differs from estimating an expectation using separately weighted independent importance samples or from a state-centered random-walk proposal. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Choose a fixed proposal density or mass g and draw each candidate y from g independently of the current state x. Substitute q(y|x)=g(y) into the Metropolis–Hastings ratio, obtaining alpha=min(1,pi(y)g(x)/(pi(x)g(y)))=min(1,w(y)/w(x)) for w=pi/g where defined. Accept y or retain x. Hastings Section 2.5 explicitly chooses q_ij=pi_prime_j while keeping pi, rather than pi_prime, as the chain’s stationary target. The displayed acceptance formula here is the substitution into his Section 2.2 rule.
  • supports Source: Monte Carlo sampling methods using Markov chains and their applications · Locator: Sections 2.2 and 2.5, Metropolis choice and final paragraph setting q_ij=pi_prime_j, printed pp. 99–101 and 103 / PDF pp. 3–5 and 7

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The proposal must cover the target support required for irreducibility, and its probability or density must be evaluable for the ratio.
  • Independent candidates do not imply independent retained draws: rejection leaves the chain at the same state.
  • The ratio shows why a state with very large pi(x)/g(x) can be hard to leave for ordinary candidates. This is an algebraic caution, not a measured mixing rate or a universal convergence theorem.
  • Unweighted retained-chain averages and importance-weighted independent-sample estimates are different procedures. Validity still requires appropriate chain and integrability conditions.
  • Adaptive or learned changing proposals are outside this fixed-proposal definition and require their own validity arguments.
