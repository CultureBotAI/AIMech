Exemplar-Based Convex Clustering

Algorithms AIMech:ExemplarBasedConvexClustering DRAFT
Aliases: Convex Clustering with Exemplar-Based Models, Convex Mixture Model Clustering
An exemplar-based clustering procedure that fixes candidate component centers at every observed point, fits their simplex weights through a convex soft information-distortion objective, and derives a hard grouping from selected posterior-MAP exemplars.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the Lashkari-Golland procedure, also described as convex mixture model clustering. Its data-derived exemplar dictionary and hard extraction policy specialize fixed-component weight EM and finite rate-distortion Blahut-Arimoto. It is distinct from moving-centroid k-means, neural balanced transport in SeLa, scale-persistence melting, and fusion-penalty methods also called convex clustering. 5 Claims
Mechanism #soft-objective-and-update
For N indexed observations, fixed finite dissimilarities d_ij and beta>=0, set S_ij=exp(-beta*d_ij). Minimize J(q)=-(1/N)sum_i log(sum_j q_j S_ij) over simplex weights with positive initialization. With z_i=sum_j q_j S_ij, set r_ij=q_j S_ij/z_i and q_j_new=(1/N)sum_i r_ij. Equivalently, the row-conditional channel minimizes I(I;J)+beta*E[d_ij] with a uniform data-index source. A valid common-base component family additionally gives the stated mixture-likelihood interpretation; Gaussian components use squared Euclidean distance.
  • supports Source: Convex Clustering with Exemplar-Based Models · Locator: Sections 2-4, PDF pp. 2-4, Equations 2, 4, 6-9; conditional normalization from Equations 6-8
Scope #convexity-and-support
For finite positive kernels and positive initialization, the exact unpruned iteration converges to a global optimum of the restricted soft weight objective. This is not a global fit of freely moving mixture components. Convexity does not imply unique coefficients: at beta=0 every q is fixed and optimal. Zero weights stay zero and can exclude useful exemplars; exact positive-kernel finite iterations preserve positive weights. At an optimum eta_j=(1/N)sum_i S_ij/z_i equals one on positive weights and is at most one on inactive coordinates, rather than necessarily strictly less.
  • supports Source: Convex Clustering with Exemplar-Based Models · Locator: Section 2, PDF p. 3, Equation 4 and positive-support condition; Section 4, PDF p. 4, Equation 9; curator support and beta-zero checks
Mechanism #hard-exemplar-extraction
The 2007 visualization policy first selects every exemplar index that maximizes r_ij for at least one observation, then assigns each observation to its closest selected exemplar. Tie handling is not specified. This differs from the later multiview top-M policy. Beta controls the soft distortion tradeoff but does not uniquely determine a universally correct hard cluster count. Hardening does not retain the soft optimum guarantee or a general bound I<=log(K_hard): for points (0,1,3), squared distance and beta=1/4, the saved near-optimal soft solution has I about 0.03228 nats while this MAP policy selects only the middle point.
  • supports Source: Convex Clustering with Exemplar-Based Models · Locator: Section 5, PDF p. 5, hard assignment and loss of optimality; Figure 1 caption; curator finite soft-channel/MAP calculation
Limitation #approximations-and-cost
The dense exact update uses two matrix-vector operations with O(N^2) work per iteration. The paper separately proposes deleting weights below 10^-3/N and renormalizing, and retaining only a rowwise subset of large similarities. These approximations can change the feasible set or objective and need not preserve the full soft optimum. The saved two-point example shows the stated pruning threshold removing a small but useful positive component.
  • supports Source: Convex Clustering with Exemplar-Based Models · Locator: Section 4, PDF pp. 4-5, Equation 9, pruning and similarity sparsification; curator finite pruning example
Limitation #printed-normalization-boundaries
The expression r_ij=q_j S_ij/(N z_i) after Equation 9 is joint data-index/exemplar mass, not the row-conditional r defined in Equation 6; the conditional omits N. Literal KL from an atomic empirical measure to a continuous density is not finite merely by evaluating that density at data points. The finite-index objective and normalized responsibilities above avoid transferring that common-measure ambiguity from Equation 3.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Sparsity at an asymptotic limit, numerical pruning, and the selected hard exemplars are different notions. Neither every beta change nor every initialization produces a distinct optimum.
  • Natural-log objectives use nats; bit rates require consistent log-base and multiplier conversion. A slope statement for the rate-distortion curve needs differentiability or a supporting-slope interpretation.
  • Synthetic source comparisons tune beta using class labels and do not establish universal performance or a biological application. No historical software or benchmark was reproduced.
