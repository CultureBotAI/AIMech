NeXO ontology alignment

Algorithms AIMech:NeXOOntologyAlignment DRAFT
Aliases: AlignOntology
An iterative ontology-alignment algorithm that combines shared-terminal set overlap with parent and child neighborhood similarities, then greedily selects one-to-one term correspondences while rejecting reversed ancestor relations.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #intrinsic-and-relational-scores
The initial term-pair scores are Jaccard overlaps of their assigned gene sets. Later iterations combine three quarters intrinsic similarity with one quarter relational similarity. Relational similarity compares parent and child neighborhoods using the previous score matrix; each neighborhood comparison greedily chooses unique pairs and normalizes their summed scores. The root has a separate child-only rule.
  • supports Source: A gene ontology inferred from molecular networks · Locator: Online Methods, Ontology alignment, PDF p. 10, intrinsic, relational and set-similarity equations
Mechanism #constrained-greedy-alignment
Each iteration sorts term pairs by score and accepts them greedily when neither term is already used and the proposed pair creates no reversed ancestor-descendant correspondence with an accepted pair. Processing stops when all terms of one ontology are matched or only scores below the stated threshold remain. These uniqueness and no-crisscross constraints do not require exact equality of gene sets or a full graph isomorphism.
  • supports Source: A gene ontology inferred from molecular networks · Locator: Online Methods, Ontology alignment, PDF p. 10, steps 0–5
Limitation #greedy-optimality-boundary
The first greedy pass need not maximize total admissible similarity. A finite sibling-term example with Jaccard matrix [[1/2,1/3],[1/3,0]] selects the unique highest pair for a total of one half, whereas the two cross-pairs total two thirds without violating uniqueness or ancestor constraints. This assesses a first pass only; it does not predict the final iterated alignment or the author implementation.
  • supports Source: A gene ontology inferred from molecular networks · Locator: Online Methods, Ontology alignment, PDF p. 10; curator-derived Jaccard example in research/discovery/nexo-alignment-boundaries.py
Scope #iteration-and-conventions
The source returns an alignment when it matches any previously encountered alignment. This repetition rule does not require successive score matrices to converge or successive mappings to agree. The printed formulas also leave empty-neighborhood, mixed root/non-root and tie conventions to implementation. No occurrence of a cycle in author code or general failure to terminate is asserted.
  • supports Source: A gene ontology inferred from molecular networks · Locator: Online Methods, Ontology alignment, PDF p. 10, relational cases and final repetition rule
Scope #alignment-calibration
The paper estimates false discovery from randomized ontologies and uses size-dependent score thresholds, with a stated alignment FDR below ten percent in each size group. This is a separate calculation from the five-percent thresholds in functional-enrichment comparisons. Alignment scores, semantic identity and experimentally supported biological function remain distinct.
  • supports Source: A gene ontology inferred from molecular networks · Locator: Online Methods, Calculating the false-discovery rate for ontology alignment, PDF p. 10; Figure 3 and functional enrichment, p. 4

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The record covers the algorithm described in the 2013 NeXO paper. Michael Kramer’s AlignOntology landing page attributes the software to that paper, but its later code revisions were not audited and are not assumed equivalent in every detail.
  • Comparable terminal identities are required for meaningful gene-set overlap. Missing or inconsistent annotations and shared evaluation inputs can affect scores and matches.
  • Greedy local and global matching is not a maximum-weight assignment or a semantic equivalence proof. The finite counterexample concerns k=0 only, with a common matched root and sibling terms.
  • Repeated-mapping termination differs from convergence of the numeric similarities. The source does not fully specify empty parent/child sets, mixed root status or tie ordering.
  • No author implementation, experimental dataset or supplementary archive was audited or executed. Finite curator calculations assess printed formulas and bounded examples, not benchmark replication or global optimality. The record remains DRAFT.
