Differential Evolution

Algorithms AIMech:DifferentialEvolution DRAFT
Aliases: DE
A population-based continuous optimization method that generates trial vectors using scaled differences between population members, combines mutant and target coordinates, and selects replacements through target-trial objective comparisons.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes differential evolution, with the 1997 DE/rand/1/bin procedure as its explicitly inspected core. Mutation/crossover variants are distinguished by the source’s DE/x/y/z notation rather than treated as identical executable rules. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Maintain a fixed-size population of real-valued vectors. In the inspected DE/rand/1/bin scheme, for each target select three mutually distinct current-generation population indices that also differ from the target index. Add a scaled difference of two selected vectors to the third to form a mutant. Build a trial by independently choosing mutant or target coordinates according to the crossover probability, forcing one randomly selected coordinate to come from the mutant. Evaluate the trial and use a greedy comparison against its target to choose the next-generation member. Complete all target comparisons using the old population before replacing it with the next population. Repeat until the configured stopping criterion is met. Population size, mutation scale, crossover probability and initialization are configuration choices.
  • supports Source: Differential Evolution – A Simple and Efficient Heuristic for Global Optimization over Continuous Spaces · Locator: Section2, PDF pp.3–7 (printed343–347), especially Equations2–4 and Figure3; Section4, PDF pp.16–17; Section5, PDF pp.17–18

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The prose specifies replacement for a strictly smaller objective, whereas the displayed C-style Figure3 uses <=. The tie policy must therefore be identified when implementing or comparing versions; these source statements are not treated as identical code. A forced mutant coordinate need not change its numerical value.
  • DE/rand/1/bin needs at least four population members for its distinct-index rule. Difference-vector proposals can leave the initialization range; that range is not a hard feasible-domain boundary. Bound repair, general constraint handling, noise treatment and categorical encodings are additional choices.
  • The method uses function evaluations, not derivatives of the objective. Population collapse can eliminate difference-vector variation, and finite-budget greedy selection does not certify a global optimum. The source’s control-variable suggestions and comparative results are empirical, not universal guarantees or reproduced experiments.
