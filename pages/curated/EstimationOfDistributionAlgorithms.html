Estimation of distribution algorithms

Algorithms AIMech:EstimationOfDistributionAlgorithms DRAFT
A family of population-based optimization algorithms whose central offspring-generation mechanism estimates a search distribution from selected evaluated candidates and samples new candidates from that distribution. The family leaves the distribution model, estimation rule and selection policy to concrete algorithms; it is not one fully specified optimizer.

This record denotes the named family of optimization procedures that estimate a search distribution from selected candidates and resample. It is broader than original binary UMDA and distinct from a requirement to use natural-gradient NES/IGO, a particular incremental PBIL update, or any standalone probabilistic model. Specific overlapping choices in CEM or PBIL do not identify every whole algorithm with this family definition. The schema algorithm type is used with explicit family-level definition; no acronym alias is asserted.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #selected-population-distribution-sampling
The defining variation step estimates a probability law from selected evaluated candidates and generates new candidates by sampling it. In the inspected binary procedures, evaluation and selection supply the data used by the next distribution estimate. This replaces pairwise chromosome recombination as the central offspring-generation mechanism. It does not make every method that samples candidates an estimation of distribution algorithm, or prohibit additional operators in later hybrid methods.

  • supports Source: From recombination of genes to the estimation of distributions I. Binary parameters · Locator: Introduction, PDF p. 1; Section 3, PDF pp. 3–4, UMDA Steps 1–3.
Mechanism #model-structure-is-an-algorithm-choice
The distribution model determines which dependencies the fitted search law can represent. The original UMDA uses a product of univariate binary marginals, whereas the later chapter describes factorized models with conditional distributions for interacting variables. Model structure can be supplied or estimated in particular procedures. A Bayesian network, learned graph or independent-coordinate model is therefore not mandatory for the entire family.
  • supports Source: Evolutionary Algorithms: From Recombination to Search Distributions · Locator: Section 3, internal/PDF pp. 9–10, Equation 13 and UMDA; Section 7, internal/PDF pp. 30–31, Equations 60–62 and FDA discussion; Section 9, internal/PDF p. 35, structure search.
Mechanism #selection-and-estimation-are-distinct-choices
A selection policy determines which evaluated candidates, or their weighted population law, contribute to distribution estimation. The inspected chapter distinguishes fitness-proportionate selection from truncation and tournament selection, and separately studies Boltzmann selection. Changing that policy changes the distribution being estimated. The family does not require one universal elite fraction, fitness weighting, objective direction or treatment of ties.
  • supports Source: From recombination of genes to the estimation of distributions I. Binary parameters · Locator: Section 3, PDF p. 4, selection schedule in UMDA Step 1 and separately qualified Theorem 3.
  • supports Source: Evolutionary Algorithms: From Recombination to Search Distributions · Locator: Section 3.2, internal/PDF p. 13, selection alternatives; Section 7, internal/PDF p. 30, Definition 7 and Equation 58.
Scope #refitting-and-incremental-memory-are-distinct
Replacing binary marginals by the selected empirical frequencies and blending those frequencies with the preceding parameter vector are different update choices. Original UMDA uses the replacement rule. The 1996 paper separately describes SUMDA with p_new=p+lambda*(r-p); the later chapter discusses that blend as a PBIL update and identifies lambda=1 with the unsmoothed rule. This formula-level boundary does not identify every full PBIL implementation, with its selection and optional mutation rules, as UMDA.
  • supports Source: From recombination of genes to the estimation of distributions I. Binary parameters · Locator: Section 3, PDF p. 4, UMDA; Section 4, PDF p. 6, Equation 14 and SUMDA.
  • supports Source: Evolutionary Algorithms: From Recombination to Search Distributions · Locator: Section 4, internal/PDF p. 16, Equations 38–39 and the lambda=1 statement.
Limitation #population-theory-does-not-remove-sampling-error
An exact search-distribution recurrence and a finite run that estimates the distribution from sampled candidates are different objects. The chapter explicitly conditions its UMDA proportionate-selection equations on infinite populations and separately discusses finite-population fixation. Consequently the family abstraction supplies no universal finite population size, convergence rate or guarantee that useful values and dependencies remain represented after sampling and selection.
  • supports Source: Evolutionary Algorithms: From Recombination to Search Distributions · Locator: Introduction, internal/PDF p. 2, infinite-population theory and finite-sample scope; Section 3, internal/PDF p. 10, Theorem 5; Section 8, internal/PDF pp. 32–34, finite-population fixation and qualified sample-size discussion.
Scope #family-and-concrete-optimizer-boundary
The family name denotes a construction pattern, while a runnable member must specify its candidate domain, selection rule, model and estimation rule, sampling procedure, initialization and stopping policy. The inspected sources instantiate this pattern with binary marginal procedures and discuss richer conditional models. These examples support the family identity without asserting that all members share UMDA equations, graphical-model structure, numerical behavior or optimization guarantees.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This is a named algorithm family, not one executable procedure or a claim that every stochastic optimizer is a member. The inspected method evidence is concentrated on binary search. It does not establish a universal continuous, constrained, noisy or mixed-variable implementation.
  • Concrete methods can overlap under matched model, selection and update choices without being exact identities. In particular, a matching unsmoothed product-Bernoulli cross-entropy update can coincide with UMDA, while incremental PBIL with additional mutation has a different full procedure. Distribution fitting alone does not equate this family with natural evolution strategies or information-geometric optimization.
  • The 1996 article expressly does not fully describe its conditional distribution algorithm. Its statement that full conditional laws determine a unique joint law requires support qualifications: laws supported only on 00 and 11 can share deterministic full conditionals while assigning different masses to the two points. This is a bounded mathematical caveat, not an independent audit or erratum of the cited Besag work.
  • The later author chapter prints FDA Step 5 as a return to Step 2, bypassing the separately listed selection step. It maximizes the displayed BIC score in Section 9 but describes minimization in its conclusion. Neither a repaired FDA/LFDA implementation nor general score superiority is asserted by this family record.
  • Infinite-population equations, selected finite-population examples and empirical sample-size discussions do not establish global optimization, universal preservation of dependencies, an optimal population size or superiority over other optimizers. No optimizer, author implementation or benchmark was executed for this assessment.
  • The 1996 source is a ten-page author-hosted proceedings copy; its PDF build date is not its 1996 publication date. The later source is an author PostScript manuscript indexed as 2000 for a chapter published in 2001. Its 39-page reading PDF was derived after removing only a trailing MIME delimiter after the unique document EOF; the original PostScript and conversion failures remain separately retained. Publisher-byte equivalence is not established. The author webpage supports the family name, not a historical priority claim.
