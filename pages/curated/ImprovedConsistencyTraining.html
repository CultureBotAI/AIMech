Aliases: iCT (consistency training)
The consistency-model training procedure of Song and Dhariwal that learns directly from paired shared-noise data perturbations using a stop-gradient teacher with current student parameters, a Pseudo-Huber discrepancy, revised noise weighting and sampling, and a staged increase in noise-discretization intervals.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #generative-mechanism
The student and stop-gradient teacher evaluate the same data sample plus the same Gaussian noise scaled to adjacent noise levels, with teacher EMA decay set to zero. The revised objective uses a Pseudo-Huber discrepancy and inverse adjacent-noise-gap weighting. Training samples adjacent intervals according to discretized lognormal masses and doubles the interval count at scheduled milestones up to a cap. Noise-embedding sensitivity and shared dropout randomness are additional prescribed training choices; the separate inference-time EMA remains distinct.
  • supports Source: Improved Techniques for Training Consistency Models · Locator: Section 2, equations (3)–(5), PDF p. 3; Table 1 and Sections 3.1–3.5, equations (6)–(10), PDF pp. 4–8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Removing teacher EMA retains stop-gradient and does not remove inference-time parameter averaging; it is not a blanket recommendation to eliminate EMA from consistency distillation.
  • The paper's counterexample diagnoses a flaw in an earlier asymptotic argument. Its correction and finite-noise curriculum do not establish universal finite-training convergence.
  • Noise distribution, loss scale, discretization cap, dropout and embedding settings are choices evaluated in particular image-generation settings, not universal hyperparameters. The interval count doubles; the number of noise grid points is that count plus one.
  • The record describes the inspected training procedure and retains DRAFT status; it does not reproduce performance comparisons, certify every proof, or identify all future variants with this version.
