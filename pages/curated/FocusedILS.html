FocusedILS

Algorithms AIMech:FocusedILS DRAFT
A ParamILS algorithm-configuration method that adaptively extends paired evaluation prefixes until one configuration dominates the other, and allocates bonus evaluations to configurations that win the comparison.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope FocusedILS is the adaptive comparison instantiation of ParamILS. Its domination relation compares common instance/seed prefixes and requires the dominating configuration to have at least as many cached runs. It is not a fixed-sample BasicILS comparison, a Friedman significance test, or ROAR’s uniform proposal method with a different intensification routine. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Within the ParamILS search framework, compare configurations using cached target-algorithm runs on a common ordered instance/seed sequence. A configuration dominates another when it has at least as many runs and its estimated cost over the other’s full prefix is no greater. Begin a comparison by extending the shorter prefix by one run, or both when their lengths are equal; continue extending until either configuration dominates. Procedure3 increments its global bonus counter once per comparison and again when the initial run counts tie. When the first configuration dominates, add the accumulated number of bonus evaluations to it and reset the counter; otherwise return the unsuccessful comparison without that reset. Keep an incumbent while the search continues.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The 2009 formulation has a finite configuration domain; numerical parameters require discretization. Objective, run cutoff and training-instance distribution affect the selected configuration. Independent held-out evaluation is needed to assess generalization; no universal solver speedup is asserted.
  • The printed first-improvement loop accepts the supplied non-strict comparison. Equal-cost neighboring configurations can therefore cycle under a literal reading; this record does not assert unconditional termination of that loop or executable equivalence to a software release.
  • Adaptive capping is a separable acceleration, not the defining identity of this search/comparison method. Section4 describes trajectory-preserving and more aggressive alternatives. Procedure4 prints a max operation inconsistent with shortening the run cutoff described in the prose, and uses inconsistent cutoff symbols; the printed routine is not treated as verified executable code.
  • The paper’s asymptotic statements require a finite space, positive-probability random restarts, consistent cost estimates and indefinitely continuing iterations/evaluations. They are not a finite-budget optimality certificate. Repeated sampling from a finite training set does not by itself establish consistency for a different population of instances.
