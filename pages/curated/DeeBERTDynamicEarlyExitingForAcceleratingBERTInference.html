DeeBERT (Dynamic Early Exiting for Accelerating BERT Inference)

Algorithms AIMech:DeeBERTDynamicEarlyExitingForAcceleratingBERTInference DRAFT
Aliases: DeeBERT, Dynamic Early Exiting for Accelerating BERT Inference, DeeBERT Two-Stage Off-Ramp Early Exiting
DeeBERT (Dynamic Early Exiting for Accelerating BERT Inference) is a dynamic multi-exit fine-tuning and inference method for pretrained Transformer language models that inserts one intermediate classification off-ramp f_i(x; theta) after each Transformer layer i = 1..n (where f_n is the original final classifier), trains the network with a two-stage supervised protocol that first fine-tunes the embeddings, all n Transformer layers, and f_n under the final-layer cross-entropy loss L_n and then freezes the backbone and f_n while optimizing only the intermediate off-ramps f_1..f_{n-1} under sum_{i=1}^{n-1} L_i, and accelerates inference by exiting at the first off-ramp i whose output distribution z_i = f_i(x) has Shannon entropy H(z_i) < S.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from static layer-dropping and distillation methods (DistilBERT, TinyBERT, BERT-PKD, LayerDrop) that apply fixed depth reduction to all inputs regardless of difficulty, distinct from FastBERT (AIMech:FastBERTSelfDistillingBERTWithAdaptiveInferenceTime) which trains student branches via KL self-distillation from the final classifier's soft labels rather than supervised ground-truth cross-entropy in Stage 2, and distinct from PABEE (AIMech:PABEEPatienceBasedEarlyExitForBERT) which exits based on consecutive cross-layer prediction agreement (patience) rather than single-layer entropy H(z_i) < S.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #deebert-two-stage-off-ramp-fine-tuning-isolation
DeeBERT attaches n classification off-ramps with per-layer cross-entropy loss L_i(D; theta) = (1/|D|) sum_{(x,y) in D} H(y, f_i(x; theta)) (Eq. 1) and decouples fine-tuning into two stages: Stage 1 updates embeddings, all n Transformer layers, and the final off-ramp f_n using L_n, while Stage 2 freezes all parameters updated in Stage 1 and updates only the intermediate off-ramps f_1..f_{n-1} using sum_{i=1}^{n-1} L_i, strictly preserving the full-depth Transformer's predictive capacity at layer n.
  • supports Source: DeeBERT: Dynamic Early Exiting for Accelerating BERT Inference · Locator: Section 3.1 (Fine-Tuning), Equation 1, pp. 2247-2248
  • supports Source: DeeBERT: Dynamic Early Exiting for Accelerating BERT Inference · Locator: verify_two_stage_isolation and main
Mechanism #deebert-entropy-threshold-early-exit-and-layer-saving
At inference time (Algorithm 1), DeeBERT evaluates Transformer layers i = 1..n sequentially and immediately returns z_i = f_i(x) at the first layer satisfying entropy(z_i) < S, achieving analytical expected layer execution savings of 1 - (sum_{i=1}^n i * N_i) / (sum_{i=1}^n n * N_i) (Eq. 2), where N_i is the number of examples exiting at layer i.
  • supports Source: DeeBERT: Dynamic Early Exiting for Accelerating BERT Inference · Locator: Section 3.2 (Inference), Algorithm 1 and Equation 2, p. 2248
  • supports Source: DeeBERT: Dynamic Early Exiting for Accelerating BERT Inference · Locator: deebert_early_exit and expected_layer_saving
Application #deebert-glue-inference-acceleration-for-bert-and-roberta
On six GLUE benchmark tasks (SST-2, MRPC, QNLI, RTE, QQP, and MNLI) across both BERT-base and RoBERTa-base, DeeBERT reduces inference time by up to ~40% with minimal accuracy degradation and traces a single continuous quality-efficiency trade-off curve by varying the entropy threshold S without retraining.
  • supports Source: DeeBERT: Dynamic Early Exiting for Accelerating BERT Inference · Locator: Section 4.2 (Main Results), Table 1 and Figure 2, pp. 2248-2249
Limitation #deebert-layerwise-redundancy-and-threshold-sensitivity-limitation
Because early off-ramp classifiers can exhibit overconfidence on hard inputs and ensemble effects across layers are absent in single-layer entropy checks, aggressive entropy thresholds S cause accuracy degradation on complex reasoning tasks, and wall-clock speedup requires batch-size-1 or dynamic batching execution.
  • supports Source: DeeBERT: Dynamic Early Exiting for Accelerating BERT Inference · Locator: Section 4.1 (Experimental Setup) and Section 4.3 (Layerwise Results), pp. 2248-2250

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Single-layer entropy thresholding H(z_i) < S is vulnerable to overconfident early off-ramp predictions on hard examples when S is large.
  • Instance-adaptive early exiting requires batch size 1 or dynamic stream batching at inference time to translate layer savings directly into wall-clock latency reductions.
