Natural Evolution Strategies

Algorithms AIMech:NaturalEvolutionStrategies DRAFT
Aliases: NES
A family of black-box optimization algorithms that adapts a parameterized search distribution using estimates of the natural gradient of expected fitness with respect to the distribution parameters.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope NES is the shared natural-gradient search-distribution framework, not a single Gaussian implementation, a trained neural network or ordinary stochastic gradient descent on model weights. Its canonical method differentiates the search distribution’s log density, not the black-box objective itself. Exponential NES and separable NES are concrete variants with additional parameterization and computational choices; the framework record does not equate them. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Draw candidate points from a parameterized search distribution and evaluate their fitness. Compute the log-density derivative, or score, of each sample with respect to distribution parameters. In the canonical raw-fitness formulation, estimate the expected-fitness gradient by averaging score vectors weighted by fitness. Obtain the Fisher information of the distribution, analytically when available or through score outer products, and use its inverse action to transform the search gradient into a natural-gradient direction. Update the distribution parameters with a chosen learning rate and repeat. The paper additionally describes rank-based fitness shaping, which replaces raw fitness by ordered utility weights, and specialized distribution parameterizations that avoid a literal general Fisher-matrix inversion.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The score identity and Fisher construction require suitable regularity, integrability and a usable parameterization. The empirical Fisher matrix can be singular or numerically unstable even when the population Fisher is invertible; a small batch does not automatically support the displayed inverse.
  • Natural-gradient invariance describes the local direction under the appropriate exact metric. It is not an assertion that every finite, sampled parameter update is invariant under arbitrary reparameterization, stays in a valid distribution domain or improves expected fitness. Variant-specific parameterizations and learning rates matter.
  • Rank-based shaping changes the weighting criterion; its update should not be described as an unbiased estimate of the raw expected-fitness gradient in general. A Gaussian search distribution, exponential covariance update and adaptation sampling are not mandatory features of every NES family member.
  • Only the declared method and discussion pages were inspected. The record does not certify every displayed variant formula, an executable xNES/SNES implementation, universal benchmark superiority or a finite-budget global optimum.
