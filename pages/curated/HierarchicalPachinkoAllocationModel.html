Hierarchical pachinko allocation model

Model Families AIMech:HierarchicalPachinkoAllocationModel DRAFT
Aliases: hPAM
A pachinko topic-model family that associates word-emission distributions with internal topic nodes as well as terminal topics, allowing document tokens to use multiple paths and emit from different levels of a topic DAG.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Scoped to Mimno, Li and McCallum's ICML2007 hierarchical PAM family and its two explicit variants. Internal vocabulary emissions distinguish it from the demonstrated four-level PAM with word emissions only at subtopics. Token-specific DAG paths and shared subtopics distinguish it from hierarchical LDA's single document path in a tree. The path-level and local-exit variants remain one source-defined family with explicit differences, not asserted interchangeable parameterizations. This paper uses fixed topic counts; nonparametric PAM is a separate construction. Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #path-level-variant
In model 1 each document draws a root-to-supertopic vector and a subtopic vector for each supertopic. Each token chooses a supertopic and subtopic, then a level from a corpus-shared distribution specific to that path. It emits from the shared root, supertopic or subtopic word vector according to the selected level. Even root-emitted tokens have a latent complete path and contribute routing counts. With fixed priors, collapsed assignment weights combine document routing, corpus path-level and actual emitting-node word counts. Word counts must pool all paths that use the same emission vector.
  • supports Source: Mixtures of Hierarchical Topics with Pachinko Allocation · Locator: Section 2.4, PDF p.3, Figure 1, model 1 and Equation 1; Figure 2 p.4
Mechanism #local-exit-variant
Model 2 adds an exit coordinate to each document-local routing vector. A root exit emits from the root; otherwise a supertopic is selected and either exits to its word distribution or routes to a subtopic. The path stops at the emitting node, so unused lower routing choices are not sampled. There is no model 1 path-specific level vector. The variants have different cross-document sharing: in a one-path example a shared Beta(1,2) root-level probability gives joint root emission probability 1/6 across two documents, whereas independent document-local Beta(1,2) exits give 1/9 despite the same marginal probability.
  • supports Source: Mixtures of Hierarchical Topics with Pachinko Allocation · Locator: Section 2.4, PDF p.3, model 2 and comparison; Figure 2 p.4
Mechanism #collapsed-fitting
The paper describes Gibbs sampling with a factorization that first marginalizes paths to sample an emitting node, then samples a compatible path conditional on that node. This preserves the conditional when those probabilities are computed exactly. Document routing hyperparameters are periodically fitted from sampled counts by a Gibbs-EM procedure using fixed-point Dirichlet estimation. Such adaptation is distinct from a chain targeting one fixed-hyperparameter posterior throughout training.
  • supports Source: Mixtures of Hierarchical Topics with Pachinko Allocation · Locator: Section 2.4, PDF pp.3-4, sampling factorization and parameter fitting

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Experiments use fixed three-level topic structures and finite topic counts. Probabilistic routing hierarchies are not automatically ontological subsumption or causal graphs. Private/public children, separate Beta exits and other proposed DAG variations are sketches outside the two evaluated constructions.
  • Collapsed counts exclude the current token and respect shared emitting nodes. A literal independent word distribution per path/level triple would change the model. Finite calculations verify both variants' conditionals on synthetic examples, not historical software or chain convergence.
  • Selected Medline and newsgroup studies use particular hyperparameters, finite topic grids and simulated-document likelihood estimates with plug-in shared parameters. Topic/journal mutual information is not direct taxonomy recovery or document classification accuracy. Some numeric prose and table summaries disagree; no disputed summary is adopted.
  • Reported speed and performance comparisons depend on author implementations and chosen datasets. No author software, benchmark, universal superiority or exact marginal evidence is certified.
