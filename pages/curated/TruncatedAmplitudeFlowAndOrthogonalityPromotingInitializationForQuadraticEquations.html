Truncated Amplitude Flow and Orthogonality-Promoting Initialization for Random Quadratic Equations

Algorithms AIMech:TruncatedAmplitudeFlowAndOrthogonalityPromotingInitializationForQuadraticEquations DRAFT
Aliases: Truncated Amplitude Flow (TAF), Orthogonality-Promoting Initialization and Truncated Generalized Gradient Flow, Wang-Giannakis-Eldar Truncated Amplitude Flow for Phase Retrieval
A two-stage nonconvex-nonsmooth phase retrieval algorithm (introduced by Gang Wang, Georgios B. Giannakis, and Yonina C. Eldar in 2018) that recovers an unknown signal $\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ or $\mathbb{C}^n$ from $m$ magnitude measurements $\psi_i = |\langle \boldsymbol{a}_i, \boldsymbol{x}\rangle|$ by minimizing the amplitude-based empirical loss $\ell(\boldsymbol{z}) = \frac{1}{2m}\sum_{i=1}^m (\psi_i - |\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{z}|)^2$ (Equation 5). Stage 1 introduces *orthogonality-promoting initialization* (Equations 14–23, Algorithm 1): exploiting the concentration of $\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m \frac{\boldsymbol{a}_i\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top}{\|\boldsymbol{a}_i\|_2^2} \approx \frac{1}{n}\boldsymbol{I}_n$, it selects the subset $\overline{\mathcal{I}}_0$ of cardinality $|\overline{\mathcal{I}}_0| = \lceil \frac{1}{6}m\rceil$ corresponding to the largest normalized inner products $\psi_i / \|\boldsymbol{a}_i\|_2$, computes the leading unit eigenvector $\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}}_0$ of $\overline{\boldsymbol{Y}}_0 = \frac{1}{|\overline{\mathcal{I}}_0|}\sum_{i\in\overline{\mathcal{I}}_0} \frac{\boldsymbol{a}_i\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top}{\|\boldsymbol{a}_i\|_2^2}$ via power iterations without fourth-moment heavy tails, and scales $\boldsymbol{z}_0 = \sqrt{\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m \psi_i^2}\,\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}}_0$. Stage 2 refines $\boldsymbol{z}_t$ via *truncated generalized gradient* iterations $\boldsymbol{z}_{t+1} = \boldsymbol{z}_t - \frac{\mu}{m}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{t+1}}(\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{z}_t - \psi_i \frac{\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{z}_t}{|\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{z}_t|})\boldsymbol{a}_i$ over the watershed truncation set $\mathcal{I}_{t+1} = \{1\le i\le m : |\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{z}_t| / \psi_i \ge \frac{1}{1+\gamma}\}$ (Equations 6–7 and 11), eliminating measurements near the hyperplane $\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{z}_t = 0$ whose estimated signs $\mathrm{sign}(\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{z}_t)$ disagree with $\mathrm{sign}(\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{x})$.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Resolved against all 442 pinned AIO concepts and 692 maintained AIMech concepts. Distinct from `AIMech:WirtingerFlowPhaseRetrievalAndSpectralInitialization` (Candès, Li, & Soltanolkotabi 2015) and `AIMech:TruncatedWirtingerFlowAndTruncatedSpectralInitializationForQuadraticSystems` (Chen & Candès 2017), which minimize smooth fourth-order intensity-based or Poisson losses using fourth-moment spectral initialization and discard large-residual measurements, whereas Truncated Amplitude Flow minimizes the nonsmooth second-order amplitude loss $\frac{1}{2m}\sum_i(\psi_i - |\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{z}|)^2$ using magnitude-free spherical covariance initialization $\overline{\boldsymbol{Y}}_0$ and truncates small-$|\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{z}_t|/\psi_i$ watershed measurements that carry corrupted signs.

Mechanism #claim-orthogonality-promoting-initialization-subspace-ratio-and-sample-complexity
For unit signal $\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_2 = 1$ and leading unit eigenvector $\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}}_0$ of $\overline{\boldsymbol{Y}}_0 = \frac{1}{|\overline{\mathcal{I}}_0|}\overline{\boldsymbol{S}}_0^\top\overline{\boldsymbol{S}}_0$, Lemma 1 establishes the deterministic subspace perturbation inequality $\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{x}\boldsymbol{x}^\top - \tilde{\boldsymbol{z}}_0\tilde{\boldsymbol{z}}_0^\top\|_F^2 = \sin^2\theta \le \frac{\|\overline{\boldsymbol{S}}_0\boldsymbol{x}^\perp\|_2^2}{\|\overline{\boldsymbol{S}}_0\boldsymbol{x}\|_2^2}$ (Equations 29 and 68), and combining the conditional spherical sub-Gaussian upper bound $\|\overline{\boldsymbol{S}}_0\boldsymbol{x}^\perp\|_2^2 \le 1.01|\overline{\mathcal{I}}_0|/n$ (Lemma 2) with the order-statistic chi-square lower bound $\|\overline{\boldsymbol{S}}_0\boldsymbol{x}\|_2^2 \ge \frac{0.99|\overline{\mathcal{I}}_0|}{2.3n}[1 + \log(m/|\overline{\mathcal{I}}_0|)]$ (Lemma 3) proves Proposition 1: $\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{z}_0, \boldsymbol{x}) \le \rho\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_2$ with high probability once $m \ge c_1|\overline{\mathcal{I}}_0| \ge c_2 n$ (Section V-A, Proposition 1, Lemmas 1–3, Appendix A–C).

Mechanism #claim-watershed-sign-truncation-and-local-regularity-condition
Decomposing each generalized gradient summand as $\partial\ell_i(\boldsymbol{z}) = \boldsymbol{a}_i\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{h} + (\frac{\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{x}}{|\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{x}|} - \frac{\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{z}}{|\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{z}|})\psi_i\boldsymbol{a}_i$ where $\boldsymbol{h} = \boldsymbol{z} - \boldsymbol{x}$ (Equation 10), Lemma 4 proves that any surviving wrong-sign measurement in $\mathcal{E}_i \cap \mathcal{K}_i$ ($|\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{z}|/|\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{x}| \ge \frac{1}{1+\gamma}$ and $\mathrm{sign}(\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{z}) \ne \mathrm{sign}(\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{x})$) must satisfy $|\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{h}|/|\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{x}| \ge \frac{2+\gamma}{1+\gamma}$ ($\mathcal{D}_i$), and Lemmas 5–6 bound the Cauchy ratio tail to prove the Local Regularity Condition $\langle \boldsymbol{h}, \frac{1}{m}\nabla\ell_{\mathrm{tr}}(\boldsymbol{z})\rangle \ge (1 - \zeta_1 - \zeta_2 - 2\epsilon)\|\boldsymbol{h}\|_2^2$ with $\zeta_1 \approx 0.08$ and $\zeta_2^\prime \approx 0.2463$ at $\rho = 1/10, \gamma = 0.7$ (Section V-B, Propositions 2–3, Lemmas 4–6, Appendices D–E).

Application #claim-linear-time-geometric-convergence-and-noise-stability-near-information-limit
Theorem 1 and Equation 26 prove that with step size $\mu \le \mu_0 \approx 0.8388$ and truncation threshold $\gamma \ge 1/2$, Truncated Amplitude Flow contracts geometrically as $\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{z}_t,\boldsymbol{x}) \le \rho(1-\nu)^t\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_2$ in $O(mn\log(1/\epsilon))$ total flops and satisfies $\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{z}_t,\boldsymbol{x}) \lesssim (1-\nu)^t\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_2 + \frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\|\boldsymbol{\eta}\|_2$ under additive amplitude noise $\psi_i = |\boldsymbol{a}_i^\top\boldsymbol{x}| + \eta_i$, achieving 100% empirical recovery at $m = 3n$ and $>50\%$ recovery at the real-valued information-theoretic limit $m = 2n - 1$ (Sections III–IV, Theorem 1, Equations 24–26).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The watershed truncation rule |a_i^T z_t| >= psi_i / (1 + gamma) relies on the initial estimate z_0 lying within a constant relative neighborhood dist(z_0, x) <= rho ||x||_2; if initialization fails at very small sample sizes m < 2n - 1, the problem is information-theoretically underdetermined.
  • Replacing the minimum-eigenvalue formulation (Eq. 16) with the complementary maximum-eigenvalue matrix Y_bar_0 (Eq. 18) relies on the concentration of (1/m) sum_i a_i a_i^T / ||a_i||_2^2 around (1/n) I_n, which requires isotropic or pre-whitened sensing vectors.
