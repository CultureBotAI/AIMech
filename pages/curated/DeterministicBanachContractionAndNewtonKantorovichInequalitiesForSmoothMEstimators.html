Deterministic Banach contraction and Newton-Kantorovich inequalities for smooth M-estimators

Mathematical Methods AIMech:DeterministicBanachContractionAndNewtonKantorovichInequalitiesForSmoothMEstimators DRAFT
Aliases: Banach Contraction Inversion and Semilocal Newton-Kantorovich Bahadur Expansions for M-Estimators, Generalized Self-Concordant Hessian-Ratio C(u, w) Deterministic M-Estimation Inequalities, Deterministic Leave-k-Out Cross-Validation and Cox Partial Likelihood Bahadur Bounds
Deterministic Banach contraction and Newton-Kantorovich inequalities for smooth M-estimators are a finite-sample, assumption-free analytical framework introduced by Kuchibhotla (2018, arXiv:1809.05172v1) that decouples the existence, localization, and first-order Bahadur influence-function expansion of zeros of smooth estimating equations f(theta) = 0 from any probabilistic or i.i.d. data-generating assumptions. Using the Banach fixed-point theorem applied to phi(theta) := theta - A^{-1} f(theta) on B(theta_0, r), Theorem 2.1 proves that whenever sup_{theta in B(theta_0, r)} ||A^{-1}(A - nabla f(theta))||_{op} <= varepsilon < 1 and ||A^{-1} f(theta_0)||_2 <= r(1 - varepsilon) for an arbitrary reference point theta_0 (not necessarily satisfying E[f(theta_0)] = 0), there exists a unique root theta^* in B(theta_0, r) satisfying the two-sided norm sandwiching (1 + varepsilon)^{-1} ||A^{-1} f(theta_0)||_2 <= ||theta^* - theta_0||_2 <= (1 - varepsilon)^{-1} ||A^{-1} f(theta_0)||_2; under local Holder Jacobian continuity ||[nabla f(theta_0)]^{-1}(nabla f(theta_0) - nabla f(theta))||_{op} <= L ||theta - theta_0||_2^alpha, Theorem 2.2 establishes the semilocal Newton-Kantorovich Bahadur expansion ||theta^* - theta_0 + [nabla f(theta_0)]^{-1} f(theta_0)||_2 <= (1.5)^{1+alpha} L ||[nabla f(theta_0)]^{-1} f(theta_0)||_2^{1+alpha}. For smooth convex losses L(theta; w), Section 3 introduces the scale-invariant directional Hessian-ratio modulus C(u, w) := sup_{||theta_1 - theta_2||_2 <= u} sup_{||e||_2 = 1} (e^T nabla_2 L(theta_1, w) e) / (e^T nabla_2 L(theta_2, w) e) >= 1 (forming a convex cone C_T under nonnegative combinations, Proposition 3.1) and proves in Theorem 3.1 that whenever max_{1<=i<=n} C(delta_n(theta_0), W_i) <= 4/3 with delta_n(theta_0) := 1.5 ||[Q_hat_n(theta_0)]^{-1} Z_hat_n(theta_0)||_2, a root theta_hat_n exists with (1/2) delta_n(theta_0) <= ||theta_hat_n - theta_0||_2 <= delta_n(theta_0) and ||theta_hat_n - theta_0 + [Q_hat_n(theta_0)]^{-1} Z_hat_n(theta_0)||_2 <= max_{1<=i<=n} {C(delta_n(theta_0), W_i) - 1} delta_n(theta_0), extending directly to generalized linear models (Corollary 3.1), Cox proportional hazards partial likelihood (Theorem 4.1), non-convex least squares (Corollary 5.1), equality-constrained KKT systems (Corollary 6.1), and sample-computable leave-k-out cross-validation, marginal screening, and uniform-in-submodel post-selection inference (Corollaries 7.1-7.4).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Smoothness / Differentiability Requirement on the Estimating Equation: Theorems 2.1, 2.2, 3.1, and 4.1 require the estimating equation f(theta) = Z_hat_n(theta) to be everywhere differentiable (or the loss L(theta; w) to be twice differentiable with non-singular empirical Hessian Q_hat_n(theta_0)); non-smooth M-estimators such as median/quantile regression or hinge-loss SVM require smooth convolution/mollifier approximation as discussed in Section 8.1.
  • Local Contraction Radius and Empirical Hessian Invertibility Condition: The deterministic guarantees in Theorems 2.2 and 3.1 hold conditional on the empirical Hessian Q_hat_n(theta_0) (or block Jacobian J_n(beta_0)) being invertible and the scaled Newton step delta_n(theta_0) := 1.5 ||[Q_hat_n(theta_0)]^{-1} Z_hat_n(theta_0)||_2 satisfying the local Hessian-ratio bound max_{1<=i<=n} C(delta_n(theta_0), W_i) <= 4/3 (or sup_s mu_n(s) delta_n(beta_0) <= 1/16 for the Cox partial likelihood in Theorem 4.1).
