A distributed adaptive packet-routing algorithm that learns destination-specific delivery-time estimates by combining observed local queue and transmission delays with a neighboring node’s estimate of remaining delivery time.
Definition Evidence

Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes Boyan and Littman’s original tabular Q-routing method. It minimizes predicted delivery time using local bootstrapped information, unlike AntNet’s measured complete forward-trip feedback and probabilistic routing-table reinforcement. The exploratory full-echo scheme is a distinct variant, not an unmentioned mandatory part of the baseline. A neural approximation was explored but is not the defining original representation. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
At node x, maintain Q_x(d,y), the estimated time to deliver a packet to destination d through neighbor y, including waiting in x’s queue. Route using the neighbor with the smallest current estimate. Upon sending the packet to y, obtain its estimate t=min_z Q_y(d,z). With observed waiting time q at x and link-transmission time s from x to y, update the used table entry by Q_x(d,y)_new=Q_x(d,y)+eta*(q+s+t−Q_x(d,y)). This is an asynchronous local cost backup; it uses a minimum because delivery times are costs and the displayed target has no discount factor. The baseline only updates the selected neighbor entry. The full-echo extension separately queries all neighbors and updates their estimates, under a simulation assumption that the extra queries do not themselves congest the network.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The greedy baseline has no deliberate exploration once its initial policy is established. Overestimated unused alternatives can remain stale, impeding rediscovery of shortcuts when load decreases; the source explicitly reports this weakness. Full echo changes the information and update scheme and may introduce oscillation under heavy traffic.
  • Queue-dependent delays, other adaptive routers and changing traffic make the environment nonstationary relative to a destination/neighbor table. The record imports no stationary tabular Q-learning convergence theorem, optimality certificate, loop-freedom guarantee or universal routing superiority.
  • The displayed interior-node update needs declared destination-boundary, initialization, tie-breaking, disconnected-network and message-delay conventions in an executable implementation. The method text does not fully specify all these cases, and a simulator or complete protocol is not supplied by this conceptual record.
  • Results come from a simplified discrete-event network simulation; the paper explicitly says it is not fully realistic. The neural-network experiments were inconclusive. No empirical performance claim or deployment validation is curated.
