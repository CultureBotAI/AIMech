Particle swarm optimization

Algorithms AIMech:ParticleSwarmOptimization DRAFT
Aliases: PSO
A population-based optimization method that moves candidate particles through a search space using retained velocities and stochastic attraction toward remembered personal and shared best positions.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This existing continuous PSO identity covers the retained global-best/inertia evidence and the original MHS 1995 local-information variant. The ICNN 1995 simplified rule retains velocity with coefficient 1 and attraction coefficients 2; MHS uses its own ACC_CONST and velocity limits, while the later inertia source is a distinct configuration. It is not differential evolution, an objective-gradient method, a biological flock model, or every discrete, neighborhood or constricted PSO variant. Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #method
Initialize a population of real-valued positions and velocities and evaluate the objective. Retain each particle’s best evaluated position and the best position shared across the population. For each coordinate in the inspected global-best rule, add two randomly scaled displacements to the retained velocity: one from the current position toward the personal best and one toward the global best. The baseline is v_new=v+c1*r1*(p−x)+c2*r2*(g−x), followed by x_new=x+v_new; the source gives random factors in [0,1]. Evaluate the resulting positions and refresh the remembered bests when improved. Particles remain population members rather than undergoing target-trial survival selection. In the 1995 simplified version c1=c2=2. The 1999 inertia variant replaces the retained term v by w*v and studies a decreasing w schedule. Initialization, population size, coefficients, velocity limits, boundary treatment, update ordering and stopping rules must be specified for an executable configuration.

Mechanism #original-local-social-information
In the MHS 1995 local-information variant, each particle retains its own best evaluated position and has access to the best solutions remembered by its array neighbors. A neighborhood best replaces the population-wide best as the social attractor, while the particle retains its velocity and the separate random attraction toward its personal best. The local social input is a selected best remembered position, not the arithmetic average of neighboring positions or a certified optimum near the particle in the objective domain. This changes the information available to the same particle-swarm search mechanism rather than defining a new particle identity or requiring a learned graph.

Scope #original-array-neighborhood-configurations
The source describes a two-neighbor configuration using the immediately adjacent array positions i-1 and i+1, and a six-neighbor configuration using three positions on each side. It explicitly states wrapped array endpoints for the six-neighbor description. These neighborhoods concern particle-array adjacency, not nearest current positions in the search space. The names neighborhood=2 and neighborhood=6 count the described other array positions; the particle also retains its own personal-best information. These selected configurations do not specify a universal meaning of local-best PSO or a complete implementation for every population size.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Current positions can overshoot remembered bests and have worse objective values. Attraction to a best-so-far position is not attraction to a known global optimum. If every particle has zero velocity and its current position equals both remembered attractors, this displayed rule has no motion; the fixed state need not be optimal.
  • The original coefficient 1 on old velocity and the later inertia weight must not be conflated. The 1999 experiment’s decreasing weight and Vmax/Xmax settings are particular choices, not universal optimal settings or a complete bound-repair specification. Clipping a velocity alone does not ensure a feasible position.
  • The full random-stream coupling, synchronous versus asynchronous best updates, ties, constraints and noisy evaluations require declared implementation choices. The social analogy is not a convergence proof. The 1999 discussion explicitly allows failure to find the required optimum; no universal speed, accuracy or finite-budget global-optimum guarantee is asserted.
  • The MHS local-neighborhood description supplies prose rather than complete local-version pseudocode. An implementation must declare the social-best candidate set and self membership, tie handling, finite-size endpoint conventions and update scheduling. Wrapping is explicit in the six-neighbor passage and is not evidence that every local-best variant uses the same ring. The neighborhood best denotes a remembered evaluated solution; noisy evaluations and uncertainty are not resolved by that selector.
  • The MHS paper’s apparent immunity of its two-neighbor runs to local optima is an empirical interpretation, not a guarantee of escape or global optimality. If all particles share a nonoptimal position, zero velocity and personal memories at that position, every neighborhood best coincides with it and the displayed attraction rule remains fixed. This is an admissible-state algebraic boundary, not a claim about the probability of that state under a specified initialization. The paper’s comparative observations do not establish a universal ordering of neighborhood sizes or topologies.
