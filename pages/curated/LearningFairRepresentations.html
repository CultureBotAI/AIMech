Learning Fair Representations

Algorithms AIMech:LearningFairRepresentations DRAFT
Aliases: LFR, Learned Fair Representations
A prototype-based representation-learning algorithm that jointly balances reconstruction, supervised prediction and differences between group-average prototype assignment probabilities.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The named LFR algorithm learns a prototype representation and a prediction rule. It is distinct from the general aim of learning fair representations, the finite-population FairnessLP formulation, and the later Variational Fair Autoencoder. The learned metric variant does not make it identical to AIO MetricLearning, which denotes a deep neural network in this pin. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method-scope
Map an individual to softmax probabilities over learned prototypes using negative distances. Optimize prototype locations and bounded prototype label probabilities with a weighted sum of squared reconstruction loss, predictive cross-entropy and absolute differences of mean assignment probabilities between two protected groups. The paper also learns feature-distance weights separately by group and uses L-BFGS for its optimization.
  • supports Source: Learning Fair Representations · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.3, equations (1)–(12), PDF pp. 2–4; Section 4.1, PDF p. 6 for optimization
Limitation #representation-distribution-limit
Louizos and colleagues identify a higher-order-distribution limitation of matching average LFR assignment probabilities: groups can have the same average assignment while the distribution of those probability values differs. This concerns the exposed probability-vector representation, not a refutation of parity of an exactly matched sampled prototype index.
  • supports Source: The Variational Fair Autoencoder · Locator: Related Work, Section 4, PDF pp. 8–9, paragraph beginning with the comparison to Zemel et al.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Fairness, reconstruction and predictive utility are weighted objectives; finite training and a nonzero penalty weight do not guarantee exact population parity.
  • Equal group means of assignment probabilities do not imply equality of the distributions of the full assignment-probability vectors. Arbitrary nonlinear downstream processing or thresholding requires a separate fairness assessment.
  • The method encourages local similarity; it does not certify the full supplied-metric pairwise Lipschitz criterion for every pair of individuals.
  • The group-specific distance variant uses group membership for its mapping. Out-of-sample representation availability is not a proof of out-of-sample fairness.
