Variable neighborhood search

Algorithms AIMech:VariableNeighborhoodSearch DRAFT
A local-search metaheuristic that repeatedly perturbs an incumbent through an indexed collection of neighborhoods, locally optimizes the perturbed point, resets the neighborhood index after strict incumbent improvement and advances it after failure, under a declared stopping rule.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The selected identity is the basic 1997 Mladenovic–Hansen VNS controller: configured shaking neighborhoods, a separately declared inner local search and strict-improvement neighborhood reset. It is not an exact alias of ILS, FLS, GLS, tabu search, simulated annealing, HillClimbing or ParamILS; overlap and hybrid composition remain possible. Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Scope #configured-neighborhood-controller
Choose an initial feasible incumbent x and a finite indexed collection of shaking neighborhood structures N_1 through N_kmax. The basic VNS controller varies which neighborhood supplies the next perturbed starting point. Successive neighborhoods are often nested, but nesting is not required by the stated method.
  • supports Source: Variable neighborhood search · Locator: Section 2, PDF pp. 1–2 / printed pp. 1097–1098, neighborhood questions, initialization and paragraph after the main step.
Mechanism #random-shaking-and-local-optimization
At the current neighborhood index k, generate x_prime at random from N_k(x), then apply a configured local-search method with x_prime as its initial solution. Denote its locally optimized result by x_double_prime. The source does not prescribe a uniform sampling distribution, and the shaken point need not improve the incumbent.
  • supports Source: Variable neighborhood search · Locator: Section 2, PDF p. 2 / printed p. 1098, main step 2(a–b) and the subsequent descent characterization.
Mechanism #strict-improvement-reset
In the basic descent version, if x_double_prime is strictly better than the incumbent, replace x by x_double_prime and reset k to 1. Otherwise retain the incumbent and advance k by one. The improvement test is applied after local optimization; it is not a requirement that every intermediate perturbation improve the objective.
  • supports Source: Variable neighborhood search · Locator: Section 2, PDF p. 2 / printed p. 1098, main step 2(c) and characterization as a descent, first-improvement method.
Mechanism #separate-local-search-neighborhood
The baseline local-search routine may use a fixed neighborhood distinct from the shaking neighborhoods, and that neighborhood need not belong to the selected collection N_1 through N_kmax. The source also discusses changing local-search neighborhoods as an extension; the basic controller does not require that extension.
  • supports Source: Variable neighborhood search · Locator: Section 2, PDF p. 2 / printed p. 1098, variant (iii), distinction between steps 2(a) and 2(b).
Scope #repeated-budgeted-main-cycle
The main neighborhood cycle can be repeated until a declared stopping condition, such as a maximum iteration count, CPU-time allowance or iterations without improvement. The basic strict-improvement controller is distinguished from the paper’s optional descent-ascent, best-neighborhood and generalized index-schedule variants.
  • supports Source: Variable neighborhood search · Locator: Section 2, PDF p. 2 / printed p. 1098, paragraph following the main step and variants (i–iii); Section 4, PDF p. 4 / printed p. 1100.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The printed main loop says until k=k_max although initialization lists N_1 through N_kmax. That endpoint wording does not unambiguously specify whether the last listed neighborhood is processed, especially when k_max=1. The mechanism description preserves the reset and advancement rules without claiming a uniquely reconstructed executable loop.
  • Random sampling is not specified as uniform and does not guarantee that samples never repeat. The anti-cycling motivation is not a proof of cycle freedom. An unsuccessful shaken point followed by local search does not exhaust its shaking neighborhood or certify that no improving point exists there.
  • Strictly improving accepted incumbents imply only finitely many accepted improvements when the feasible solution set is finite. This hand consequence does not ensure termination of an arbitrary inner optimizer or an outer loop lacking a finite stopping rule, and does not prove convergence to a global optimum or a finite-run optimality certificate.
  • Neighborhood feasibility, coverage, sampling, the inner optimizer and its stopping convention must be declared. A local optimum is relative to that inner method’s neighborhood and accuracy, not automatically to every shaking neighborhood. Nested neighborhoods and connectivity are not supplied as universal guarantees by the basic description.
  • The source’s optional floor-based neighborhood-increment expression can give zero. It is outside the selected basic k:=k+1 schedule and is not silently repaired or promoted to a verified executable variant. Accepting worsening incumbents, taking the best among multiple neighborhoods or adding tabu-style mechanisms likewise requires its separately stated variant choices.
  • Evidence here is the four-page 1997 article and its basic controller, not an author-code execution or benchmark reproduction. GENIUS/TSP and traveling-salesman-with-backhauls comparisons do not establish universal speed, quality or complexity claims. Detailed GENI formulas and numerical table values extracted from the scanned pages are not asserted as exact implementation specifications.
