R-Novelty+ SAT heuristic

Algorithms AIMech:RNoveltyPlus DRAFT
Aliases: R-Novelty+
The 1999 R-Novelty extension that replaces its periodic random override with a fixed per-step probability of a random clause-variable flip and otherwise uses the recency- and score-gap-dependent R-Novelty selector.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The record is scoped to Hoos’s 1999 R-Novelty+ definition. It removes the original every-100-step override, rather than adding a random-walk branch while silently retaining that schedule. The 2004 competition report uses Rnovelty+ for a periodic configuration; that differently specified variant is not the present identity. At zero walk probability this method reduces to the core R-Novelty selector without the 1997 periodic escape, not the entire original 1997 strategy. Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #method
Randomly select an unsatisfied clause. With fixed walk probability w in [0,1], choose a variable uniformly from that clause and flip it. Otherwise apply the core R-Novelty selector: rank candidates by global post-flip violation count with oldest-variable tie breaks. Choose the best if it is not the most recently flipped variable in the clause. If it is most recent, let d be the second-best count minus the best count, with d>=1 under the stated age/score assumptions, and use inner noise p in [0,1]. For p<0.5 choose second-best with probability 2p when d=1 and with probability zero when d>1. For p>=0.5 choose second-best with probability one when d=1 and with probability 2(p−0.5) when d>1. Choose best otherwise. Update the timestamp of the flipped variable. Omit the periodic every-100-step override: the new walk coin is evaluated at each step. A fixed w>0 supports the source’s asymptotic result; w=1 gives pure clause-first random walk and w=0 retains only the core selector.

  • supports Source: Evidence for Invariants in Local Search · Locator: PDF2 / printed322, Novelty and R-Novelty selector definitions; PDF3 / printed323, original periodic R-Novelty override
Scope #asymptotic-model-finding
For a finite satisfiable CNF and a well-defined remaining move policy, a fixed positive lower bound on the probability of a uniform random-clause/random-variable step at every search step gives success probability approaching one as the number of steps tends to infinity. Hoos1999 Theorem 2 establishes this via a positive probability of a sequence reducing Hamming distance to a model. The conclusion does not give a polynomial or practically small runtime, a finite-run guarantee, or an UNSAT certificate.
  • supports Source: On the Run-time Behaviour of Stochastic Local Search Algorithms for SAT · Locator: PDF3, Definition 2 and no-restart scope; PDF3–4, Lemma 1 and Theorem 2; PDF5–6, application to the plus extensions
Scope #plus-source-version-boundary
Hoos1999 explicitly removes the periodic escape when defining R-Novelty+, while the 2004 competition report calls an every-100-step configuration Rnovelty+. This record follows the former definition and does not treat the latter’s cycling-prevention statement as its theorem.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The asymptotic guarantee requires a strictly positive walk probability at every step, bounded below independently of elapsed search history. A scheduled-only override or a probability that tends to zero does not automatically satisfy that premise. At w=0 the random-walk argument no longer applies.
  • The guarantee concerns finding a model on a finite satisfiable formula in the limit of continued search. A finite budget can miss an existing model and cannot certify UNSAT. The source’s lower bound can be extremely small; no polynomial or practical runtime follows.
  • The walk probability w is separate from the inner selector’s noise parameter p. The reported experimental w=0.01 is not a defining or universally optimal constant. Optional outer restarts and cutoffs must be specified separately.
  • Empty clauses, unit clauses lacking a second candidate, initial age ties, duplicate literals and timestamp/reset conventions require a well-defined implementation. The general random-walk proof does not certify undocumented choices in the short selector description. No author solver or benchmark was executed.
  • The asymptotic guarantee for the 1999 per-step extension must not be transferred to the 2004 periodic configuration merely because both use a plus label. The previously proved trapping example shows why scheduled random steps can miss an essential variable.
