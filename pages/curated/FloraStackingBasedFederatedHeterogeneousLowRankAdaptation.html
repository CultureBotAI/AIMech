FLoRA: Stacking-Based Federated Fine-Tuning with Heterogeneous Low-Rank Adaptations

Algorithms AIMech:FloraStackingBasedFederatedHeterogeneousLowRankAdaptation DRAFT
Aliases: FLoRA, Stacking-Based Federated Heterogeneous LoRA, FLoRA Federated Fine-Tuning with Heterogeneous Low-Rank Adaptations
A noise-free, rank-heterogeneous federated parameter-efficient fine-tuning algorithm (FLoRA; Wang, Shen, He, Sun, Wang, Lyu, and Li, NeurIPS 2024) that enables K distributed clients with heterogeneous LoRA ranks {r_k}_{k=0}^K to collaboratively fine-tune a pre-trained model W in R^{m x n}. In each communication round, client k initializes and trains local low-rank adapter matrices A_k in R^{r_k x n} and B_k in R^{m x r_k}; instead of independently averaging A_k and B_k (which introduces cross-client error sum_{i != j} p_i p_j B_i A_j and squared diagonal weights p_k^2, Eq. 6), the server constructs global stacked adapters by vertically concatenating scaled A_k blocks A = p_0 A_0 (+) p_1 A_1 (+) ... (+) p_K A_K in R^{(sum_{k=0}^K r_k) x n} and horizontally concatenating B_k blocks B = B_0 (+) B_1 (+) ... (+) B_K in R^{m x (sum_{k=0}^K r_k)} (Eqs. 9-12) (optionally permuting rank-1 sub-modules at random for cross-client privacy), satisfying the exact identity B A = sum_{k=0}^K p_k B_k A_k before redistributing (B, A) to update local base models W <- W + B A.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from FFA-LoRA (AIMech:FfaLoraFederatedFreezeALowRankAdaptation; Sun et al., ICLR 2024), which eliminates aggregation cross-terms by freezing a homogeneous random projection matrix A_0 across all clients rather than training both A_k and B_k with heterogeneous client ranks r_k and stacking them on the server. Distinct from FedIT (Zhang et al., 2023) and zero-padded heterogeneous LoRA (HetLoRA; Cho et al., 2023), which average A_k and B_k separately and suffer from cross-client product noise (sum_k p_k B_k)(sum_k p_k A_k) != sum_k p_k B_k A_k. Also distinct from centralized memory-efficient low-rank gradient projection (Flora; Hao et al., 2024), which resamples random projection matrices on a single machine rather than stacking multi-client heterogeneous LoRA adapters in federated learning.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #flora-block-matrix-stacking-exact-aggregation-and-rank1-permutation-mechanism
Given K + 1 participating clients with arbitrary local LoRA ranks r_k and sample-proportion weights p_k = |D_k| / sum_{j=0}^K |D_j| applied to A_k only, FLoRA vertically stacks A = p_0 A_0 (+) ... (+) p_K A_K in R^{(sum_k r_k) x n} and horizontally stacks B = B_0 (+) ... (+) B_K in R^{m x (sum_k r_k)} (Eqs. 9-12, pp. 4-6), guaranteeing the exact noise-free outer-product decomposition B A = sum_{k=0}^K p_k B_k A_k (Eq. 10) while allowing the server to decompose (B, A) into sum_k r_k rank-1 sub-modules and randomly permute their order so clients cannot identify individual peers' adapters (Section 5, p. 9).

Scope #flora-non-iid-strongly-convex-convergence-rate-scope
Under Assumptions 1-5 (L-smoothness, mu-strong convexity, bounded stochastic gradient variance sigma_k^2 and second moment G^2, and unbiased LoRA local update steps B_k^{(t+1)} A_k^{(t+1)} - B_k^{(t)} A_k^{(t)} = eta^{(t)} nabla F_k(W_k^{(t)} | xi_k^{(t)})), Theorem 1 (Appendix B, Eqs. 13-14, p. 14) proves that FLoRA with step size alpha_k / r_k = 2 / (mu (gamma + t)), kappa = L / mu, and gamma = max{8 kappa, E} bounds the expected fine-tuning optimality gap by delta^{(T)} = E[F(w^{(T-1)} + B^{(T)} A^{(T)})] - F* <= (2 kappa / (gamma + T))(M / mu + 2 L ||W^{(1)} - W*||^2) = O(1 / T) where M = sum_{k=1}^K p_k^2 sigma_k^2 + 6 L Gamma + 8 (E - 1)^2 G^2.

Limitation #flora-downlink-stacked-rank-communication-and-reinitialization-limitation
Because the server broadcasts the stacked matrices A in R^{(sum_k r_k) x n} and B in R^{m x (sum_k r_k)} whose inner dimension equals the sum of all participating clients' ranks sum_{k=0}^K r_k, FLoRA increases per-round downlink communication relative to FedIT (e.g., 0.215 vs 0.177 of full fine-tuning over 3 rounds in Figure 6, p. 9) and requires clients to merge B A into W and re-initialize fresh local LoRA adapters each round (Section 3.2, pp. 5-6; Section 5, pp. 8-9; Appendix C, p. 14).

Application #flora-heterogeneous-rank-federated-llm-instruction-tuning-application
Applied to homogeneous and rank-heterogeneous (r_k in {4, 8, 16, 32, 64} and dynamic AdaLoRA) federated instruction tuning of TinyLlama-1.1B, LLaMA-7B, and Llama2-7B on Dolly-15K, Alpaca, Wizard, and ShareGPT evaluated on MMLU and MT-bench.
  • supports Source: FLoRA: Federated Fine-Tuning Large Language Models with Heterogeneous Low-Rank Adaptations · Locator: Section 4 Experiments, Table 1, Figures 4-5, pp. 6-8; Appendix A.3 Tables 3-4, p. 13

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Downlink communication per round scales with the sum of participating clients' LoRA ranks sum_{k=0}^K r_k rather than a single average rank (Section 3.2, p. 6; Appendix C, p. 14).
  • Merging the stacked global update B A into the base weight matrix W at each round requires re-initializing local LoRA adapters before the next round of local training (Section 3.2, p. 5).
  • Empirical performance is sensitive to the dataset- and model-dependent choice of the scaling factor p_k (Section 4.2, Figure 5, p. 8).
