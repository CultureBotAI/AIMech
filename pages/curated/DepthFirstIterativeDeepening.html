Depth-first iterative deepening

Algorithms AIMech:DepthFirstIterativeDeepening DRAFT

Aliases: DFID, Iterative deepening depth-first search, IDDFS
A search algorithm that repeatedly performs depth-first searches from the start with successively larger depth limits, redoing shallower work until it reaches a goal.
Definition Evidence
  • supports Source: Depth-First Iterative-Deepening: An Optimal Admissible Tree Search · Locator: Section 4, printed pp. 100–102 / PDF pp. 4–6; procedure and exponential-tree analysis
  • supports Source: Linear-Space Best-First Search: Summary of Results · Locator: Previous Work: Iterative Deepening, printed p. 533 / PDF p. 1
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes single-agent depth-bounded iterative deepening, also called iterative deepening depth-first search. It differs from IDA-star, whose limits apply to estimated path cost, and from time-managed minimax iterations in game-playing applications. No normalized identity match was found across the 442 AIO and 119 prior native records; ignored-inclusive text hits in the main checkout were dependency-package documentation and an unrelated package-metadata substring, not existing concept records.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #search-procedure
Complete a depth-limited depth-first search, discard its generated search tree, increase the depth limit, and restart from the initial state. The procedure repeats shallow expansions while retaining only the current depth-first traversal state rather than the complete breadth-first frontier.
  • supports Source: Depth-First Iterative-Deepening: An Optimal Admissible Tree Search · Locator: Section 4, printed pp. 100–102 / PDF pp. 4–6; procedure and exponential-tree analysis
Mechanism #depth-threshold-distinction
The later primary paper identifies depth-first iterative deepening by a depth-based cutoff, in contrast to IDA-star’s g+h cutoff, and describes repeated depth-first passes that keep the current search path.
  • supports Source: Linear-Space Best-First Search: Summary of Results · Locator: Previous Work: Iterative Deepening, printed p. 533 / PDF p. 1

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Depth measures operator count, not arbitrary accumulated edge cost. Finite branching is needed for each bounded iteration to finish.
  • Repeated shallow work remains real. The paper’s exponential-tree analysis is conditional and does not establish uniform efficiency on graphs with many duplicate paths or near-unit branching.
  • The 1986 erratum to the original journal paper was identified but its text could not be accessed. The procedure is corroborated by the 1992 primary paper; this record does not assert the original global time-and-space optimality theorem.
