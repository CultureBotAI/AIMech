Normalized temperature-scaled cross entropy loss

Mathematical Methods AIMech:NTXentLoss DRAFT
Aliases: NT-Xent, NT-Xent loss
A contrastive cross-entropy objective that uses cosine-normalized, temperature-scaled similarities to identify a paired positive representation among nonself candidates, including that positive in the denominator.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope NT-Xent is the named normalized temperature-scaled configuration of the contrastive categorical objective in the SimCLR paper. It specializes the generic InfoNCE score and candidate construction rather than defining a mathematically unrelated loss family. The loss is not the full SimCLR augmentation/encoder/projection/training procedure, and its symmetric two-view minibatch aggregation is stated explicitly. The paper credits prior uses; no claim of first invention by SimCLR is made. Mechanism #method
For nonzero representations z_i,z_k and temperature tau>0, use s_ik=z_i^T z_k/(||z_i|| ||z_k||). In the SimCLR two-view configuration, N>=2 source examples produce 2N views; j is the other view of the same source as anchor i. Define ell(i,j)=−log[exp(s_ij/tau)/sum_(k=1..2N,k≠i) exp(s_ik/tau)]. The denominator contains 2N−1 terms: the paired positive and 2N−2 other-source views; only the anchor itself is excluded. Average ell over both directions of every positive pair, giving 2N anchor losses. Temperature changes the softmax weighting of candidate similarities. The paper applies this objective to projected representations, but the mathematical loss does not itself prescribe an encoder, augmentation family or optimizer.

Limitation #information-bound-scope
Curator qualification from comparing the two sampling constructions: SimCLR uses both augmented views of every other source as negative candidates, whereas Poole et al.’s Equation (10) states its InfoNCE expectation over independent joint pairs. The latter proof is not automatically a proof for the full SimCLR candidate construction. This comparison does not assert that no alternative dependent-candidate bound can hold.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Cosine similarity requires nonzero representations and the specified temperature is positive. The positive is included in the normalization; dropping it or retaining the self-comparison yields a different expression.
  • The two views of each other source are dependent candidates. The standard independent-negative InfoNCE mutual-information argument is not automatically established for this minibatch construction; this record asserts a loss, not an unconditional information certificate.
  • Other-source views are treated as negatives without guaranteeing semantic dissimilarity. Learned usefulness depends on positive construction, candidate sampling and representation choices; minimizing the objective alone does not certify downstream quality.
  • The named configuration shares the categorical form of InfoNCE. Its record preserves the explicit normalization, temperature and batching convention rather than claiming a new unrestricted variational bound. No model training or benchmark was reproduced.
