Reciprocal-nearest-neighbors supported clustering

Algorithms AIMech:ReciprocalNearestNeighborsSupportedClustering DRAFT
Aliases: RS clustering
A hierarchical clustering algorithm that follows nearest-neighbor chains to construct sub-clustering trees around reciprocal pairs, prunes overly deep nodes into singleton trees, and recursively clusters representatives formed from the supporting pairs.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #chain-subtrees
Begin with the current nodes as candidates. Choose an initial candidate at random and follow nearest-neighbor pointers until reaching a reciprocal pair or an existing sub-clustering tree. A reciprocal pair supports a new artificial root; chains reaching an existing tree attach to it. Remove assigned nodes from the candidate set and continue until all nodes have been assigned. The paper perturbs distances by small random amounts to avoid tied nearest neighbors.
  • supports Source: Hierarchical Clustering Supported by Reciprocal Nearest Neighbors · Locator: Algorithm, PDFp2; Figure1, PDFp3
Mechanism #depth-pruning
Define a node’s depth as one half of the sum of its path lengths to the two supporting nodes plus one, treating the supporting pair as directly connected. For a tree C, prune nodes deeper than ceil(log_alpha(|C|+1)) into separate singleton-root trees. The logarithmic threshold requires alpha>1; the reported experiments use alpha 1.5 and the illustration uses 2.
  • supports Source: Hierarchical Clustering Supported by Reciprocal Nearest Neighbors · Locator: Algorithm, Equations1–2 and Figure1, PDFpp2–3; Figure S1, PDFp12
Mechanism #recursive-representatives
Represent an ordinary sub-clustering tree by the midpoint of its two supporting nodes, and a singleton tree by its unchanged point. Repeat the construction and pruning on these representatives to build the hierarchy. A supporting-pair midpoint generally differs from the centroid of all tree members; the procedure does not preserve a prescribed Ward, average or other standard agglomerative linkage.
  • supports Source: Hierarchical Clustering Supported by Reciprocal Nearest Neighbors · Locator: Algorithm, PDFpp3–4 and Figure2
Scope #geometric-distance-updates
For Euclidean point locations, midpoint-to-point and midpoint-to-midpoint squared distances can be computed from the pairwise squared distances among their supporting endpoints. These are geometric identities in that setting. The paper’s additional use with nonembedded dissimilarities requires separate admissibility checks; it is not accepted here as an unrestricted identity for arbitrary distance matrices.
  • supports Source: Hierarchical Clustering Supported by Reciprocal Nearest Neighbors · Locator: Algorithm, three distance-only cases, PDFp4
Performance #reported-evaluation
The source reports comparisons on eight UCI datasets and an Olivetti face dataset using unadjusted Rand Index against group average, CURE, affinity propagation and density-peaks methods. Cluster counts vary across methods and iterations; the affinity-propagation optdigits result lies outside the displayed range. Supplementary CPU timings use generated data and a 3.4 GHz four-core Intel Core i5. The fitted runtime exponent is an empirical summary, not an asymptotic complexity guarantee or universal accuracy ranking.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This record covers the main clustering construction, with Euclidean representative geometry. The paper’s network extension adds pseudoinverse distances, infinite nonadjacent distances, stopping/attachment rules and betweenness-based postprocessing; that extension is outside the maintained core claim scope.
  • The described random tie perturbation does not fully specify its distribution, seed, symmetric treatment or zero-distance handling. No author implementation was inspected; no deterministic tie convention is silently supplied.
  • Finite curator checks illustrate a seven-point chain, depth thresholds for alpha 2 and 1.5, a supporting midpoint that differs from a retained-member centroid, and Euclidean midpoint identities. They are not a complete RS implementation or benchmark replication.
  • A separate four-cycle metric makes the unrestricted midpoint-pair formula negative, but its chosen endpoint pairs are not reciprocal in that input. This is only a counterexample to an unrestricted geometric identity, not an actual RS-execution failure.
  • The evaluation descriptions contain provenance ambiguities, including the group-average baseline citing Ward 1963 and unusual iris/glass table entries. The exact historical datasets, implementations, preprocessing, repetitions and corresponding 2020 journal revision were not reproduced or equated with current versions.
  • All 13 pages of arXiv1907.04915v1 were visually read, including five main figures, three supplementary figures and two tables. The record remains DRAFT; no author code, model fitting or performance experiment was executed.
