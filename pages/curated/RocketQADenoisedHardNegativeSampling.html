RocketQA (Cross-Batch and Denoised Hard Negative Dense Passage Retrieval Training)

Algorithms AIMech:RocketQADenoisedHardNegativeSampling DRAFT
Aliases: RocketQA Dense Passage Retrieval Training, RocketQA Denoised Hard Negative Pipeline, RocketQA Multi-Stage Dual-Encoder Training
A four-step multi-stage training algorithm for dual-encoder dense passage retrieval that (1) trains an initial dual-encoder M_D^(0) on labeled data D_L using differentiable all-gather cross-batch negatives across A GPUs (yielding A*B - 1 negatives per question for per-GPU batch size B), (2) trains a cross-encoder M_C on top-k candidates retrieved by M_D^(0), (3) trains a second dual-encoder M_D^(1) using denoised hard negatives mined from M_D^(0)'s top ranks that M_C predicts as negative with high confidence (score < 0.1), and (4) trains a final dual-encoder M_D^(2) on D_L plus pseudo-labeled external questions D_U filtered by M_C (< 0.1 for negatives, > 0.9 for positives).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Introduced by Qu, Ding, Liu, Liu, Ren, Zhao, Dong, Wu, and Wang (NAACL-HLT 2021, pp. 5835-5847, arXiv:2010.08191). Distinguished from DPR (Karpukhin et al., 2020), which uses single-GPU in-batch negatives and BM25 hard negatives, from ANCE (Xiong et al., 2021), which asynchronously mines top-k dense negatives without cross-encoder denoising (suffering from ~70% false negatives among unannotated top retrieved MS MARCO passages), and from GPL (Wang et al., 2022), which uses synthetic T5 query generation and MarginMSE soft-label distillation for unsupervised target domain adaptation.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #rocketqa-cross-batch-and-denoised-hard-negative-mechanism
RocketQA shares passage embeddings across A GPUs via a differentiable all-gather operator to expand the negative pool from B - 1 to A*B - 1 negatives per question (e.g., 8 * 512 - 1 = 4095 on MS MARCO), trains a cross-encoder M_C on M_D^(0)'s top-k retrieval distribution, and removes false negatives (which account for ~70% of top unannotated passages retrieved on MS MARCO, including 75% at rank 5 and 52% at rank 10) by retaining only top-retrieved passages with M_C(q, p) < 0.1 as denoised hard negatives and labeling unlabeled queries Q_U with M_C(q, p) > 0.9 as positives.
  • supports Source: RocketQA: An Optimized Training Approach to Dense Passage Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering · Locator: Section 3.3-3.4 (Figures 2-3), Section 4.1.3, & Section 4.2.2 (Figure 5), pp. 4-6, 8
  • supports Source: RocketQA: An Optimized Training Approach to Dense Passage Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering · Locator: count_negatives_per_question, filter_rocketqa_candidates & main
Scope #rocketqa-msmarco-and-nq-progressive-stage-gains
On MS MARCO Passage Ranking, RocketQA's consecutive stages improve Dev MRR@10 from 32.39 (in-batch negatives) to 33.32 (Step 1 cross-batch negatives), whereas un-denoised hard negatives collapse MRR@10 to 26.03; cross-encoder hard-negative denoising (Step 3) raises MRR@10 to 36.38 and cross-encoder data augmentation (Step 4) reaches 37.02 MRR@10 (and 82.7% Recall@20 on Natural Questions).
  • supports Source: RocketQA: An Optimized Training Approach to Dense Passage Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering · Locator: Section 4.2.1 (Table 2) & Section 4.2.2 (Table 3), pp. 7-8
  • supports Source: RocketQA: An Optimized Training Approach to Dense Passage Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering · Locator: dual_encoder_nll_loss & main
Limitation #rocketqa-large-batch-and-sequential-pipeline-limitation
RocketQA requires a costly four-stage sequential training and re-indexing pipeline (three dual-encoder training stages totaling 60 epochs on MS MARCO plus an ERNIE-large cross-encoder training and inference pass over millions of pairs), and increasing the random negative batch size beyond an optimal threshold (e.g., > 4096) degrades optimization performance on fixed-size datasets.
  • supports Source: RocketQA: An Optimized Training Approach to Dense Passage Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering · Locator: Section 3.4, p. 5; Section 4.1.3, pp. 6-7; Section 4.2.2 (Figure 4), p. 8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Requires four sequential training and corpus-indexing stages (M_D^(0) -> M_C -> M_D^(1) -> M_D^(2)) and multi-GPU large-batch hardware (e.g., 8 V100 GPUs with batch size 4096).
  • Excessively large random-negative batch sizes degrade dual-encoder optimization on datasets of limited size.
