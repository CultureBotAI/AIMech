Random-walk Metropolis–Hastings sampling

Algorithms AIMech:RandomWalkMetropolisHastings DRAFT
Aliases: RWMH
A Metropolis–Hastings sampler that proposes a new state by adding an increment drawn from a fixed distribution independent of the current state.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The random walk describes the proposal Y=x+Z, not an assertion that accepted chain increments are independent or target-free. The increment density may be asymmetric; the common symmetric random-walk Metropolis case is included. This differs from a fixed independence proposal g(Y), which does not center on x, and from gradient-directed Langevin proposals. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
At state x draw Z from a fixed increment density f and set y=x+Z, so q(y|x)=f(y−x). Accept with min(1,pi(y)f(x−y)/(pi(x)f(y−x))) where the ratio is defined; retain x on rejection. For f(z)=f(−z), the increment-density terms cancel and acceptance is min(1,pi(y)/pi(x)). The formula is the substitution of Tierney’s random-walk proposal into his general Metropolis ratio. The proposal scale and shape determine which candidate movements are attempted.
  • supports Source: Markov Chains for Exploring Posterior Distributions · Locator: Section 2.3, Metropolis Kernels and Random Walk Chains; printed pp. 3–4 / PDF pp. 5–6

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The simplification to the target-density ratio requires a symmetric increment law, not merely an additive proposal.
  • Proposal and target support determine irreducibility. An invariant kernel is not a guarantee of rapid convergence or movement between separated modes.
  • Accepted-state increments and retained draws are generally dependent. Rejected states remain in the chain.
  • This definition uses a fixed increment distribution; adaptive scales and state-dependent covariance require their own proposal ratios and validity analysis.
  • No universal acceptance-rate target, optimal scale, dimension-scaling law or benchmark advantage is asserted.
