Stepping-out slice sampling

Algorithms AIMech:SteppingOutSliceSampling DRAFT
A univariate slice-sampling algorithm that randomly places a width-w interval around the current point, expands it in width-w steps under a randomly divided budget, and samples by slice-aware interval shrinkage.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record specifies Neal’s stepping-out procedure paired with shrinkage, Figures 3 and 5. The interval-search component alone is not a complete sampler. It differs from doubling in expansion increments and in not requiring doubling’s retrospective acceptability test. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Draw height y uniformly below f(x0). For w>0 and integer m≥1, set L=x0−wU and R=L+w with U uniform on (0,1); independently choose J uniformly from {0,...,m−1} and K=m−1−J. Extend L left by w while J remains positive and f(L)>y, decrementing J; similarly extend R right using K. From this interval repeatedly propose x1 uniformly: accept if f(x1)>y; otherwise replace the left endpoint by x1 when x1<x0, or the right endpoint otherwise. Shrinkage retains x0. The random placement and random left/right budget split are part of the invariance construction; the resulting interval need not cover every slice component.
  • supports Source: Slice Sampling · Locator: Sections 4.1–4.3, Figures 3 and 5, budget construction and correctness proof, PDF/printed pp. 8–16

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The finite expansion cap limits the interval length to at most mw. It is not a certificate that all of the slice has been found.
  • The width and expansion budget affect computational work and exploration; this method does not remove all tuning choices.
  • Shrinking the rejected side relative to the current state is essential. Arbitrary interval placement or budget allocation is not the proven kernel.
  • Unimodal shortcuts and tuning width from past draws in Section 4.4 require the source’s additional conditions, including unlimited expansion for that history-based width argument; they are not generalized here.
  • Multimodality, constrained supports, numerical density evaluation and finite precision require care. No universal mixing guarantee is asserted.
