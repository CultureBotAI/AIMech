Infomax Independent Component Analysis

Algorithms AIMech:InfomaxICA DRAFT
Aliases: Infomax ICA, Bell-Sejnowski ICA
An unsupervised source-separation learning algorithm that adjusts an invertible square linear transform and biases by the ordinary gradient of the differential entropy of coordinatewise sigmoid-transformed outputs.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Scoped to the standard logistic instantaneous algorithm of Bell and Sejnowski 1995. The unsquashed linear outputs estimate sources; the sigmoid outputs define its entropy contrast. This is distinct from the broader Infomax principle, Deep InfoMax’s learned dependence critics, later natural-gradient ICA, FastICA, and the paper’s separately developed temporal deconvolution and generalized-sigmoid variants. Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #entropy-gradient
Let u=Wx+b with square invertible W and y_i=1/(1+exp(-u_i)). The output differential entropy differs from H(X) by log|det W|+sum_i E[log(y_i(1-y_i))]. Its ordinary gradient updates are proportional to W^(-T)+(1-2y)x^T for W and 1-2y for b, averaged or sampled over observations. This is the ordinary parameter gradient, not a natural-gradient replacement. The inverse term is undefined at singular W, and finite steps need not preserve invertibility automatically.

Limitation #information-and-independence
The curated objective is output differential entropy. For noiseless invertible transformations of continuous variables, mutual information between input and output is infinite, so the finite change-of-variables contrast is not a finite noiseless mutual-information value. A finite added output-noise model has a different noisy-output entropy unless the appropriate limiting approximation is justified. Maximizing the sigmoid-output entropy also trades off marginal entropy and dependence: a fixed nonlinearity mismatched to source distributions can prefer mixed outputs. The source illustrates this for uniform sources; arbitrary stationary points do not certify independent components.

Scope #source-separation-scope
The instantaneous application assumes a linear mixture x=A s and seeks unsquashed outputs u that recover sources up to scale, sign and permutation under suitable distributional and identifiability conditions. The historical demonstrations mix recorded audio and discuss failures with multiple Gaussian white-noise sources and ill-conditioned mixing. The paper separately derives FIR blind deconvolution, which can whiten a nonwhite source instead of recovering the exact original. Neither arbitrary acoustic propagation delays nor unmodeled input noise is handled by the standard instantaneous rule.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Square nonsingular mixing, suitable source distributions and an appropriate contrast are substantive assumptions. The standard fixed logistic rule is not a universal source-separation or independence guarantee.
  • The generalized sigmoid ODE in Equation 2.32 gives a chain-rule factor y^(p-1)(1-y)^(r-1) multiplying p(1-y)-r*y; Equations 2.33-2.34 omit it outside the standard p=r=1 case. Curator arithmetic verifies this discrepancy. Those generalized variants are excluded here; no uninspected author-code defect is asserted.
  • Gradient ascent with a chosen finite learning rate need not converge globally or monotonically. The inverse-weight term is nonlocal; the source does not establish a biological learning mechanism.
  • Audio demonstrations, annealing schedules and comparative timings are historical source-specific evidence. No author implementation, audio separation experiment or benchmark was run.
