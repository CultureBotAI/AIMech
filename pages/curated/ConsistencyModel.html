Aliases: Consistency models, CM (consistency model)
A generative model family that learns a noise- or time-conditioned function mapping points on a probability-flow ODE trajectory to its common low-noise endpoint, enabling a sample to be obtained with one model evaluation and allowing optional multistep refinement.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #generative-mechanism
A consistency function assigns the same low-noise endpoint to states on the same probability-flow trajectory. The paper's skip-connected parameterization combines an input skip term and a learned term whose coefficients enforce the identity boundary at the minimum noise level. Generation applies the model to high-noise input; multistep generation interleaves model evaluation with noise injection. The original paper describes both diffusion-model-assisted distillation and direct consistency training with shared-noise adjacent perturbations of a data sample.
  • supports Source: Consistency Models · Locator: Section 3, equation (5), Algorithm 1 and boundary-condition discussion, PDF pp. 3–4; Sections 4–5, Algorithms 2–3, PDF pp. 4–6
Limitation #original-training-theory-limitation
The later paper gives a point-mass-data counterexample in which the limiting consistency-training/matching objective with unequal teacher and student parameters loses dependence on the data location. It motivates zero teacher EMA decay, with a stop-gradient teacher sharing current student parameters, for consistency training; this is distinct from averaging the model used for inference.
  • supports Source: Improved Techniques for Training Consistency Models · Locator: Section 2, teacher versus inference EMA, PDF p. 3; Section 3.2, Proposition 1 and equations (6)–(7), PDF pp. 5–6

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The learned consistency model is not required to be invertible. Ideal probability-flow properties do not establish exact data recovery or perfect self-consistency for a finite trained model.
  • The original direct consistency-training teacher uses EMA; the later inspected correction identifies a failure of its original asymptotic justification when teacher and student parameters differ. This record does not endorse that original training argument as generally valid.
  • The correction concerns consistency training and does not remove EMA from all distillation procedures or from the separate inference-time averaged model.
  • One-evaluation generation is an available procedure, not a universal image-quality guarantee. No benchmark reproduction or independently verified convergence proof is claimed.
