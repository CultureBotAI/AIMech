Semiparametric linear programming regression

Algorithms AIMech:SemiparametricLinearProgrammingRegression DRAFT
Aliases: Semiparametric LP regression
A supervised regression algorithm that jointly fits a finite kernel expansion and an unpenalized supplied parametric basis by minimizing fixed epsilon-insensitive residual loss plus an L1 penalty on the kernel expansion coefficients, yielding a linear program.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #coefficient-penalty-objective
For scalar responses, a supplied finite basis phi_j, a fixed training-centered kernel dictionary k(x_i,.) and fixed epsilon>=0, fit f(x)=sum_i c_i*k(x_i,x)+sum_j beta_j*phi_j(x). With lambda>0, minimize sum_l max(0,abs(y_l-f(x_l))-epsilon)+lambda*sum_i abs(c_i). The supplied-basis beta coefficients are unpenalized. The notation c distinguishes these optimized expansion coefficients from the dual multipliers used in the squared-norm SV formulation; equality of those roles is not assumed.
  • supports Source: Semiparametric Support Vector and Linear Programming Machines · Locator: Section 3, Equation 9 with Equations 4 and 7, PDF pp. 2–4 / printed pp. 586–588
Mechanism #linear-program-form
Introduce c_i=c_i_plus-c_i_minus with nonnegative parts and nonnegative residual slacks xi_l,xi_star_l. Minimize lambda*sum_i(c_i_plus+c_i_minus)+sum_l(xi_l+xi_star_l), with y_l-f(x_l)<=epsilon+xi_l and f(x_l)-y_l<=epsilon+xi_star_l. These are linear constraints and a linear objective in the coefficients and slacks; beta is free. This is an algebraic expansion of Equation 9 with Equation 4, not a claimed verbatim algorithm listing or a specific solver implementation.

Scope #regularizer-distinction
The coefficient-L1 penalty is different from the squared kernel norm c^T*K*c/2. With x=y=(-1,1), linear kernel, constant supplied basis, epsilon=0 and lambda=3, a coefficient-L1 optimum has c=0,beta=0 and raw objective 2; the squared-norm formulation instead has w=2/3,beta=0 and raw objective 4/3. These curator-derived exact optima show different fitted functions under the stated conventions; their differently defined objective values are not comparative performance scores.

Limitation #sparsity-and-nonuniqueness
An L1 penalty does not guarantee a unique expansion or make every optimum equally sparse. For the same two-point linear-kernel dictionary with lambda=1/2 and epsilon=0, coefficient vectors (-1,0), (0,1) and (-1/2,1/2), all with beta=0, attain objective 1/2 and represent f(x)=x. The last vector has two nonzero entries while the first two have one. This curator-derived certificate also prevents interpreting individual kernel coefficients as uniquely identified contributions; the penalty depends on the chosen dictionary and its scaling.

Scope #other-regularizers-excluded
Section 3 also gives a squared feature-space norm plus an L1 penalty on beta (Equation 8), and a coefficient-L1 penalty plus beta^T*M*beta/2 for positive-semidefinite M (Equation 10). Those generally have quadratic terms and are not automatically linear programs. Equation 9, selected here, leaves beta unpenalized. Merely appearing under the paper’s linear-programming heading does not make all its variants this algorithm.

Limitation #evidence-and-use-limits
The supplied parametric basis provides a way to favor domain structure relative to the penalized kernel dictionary. The paper’s experiments concern the squared-norm semiparametric support-vector formulation, not a measured evaluation of Equation 9. No LP speed advantage, generalization benefit, support-count guarantee or result transferred from the SV experiments is claimed. Other loss functions, learned tube width, penalized supplied bases and classification LP machines require separate evidence and identity assessment.
