Gaussian Factor Analysis

Model Families AIMech:GaussianFactorAnalysis DRAFT
Aliases: Linear Gaussian Factor Analysis
A linear Gaussian latent-variable model in which observed variables share a lower-dimensional Gaussian factor and have conditionally independent residuals with variable-specific nonnegative variances.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record covers the normal linear factor model with diagonal uniqueness covariance. It does not encompass arbitrary non-Gaussian, nonlinear, nonnegative or count-data factor models. PPCA adds an isotropic uniqueness restriction; EM and ECME are fitting procedures for the factor model, not model synonyms. Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #latent-covariance
In column-vector notation, let y=mu+Wz+epsilon, z~Normal(0,R) with positive-definite R, and independent epsilon~Normal(0,Psi), where Psi is diagonal and nonnegative. Then y has covariance C=WRW^T+Psi. The residual coordinates are conditionally independent given z; observed coordinates are generally dependent. R is often fixed to identity. Unlike isotropic-noise PPCA, the uniqueness variances can differ by observed variable, and the loading span need not be the leading covariance eigenspace.
  • supports Source: Maximum Likelihood Estimation of Factor Analysis Using the ECME Algorithm with Complete and Incomplete Data · Locator: Section 2, pp. 731-732 / PDF pp. 3-4; Section 4 joint Gaussian distribution, p. 735 / PDF p. 7; curator two-dimensional covariance comparison
Mechanism #conditional-latent-moments
For nonsingular C, the conditional factor mean is R W^T C^(-1)(y-mu), and its covariance is R-RW^T C^(-1)WR. Complete-data EM uses these first moments and second moments, including the posterior covariance. It updates allowed loading coefficients through expected Gaussian regression sufficient statistics and uniqueness variances through expected residual squares. Replacing missing factors by their means while discarding posterior uncertainty is not this EM procedure.
  • supports Source: Maximum Likelihood Estimation of Factor Analysis Using the ECME Algorithm with Complete and Incomplete Data · Locator: Section 3, E-step and M-step, pp. 732-733 / PDF pp. 4-5; Section 4, p. 735 / PDF p. 7; column-vector translation and curator moment/EM calculations
Limitation #identifiability-and-boundaries
With R fixed to identity and unrestricted loadings, an orthogonal rotation of the latent coordinates changes W without changing WW^T. Further loading/uniqueness nonidentifiability can remain in underspecified models, including constrained examples. A zero uniqueness variance is a parameter boundary; it does not alone imply singular marginal covariance, because latent contributions can supply that direction. Positive-definite observed covariance and the specified parameter constraints must be checked separately.
  • supports Source: Maximum Likelihood Estimation of Factor Analysis Using the ECME Algorithm with Complete and Incomplete Data · Locator: Section 2 identifiability discussion, p. 732 / PDF p. 4; Section 5.2, pp. 742-745 / PDF pp. 14-17; curator rotation and zero-uniqueness covariance examples
Scope #fitting-and-missingness
EM can progress so slowly that an apparent plateau is not convergence. The paper’s ECME alternative updates loadings using expected complete-data likelihood, then updates uniqueness variances using actual observed likelihood; its Newton steps must be checked for an increase. Missing observed coordinates can be integrated using Gaussian conditional first and second moments and observed covariance submatrices. The demonstrated incomplete-data likelihood assumes an ignorable missingness mechanism. Neither EM nor the examples establish global optimality, universal speed improvement or applicability to arbitrary missingness.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Latent dimension, covariance constraints and loading restrictions are part of model specification. Gaussianity and conditional residual independence can be misspecified; fitted factors are not automatically causal, unique or biologically meaningful.
  • Nonnegative uniqueness and positive-definite marginal covariance are different conditions. Log-variance parameterization remains strictly positive at finite coordinates; exact zero is a boundary limit.
  • This record uses the fully inspected Liu-Rubin 1998 primary follow-up. The Rubin-Thayer 1982 full-text request returned HTTP 401; it was not bypassed or treated as read. The 1983 critique remains abstract-level context, not a fully reviewed proof about all stationary points.
  • All 19 source pages were read. Finite calculations check Gaussian conditioning, inclusion of posterior variance, one EM improvement, rescaling and zero-uniqueness boundaries. Source tables have inconsistent example labels and an apparent observation-count discrepancy; those labels/counts are not asserted here. Historical fits and software were not reproduced.
