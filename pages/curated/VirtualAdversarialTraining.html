Virtual adversarial training

Algorithms AIMech:VirtualAdversarialTraining DRAFT
Aliases: VAT
A prediction-smoothing regularization procedure that trains a model to preserve its current output distribution under approximately worst-case small input perturbations, constructing those perturbations without using target labels.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope VAT denotes the Miyato et al. procedure described in the 2018 manuscript revision. The virtual target is the model’s current prediction. Label-free perturbation construction does not mean the complete supervised or semi-supervised training objective has no labels. VAT is a component of IMSAT but is not that information-maximization framework or a generative adversarial network. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a fixed parameter snapshot theta_hat, approximately maximize D(p_theta_hat(y|x)||p_theta_hat(y|x+r)) over ||r||_2<=epsilon; the reported classifier implementation uses KL divergence. At r=0 the smooth KL objective has zero value and first derivative. Its local quadratic approximation gives a dominant-Hessian-eigenvector direction. Approximate that direction by initializing a random unit vector, taking the gradient with respect to r at a small displacement xi*d, normalizing it, and optionally repeating this power-iteration approximation. Scale the resulting direction by epsilon. For a model update, hold both the teacher prediction and the selected perturbation fixed while minimizing D(p_theta_hat(y|x)||p_theta(y|x+r_vadv)). Average this regularizer over available inputs to obtain R_VAT, and minimize supervised negative log likelihood plus alpha times R_VAT. The general formulation can use labeled and unlabeled inputs; the inspected semi-supervised driver applies its VAT branch to the unlabeled-input tensor. The pinned code defaults to one iteration, detaches the teacher and perturbation construction, and computes KL from softmax probabilities and stable log-softmax values. Its vatent branch separately adds conditional entropy minimization.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The Hessian direction relies on a local quadratic approximation and finite-difference power iteration. One iteration does not solve the exact finite-radius maximization and provides no certified adversarial robustness.
  • The perturbation radius, norm, input representation and regularization weight affect which prediction changes are penalized. Semantic label preservation is not guaranteed for arbitrary perturbations.
  • A constant classifier can have zero consistency loss; VAT alone does not identify meaningful classes. The task loss or other learning signals remain material.
  • The author code uses epsilon-stabilized vector normalization, so its returned vector need not have exactly the ideal radius. Batch-normalization handling and finite arithmetic also belong to implementation scope.
  • Three source files were inspected at the declared ranges without execution. The earlier 2015 precursor was identified but its full methods were not compared; no historical-version equivalence or benchmark reproduction is asserted.
