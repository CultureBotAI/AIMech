Temporal-kernel recurrent neural network

Architectures AIMech:TemporalKernelRecurrentNeuralNetwork DRAFT
Aliases: TKRNN
A recurrent architecture that factors connections across time into learned spatial weights and source-specific exponential temporal kernels, maintaining recursive traces of past inputs and hidden activations before the nonlinear state update.

The temporal-kernel architecture factors lag weights and filters past input and hidden activities before its nonlinear state computation. SCRN has a separate context trace and constrained recurrent block; Clockwork uses prescribed module ticks; GF-RNN uses input-dependent pair gates. The existing NARX record denotes a generic finite-lag input-output model family, not this source-specific exponential neural realization. Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #factored-temporal-connections
For a source hidden unit j and destination i, the displayed lag-k connection has spatial weight W_(j,i) multiplied by lambda_j^(k-1). Input units use a separate set of temporal parameters. Hidden-state computation combines these weighted histories before applying the nonlinearity; it does not average separately evolved nonlinear trajectories. Source-specific factors tie lag weights rather than learning an unrelated full matrix for each lag. This mechanism differs from discrete clock schedules or input-dependent gate values.
  • supports Source: Temporal Kernel Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 3, Equations 6–7 and footnote 1, manuscript/PDF pp. 6–7
Mechanism #recursive-traces-and-time-indexing
The source initializes its traces at zero and rewrites each hidden trace as S_y(t)=y(t-1)+lambda_y*S_y(t-1), with an analogous input trace S_x(t)=x(t-1)+lambda_x*S_x(t-1). These recurrences reproduce the displayed finite exponential sums without explicitly revisiting all previous times. The kernel equations use delayed inputs, whereas the background ordinary-RNN equation uses current input. Setting lambda to zero selects the most recent term under the chosen indexing convention; a literal comparison to the background equation must account for that input-time alignment.
  • supports Source: Temporal Kernel Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Equations 1 and 6–11, manuscript/PDF pp. 3, 6–7; Section 3.1, p. 8; curator indexing consequence
Limitation #unnormalized-memory-and-stability
The displayed traces are unnormalized sums, without a (1-lambda) multiplier. For a fixed bounded driving sequence of magnitude at most M and 0<=lambda<1, their magnitude is at most M/(1-lambda), so the bound grows as lambda approaches one. Decay of a trace does not ensure contraction of the coupled learned recurrent system, permanent memory, or nonvanishing gradients. Learned spatial weights and nonlinear derivatives also enter the full transition Jacobian.
  • supports Source: Temporal Kernel Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Equations 8–11 and Section 3.1, manuscript/PDF pp. 7–8; curator geometric-sum and derivative consequences
Scope #learned-timescales-and-multiple-kernels
The positive-decay configuration learns a time-shared unconstrained parameter ell through lambda=sigmoid(ell); it is not a gate recomputed from each input. The source discusses negative lambda as a mathematical extension outside that positive-sigmoid parameterization. It also defines TKRNN+n by summing multiple exponentially factored connection components with different scales and weights. Those components combine before the state nonlinearity, rather than constituting an ensemble of independently run networks. Their learned coefficients need not define nonnegative normalized attention weights.
  • supports Source: Temporal Kernel Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 3 and Section 3.1, manuscript/PDF pp. 5–9
Limitation #timescale-and-computation-boundaries
The authors note that direct influence at long lags becomes small, that unsuitable initial decay scales can impede learning, and that very slow traces provide coarse temporal summaries. Recursive traces make a fixed-component forward computation linear in sequence length for fixed network dimensions, but this does not imply constant total training memory or a hardware-independent speed guarantee. Adding kernel components increases the maintained traces and arithmetic. The author manuscript supplies figure captions without the corresponding artwork, and no implementation or runtime was inspected.
  • supports Source: Temporal Kernel Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 3 and Section 3.1, manuscript/PDF pp. 5–9; curator recurrence-cost consequence
Limitation #conditional-recall-and-negative-result
The main experiment uses a selected multiple-kernel model on cued serial recall with a small alphabet, chosen word length and variable delay, truncated long sequences, and specified capacities and optimization settings. Its top-one and top-two character predictions during the second occurrence measure partial next-symbol recall, not perfect reconstruction of every whole sequence or universal memory capacity. The manuscript also reports failure on a multiple quadratic-function prediction task where the cited LSTM comparison succeeded. The suggestion that a different optimizer should resolve this is not a demonstrated result. No numerical benchmark, historical priority, universal LSTM equivalence or independent reproduction is asserted.
  • supports Source: Temporal Kernel Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Sections 5–5.1 and conclusion, manuscript/PDF pp. 10–13

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The inspected source is the author manuscript dated 30 July 2009, not the final 2010 journal layout. All fifteen manuscript pages were read, but the two figure artworks are absent; captions alone do not establish graphical details.
  • The delayed-input indexing in the kernel equations is retained explicitly. No silent substitution of current input or claim of unqualified literal equality to the background update is made.
  • Exponential traces are not normalized attention or an automatic whole-network stability guarantee. Learned timescales are time-shared parameters, not input-dependent gates.
  • Multiple kernels are configurations of this family. Negative decay is a discussed mathematical extension outside the positive-sigmoid configuration; neither is assumed to have all the properties of a single positive kernel.
  • Selected serial-recall metrics and the reported quadratic-task failure qualify empirical conclusions. An architectural special case does not guarantee optimization success, and the number of possible strings alone does not prove absence of overfitting.
  • The published version, figure artwork, author implementation, backward routine, training and hardware runtime were not inspected. Earlier related work is acknowledged without a priority claim; no numerical benchmark is curated.
