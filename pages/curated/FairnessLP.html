Fairness LP

Algorithms AIMech:FairnessLP DRAFT
Aliases: Dwork fairness linear program
A finite-population optimization procedure that assigns outcome distributions to individuals to minimize expected loss under pairwise Lipschitz similarity constraints, expressible as a linear program for the total-variation or relative-infinity distribution distances used by Dwork et al.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The source names this optimization formulation Fairness LP. It is a procedure enforcing LipschitzIndividualFairness, not the criterion itself, a generic linear-program solver or a learned similarity metric. The two documented distribution-distance choices are retained as configurations of one formulation. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method-scope
Choose a probability vector for each individual and minimize the supplied expected loss. Enforce normalization, nonnegativity and distribution distances bounded by the supplied task metric for every individual pair. Total-variation constraints admit a linear-program formulation; the relative-infinity choice yields pairwise linear probability-ratio bounds with fixed metric-dependent coefficients.
  • supports Source: Fairness Through Awareness · Locator: Section 2 and Section 2.1, Figure 1 equations (2)–(4), Lemmas 2.1–2.2 and Remark 2.1, PDF pp. 5–6 (printed pp. 4–5)

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The individual metric and utility loss are inputs; solving the program does not establish that either is normatively appropriate.
  • The cited polynomial-size result concerns a finite set of individuals and outcomes with the specified distribution distances, not arbitrary metrics on output distributions or an unrestricted population.
  • Extrapolating from sampled individuals to unseen individuals is a separate challenge explicitly noted in Remark 2.1.
  • Individual constraints do not automatically imply demographic parity without additional assumptions about the population and metric.
