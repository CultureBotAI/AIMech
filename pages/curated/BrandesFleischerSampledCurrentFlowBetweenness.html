Aliases: Brandes-Fleischer current-flow betweenness approximation
A randomized network-analysis algorithm that estimates normalized, endpoint-excluded current-flow betweenness by independently sampling uniform source–sink pairs, solving their grounded electrical-network systems, and averaging scaled vertex throughputs.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Scope #domain-and-target
The maintained domain is a simple connected undirected graph with n>=3 and positive edge conductances. For ordered distinct source–sink vertices s,t, potentials satisfy Lp=e_s-e_t and edge current is c(u,v)[p(u)-p(v)]. Throughput tau_st(v) is half the incident absolute current minus half the absolute supply; source and sink throughputs are zero. The target B(v) sums throughputs over ordered pairs and divides by (n-1)(n-2). Edge orientation is only a computational convention.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Sections 2 and 3.1, Definition 2, PDF pp. 2–5 / printed pp. 534–537; Section 5, p. 10 / p. 542
Mechanism #independent-pair-estimator
Draw k>=1 ordered pairs uniformly from the n(n-1) distinct-endpoint pairs, independently with replacement across iterations. Solve each grounded unit-supply system. For nonendpoints accumulate half the incident absolute current; endpoints contribute zero. With a=n/(n-2), return Bhat(v)=a/k times the sum of sampled throughputs. Under exact currents its expectation is B(v). Independence concerns pair draws, not the different vertices estimated from one shared draw.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Section 5, proof of Theorem 3 and Algorithm 3, PDF p. 10 / printed p. 542
Scope #absolute-error-guarantee
Each throughput lies in [0,1]. For epsilon>0 and exact currents, a fixed vertex has probability at most 2 exp[-2k(epsilon/a)^2] of absolute error at least epsilon. A union bound gives simultaneous error below epsilon for every vertex with failure at most delta when k>=ceil[a^2 ln(2n/delta)/(2 epsilon^2)], where 0<delta<1. This explicit sufficient count is curator algebra, not an optimal count. The source uses k=ell ceil[(a/epsilon)^2 ln n]; for positive integer ell this yields the stated per-vertex 2/n^(2ell) bound and, by a union bound, all-vertex 2/n^(2ell-1). These are additive guarantees, not relative-error or rank-preservation guarantees.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Section 5, Theorem 3 and its displayed Hoeffding bound, PDF p. 10 / printed p. 542; union-bound algebra in research/discovery/sampled-current-flow-boundaries.py
Limitation #sample-range-and-repetition
Although the exact normalized score is at most one, an individual estimate can exceed one because a sampled contribution is scaled by a. On the unit three-vertex path, the middle single-draw contribution is 3 with probability 1/3 and zero otherwise. For two draws, its estimate is 0, 3/2 or 3 with probabilities 4/9, 4/9 and 1/9, while its expectation and exact score are one. Hypothetically clipping to one changes this expectation to 5/9 and is not part of the maintained method. Repetition remains possible when k equals the number of distinct pairs; that sample size does not imply exact enumeration. These are curator finite examples.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Section 5, estimator and Algorithm 3, PDF p. 10 / printed p. 542; explicit curator consequences in research/discovery/sampled-current-flow-boundaries.py
Limitation #solver-and-work-boundary
The procedure performs k grounded Laplacian solves and O(km) work to aggregate edge currents. The source proposes conjugate gradients to avoid the dense inverse used by its separate exact sorted-row algorithm. Its displayed condition-number runtime does not specify a numerical tolerance. Sampling concentration assumes exact throughputs; approximate-solver error requires additional analysis and is not covered by that probability bound. No unconditional exact floating-point runtime, solver accuracy or implementation-independent memory guarantee is asserted.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Sections 4 and 5, conjugate-gradient discussion and Algorithm 3, PDF pp. 8–10 / printed pp. 540–542
Limitation #evidence-and-method-boundary
This is the chapter’s sampled-pair Algorithm 3, distinct from exact sorted-row betweenness, grounded closeness, shortest-path dependency accumulation and sampling source pivots for shortest-path centrality. Appendix A describes an ell=1 Java approximation experiment, and Figure 1 presents maximum absolute errors for six random and six AS graphs. This record asserts no digitized numerical advantage, replicated benchmark, causal influence or learned predictive performance. The paper’s phrase fully polynomial randomized approximation scheme is not treated as a relative-error theorem.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Sections 4–6, PDF pp. 7–11 / printed pp. 539–543; Appendix A and Figure 1, PDF p. 12 / printed p. 544

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Connected simple undirected positive-conductance graphs with n>=3, distinct pair endpoints and endpoint-excluded normalization only. Disconnected components, signed weights, endpoint inclusion and edge centrality need separate conventions.
  • Independent uniform pair sampling and exact electrical currents are assumptions of the estimator and concentration analysis. Dependence between vertex estimates is allowed by the union bound; biased pair sampling or approximate solves require separate analysis.
  • Absolute-error probability does not guarantee relative accuracy at near-zero scores, deterministic accuracy or preserved rankings. Estimates above one are possible; no clipping, resampling or fallback is introduced.
  • The chapter has a current-versus-potential cycle equation discrepancy and inconsistent incidence dimensions and intermediate normalization in its separate exact computation. This record uses its explicit weighted Laplacian, Ohm law, throughput definition and Algorithm 3. It does not adopt the separate closeness normalization or claim a corrected source edition.
  • The complete twelve-page chapter was read, including mathematical renderings and Figure 1; no author implementation, raw results or benchmark replication. Finite rational examples check selected boundaries rather than proving every graph or numerical solver. DRAFT remains after author self-review.
