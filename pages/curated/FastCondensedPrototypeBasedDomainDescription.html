Fast condensed prototype-based domain description

Algorithms AIMech:FastCondensedPrototypeBasedDomainDescription DRAFT
Aliases: FastCPDD, FCPDD
A heuristic for selecting PDD prototypes that samples remaining reference observations, computes their radius offsets against the full dataset, accumulates eligible candidates, and greedily selects them by uncovered-reference gain with an adaptive sampling schedule.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope FastCPDD, also called FCPDD, is the sampled candidate-selection variant introduced in Section 5 of Angiulli's PDD paper. It is distinct from full-candidate CPDD and the PDD prediction rule. The conceptual sampling and selection policy is supported; the printed pseudocode has unresolved boundary conventions, and its stated exhaustion modification does not by itself establish the claimed consistency guarantee. Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #sampled-candidate-selection
Maintain selected prototype pairs S, covered reference points C, eligible candidate pool E and remaining sampling pool Dprime. Draw T from Dprime, compute each sampled point's kth self-included neighbor distance against full D, retain sampled candidates P with r_i<=theta, and form their neighborhoods using d(x_i,y)+r_i<=rho*theta. Add P to E. Greedily select from E by largest residual neighborhood gain, breaking ties by smaller radius, and insert while gain>(1-eta)*k. The inner loop ends when the chosen candidate belongs to the current P or fails the gain test; older candidates can be inserted first. Remove T union C from Dprime after the iteration. Stored radii are unchanged at prediction.
  • supports Source: Prototype-based Domain Description for One-Class Classification · Locator: Figure 3 steps 1-2g and accompanying Section 5 prose, author p6 / PDF p7
Mechanism #adaptive-sampling-and-stopping
Initialize sigma=1, count=k+1 and countstar=0. The nominal sample size is ((1-a)*sigma+a)*s, with a=0.1 in the source. After selecting candidates, count is the last residual gain, countstar is the maximum gain seen, and sigma is updated to log(count)/log(countstar). The default outer loop continues while count>(1-eta)*k. Here eta controls marginal gain, not CPDD's total covered fraction. A low-gain sample does not prove that no useful unsampled candidate remains; the source acknowledges that default stopping is heuristic.
  • supports Source: Prototype-based Domain Description for One-Class Classification · Locator: Figure 3, author p6 / PDF p7; Section 5 stopping discussion, author p7 / PDF p8
Limitation #exhaustion-theorem-counterexample
Theorem 5.1 proposes eta=1 and changing the outer guard to Dprime nonempty as a consistency condition. A curator trace of the printed flow disproves sufficiency of that change alone: let D={0,1/10,10,101/10}, k=1, theta=1/5, rho=eta=1 and s=4. The first sample T=D has zero radii and two disconnected two-point neighborhoods. Selecting any current-sample candidate covers only one pair; the inner loop then ends. Removing T union C empties Dprime, so the modified guard exits while two originally accepted points remain uncovered. Gain=count=countstar=2 makes the log update defined, so this failure is independent of log singularities. The trace does not certify how any historical implementation behaved.
  • supports Source: Prototype-based Domain Description for One-Class Classification · Locator: Figure 3, author p6 / PDF p7; Theorem 5.1 and proposed guard change, author p7 / PDF p8; executable curator trace in research/discovery/prototype-domain-boundaries.py
Limitation #implementation-and-complexity-limits
The printed description leaves sample-size rounding or clipping, empty candidate maxima, exhausted sampling pools and log-zero or log-one updates unresolved. Isolated k=1 neighborhoods can give count=countstar=1, making the stated update 0/0. If M distinct observations are sampled without replacement from the remaining pool, computing their distances to full D requires M*|D|<=|D|^2 distance evaluations; this permits quadratic worst-case work and does not establish subquadratic total runtime. The baseline CPDD set-cover approximation is not transferred to this heuristic.
  • supports Source: Prototype-based Domain Description for One-Class Classification · Locator: Figure 3 and Section 5 computational analysis, author pp6-7 / PDF pp7-8; exact unit-gain boundary calculation
Limitation #probability-and-experiment-scope
The sample-hit analysis assumes a very large dataset with kth-neighbor radii approximated near zero and a specified population density. Its region-hit probability does not establish distribution-free selection of an optimal prototype. Reported synthetic and UCI comparisons are conditional on sample size, k, thresholds, metrics, hardware and chosen classes; they do not establish exact agreement with CPDD or universal compression, speed or accuracy gains. No numerical performance advantage is curated.
  • supports Source: Prototype-based Domain Description for One-Class Classification · Locator: Section 5.1 and Figure 4, author pp7-8 / PDF pp8-9; Section 6.5, Figures 11-12 and tables, author pp12-13 / PDF pp13-14

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This is a supported heuristic concept with explicitly unresolved pseudocode boundaries. Neither default gain stopping nor the printed Dprime-exhaustion modification is represented as a general consistency guarantee.
  • A full implementation would need documented boundary choices; no unpublished repair is attributed to the author. Exact-name, code and erratum searches and the public 15-repository lab inventory did not identify a correcting implementation, a bounded search result rather than proof of absence.
  • All 15 primary PDF pages were visually read. The consistency counterexample and logarithm boundary were checked by exact finite calculations; no historical software, statistical training or benchmark execution was performed.
