Asymmetric Low-Rank Matrix Eigenvalue and Linear-Form Eigenvector Perturbation Analysis

Aliases: Asymmetrically Perturbed Low-Rank Eigen-Decomposition, Chen-Cheng-Fan Asymmetric Matrix Perturbation Theory, Asymmetrized Dilation Trick for Low-Rank Singular Value Estimation
A non-asymptotic spectral perturbation theory (established by Yuxin Chen, Chen Cheng, and Jianqing Fan in 2021) for estimating the leading eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a low-rank symmetric matrix $\mathbf{M}^\star \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}$ from an asymmetrically perturbed observation $\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{M}^\star + \mathbf{H}$ whose noise entries $\{H_{ij}\}_{1\le i,j\le n}$ are mutually independent, zero-mean, and potentially heteroscedastic ($\text{Var}(H_{ij}) \le \sigma^2$, $|H_{ij}| \le B$). Using the Neumann series eigenvector expansion $\boldsymbol{u} = \frac{\lambda^\star}{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{u}^{\star\top}\boldsymbol{u})\sum_{s=0}^\infty \frac{1}{\lambda^s}\mathbf{H}^s\boldsymbol{u}^\star$ (Theorem 2) and a combinatorial graph-partition moment bound on high-order bilinear forms $|\mathbb{E}[(\boldsymbol{a}^\top\mathbf{H}^s\boldsymbol{u}^\star)^k]|$ (Lemma 5, Corollary 4), Theorem 3 establishes the master linear-form perturbation bound $|\boldsymbol{a}^\top(\boldsymbol{u} - \frac{\boldsymbol{u}^{\star\top}\boldsymbol{u}}{\lambda/\lambda^\star}\boldsymbol{u}^\star)| \lesssim \frac{\max\{\sigma\sqrt{n\log n}, B\log n\}}{|\lambda^\star|}\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{n}}$ for any deterministic unit vector $\boldsymbol{a} \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Because $\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{a}^\top\mathbf{H}^2\boldsymbol{u}^\star] = O(\sigma^2)$ for asymmetric independent noise versus $n\sigma^2\boldsymbol{a}^\top\boldsymbol{u}^\star$ for symmetric noise (Section 4.3), the leading eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $\mathbf{M}$ achieves $|\lambda - \lambda^\star| \lesssim \max\{\sigma\sqrt{n\log n}, B\log n\}\sqrt{\mu/n} = O(\sigma\sqrt{\mu\log n})$ (Corollary 1) — up to $O(\sqrt{n})$ times more accurate than the unadjusted leading singular value $\sigma_1(\mathbf{M})$ or the leading eigenvalue of $(\mathbf{M}+\mathbf{M}^\top)/2$, and fully adaptive to unknown heteroscedastic noise variances — while Corollaries 2–3 and Theorem 5 yield sharp linear-form and $\ell_\infty$ entrywise eigenvector bounds including for rectangular rank-1 matrices via the asymmetrized dilation matrix $\mathbf{M}_{\text{d}} = \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0} & \mathbf{M}_1 \\ \mathbf{M}_2^\top & \mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}$ (Section 5.2, Equation 43).

Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #claim-neumann-master-bound-and-combinatorial-walk-moment-control
For a rank-1 symmetric matrix $\mathbf{M}^\star = \lambda^\star\boldsymbol{u}^\star\boldsymbol{u}^{\star\top}$ with incoherence $\|\boldsymbol{u}^\star\|_\infty \le \sqrt{\mu/n}$ perturbed by an asymmetric independent noise matrix $\mathbf{H}$ obeying $\max\{\sigma\sqrt{n\log n}, B\log n\} \le c_1|\lambda^\star|$, Lemma 5 bounds the $k$-th moment $|\mathbb{E}[(\boldsymbol{a}^\top\mathbf{H}^s\boldsymbol{u}^\star)^k]|$ by partitioning $sk$ directed walk edges into equivalence types $\mathcal{T}\in\Gamma^+$ of cardinality at least 2, and Theorem 3 sums the Neumann series to prove $|\boldsymbol{a}^\top(\boldsymbol{u} - \frac{\boldsymbol{u}^{\star\top}\boldsymbol{u}}{\lambda/\lambda^\star}\boldsymbol{u}^\star)| \lesssim \frac{\max\{\sigma\sqrt{n\log n}, B\log n\}}{|\lambda^\star|}\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{n}}$ with probability $1 - O(n^{-10})$ (Sections 3.2, 4.1.1, and 4.4, Theorem 3, Lemma 5, Corollary 4).
  • supports Source: Asymmetry Helps: Eigenvalue and Eigenvector Analyses of Asymmetrically Perturbed Low-Rank Matrices · Locator: Section 3.2 (Theorem 2, p. 6), Section 4.1.1 (Theorem 3, p. 7), Section 4.4 & Appendices C-E (Lemma 5, pp. 13-14, 26-32)
Mechanism #claim-sqrt-n-eigenvalue-accuracy-gain-and-entrywise-eigenvector-control
Setting $\boldsymbol{a} = \boldsymbol{u}^\star$ in Theorem 3 yields Corollary 1: $|\lambda - \lambda^\star| \lesssim \max\{\sigma\sqrt{n\log n}, B\log n\}\sqrt{\mu/n}$ (which simplifies to $O(\sigma\sqrt{\mu\log n})$ when $B \lesssim \sigma\sqrt{n/\log n}$, matching the Fano minimax lower bound of Lemma 4 up to $\sqrt{\log n}$), improving by a factor of $O(\sqrt{n})$ over the unadjusted singular value $|\lambda_{\text{svd}} - \lambda^\star|$ and the symmetrized eigenvalue bias $\frac{n\sigma^2}{2\lambda^\star}$ without requiring knowledge of heteroscedastic variances; Corollaries 2–3 further bound linear forms and entrywise error $\min\|\boldsymbol{u} \pm \boldsymbol{u}^\star\|_\infty \lesssim \frac{\max\{\sigma\sqrt{n\log n}, B\log n\}}{|\lambda^\star|}\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{n}}$ (Sections 4.1.2–4.1.4 and 4.3, Corollaries 1–3, Lemma 4).
  • supports Source: Asymmetry Helps: Eigenvalue and Eigenvector Analyses of Asymmetrically Perturbed Low-Rank Matrices · Locator: Sections 4.1.2-4.1.4 and 4.3 (Corollaries 1-3, Lemma 4, pp. 8-13)
Application #claim-rank-r-extension-and-asymmetrized-dilation-singular-estimation
For general rank-$r$ symmetric matrices, Theorem 4 and Corollaries 5–7 establish eigenvalue perturbation $|\lambda_l - \lambda_j^\star| \lesssim \max\{\sigma\sqrt{n\log n}, B\log n\}\kappa r\sqrt{\mu/n}$ and eigenspace incoherence $\|\mathbf{U}\|_{2,\infty} \lesssim \kappa r\sqrt{\mu/n}$; for an asymmetric rank-1 matrix $\mathbf{M}^\star = \lambda^\star\boldsymbol{u}^\star\boldsymbol{v}^{\star\top} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_1\times n_2}$ with two independent noisy copies $\mathbf{M}_1, \mathbf{M}_2$, eigen-decomposition of the asymmetrized dilation $\mathbf{M}_{\text{d}} = \begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0} & \mathbf{M}_1 \\ \mathbf{M}_2^\top & \mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}$ achieves $|\lambda_1^{\text{d}} - \lambda^\star| \lesssim \max\{\sigma\sqrt{n\log n}, B\log n\}\sqrt{\mu/n}$ and optimal entrywise singular-vector accuracy (Section 5, Theorem 4, Corollary 8, Theorem 5).
  • supports Source: Asymmetry Helps: Eigenvalue and Eigenvector Analyses of Asymmetrically Perturbed Low-Rank Matrices · Locator: Sections 5.1-5.2 (Theorem 4, Corollaries 5-8, Theorem 5, pp. 14-18), Appendix I (pp. 33-36)

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • For general rank r > 1, Corollary 5's eigenvalue bound scales with r * sqrt(mu / n) and Theorem 4 does not yield individual eigenvector perturbation bounds when nonzero eigenvalues of M^* are close because eigenvectors of an asymmetric matrix M are non-orthogonal.
  • When only a single symmetric noisy matrix is observed under Gaussian noise, the skew-symmetric Gaussian asymmetrization M + Delta in Appendix J doubles the entrywise noise variance and increases eigenvector estimation error unless multiple independent noise draws are averaged (Remark 9).
