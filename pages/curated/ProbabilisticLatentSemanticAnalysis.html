Probabilistic latent semantic analysis

Model Families AIMech:ProbabilisticLatentSemanticAnalysis DRAFT
Aliases: PLSA, Probabilistic latent semantic indexing, PLSI
A latent-class model of document–word or other dyadic co-occurrences that represents their joint probability as a mixture of conditionally independent distributions over the two observed entities.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope PLSA denotes the aspect-model probability construction, also called the probabilistic latent semantic indexing model in the LDA comparison. A document can mix several aspects because each co-occurrence has its own latent class. It differs from an SVD reconstruction, a single class for a whole document, and LDA’s prior over document-specific topic weights. The alias PLSI identifies this underlying model, not every cosine, interpolation or model-averaging choice in a retrieval pipeline. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For finite document indices d, vocabulary items w and K latent classes z, choose normalized nonnegative distributions pi_z, a_z(d) and b_z(w), and define p(d,w)=sum_z pi_z*a_z(d)*b_z(w). Given z, d and w are independent. Equivalently, for p(d)>0, p(w|d)=sum_z theta_dz*b_z(w), where theta_dz=pi_z*a_z(d)/p(d). Fit observed counts n(d,w) with sum_dw n(d,w)*log p(d,w). An exact EM E-step computes r_dwz=pi_z*a_z(d)*b_z(w)/p(d,w); M-steps normalize the corresponding count-weighted responsibilities over words, documents and classes. Each conditional document word distribution is an arithmetic convex combination of topic probabilities. In the original fold-in procedure, keep b_z(w) fixed and fit a new query or document’s mixture weights. This introduces new fitted weights rather than integrating over a population prior as in LDA.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The base construction is a normalized distribution of indexed co-occurrences. It has no LDA-style population prior on new document mixtures; fold-in estimation is possible, so this is not a claim that unseen documents cannot be represented at all.
  • Training-document mixture parameters grow with the corpus. Maximum-likelihood or tempered fitting can overfit; local EM optimization, initialization and rank selection do not guarantee global optimality or unique factors. Permuting topic labels preserves the law, and linearly dependent topic distributions can yield nonunique mixture weights.
  • The bag-of-words representation omits token order and explicit within-topic word interactions. It is not a raw-count Poisson MRF, a canonical-parameter admixture, or a neural density estimator. Topic labels and selected high-probability words are interpretations, not verified semantic or causal categories.
  • Positive-count cells require positive modeled mass. Responsibility updates need nonzero denominators; unused components require a specified handling convention. Zero-count terms use the standard zero-contribution convention.
  • Historical retrieval experiments use particular corpora, ranks, held-out tempering and cosine interpolation. No universal retrieval advantage, exact new-document marginal likelihood or author implementation is certified.
