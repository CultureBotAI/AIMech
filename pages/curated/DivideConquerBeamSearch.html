Divide-and-conquer beam search

Algorithms AIMech:DivideConquerBeamSearch DRAFT
A memory-limited breadth-first heuristic search that discards least-promising open nodes when its chosen capacity is reached and recovers a solution through relay-based divide-and-conquer reconstruction instead of retaining every predecessor layer.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Divide-and-conquer beam search is the approximate search procedure introduced by Zhou and Hansen in 2004. It differs from divide-and-conquer beam-stack search, which adds systematic backtracking and replay under saved beam ranges. Relay reconstruction alone does not revisit every branch discarded by beam pruning. Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #search-procedure
Use breadth-first heuristic search with layered duplicate handling and relay pointers. When memory is full, remove open nodes with the worst evaluation values and continue with the surviving beam. On reaching a goal, recover its path by recursively searching the start-to-relay and relay-to-goal subproblems. Those reconstruction searches can find shorter subpaths, but this does not restore a global completeness or optimality guarantee.

Scope #capacity-pruning
The later journal account identifies the same approximate procedure as layered BFBnB plus deletion of high-f open nodes at the memory limit, and distinguishes it from a subsequent complete backtracking extension.
  • supports Source: Breadth-first heuristic search · Locator: Section 6.2, printed p. 406 / PDF p. 22
Limitation #ranking-versus-bound
The planning experiments use the nonadmissible additive heuristic to guide approximate search while separately evaluating an admissible max-pair lower bound. A favorable experimental comparison does not convert the ranking function into an admissible cost bound.
  • supports Source: Breadth-first heuristic search · Locator: Divide-and-Conquer Beam Search vs. weighted A*, PDF p. 8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Permanent width pruning can discard every solution path; ordinary DCBS is neither complete nor guaranteed optimal.
  • The reported current, previous, next and relay layers count retained search-node layers, not all temporary successors, recursive reconstruction state or output storage.
  • The original developed search setting has uniform edge costs. Its planning implementation uses an inadmissible additive heuristic for ranking and a separate admissible heuristic for lower bounds; ranking and safe cost pruning must not be conflated.
  • Larger permitted beams and reconstruction improvements do not establish monotonic quality or a universal speed advantage.
