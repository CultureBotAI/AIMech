Heuristic tree sampling

Algorithms AIMech:HeuristicTreeSampling DRAFT
Aliases: Chen heuristic sampling
A randomized estimator of additive properties of an implicit rooted tree that processes ordered strata using one sampled representative and an accumulated weight per stratum.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #ordered-weighted-representatives
Start with root weight one and process strata in parent-before-child order. A child inherits its parent’s weight. Merge arrivals in the same pending stratum by adding weights, selecting the incoming representative with probability equal to its weight divided by the new total. Report the sum of representative weights times their node-function values.
  • supports Source: Heuristic Sampling: A Method for Predicting the Performance of Tree Searching Programs · Locator: Section3, PDF pp.2–3 / printed296–297; Algorithm HS
Scope #expectation-and-tree-scope
For a finite tree, fixed node functional and valid ordering and random choices, the weighted estimator is unbiased. This expectation statement does not make each sample accurate. Repeated graph states remain distinct tree-path occurrences unless a separately justified graph-counting method is supplied.
  • supports Source: Heuristic Sampling: A Method for Predicting the Performance of Tree Searching Programs · Locator: Sections2–4.3, PDF pp.2–6 / printed296–300, Theorem 4
Limitation #strata-and-work-boundaries
Stratum homogeneity can reduce variance, but stratum labels alone do not guarantee accuracy. The number of expanded representatives is bounded by the number of strata; generating children and evaluating the stratifier still incur work. The source’s stronger variance bounds have additional assumptions.
  • supports Source: Heuristic Sampling: A Method for Predicting the Performance of Tree Searching Programs · Locator: Sections3–4.7, PDF pp.3–13 / printed297–307; Section5, pp.14–15 / printed308–309
Scope #ida-star-variant
The later IDA* application uses heuristic-informed types and samples the actual bounded search tree, including ancestor cutoffs. Its parent, child and grandchild contexts are variants of the weighted-stratum estimator, not CDP’s domain-model extrapolation.
  • supports Source: Predicting the size of IDA*’s search tree · Locator: Sections8–10, PDF pp.17–23 / printed68–74
Limitation #finite-probe-limitations
An unbiased estimator can miss rare deep branches in every run of a small sample. The reported knight-tour experiments include zero estimates at depths with nonzero actual counts. Such observations do not establish absence of deep nodes or solutions, and examined-node counts alone do not certify runtime gains.
  • supports Source: Heuristic Sampling: A Method for Predicting the Performance of Tree Searching Programs · Locator: Section5, PDF pp.13–17 / printed307–311, Tables 1–2

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The ordering must keep every parent stratum before its child strata. Arbitrary heuristic values that can repeat or reverse along edges are insufficient without a valid ordering component such as depth.
  • For incoming weight 3 and existing weight 1 with subtree values 5 and 1, weighted representative replacement has expectation 16. Uniform replacement with the unchanged total weight instead gives 12. The source’s importance-sampling variant changes the survivor weight as well as the selection probability.
  • A constructed rare-branch tree has 2001 nodes and an unbiased depth-only estimate, yet five independent probes miss its deep nodes with probability greater than 0.99. Unbiasedness does not certify finite-probe accuracy, confidence intervals or solution absence.
  • The fixed-tree unbiasedness result is distinct from nonnegative-functional variance bounds and random-graph average results. Appendix Equation 99 prints an inequality in the wrong direction for k>r: k=4, r=3 gives 3 rather than at most 1.5. That printed asymptotic-proof step is qualified; the core sampling algorithm and uninspected author implementation are not thereby declared incorrect.
  • All 21 pages of the Chen journal copy and 25 pages of the 2013 journal copy were visually read. Dissertation, earlier conference full texts, author software and benchmark replication were not inspected or executed. Constructed checks are finite mathematical examples.
