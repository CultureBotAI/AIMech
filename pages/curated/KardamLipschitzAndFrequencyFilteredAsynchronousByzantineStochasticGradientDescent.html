Kardam Lipschitz- and Frequency-Filtered Asynchronous Byzantine Stochastic Gradient Descent

Algorithms AIMech:KardamLipschitzAndFrequencyFilteredAsynchronousByzantineStochasticGradientDescent DRAFT
Aliases: Kardam, Kardam Asynchronous Byzantine SGD, Kardam Empirical Lipschitz and Frequency Filtered SGD
A Byzantine-resilient asynchronous stochastic gradient descent algorithm proposed by Damaskinos, El Mhamdi, Guerraoui, Patra, and Taziki (ICML 2018) that decouples gradient verification from synchronous communication barriers by combining a staleness-aware gradient dampening rule with a two-stage server filter consisting of an empirical Lipschitz continuity check against the (n-f)/n worker quantile and a sliding-window worker frequency filter that guarantees at least f+1 honest updates in every 2f+1 consecutive model transitions.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from Zeno++ (AIMech:ZenoPlusPlusRobustFullyAsynchronousStochasticGradientDescent, which scores candidate asynchronous updates using a server validation mini-batch loss descent test without worker frequency bounds or empirical Lipschitz quantiles) and from synchronous Byzantine aggregators such as Krum (AIMech:KrumAndMultiKrumByzantineResilientGradientAggregation), Bulyan (AIMech:BulyanTwoStageMetaAggregationForByzantineResilientDistributedLearning), and Geometric Median / RFA (AIMech:RobustFederatedAggregationViaSmoothedWeiszfeldGeometricMedian). Kardam (Damaskinos et al., ICML 2018, PMLR 80:1153-1162, Section 3.1-3.2, Definitions 3-5, pp. 3-6) comprises two coupled components on the parameter server: (1) the Dampening component scales any accepted gradient g_{tl} computed on stale model x_l (with staleness tau_{tl} = t - l) by a strictly decreasing bijective staleness decay function Lambda(tau_{tl}) satisfying Lambda(0) = 1 (such as exponential dampening Lambda(tau) = exp(-alpha * tau^(1/beta))) with adaptive step size gamma_t = gamma * M / sum_{lambda in Lambda_t} lambda |G_{t,lambda}|; and (2) the Filtering component accepts a candidate gradient g_p from worker p only if it simultaneously passes the Frequency Filter—which maintains the list L of workers for the last 2f accepted gradients and rejects worker q if the top-f most frequent workers in (L; q) appear more than f times in total (Definition 4, Lemma 1)—and the Empirical Lipschitz Filter—which checks whether the server-side empirical Lipschitz ratio hat{K}_t^p = ||g_p - g_q||_2 / ||x_t - x_{t-1}||_2 is at most the (n-f)/n quantile hat{K}_t of all workers' local empirical Lipschitz coefficients hat{K}_p = ||g_p - g_p^{prev}||_2 / ||x_{l_p} - x_{l_p}^{prev}||_2 (Definition 5).

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #kardam-frequency-filter-and-empirical-lipschitz-quantile-test
Kardam filters each incoming asynchronous gradient g_p at step t using two checks: the Frequency Filter (Definition 4) tracks the worker IDs L of the last 2f accepted updates and rejects worker p if the sum of occurrences of the f most frequent workers in (L; p) exceeds f (guaranteeing at least f+1 honest updates in every 2f+1 accepted transitions by Lemma 1), and the Empirical Lipschitz Filter (Definition 5) accepts g_p only if hat{K}_t^p = ||g_p - g_q||_2 / ||x_t - x_{t-1}||_2 <= hat{K}_t, where hat{K}_t is the (n-f)/n quantile of per-worker empirical Lipschitz coefficients {hat{K}_w}_{w=1}^n.
  • supports Source: Asynchronous Byzantine Machine Learning (the case of SGD) · Locator: Section 3.2, Definitions 4-5 and Lemma 1, pp. 4-5
Scope #kardam-staleness-dampening-convergence-and-tight-slowdown-bounds
Under n >= 2f + 1 workers, Theorem 1 proves that Kardam achieves a tight optimal Byzantine slowdown factor bounded by (n - 2f)/(n - f) <= SL <= (n - f)/n, and Theorems 2-5 prove almost-sure convergence of the staleness-dampened asynchronous updates (Equations 1-2) to a neighborhood of a stationary point under bounded variance and Lipschitz-smooth non-convex objectives.
  • supports Source: Asynchronous Byzantine Machine Learning (the case of SGD) · Locator: Section 3.1, Equations (1)-(2), p. 3; Section 3.2.2, Theorem 1, p. 5; Section 4, Theorems 2-5, p. 6
Limitation #kardam-two-f-plus-one-worker-bound-and-staleness-horizon-limitation
Kardam requires a strict honest majority n > 2f so that the (n-f)/n empirical Lipschitz quantile and the 2f-window frequency filter do not permanently stall training, and its convergence rate degrades as the maximum worker staleness tau_max increases.
  • supports Source: Asynchronous Byzantine Machine Learning (the case of SGD) · Locator: Section 3.2.2, Theorem 1 & Proof sketch, p. 5; Section 4, Theorem 5, p. 6
Application #kardam-asynchronous-distributed-deep-learning-application
Applied to asynchronous distributed training of deep neural networks on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18) and ImageNet across heterogeneous worker clusters under Byzantine gradient corruption and straggler delays.
  • supports Source: Asynchronous Byzantine Machine Learning (the case of SGD) · Locator: Section 5, Figures 1-6, pp. 6-9

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Requires an honest majority n >= 2f + 1 so that the 2f-window frequency filter can always admit honest workers without deadlocking.
  • Relies on estimating empirical Lipschitz ratios between consecutive parameter states, which can exhibit high variance under small mini-batch sizes or non-IID local data distributions.
