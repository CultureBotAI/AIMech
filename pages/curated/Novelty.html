Novelty SAT heuristic

Algorithms AIMech:Novelty DRAFT
Aliases: Novelty
A clause-based SAT local-search heuristic that ranks candidate flips by total violation count with oldest-variable tie breaks, normally selects the best candidate, and sometimes selects the second-best when the best was the most recently flipped variable in that clause.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the Novelty SAT heuristic introduced by McAllester, Selman and Kautz in 1997, not the general idea of novelty or novelty-search objectives in evolutionary computation. It ranks only variables of a selected unsatisfied clause by the global post-flip objective. Its age-conditional choice differs from HSAT’s global best-score tie selection, WalkSAT/SKC’s break-only score and freebie priority, and tabu inadmissibility. Novelty+ adds a separate random-walk branch and is not an exact alias. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method
Begin from a complete assignment and, while it is unsatisfying, randomly choose an unsatisfied clause. Rank its candidate variables by the total number of unsatisfied clauses that would remain after each flip, preferring a less recently flipped variable when objective scores tie. Let the first two candidates be best and second-best. If the best is not the most recently flipped variable anywhere in the selected clause, choose it. Otherwise choose the second-best with probability p and the best with probability 1−p, for p in [0,1]. Flip the chosen variable, update its timestamp and repeat under the chosen outer search and stopping policy. The score uses the global net effect of a flip, not breakcount alone. The noise coin is conditional on recency; it is not an unconditional random selection from all clause variables.

Limitation #unrestarted-incompleteness
The unrestarted Novelty heuristic can remain confined to nonsatisfying assignments on a satisfiable formula, regardless of its noise parameter. Hoos1999 gives an explicit five-variable counterexample; the theorem does not assert that an unbounded uniform random-restart wrapper is also incomplete.
  • supports Source: On the Run-time Behaviour of Stochastic Local Search Algorithms for SAT · Locator: PDF3, definition of PAC and pure no-restart scope; PDF4, Theorem 3 and F2 proof

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Hoos’s 1999 Theorem 3 shows that Novelty without random restart is essentially incomplete for every noise setting: there are satisfiable inputs and reachable search states from which a model is never found. This statement is about the unrestarted heuristic, not every solver that wraps it in an unbounded random-restart scheme.
  • A verified model is a sound positive result, but exhausting a finite budget is not an UNSAT certificate. The 1997 empirical noise-level and optimality invariants are not general convergence theorems or guaranteed optimal tuning rules.
  • The two-candidate rule needs an explicit convention for unit clauses and for initial/tied timestamps among never-flipped variables. Empty clauses require separate handling. The short source description does not certify an executable choice for every such boundary.
  • A nonrecent best candidate is selected even when p=1; a score tie is first resolved by age. No SKC zero-break priority, tabu prohibition or R-Novelty score-gap formula is silently inserted. Weighted MAX-SAT extensions and later Novelty variants need separate scope.
