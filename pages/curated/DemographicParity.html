Demographic parity

Evaluation Criteria AIMech:DemographicParity DRAFT
Aliases: Statistical parity
A group-level prediction criterion requiring the distribution of a decision or prediction to be independent of a specified protected attribute; for a binary decision, the positive-decision rate is equal across the compared groups.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The record denotes exact distributional parity, also called statistical parity in the inspected fairness literature. It is not equalized odds, equality of true-positive rates, an approximate disparity tolerance, or a complete fairness judgment. Conditional parity and parity for selected subgroups require separately stated conditioning and scope.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Scope #criterion-scope
Compare the prediction distributions conditional on each protected group without conditioning on the target outcome. In binary classification the condition reduces to equality of P(prediction=1 | A=a) across groups. For the statistical-parity formulation of Dwork and colleagues, exact parity is the zero-tolerance case in which the total-variation distance between group output distributions vanishes.
  • supports Source: Equality of Opportunity in Supervised Learning · Locator: Section 1, demographic-parity definition and limitations, PDF pp. 1–2
  • supports Source: Fairness Through Awareness · Locator: Section 3, Definition 3.1, printed p. 7 / PDF p. 8; Section 3.1 examples, printed p. 8 / PDF p. 9
Scope #group-parity-boundary
The statistical-parity formulation compares group output distributions by total variation with a stated tolerance; exact parity is its zero-tolerance case. The accompanying examples show that group parity can coexist with unequal treatment within groups and does not imply parity for all subgroups.
  • supports Source: Fairness Through Awareness · Locator: Section 3, Definition 3.1, printed p. 7 / PDF p. 8; Section 3.1 examples, printed p. 8 / PDF p. 9
Limitation #causal-equivalence-assumptions
Rosenblatt and Witter derive connections with counterfactual fairness using their Criteria 3 independence assumption. Their conversion from demographic parity introduces a causal model and latent-variable construction of their choice; they explicitly distinguish that setting from checking a predictor against a fixed causal model. These results do not justify treating the two criterion identities as synonyms.
  • supports Source: Counterfactual Fairness Is Basically Demographic Parity · Locator: Related Work distinction between a fixed and freely constructed causal model, PDF p. 3; Criteria 3 and Theorems 4–5, PDF p. 4

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Matching group output rates does not ensure similar individuals receive similar treatment or that parity holds within every subgroup.
  • If target outcomes have different prevalences by group, a perfectly accurate predictor can violate demographic parity. This statistical observation does not by itself choose which fairness criterion a domain should use.
  • Exact independence is distinct from approximate parity within a specified tolerance; an empirical estimate needs uncertainty and population context.
  • Connections to counterfactual fairness depend on causal-model assumptions and how the predictor/model is constructed. Shared population statistics do not make the two definitions interchangeable for a fixed causal model.
