Girvan–Newman community detection

Algorithms AIMech:GirvanNewmanCommunityDetection DRAFT
Aliases: Girvan–Newman algorithm, edge-betweenness community detection
A divisive community-detection algorithm that repeatedly removes an edge with maximal current shortest-path betweenness and recalculates affected betweenness scores, using the resulting connected-component splits to form a hierarchy of graph-vertex groups.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #fractional-shortest-paths
Edge betweenness sums contributions from shortest paths between vertex pairs. When a pair has multiple shortest paths, the source gives them equal weights summing to one, so the pair contributes to an edge in proportion to the fraction of its shortest paths using that edge. This is shortest-path centrality, not a count of every walk or a measured physical flow.
  • supports Source: Community structure in social and biological networks · Locator: Section II.B, PDF p. 3, edge-betweenness definition
Mechanism #iterative-recomputation
After each maximal-betweenness edge removal, affected scores are recomputed before the next removal. A fixed ordering of the initial scores is a different procedure. On a four-cycle with fixed lexicographic ties, the constructed reference removes edge 0–1 first; recomputation then selects 2–3 and gives two groups of two vertices, while static sorting next removes 0–3 and first isolates a single vertex.
  • supports Source: Community structure in social and biological networks · Locator: Section II.B, PDF p. 3, four-step algorithm and rejection of one-time sorting; curator calculation in research/discovery/girvan-newman-boundaries.py
Scope #hierarchy-and-selection
The repeated deletions induce nested connected-component partitions and continue until no edges remain. The inspected original method does not prescribe greedy modularity maximization or a universal unique flat partition. Selecting a useful level of the hierarchy is separate from the edge-removal rule; ties and any later cut-selection criterion need their own conventions.
  • supports Source: Community structure in social and biological networks · Locator: Sections II.B–III, PDF pp. 3–5, stopping rule and illustrated hierarchical splits
Scope #runtime-and-domain
Using the cited O(mn) shortest-path betweenness calculation on a graph with m edges and n vertices gives O(m-squared n) worst-case time for repeated removals, or cubic time on sparse graphs. Recalculation can be confined to the affected component. The inspected source treats unweighted undirected networks and explicitly lists directed and weighted generalizations as future work.
  • supports Source: Community structure in social and biological networks · Locator: Section II.B, PDF p. 3, runtime and affected-component discussion; Conclusions, p. 7
Scope #evaluation-scope
The synthetic test uses 128 vertices in four groups with total degree sixteen, varying the external connections; the figure averages one hundred realizations per point. The karate-club split misclassifies one vertex, and the football analysis reports discrepancies where scheduling and conference membership differ. These results are conditional evaluations rather than guarantees of complete recovery in arbitrary graphs.
  • supports Source: Community structure in social and biological networks · Locator: Sections III.A–III.C, PDF pp. 3–5, Figures 3–5 and their captions
Limitation #application-interpretation
The collaboration-network groups receive interpretive topic and methodology labels. The Chesapeake Bay example ignores trophic direction and finds groups associated roughly with habitat, spanning multiple trophic levels. These partitions do not establish directed causal ecological mechanisms. The paper also reports that some other food webs show no clear communities and leaves dense-graph sensitivity versus absence of structure unresolved.
  • supports Source: Community structure in social and biological networks · Locator: Sections IV.A–IV.B and Conclusions, PDF pp. 5–7, Figures 6–7

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This record covers the original shortest-path edge-betweenness removal method in arXiv cond-mat/0112110v1. Later random-walk, weighted, directed, approximate-betweenness and modularity-based selection variants require separate evidence.
  • The method is distinct from the original Newman matrix-based greedy modularity algorithm and its CNM acceleration: it removes edges and recomputes shortest-path centrality rather than joining groups by modularity gain.
  • The finite four-cycle comparison fixes one tie policy and illustrates recomputation; it neither executes the authors’ code nor proves that every graph yields different static and recomputed partitions.
  • The reference calculation sums over unordered reachable vertex pairs and retains isolated vertices. It assigns no contribution to unreachable pairs and performs no deletion on an edgeless graph; the authors’ exceptional-input handling was not audited.
  • A high edge-betweenness score is a graph property, not proof of a social boundary, biological function or actual information-flow mechanism. A hierarchy does not itself determine one uniquely correct community partition.
  • All primary pages and references were read, but the journal version, source code and experimental datasets were not inspected. No model training, paid service or benchmark replication was performed. The record remains DRAFT.
