Gumbel-Top-k trick

Mathematical Methods AIMech:GumbelTopKTrick DRAFT
Aliases: Gumbel top-k sampling
A categorical sampling construction that adds independent standard Gumbel noise to each category’s log weight and returns the indices of the k largest perturbed values in descending order, producing an ordered sample without replacement under successive renormalization of the remaining weights.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the finite categorical Gumbel-Top-k construction formalized by Kool and colleagues, who credit an earlier observation by Vieira. The k=1 case is the Gumbel-Max trick. It is distinct from truncating a language model to its k most probable tokens and then sampling, from a continuous Gumbel-Softmax relaxation, and from the separate stochastic beam algorithm that applies the construction implicitly to sequences. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method
For n categories with positive finite weights w_i and 1≤k≤n, draw independent G_i with the standard Gumbel distribution, equivalently G_i=−log(−log U_i) for independent U_i uniform on (0,1). Rank log(w_i)+G_i and return the first k distinct indices in that perturbed order. The probability of an ordered distinct tuple (i_1,…,i_k) is the product over j of w_i_j divided by the total weight remaining after indices i_1,…,i_(j−1) are removed. Common weight normalization is unnecessary because it adds only a common shift to all log scores. This is an exact distributional statement for the specified random variables, not an assertion that the selected indices are the highest-probability unperturbed categories.

Limitation #inclusion-boundary
Without replacement here means sequential categorical sampling with the remaining weights renormalized after every selected index. It does not mean that each category’s probability of appearing anywhere in the k-sample is proportional to its original probability. The source explicitly distinguishes these quantities.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The ordered sampling probabilities differ from marginal inclusion probabilities; inclusion is not generally proportional to the original weight or equal to k times its normalized probability. At k=n every supported category is included.
  • The stated construction assumes positive finite weights and k no larger than the positive support. Zero weights require exclusion or a declared limiting convention; invalid weights and k beyond support are not covered.
  • Exactness assumes the specified independent continuous noise and exact ranking. Finite precision, random-number endpoints and numerical ties require implementation policies; no software implementation was certified.
  • Direct finite-domain application perturbs all n category scores. It does not by itself supply an efficient algorithm for exponentially many complete sequences, a differentiable relaxation, or an unbiased sample-mean estimator for arbitrary without-replacement outputs.
