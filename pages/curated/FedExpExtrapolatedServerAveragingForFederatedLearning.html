FedExP (Federated Extrapolation for Speeding Up Federated Averaging)

Algorithms AIMech:FedExpExtrapolatedServerAveragingForFederatedLearning DRAFT
Aliases: FedExP, Federated Extrapolation, Extrapolated Federated Averaging
A hyperparameter-free adaptive server step-size method for federated optimization (Jhunjhunwala, Wang, & Joshi 2023, Section 3, Equations 4-6, Algorithm 1) that interprets client local SGD displacements Delta_i^(t) = w^(t) - w_i^(t, tau) in overparameterized federated learning as approximate projections onto client solution manifolds S_i^* and updates the global model via w^(t+1) = w^(t) - eta_g^(t) bar_Delta^(t) with the Projection Onto Convex Sets (POCS) extrapolated step size eta_g^(t) = max(1, (sum_{i=1}^M ||Delta_i^(t)||^2) / (2 M (||bar_Delta^(t)||^2 + epsilon))).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from FedOpt (AIMech:FedOptAdaptiveFederatedOptimization, Reddi et al. 2021), which requires tuning both the client learning rate eta_l and server learning rate eta_g via grid search or second-moment accumulators (FedAdam, FedYogi, FedAdagrad), and from FedNova (AIMech:FedNovaNormalizedAveragingForFederatedOptimization, Wang et al. 2020), which normalizes heterogeneous local step counts tau_i rather than extrapolating the server step size via the ratio of average squared pseudo-gradient norms to the squared norm of the averaged pseudo-gradient.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #r446-pocs-quasi-projection-and-adaptive-extrapolation-step-size
In overparameterized convex settings where clients share a common global minimizer w* with F_i(w*) = 0, Lemma 1 (Section 3, p. 4) proves that tau steps of local gradient descent with eta_l <= 1/L satisfy the quasi-projection inequality <Delta_i^(t), w^(t) - w*> >= 0.5 ||Delta_i^(t)||^2; minimizing the resulting upper bound on ||w^(t) - eta_g^(t) bar_Delta^(t) - w*||^2 yields the extrapolated step size eta_g^(t) = (sum_{i=1}^M ||Delta_i^(t)||^2) / (2 M ||bar_Delta^(t)||^2), which is larger whenever client pseudo-gradients point in diverse directions and automatically decays toward 1 near the optimum.
  • supports Source: FedExP: Speeding Up Federated Averaging via Extrapolation · Locator: Section 3 Motivation & Lemma 1 (Equations 4-6), pp. 3-5
Mechanism #r446-convex-and-nonconvex-convergence-rates
Theorems 1 and 2 (Section 4, pp. 5-6) establish general convex and non-convex convergence bounds for FedExP where the optimality residual is inversely weighted by the harmonic or arithmetic average of the adaptive server step sizes eta_g^(t) >= 1, guaranteeing progress at least as fast as FedAvg (eta_g = 1).
  • supports Source: FedExP: Speeding Up Federated Averaging via Extrapolation · Locator: Section 4 Convergence Analysis (Theorems 1-2, Corollaries 1-2), pp. 5-6
Limitation #r446-stochastic-client-noise-floor-and-epsilon-stabilization-limitation
Under high stochastic mini-batch variance or small client participation M, the denominator ||bar_Delta^(t)||^2 can become dominated by sampling noise near stationarity, requiring a positive stabilization constant epsilon > 0 and decaying local step size eta_l to prevent over-extrapolation (Section 3.2, Section 4).
  • supports Source: FedExP: Speeding Up Federated Averaging via Extrapolation · Locator: Section 3.2 Practical Considerations & Section 4 Discussion, pp. 5-6
Application #r446-cifar-cinic-and-shakespeare-federated-benchmark-application
Applied to federated image classification and language modeling on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, CINIC-10, and Shakespeare, accelerating convergence over FedAvg and tuned server-momentum/adaptive baselines without server step-size tuning.
  • supports Source: FedExP: Speeding Up Federated Averaging via Extrapolation · Locator: Section 5 Experiments (Figures 2-4, Tables 1-2), pp. 7-9

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Overparameterized POCS quasi-projection guarantee holds exactly only when client objectives share a common interpolation minimizer.
  • Requires a damping constant epsilon > 0 and local learning-rate decay to prevent noise amplification when ||bar_Delta^(t)||^2 approaches zero.
