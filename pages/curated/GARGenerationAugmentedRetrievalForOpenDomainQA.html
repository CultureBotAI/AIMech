GAR Generation-Augmented Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering

Algorithms AIMech:GARGenerationAugmentedRetrievalForOpenDomainQA DRAFT
Aliases: GAR, Generation-Augmented Retrieval, GAR and GAR+ Multi-Context Query Expansion for Open-Domain QA
A query-expansion and retrieval algorithm for open-domain question answering that trains sequence-to-sequence pre-trained language models (such as BART) via standard seq2seq supervision to generate three complementary in-domain query contexts from an input question q—(1) the default target answer, (2) the passage sentence containing the answer, and (3) the title of a passage containing the answer—appends each generated context to q to form separate generation-augmented queries, retrieves top candidate passages for each augmented query using sparse lexical retrieval (BM25) and fuses the retrieved rankings (or fuses them with DPR dense retrieval rankings as GAR^+), and aggregates candidate answer spans across retrieved passages in the extractive reader via Passage-level Span Voting by summing normalized span probabilities p(S_i[j]) = softmax(D)[i] * softmax(S_i)[j] over all spans sharing the same surface string.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from RM3 pseudo-relevance feedback, which expands queries using terms from initially retrieved passages rather than parametric knowledge generated by a seq2seq PLM; distinct from HyDE (AIMech:HyDEHypotheticalDocumentEmbeddings), which encodes zero-shot LLM-generated hypothetical documents into dense vectors with an unsupervised contrastive encoder; and distinct from RAG and FiD, which use a generator as the downstream reader over DPR-retrieved passages rather than generating multi-target query expansions prior to retrieval.

Mechanism #gar-multi-target-query-context-generation-and-fusion
GAR trains seq2seq generators using freely available in-domain supervision to generate three distinct query contexts—(1) Answer, (2) Sentence containing the answer, and (3) Title of a positive passage—appends each to the question to form generation-augmented queries, and fuses their separate BM25 retrieval lists (Sections 3.2-3.3), substantially increasing query-passage ROUGE-1/2/L overlap on Natural Questions from 6.00/2.36/5.01 to 13.21/6.99/10.27 (sentence) and mitigating hallucinated answers via complementary contexts (Tables 2-3, Figure 1).

  • supports Source: Generation-Augmented Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering · Locator: main() verification of multi-target query expansion recovering ground-truth passage when generated answer is wrong
Mechanism #gar-passage-level-span-voting
For extractive reading over retrieved passages D = [d_1, ..., d_k] with passage relevance logits D and top-N span logits S_i per passage, GAR computes normalized span probabilities p(S_i[j]) = softmax(D)[i] * softmax(S_i)[j] and sums them across all candidate spans sharing the same surface string across all retrieved passages (Passage-level Span Voting, Section 4.1).

Application #gar-openqa-benchmark-applications
On Natural Questions (NQ) and TriviaQA, GAR with BM25 outperforms DPR at Top-500/1000 on NQ and at all k >= 5 on TriviaQA while training and indexing at a fraction of DPR's compute cost (Tables 4 and 8), and fusing GAR with DPR (GAR^+) reaches 88.9% Top-100 retrieval accuracy on NQ and 43.8 extractive EM / 45.3 generative EM (Tables 4, 6, and 7).
  • supports Source: Generation-Augmented Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering · Locator: Sections 6.2-6.4, Tables 4-8, pp. 6-9
  • supports Source: Generation-Augmented Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering (Crossref metadata) · Locator: BibTeX citation record for arXiv:2009.08553 (ACL 2021)
Limitation #gar-small-k-and-multi-generator-overhead-limitation
Because GAR relies on sparse BM25 matching over queries expanded with potentially noisy PLM-generated contexts, standalone GAR underperforms dense retrieval (DPR) on Natural Questions at very small retrieval cutoffs (Top-5: 60.9% vs. 68.3% in Table 4, which also limits generative reader EM when only 10 passages are read in Table 6) and requires training and running three separate BART-large generators for the three context types.
  • supports Source: Generation-Augmented Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering · Locator: Sections 6.2-6.4, Tables 4, 6 & 8, pp. 6-9

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Standalone GAR with BM25 trails dense retrieval (DPR) at very small retrieval cutoffs on Natural Questions (e.g., Top-5 accuracy 60.9% vs. 68.3% in Table 4), limiting generative readers that only consume a few top passages unless fused with DPR (GAR^+).
  • Requires training and executing three separate seq2seq generators (for answer, sentence, and title targets) and running three separate BM25 retrievals per query before rank fusion.
