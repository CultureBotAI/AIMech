Fill algorithm

Algorithms AIMech:FillAlgorithm DRAFT
An idealized finite constraint-satisfaction search algorithm that takes any strictly cost-reducing single-variable change when available and otherwise increases only the stored cost of the current non-solution assignment.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is Morris’s named Fill algorithm, introduced to distinguish state-specific cost lifting from Breakout’s shared nogood reweighting. It is not flood fill, image inpainting, or LRTA*. Fill permits any lower-cost neighbor, while LRTA* selects a minimum estimated path-cost successor and backs up its value even away from local minima. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Initialize a modifiable cost for complete assignments using the original constraint-violation cost. Test the current assignment for satisfaction. If a single-variable change strictly reduces its stored cost, take any such change; otherwise increase the stored cost of that current assignment alone and continue. Costs of other assignments do not change as a side effect. Retain raised costs for later revisits. Figure 3 specifies an increase without fixing its magnitude; the finite-CSP completeness proof relies on repeated lifts of an endlessly visited minimum eventually exceeding every fixed neighboring cost. Positive unit increments satisfy that intended lifting property. The theorem concerns eventual arrival at a solution when one exists on the finite complete-assignment space with the stated single-variable adjacency.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The source’s theorem is not a finite-budget guarantee or an unsatisfiability decision procedure. Restricting moves so a solution is unreachable, or replacing the finite assignment space with an infinite one, is outside that theorem.
  • Figure 3 does not prescribe a numerical increment. Curator qualification: arbitrary positive increments with a bounded cumulative sum need not satisfy the proof’s unbounded-lifting step; completeness should not be transferred to such an implementation without a separate argument.
  • State-specific costs must survive revisits. They can require storage growing with encountered local minima; the compact shared-nogood representation used by Breakout changes the method and does not inherit Fill’s theorem.
  • The constraint-violation score need not be an admissible estimate of distance to a solution. The method is not a shortest-path optimality algorithm. No runtime advantage, numerical experiment or author implementation is asserted.
