Straight-through estimator

Mathematical Methods AIMech:StraightThroughEstimator DRAFT

Aliases: ST estimator, Identity straight-through estimator
A surrogate-gradient rule that keeps a hard threshold or sampled binary output in the forward computation while propagating its downstream derivative backward as though the hard operation were the identity function.
Definition Evidence
  • supports Source: Estimating or Propagating Gradients Through Stochastic Neurons for Conditional Computation · Locator: Section 4, Equation (13), PDF/printed p.7; Section 3, Equation (3), Theorem 1 and proof, pp.5–6; Section 5 and Figure 1, p.8
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The core identity-backward rule is the straight-through estimator named by Bengio, Léonard and Courville, who attribute the preceding idea to Hinton’s 2012 lecture. The paper also discusses a sigmoid-derivative-scaled variant; that scaling is not part of the core identity rule. This concept is distinct from a score-function estimator, a fully smooth forward relaxation, and the separately curated straight-through Gumbel-Softmax estimator with its specific temperature-dependent softmax Jacobian. Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #method
Let a be the pre-threshold or pre-sigmoid activation and h the hard binary value supplied to downstream computation. Compute the downstream derivative dL/dh using that hard forward state. In the backward pass, substitute dL/dh for the gradient with respect to a, as if dh/da were one, and then propagate through the differentiable computation that produced a. In the paper’s Bernoulli-logistic setting, the intended stochastic output law is P(h=1)=sigmoid(a), as used in its proof; the identity backward substitution is separate from that sampling law. Multiplying dL/dh by sigmoid(a)*(1-sigmoid(a)) gives the paper’s discussed scaled alternative, not the same backward rule. No derivative through the actual binary sampling decision is obtained by this substitution.
  • supports Source: Estimating or Propagating Gradients Through Stochastic Neurons for Conditional Computation · Locator: Section 4, Equation (13), PDF/printed p.7; Section 3, Equation (3), Theorem 1 and proof, pp.5–6; Section 5 and Figure 1, p.8
Scope #backward-variants
The source explicitly defines the core backward value as dL/dh and separately discusses multiplying it by the sigmoid derivative. Hard forward values are retained in either case; the two backward rules must not be conflated.
  • supports Source: Estimating or Propagating Gradients Through Stochastic Neurons for Conditional Computation · Locator: Section 4, Equation (13) and preceding paragraph, PDF/printed p.7
Limitation #sampling-notation
The indicator inequality in Equation (3) and the probability used in the following proof disagree for a general non-half sigmoid probability. The curated interpretation uses the proof’s explicit Bernoulli probability; this is a disclosed mathematical interpretation of the source, not a check of author software.
  • supports Source: Estimating or Propagating Gradients Through Stochastic Neurons for Conditional Computation · Locator: Section 3, Equation (3), PDF/printed p.5; proof leading to Equation (5), p.6

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This is a biased surrogate, not the ordinary derivative of the hard threshold and not an unbiased estimator of the discrete expected-loss gradient. A usable downstream derivative is required; an arbitrary black-box discrete reward does not provide one automatically.
  • The surrogate depends on the differentiable extension of the downstream loss between binary states. Equal values at the binary endpoints do not imply equal straight-through updates, and the source’s informal single-layer sign statement is not used as a universal guarantee for arbitrary differentiable losses.
  • Equation (3) of the inspected paper prints the indicator U>sigmoid(a), but the subsequent proof uses P(h=1)=sigmoid(a). For uniform U, the printed inequality instead gives probability 1-sigmoid(a). This record follows the explicitly used Bernoulli law and discloses the inconsistency rather than treating the printed inequality as verified executable sampling code.
  • Identity, sigmoid-scaled, clipped and other later straight-through rules require distinct conventions. A generic straight-through label does not establish that their Jacobians, bias or optimization behavior coincide.
  • The inspected conditional-computation example motivates sparse gating; no universal speedup, accuracy advantage or successful convergence is asserted.
