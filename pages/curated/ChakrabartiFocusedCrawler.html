Chakrabarti classifier-guided focused crawling

Algorithms AIMech:ChakrabartiFocusedCrawler DRAFT
Aliases: Chakrabarti focused crawler, Taxonomy-guided focused crawling
A focused web-acquisition procedure that uses a trained classifier over a supplied topic taxonomy to decide or prioritize hyperlink expansion, with hard and soft focus modes and periodic relevance-weighted hub discovery to promote promising crawl locations.

It differs from Flake flow grouping, general best-first search and the naive Bayes component alone.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #taxonomy-and-classifier
The user marks good categories in an existing topic tree, with no good category an ancestor of another. The intended classifier model assigns documents to exclusive leaf topics and represents text as a multinomial bag of words with class priors. Posterior mass for the selected categories supplies relevance; the taxonomy is an input rather than a hierarchy learned by the crawling procedure.
  • supports Source: Focused crawling: a new approach to topic-specific Web resource discovery · Locator: Sections 2.3, 3 and 3.1, printed pp. 549–551 / PDF pp. 5–7
Mechanism #focus-policies
Hard focus finds the maximum-posterior leaf and permits expansion only if a selected good category is its ancestor. Soft focus sums the posterior mass of the selected categories, assigns each unvisited neighbor the parent relevance, uses the maximum over multiple referring parents and updates the score after retrieval. Soft focus does not discard pages a priori. A document can have substantial total good-category mass while its most probable leaf lies elsewhere; these policies are not interchangeable threshold tests.
  • supports Source: Focused crawling: a new approach to topic-specific Web resource discovery · Locator: Section 3.1, printed pp. 551–552 / PDF pp. 7–8; curator finite example in research/discovery/focused-crawling-boundaries.py
Mechanism #frontier-and-distiller
The soft crawl frontier sorts first by increasing fetch-attempt count and then by decreasing relevance; hard focus orders surviving URLs by attempt count with arbitrary remaining ties. Periodically the crawler stops for relevance-weighted hub/authority analysis, promotes top hubs for revisiting and prepares unvisited pages cited by those hubs. The cited relational-database/concurrent redesign is described as ongoing work, not assumed to be this implementation.
  • supports Source: Focused crawling: a new approach to topic-specific Web resource discovery · Locator: Sections 3.2–3.3, printed pp. 552–553 / PDF pp. 8–9
Limitation #probability-equation-boundary
Equation 1 includes the class prior in its numerator but omits prior factors from the sibling-likelihood denominator, despite the surrounding intended Bayes model. Literal values therefore need not normalize: priors 1/4 and 3/4 with equal likelihoods 1/2 give 1/8 and 3/8, summing to 1/2. The record preserves the intended probabilistic model and this printed discrepancy; it does not silently repair or diagnose an uninspected author implementation.
  • supports Source: Focused crawling: a new approach to topic-specific Web resource discovery · Locator: Section 3.1, Equation 1, printed p. 551 / PDF p. 7; exact curator arithmetic in research/discovery/focused-crawling-boundaries.py
Limitation #evaluation-boundary
The historical comparison of unfocused, hard and soft crawling uses the same classifier for crawl guidance and acquired-page relevance scoring. The authors frame this as evaluation of the neighborhood-based crawling heuristic. It is not independently human-labeled relevance accuracy or classifier calibration. Plotted progression uses fetched-page counts, and overlap from disjoint seed subsets is indirect evidence of coverage, not exhaustive recall or general invariance to initialization.
  • supports Source: Focused crawling: a new approach to topic-specific Web resource discovery · Locator: Sections 4.1–4.4 and Figures 3–8, printed pp. 553–558 / PDF pp. 9–14
Limitation #failure-and-scope
Text-poor pages can be misclassified, hard pruning can lose useful paths, and soft focus can diffuse into other topics. The mutual-funds example motivates broadening selected categories; automatic detection and adaptation are future work. The stated average-relevance goal is not accompanied by a global optimum guarantee. Neither historical topical labels nor link-based examples establish medical conclusions or causal claims about the represented communities.
  • supports Source: Focused crawling: a new approach to topic-specific Web resource discovery · Locator: Sections 3, 4.1–4.3, 4.7 and 6, printed pp. 550,553–556,559–561 / PDF pp. 6,9–12,15–17

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This record covers the hard/soft 1999 acquisition family and its periodic distiller interface, not all methods called focused crawling. Flake flow-based crawling and the relevance-weighted graph-ranking component have distinct records.
  • The printed probability denominator is ambiguous. No author code, smoothing/training implementation, calibration audit, live crawl or historical dataset was inspected or executed.
  • Section 4.4 and Figure 8 axes discuss top 25 resources while the Figure 8 caption says 100 best servers. Section 4.3 rounds a cycling overlap value differently from its plotted level. No exact ranking-overlap denominator or replicated percentage is asserted.
  • All eighteen primary pages, references, figures and central mathematical expressions were inspected. The author-hosted copy has different pagination from publisher metadata; cited locators identify that copy. Publisher full text was inaccessible.
  • DRAFT remains. Historical demonstrations and constructed arithmetic do not establish current-web effectiveness, universal optimality or bias-free relevance.
