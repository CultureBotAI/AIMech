Generic tree sampling

Algorithms AIMech:GenericTreeSampling DRAFT
Aliases: Chen generic sampling, Generic importance-weighted tree sampling
An importance-sampling generalization of Chen heuristic tree sampling that merges representatives of ordered tree strata using specified positive selection probabilities and inverse-probability survivor weights to estimate an additive tree functional.

Retain Chen’s explicitly named generic sampling algorithm GS: it generalizes the maintained HS method by allowing arbitrary supported collision probabilities and changing the surviving weight by the corresponding inverse probability. The HS record specifies weight-proportional replacement and a summed survivor weight. GS is a broader algorithm family, not an alternative name for that particular rule. Knuth’s estimator follows one path; Purdom samples successor subsets; Gumbel top-k sampling and TreeSHAP have different sampling laws or targets. Bare generic sampling and GS are not aliases because the terms have unrelated uses.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #inverse-probability-collision
Start with root weight one, process parent strata before their descendants and let a newly generated child inherit its parent’s weight. When an incoming child of weight w collides with an existing representative of weight W, choose the child with a specified conditional probability p strictly between zero and one. Its surviving weight is w/p; if the existing representative survives, its weight is W/(1-p). Sum each expanded representative’s weight times its node-functional value.
  • supports Source: Heuristic Sampling: A Method for Predicting the Performance of Tree Searching Programs · Locator: Sections 2–3.2, PDF pp.2–4 / printed pp.296–298; Algorithm HS and its GS replacement clause
Scope #finite-tree-expectation
For a finite fixed tree, finite node-functional values, valid stratum order and supported random choices with the stated weight corrections, the estimator preserves the additive total in expectation. The conditional expectation argument applies to each collision; it does not require p to equal the weight-proportional HS choice. This counts tree-path occurrences and does not establish a distinct-vertex estimator for a graph.
  • supports Source: Heuristic Sampling: A Method for Predicting the Performance of Tree Searching Programs · Locator: Sections 2 and 4.1–4.3, PDF pp.2,4–6 / printed pp.296,298–300; Theorem 4, Equations 14–17
Scope #hs-and-path-specializations
Choosing p=w/(W+w) makes either survivor’s weight W+w and recovers the HS collision rule. With strata determined only by depth, the sampled tree has one path, connecting the construction to simple backtracking-tree sampling. GS also permits choices with a different distribution of weights and estimates.
  • supports Source: Heuristic Sampling: A Method for Predicting the Performance of Tree Searching Programs · Locator: Sections 3.2–4.3, PDF pp.4–6 / printed pp.298–300; Theorem 1, Corollaries 2–3
Limitation #oracle-and-variance-conditions
For nonnegative subtree totals, the source’s zero-variance choice is proportional to the true incoming and existing weighted subtree totals. Those unknown totals are generally unavailable. Zero totals require separate support and division conventions; signed totals do not justify using this nonnegative formula unchanged. The later average-variance argument adds assumptions about the distribution of subtrees within strata.
  • supports Source: Heuristic Sampling: A Method for Predicting the Performance of Tree Searching Programs · Locator: Section 3.2 and Section 4.4, PDF pp.4,6–7 / printed pp.298,300–301; Equation 18 and Theorem 5
Limitation #ordering-and-cost-scope
A valid ordering is required even though the tree total is estimated without enumerating it. The source states order invariance for HS; that result does not establish order invariance for arbitrary GS proposals. At most one representative is expanded per stratum, while generating children, computing strata and evaluating proposals still cost work. Unbiasedness alone provides no finite-probe accuracy or runtime guarantee.
  • supports Source: Heuristic Sampling: A Method for Predicting the Performance of Tree Searching Programs · Locator: Sections 3–4.4, PDF pp.2–7 / printed pp.296–301; Corollary 2 and Theorem 4

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The native name is disambiguated by tree and the Chen alias. Bare GS and generic sampling are not asserted aliases; a generalization relationship does not make all GS instances identical to the fixed HS rule.
  • For old weight1/value1 and incoming weight3/value5, corrected p=1/2 replacement has mean16 and variance196; HS has mean16 and variance48. Retaining the total weight4 with uncorrected uniform choice instead has mean12. Better or worse proposal variance does not change the required weight correction.
  • A constructed tree with incomparable parent strata A and B sends leaf values2,5 from A and11 from B into a common later stratum. Constant incoming p=1/2 gives mean18 under either valid parent order but variance34 for A then B and226 for B then A. Thus the HS-specific order-invariance result cannot be transferred to arbitrary GS choices.
  • Strict support excludes p=0 or1 in a collision with potentially contributing alternatives. A proposal based on exact positive subtree totals is an oracle illustration; no inexpensive perfect heuristic, general confidence bound or infinite-tree result is asserted.
  • All21 pages of the cited journal copy were visually read. The definition and expectation claims use Sections2–4.4, not a certification of every later variance proof: the previously identified printed Appendix Equation99 inequality remains qualified. Dissertation, author software and empirical replication were not inspected or executed.
