Population-based incremental learning

Algorithms AIMech:PopulationBasedIncrementalLearning DRAFT
Aliases: PBIL
An optimization method that samples candidate solutions from a probability-vector representation and incrementally moves that representation toward high-evaluation sampled solutions, with probability-vector mutation in its basic formulation.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The inspected baseline uses independent Bernoulli coordinates for fixed-length binary solutions and moves toward the current generation’s single best sample. Multiple-positive and negative-example updates are explicit extensions. It is not continual learning, supervised classification, direct crossover of persistent chromosomes, or the general cross-entropy optimization framework. A single-elite Bernoulli CE fit with smoothing can coincide with the PBIL attraction step; that shared subcase does not establish equivalence of the full named procedures. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Initialize each binary-coordinate probability P_i to .5. At each generation, independently sample a population of binary vectors according to P, evaluate them and choose a sample x_best with the highest objective value in that generation. Update each coordinate P_i_new=(1−LR)*P_i+LR*x_best_i. Then, with a configured mutation probability per coordinate, shift that probability toward a randomly chosen binary endpoint b using P_i_new=(1−MUT_SHIFT)*P_i+MUT_SHIFT*b. Repeat for the selected budget or stopping rule. For these convex-update interpretations, LR and MUT_SHIFT lie in [0,1]. Maintain the highest-evaluation solution vectors separately for return, as the Figure4 caption recommends. The baseline updates its distribution toward the current-generation winner, not automatically the best-so-far archive. Injecting the archived best into later populations, moving toward several winners, or learning away from differing bits of poor samples are separately described extensions.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The product of coordinate marginals cannot represent general dependencies between bits or arbitrary mixtures of dissimilar good solutions. Different populations can have the same marginals; representation and encoding affect the search.
  • A high learning rate can concentrate probabilities prematurely; a very low rate can make adaptation slow. With interior probabilities and 0<LR<1, a finite attraction step stays interior in exact arithmetic, but this does not guarantee lasting exploration or a finite-budget global optimum. Mutation events can be absent, and floating-point probabilities can collapse.
  • Probability-vector mutation is not a bit flip of every sampled chromosome and is not guaranteed to increase diversity on each event: a move toward an already favored endpoint can increase concentration. Mutation toward .5 is discussed separately as a future alternative.
  • Selection of the current-generation winner depends on objective ordering, whereas tie handling, objective direction, noise, population size and mutation randomization remain configuration choices. A preserved output archive does not force that archived vector into future samples. The report’s comparative results are not universal performance guarantees.
