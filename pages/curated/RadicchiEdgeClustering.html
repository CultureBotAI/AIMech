Radicchi edge-clustering community detection

Algorithms AIMech:RadicchiEdgeClustering DRAFT
Aliases: Radicchi divisive algorithm, modified edge-clustering community detection
A divisive graph-community algorithm family that repeatedly removes edges with the lowest modified cycle-based edge-clustering score, updates affected local scores and forms a component hierarchy, optionally retaining branches through the paper’s original-adjacency strong or weak community tests.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

7 Claims
Mechanism #modified-triangle-score
For an edge joining vertices i and j, the triangle variant scores (z + 1) / min(k_i − 1, k_j − 1), where z counts triangles containing the edge and k values are current endpoint degrees. It removes edges with smallest scores and updates affected values. The added one distinguishes this ranking from the ordinary edge-clustering coefficient; values may exceed one, as exact scores of two on a triangle and three-halves on a four-clique illustrate.
  • supports Source: Defining and identifying communities in networks · Locator: Section IV, PDF pp. 4–5, Equations 3–4 and update discussion; curator calculation in research/discovery/radicchi-boundaries.py
Limitation #leaf-and-tie-boundary
The source assigns an infinite modified score to a leaf’s unique edge, whose triangle denominator is zero. This is a stated convention, not a finite probability. A triangle-free four-cycle instead gives equal finite triangle scores of one. The inspected text does not settle every tie or terminal all-infinite case, so the record does not claim that a leaf edge can never be removed during full-tree construction.
  • supports Source: Defining and identifying communities in networks · Locator: Section IV, PDF p. 5, paragraph following Equation 5; Figure 5 legend, pp. 8–9; curator calculation in research/discovery/radicchi-boundaries.py
Scope #cycle-family-boundary
The family extends the score to (z_g + 1) / s_g using the number of cycles of order g containing an edge and the possible such cycles given the node degrees. The paper evaluates triangles and four-cycles, but the inspected presentation gives no explicit executable four-cycle denominator formula. This record preserves the described family without claiming reconstruction of a particular g = 4 implementation.
  • supports Source: Defining and identifying communities in networks · Locator: Section IV, PDF p. 5, Equation 5 and g = 3/g = 4 discussion
Mechanism #selector-and-filter
Minimum modified cycle score replaces maximum shortest-path betweenness as the edge selector. The plotted anticorrelation between those scores is imperfect and their extremal edges can differ. Either selector can be combined with the same original-adjacency community tests and at-least-two-qualifying-children retention rule; sharing a filter does not establish identical hierarchies.
  • supports Source: Defining and identifying communities in networks · Locator: Sections III–IV, PDF pp. 4–5; Figure 4 legend p. 8 and plot p. 12
Limitation #runtime-conditions
The paper’s rough aM + bM-squared cost estimate combines repeated connectivity checks with local score updates. Treating an update as independent of total edge count requires control of the local neighborhood and cycle order; it is not a universal constant-cost claim for unbounded degrees. Figure 5 measures full-tree construction without a community-validation criterion on random graphs of fixed average degree five, so its times are not complete filtered-pipeline timings.
  • supports Source: Defining and identifying communities in networks · Locator: Section IV, PDF p. 5, runtime estimate; Figure 5 legend, pp. 8–9 and plot p. 13
Scope #evaluation-scope
On the 128-vertex four-group synthetic benchmark with expected degree sixteen, the paper compares strong/weak definition outcomes and exact-group success separately from node error. Its g = 4 weak-criterion advantage is conditional on that test. The collaboration case analyzes the largest 12,722-vertex component of a 15,616-vertex graph; its reported size distribution pools nested weak communities, not merely groups from one flat partition.
  • supports Source: Defining and identifying communities in networks · Locator: Sections III–V, PDF pp. 4–6; Figure 2 and Figure 6 legends, pp. 8–9, plots pp. 10 and 14
Limitation #domain-limits
The source’s applications are social networks. It acknowledges subjective interpretation of collaboration groups and identifies a shortage of short cycles as a possible difficulty for non-social networks. Proposed biological and technological applications are future work in this source, not demonstrated general validation or proof that few triangles mean no communities exist.
  • supports Source: Defining and identifying communities in networks · Locator: Sections V–VI, PDF pp. 6–7

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This family record covers arXiv cond-mat/0309488v2, with an explicit triangle formula and bounded description of higher-cycle scoring. It is not a duplicate standalone g3 record or a complete implementation specification for g4.
  • Current-graph degrees and cycles determine deletion scores; the optional strong/weak community tests use the original full adjacency. Conflating these two graph states changes the procedure.
  • The finite calculations test score and definition boundaries only. They do not run author code or reproduce the source’s accuracy and runtime experiments.
  • Source tie resolution, higher-cycle denominator conventions and terminal all-infinite behavior were not audited in code. Weighted and directed generalizations are outside the inspected scope.
  • All fourteen text pages, equations and figures were inspected. The journal PDF, supporting community lists, datasets and author software remain uninspected. No model training or benchmark replication was performed; the record remains DRAFT.
