A-star beam search for sequence decoding

Algorithms AIMech:SequenceAStarBeamSearch DRAFT
Aliases: A* beam search for sequence decoding
A sequence-decoding beam framework that ranks active hypotheses by accumulated score plus an optimistic future-score heuristic, uses shorter length to break score ties, and limits accepted expansions to k hypotheses per sequence length.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This names the finite-beam A-star decoding framework in Meister, Vieira and Cotterell, not every algorithm informally called A-star beam search. The h=0 specialization has its own per-length best-first record. A finite per-length budget distinguishes the framework from unpruned A* and from a whole-frontier capacity cap. Use Algorithm 2 with the A-star beam settings in Table 1: prioritize the sum of current score and h, maintain a separate length counter, and suppress expansions beyond that length’s k budget. The framework’s top-completed-hypothesis stopping rule operates over this already-pruned agenda; a finite beam can exclude globally superior paths.

Limitation #finite-beam-heuristic
With finite pruning, even an admissible future-score heuristic can alter which hypotheses survive compared with ordinary width-k beam search. The paper distinguishes the zero-heuristic equivalence result from this more general framework.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The finite beam is an approximation to exact sequence-model decoding; equality to a specified beam procedure is not global MAP optimality.
  • Sequence length limits, EOS handling and compatible deterministic tie conventions are part of the comparison. Arbitrary implementations with different stopping or tie rules need not return identical hypotheses.
  • Queue counts are counts of retained hypotheses, not total bytes including neural states, model parameters, backpointers and temporary successors. No numerical speedup or benchmark-quality claim is curated.
  • Admissibility of h does not preserve the zero-heuristic beam’s output after finite pruning. This is explicitly acknowledged in Section 4.1.
  • Unpruned A-star guarantees cannot be transferred to the finite-beam framework merely because its heuristic is admissible. No unrestricted nonmonotonic stopping theorem is asserted.
  • Application to a particular score requires checking the direction and validity of its future-reward bound, terminal behavior and stopping policy.
