Guided Fast Local Search

Algorithms AIMech:GuidedFastLocalSearch DRAFT
A local-search algorithm that combines GLS feature-penalty updates with FLS activation-bit search, reactivating subneighborhoods associated with newly penalized features and retaining solutions according to the original objective.

Scope #named-penalty-activation-controller
Guided Fast Local Search is the named combined controller that repeatedly applies FLS to a feature-penalized objective and couples selected penalty updates to reactivation of related subneighborhoods.

Mechanism #initialization-and-current-objective
Initialize a constructed candidate, set every feature penalty p_i to zero, and set every activation bit to one. For binary feature indicators I_i(s), search using the current augmented objective h(s)=g(s)+lambda*sum_i p_i I_i(s). The penalties in h are the current penalty state, not fixed at their initial zero values.
  • supports Source: Guided Local Search · Locator: Section 3, Equations 1–2, PDF p. 3; Section 6.3, PDF p. 12
  • supports Source: Guided Local Search and Its Application to the Traveling Salesman Problem · Locator: Figure 2, PDF p. 16
Mechanism #strict-active-subneighborhood-search
Within a FLS call, scan active subneighborhoods in the configured order and accept the first encountered move that strictly improves the current augmented objective. Keep the successful subneighborhood active, activate the configured subneighborhoods affected by the accepted move, update the candidate, and continue to the next subneighborhood instead of restarting the complete scan. Deactivate a subneighborhood when its scan finds no improving move, and return when all activation bits are zero.
  • supports Source: Guided Local Search · Locator: Sections 5–6.2, PDF pp. 8–11, FLS pseudocode on p. 10
  • supports Source: Guided Local Search and Its Application to the Traveling Salesman Problem · Locator: PDF pp. 14–16 and Figure 2
Mechanism #utility-penalty-and-reactivation
After FLS returns, compute feature utilities I_i(s)c_i/(1+p_i), increment every maximum-utility feature penalty by one, and activate the subneighborhoods returned by the feature-to-subneighborhood mapping before the next FLS call.
  • supports Source: Guided Local Search · Locator: Equation 3, PDF p. 3; Sections 6.3–6.4.1, PDF pp. 12–13
  • supports Source: Guided Local Search and Its Application to the Traveling Salesman Problem · Locator: PDF pp. 15–16 and Figure 2
Scope #original-objective-incumbent-and-stopping
The outer procedure retains the best solution found according to the original objective g and stops by a supplied stopping criterion; feature/move activation mappings depend on the optimization problem.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • All inactive bits do not certify an exhaustive local minimum unless the move enumeration and reactivation policy establish the required coverage. Subneighborhoods may overlap, and the method can miss improvements.
  • With nonnegative feature costs, a positive maximum utility selects present features, but when every utility is zero the printed all-maxima loop can also select absent features although the prose discusses present features. A boundary convention must be specified for implementation; none is silently added here.
  • Problem-specific features, costs, move enumeration, scan order, move/feature activation callbacks, incumbent bookkeeping and stopping semantics are required. The mathematical objective must reflect current penalties, and bit changes must persist as intended across calls; pseudocode is not a language-level API specification.
  • Penalty guidance is a soft objective change, not a forbidden move or a guarantee of escape. With lambda=0, or features constant over the relevant moves, penalties do not change those move comparisons. Strict inner descent does not by itself ensure the outer process terminates or finds a global optimum.
  • The core FLS rule is strict improvement. Problem-specific variants such as the radio-link example allowing sideways moves and an extra stagnation stop should not be silently included in that core or inherit its conditional finite-descent argument.
  • The sources motivate and report efficiency gains in selected applications, but the complete controller description does not establish a general runtime advantage, universal quality improvement, global convergence or an application-independent parameter prescription.
  • The inspected evidence is the exact 1999 author manuscript and 2010 chapter layout. Their named complete procedure supports this scope; it does not establish earliest priority, equivalence of every later GLS/FLS hybrid, or exhaustion of later variants and neighboring search methods.
