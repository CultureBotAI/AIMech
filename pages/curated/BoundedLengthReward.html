Bounded length reward for sequence scoring

Mathematical Methods AIMech:BoundedLengthReward DRAFT
Aliases: Bounded word reward
A sequence scoring rule that adds a fixed nonnegative reward per generated position up to a finite length cap, giving score S(y)+r·min(l,|y|) instead of rewarding every additional position without bound.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The cap limits how much reward can accumulate; it is not itself a maximum allowed output length. This is the bounded reward in Huang et al. 2017, called bounded word-reward by Yang et al. 2018. It differs from division by output length, an uncapped word reward and a reward rate recomputed from beam statistics. Mechanism #method
Add r·min(l,n) to cumulative log probability S(y), where n is the hypothesis length and r≥0. The 2017 method estimates l from a development-set target/source length ratio times the input length. For a current prefix at depth i, all remaining reward is bounded by r·max(l−i,0). Its total completion-score bound simplifies to S(prefix)+r·l. A beam decoder may stop once the maximum such active-prefix bound is no greater than the best completed adjusted score.
  • supports Source: When to Finish? Optimal Beam Search for Neural Text Generation (modulo beam size) · Locator: Section 4, score definition and Equations (3)–(4), printed pp. 2136–2137 / PDF pp. 3–4
Scope #later-name-and-scope
The 2018 paper calls the fixed-rate capped rule bounded word-reward and distinguishes it from its predicted-length and adaptive-reward variants.
  • supports Source: Breaking the Beam Search Curse: A Study of (Re-)Scoring Methods and Stopping Criteria for Neural Machine Translation · Locator: Section 4.1 Equation (7) and Section 4.2, printed p. 3056 / PDF p. 3

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The score is a decoding preference, not a probability distribution or an intrinsic translation-quality measure.
  • The reward rate and length-cap estimate require specification. A development-set average length ratio need not transfer to every sentence or domain.
  • The stopping bound assumes non-increasing log probability, nonnegative fixed reward and a fixed finite cap for the input; its beam-relative guarantee does not undo finite-width pruning.
  • Reward saturation does not prohibit outputs longer than the cap. Tokenization, length counting and EOS handling must be specified in an implementation.
  • No numerical BLEU improvement, universal quality advantage or current implementation behavior is asserted.
