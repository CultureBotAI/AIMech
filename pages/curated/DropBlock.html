DropBlock

Layers & Modules AIMech:DropBlock DRAFT
A training-time feature-map transformation for convolutional networks that suppresses randomly placed contiguous spatial blocks of activations and rescales retained activations.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope DropBlock defines a structured dropout transformation of intermediate convolutional feature maps, rather than an entire CNN, input-only Cutout augmentation or always-whole-channel SpatialDropout. The paper’s default uses separate spatial masks for feature channels; a shared-channel mask is an examined alternative. A block spanning a whole map and a block of size1 are boundary settings, not equivalence of the general methods. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
During training, sample block seeds within the spatial region whose expanded square blocks fit inside the feature map, suppress the union of those blocks, multiply activations by the resulting keep mask and normalize using the observed retained-mask fraction. The paper uses a block size and a seed probability gamma computed approximately from the desired keep probability, block area and number of valid centers. Overlap makes the formula approximate. The author-linked pinned TensorFlow implementation confirms gamma as the probability that a valid center becomes a drop seed. It supports square feature maps, clamps block size to map width, expands zeros using minimum pooling, and computes normalization from the resulting mask tensor as a whole. During ordinary inference, the transformation returns its input unchanged. The paper recommends a linear decrease of keep probability from1 to a target during training; this schedule is an experimental choice rather than a new learned mask.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Algorithm1 writes M~Bernoulli(gamma) while expanding zero entries, yet Equation1 and the pinned implementation use gamma as the drop-seed rate. These literal conventions differ under the usual Bernoulli success convention; the implementation resolves the convention used here without claiming an author-issued erratum.
  • The gamma expression does not guarantee the target retained fraction at every location or in every realization. Block overlap and edge coverage matter. The paper’s per-mask normalization and the pinned implementation’s whole-mask-tensor normalization should not be silently equated.
  • Both the displayed ratio and inspected code require a nonzero retained fraction; the inspected function has no all-zero-mask guard. Valid block sizes, probabilities, tensor shapes and numerical safeguards are implementation requirements, not consequences of the name DropBlock.
  • Random blocks are independent of a semantic object annotation and may remove useful information. The experiments with masking at inference are diagnostic perturbations, separate from the ordinary identity behavior. Reported architecture-specific gains and robustness do not establish universal benefits, and no author code or model was executed.
