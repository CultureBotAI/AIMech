Aliases: MMAS
An ant-colony optimization algorithm that reinforces solution components from a single selected best ant per iteration, constrains pheromone trails between lower and upper limits, and initializes trails high to encourage early exploration.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Construct solutions by choosing feasible components with probabilities proportional to tau^alpha*eta^beta, as in Ant System. For positive-cost minimization, select one iteration-best or best-so-far solution according to the declared reinforcement schedule. Evaporate every trail using retention rho and add 1/f(best) only to components of that selected solution, with zero deposit elsewhere. Clip the resulting trails to [tau_min,tau_max] after each iteration. The source initializes trails at a high value that is brought to the upper limit; this preserves initial exploration pressure relative to low trails. Its upper-limit estimate is tau_max=1/((1−rho)*f(best_so_far)), updated when the best cost improves. For the source’s TSP lower-limit heuristic, n is the number of cities and avg=n/2 approximates the number of choices at each step. The displayed formula uses unit pheromone exponent alpha=1, equal decision probabilities and negligible heuristic differences: with p_dec=p_best^(1/n), tau_min=tau_max*(1−p_dec)/((avg−1)*p_dec), capped at tau_max if larger. This is an approximate configuration rule, not the exact probability of reconstructing a real tour. Positive limits, 0<=rho<1, a valid denominator and declared handling of boundary parameters are required for this account.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Positive lower trails provide nonzero probabilities only for choices that remain eligible and have positive finite heuristic weights with a finite positive normalizer. The paper’s optional nearest-neighbor candidate lists exclude otherwise feasible choices, and its fallback is deterministic maximization; positive bounds therefore do not prove full tour support for that implementation.
  • The p_best lower-limit heuristic assumes the simplified TSP branching model, alpha=1 and negligible heuristic differences; changing the pheromone exponent changes the displayed probability relation. Choosing p_best=1 yields tau_min=0 in the displayed formula, which does not satisfy a strict positive-bound premise. Small n, changing branch counts and numerical underflow require additional configuration decisions.
  • The geometric-series argument bounds a recurrence under a maximum deposit. That argument alone does not establish that every changing trail sequence has a limit, nor that a finite run reaches the global optimum. The source’s trail-bound notion of convergence must not be equated with optimality or all ants taking the same tour.
  • Equal high pheromone does not imply uniform construction when heuristic weights differ. Best-so-far reinforcement, iteration-best mixing, candidate lists, local search, smoothing and restart rules affect behavior and must be declared. The reported TSP/QAP comparisons are not reproduced and no universal performance guarantee is asserted.
