Mixed-membership model for scientific publications

Model Families AIMech:MixedMembershipModelForScientificPublications DRAFT
A generative document model in which one Dirichlet-distributed membership vector mixes aspect-specific word distributions for an abstract and aspect-specific reference distributions for its bibliography.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Scoped to the word/reference multinomial model fitted by Erosheva, Fienberg and Lafferty in their 2004 scientific-publication study. The broader mixed-membership framework and proposed Bernoulli citation alternatives are not exact aliases for this selected model. Shared document memberships couple two observation channels; no word-to-reference alignment, author identity or latent network-block membership is assumed. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #shared-membership-channels
For fixed K aspects, each aspect has a categorical word distribution and a separate categorical reference-ID distribution. Each document draws lambda from Dirichlet(alpha). Conditional on lambda, its word tokens are sampled from the lambda-weighted word mixture and its reference entries from the lambda-weighted reference mixture, with channel lengths conditioned on. Equivalently, each word or reference draws its own aspect label from the same lambda. The document likelihood integrates the product of both channel likelihoods over lambda; their conditional independence does not imply marginal independence. Removing the reference observations yields a word-only LDA-type model, whereas fixed rather than random membership vectors give a PLSA-style alternative.
  • supports Source: Mixed Membership Models of Scientific Publications · Locator: Mixed Membership Models, preprint pp.2-3; Mixed Membership Models for Documents, pp.3-4, Equations (7)-(11)
Mechanism #approximation-and-loadings
The reported analysis estimates parameters through a variational approximation to the shared-membership likelihood; expectation propagation is discussed as another approximate approach. Approximate document membership posteriors supply loadings, summarized by Dirichlet means and compared descriptively with official publication categories. This is unsupervised aspect discovery with external category comparison, not supervised recovery of those labels. General corpus fitting is computationally demanding even though small finite cases can be integrated by explicit expansion; approximate EM and posterior summaries do not guarantee exact likelihoods or global optima.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The main model treats references as multinomial draws with replacement although observed bibliographies are represented by presence indicators. Bernoulli inclusion models that use absent references and publication-time restrictions are discussed on pp.4-5 as alternatives; they are not the fitted multinomial model.
  • The p.4 display labels its integrated document likelihood p(theta|d), but it is not a posterior over parameters. Conditional independence given memberships is stronger than exchangeability alone. Shared latent proportions explain modeled dependence, not causal citation relationships or objectively true scientific disciplines.
  • Reported results concern a selected PNAS Biological Sciences 1997-2001 corpus and a chosen eight-aspect fit. Topic counts were compared informally, and aspect interpretations used high-probability words/references. Neither universal model-selection optimality nor recovery of a unique semantic partition follows.
  • An unweighted average of document loadings does not generally equal a pooled token-level percentage when document lengths differ. Source figure cross-references are inconsistent: use the actual Figure 1 on preprint p.9 and Table 4 on p.13. No source implementation or benchmark was executed.
