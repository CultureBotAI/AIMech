Centered Clipping and Worker Momentum for Byzantine-Robust Optimization

Algorithms AIMech:CenteredClippingAndWorkerMomentumForByzantineRobustOptimization DRAFT
Aliases: Centered Clipping (CC), Byzantine-Robust Worker Momentum with Centered Clipping, Learning from History for Byzantine Robust Optimization, Byzantine-Robust Momentum Variance Reduction (MVR)
A stateful Byzantine-robust distributed optimization framework combining: (1) local Worker Momentum m_{t,i} = (1 - alpha_t) m_{t-1,i} + alpha_t g_i(x_{t-1}) (or Momentum Variance Reduction, MVR) maintained across steps to average out stochastic gradient noise and defeat time-coupled adversaries; and (2) iterative Centered Clipping (CC), which aggregates worker momentum vectors {x_1,...,x_n} around a reference iterate v_0 by updating v_{l+1} = v_l + (1/n) sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - v_l) min(1, tau_l / ||x_i - v_l||_2) for l >= 0 with clipping radius tau_l, achieving (delta_max, c)-robust aggregation E||v_L - x_bar_G||^2 <= c * delta * sigma^2 in O(n d) time.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from memoryless middle-seeking aggregators such as Krum (AIMech:KrumAndMultiKrumByzantineTolerantGradientDescent), Coordinate-wise Median (AIMech:CoordinateWiseMedianAndTrimmedMeanByzantineRobustDistributedLearning), and RFA (AIMech:RfaRobustFederatedAggregationSmoothedWeiszfeldGeometricMedian), which Theorem I and Counterexamples 1-3 prove can fail to converge even with zero Byzantine workers (delta = 0) under asymmetric/bimodal stochastic gradient noise and suffer an Omega(delta * sigma^2 / mu^2) lower bound under time-coupled attacks (Theorem II), and from Bucketing (AIMech:BucketingResamplingForHeterogeneousByzantineRobustOptimization, which addresses inter-client data heterogeneity via random s-bucketing rather than intra-client stochastic noise via centered clipping).

Mechanism #r403-middle-seeker-counterexamples-and-memoryless-time-coupled-lower-bound
Section 3 (Counterexamples 1-3, Theorem I) proves that memoryless middle-seekers (CW Median, RFA, Krum) fail to converge even with delta = 0 corruption under asymmetric, bimodal, or imbalanced zero-mean noise, and Theorem II proves that any permutation-invariant memoryless algorithm incurs an Omega(delta * sigma^2 / mu^2) non-vanishing optimality gap against time-coupled Byzantine attacks.
  • supports Source: Learning from History for Byzantine Robust Optimization · Locator: Section 3 Failure of Existing Methods (Counterexamples 1-3, Theorem I) & Section 4 Lower Bounds for Memoryless Algorithms (Definition B, Theorem II), pp. 2-5
Mechanism #r403-centered-clipping-contraction-and-worker-momentum-convergence
Theorem IV and Corollary V prove that Centered Clipping contracts toward the true honest average and satisfies (delta_max = 0.1, c)-robustness E||x_hat - x_bar||^2 <= c * delta * rho^2 (matching the Omega(delta * rho^2) lower bound of Theorem III), while Theorem VI and Theorem VII prove that combining Centered Clipping with Worker Momentum or Momentum Variance Reduction (MVR) converges to a stationary point on nonconvex objectives at rates O((c * delta + 1/n)^{1/2} / T^{1/2}) and O((c * delta + 1/n)^{1/3} / T^{2/3}), respectively, driving asymptotic error to zero as T -> infinity.
  • supports Source: Learning from History for Byzantine Robust Optimization · Locator: Section 5 (Definition C, Theorems III-IV, Corollary V) and Section 6 (Theorems VI-VII), pp. 5-8
Limitation #r403-clipping-radius-tuning-and-delta-point-one-threshold-limitation
Theoretical contraction of Centered Clipping in Theorem IV requires the Byzantine fraction delta <= 0.1 (or bounded variance ratio) and requires setting the clipping threshold tau_l proportional to the honest variance and current distance to the honest mean.
  • supports Source: Learning from History for Byzantine Robust Optimization · Locator: Section 5.2 Analysis of Centered Clipping (Theorem IV, Corollary V), pp. 5-6
Application #r403-time-coupled-and-little-is-enough-cifar10-defense-application
Evaluated on distributed CIFAR-10 and MNIST training against time-coupled, Little-Is-Enough (ALIE), inner-product manipulation, and bit-flipping attacks, where Worker Momentum + Centered Clipping matches uncorrupted SGD accuracy while memoryless Krum, Median, and RFA stagnate.
  • supports Source: Learning from History for Byzantine Robust Optimization · Locator: Section 7 Experiments (Figures 1-3), pp. 7-9

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Theoretical contraction guarantees for Centered Clipping require delta <= 0.1 and an appropriately scaled clipping radius tau.
  • Requires stateful workers or synchronized momentum buffers across iterations.
