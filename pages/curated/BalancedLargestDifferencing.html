Balanced largest differencing method

Algorithms AIMech:BalancedLargestDifferencing DRAFT
Aliases: BLDM, Balanced LDM
A two-way number-partitioning heuristic that first differences disjoint adjacent pairs in the sorted original list, then repeatedly differences the two largest residuals, preserving subset cardinalities that differ by at most one.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #paired-initialization
Sort a finite nonempty multiset of nonnegative weights. Difference the first and second largest original weights, the third and fourth, and so on, carrying the smallest unpaired original weight when the input size is odd. Only this first round uses fixed adjacent pairs. Then use largest differencing on the resulting residual list, reordering after each pair, rather than continuing PDM’s whole-round processing.
  • supports Source: A complete anytime algorithm for balanced number partitioning · Locator: Section 2.1, pp. 3–4
Scope #cardinality-invariant
Each initial paired residual has signed effective cardinality difference 0, because its two original items are committed to opposite sides. An odd unpaired item contributes the only signed unit. Later residual differencing adds or subtracts these effective differences, giving final absolute cardinality difference 0 for even input size and 1 for odd size. This invariant includes zero-valued original items; it does not guarantee equal subset sums.
  • supports Source: A complete anytime algorithm for balanced number partitioning · Locator: Sections 2.1 and 2.3, pp. 4 and 6; direct invariant and indexed fixtures
Mechanism #largest-phase-and-reconstruction
For each largest-residual step with a>=b, replace the pair by a-b and combine their represented original groups with opposite orientations. Store merge history or memberships to reconstruct the final subsets. A zero residual is still a represented group. On the constructed list 200,100,90,80,70,61,50,42, the first round produces 100,10,9,8. BLDM continues to residue 73, whereas PDM’s additional paired rounds give 89; both outputs satisfy cardinality balance. This demonstrates distinct procedures, not universal BLDM dominance.
  • supports Source: A complete anytime algorithm for balanced number partitioning · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.3, pp. 3–6; constructed indexed comparison
Limitation #not-an-optimality-certificate
BLDM follows one sequence of pair differences and does not explore alternatives to the initial opposite-side commitments. On the constructed list 8,7,6,5,4 it returns balanced loads 16 and 14, while a balanced partition with loads 15 and 15 exists. A feasible heuristic result alone does not certify minimum residue; the separately named complete BLDM must explore discarded choices.
  • supports Source: A complete anytime algorithm for balanced number partitioning · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.3, pp. 4–7; constructed exhaustive balanced-assignment example
Scope #resource-cost
Initial sorting, one linear paired round and repeated largest-residual operations can be implemented in O(n log n) unit-cost comparison/arithmetic work, with O(n) storage for residuals and a merge tree. These bounds assume a suitable ordered or heap representation and exclude free arbitrary-precision arithmetic. The supplied indexed examples are not an author-code or resource-use replication.
  • supports Source: A complete anytime algorithm for balanced number partitioning · Locator: Section 2.1 complexity statement, p. 4; direct implementation interpretation
Scope #empirical-evidence-scope
The source studies its BLDM implementation on random integer inputs. Its high-precision heuristic experiment uses 2n-bit integers, rescales residues to the unit interval and averages 1000 instances per plotted point; the fitted expression is presented as a conjecture. Such measurements do not establish a universal finite-input error bound. The original Yakir proof cited by the source was not inspected, so no asymptotic expected-residue guarantee is adopted here.
