Mixed memory Markov model

Model Families AIMech:MixedMemoryMarkovModel DRAFT
A family of compact temporal probability models whose component transition distributions are convex mixtures of transition tables indexed by a single selected predecessor lag or component.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record covers the mixed-memory construction of Saul and Jordan, including higher-order scalar, multicomponent first-order and hidden-state extensions. Mixed memory means a latent selector chooses one predecessor for a component distribution. It does not mean external neural memory, a mixture selecting an entire HMM, independent factorial chains, or an unrestricted full higher-order transition table. EM and coordinate Viterbi are estimation/inference procedures, not aliases for the model. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a scalar k-order sequence, write p(i_t|i_(t-1),...,i_(t-k))=sum_mu psi(mu) a_mu(i_t|i_(t-mu)), with nonnegative selector weights summing to one and each transition table normalized over the current state. Given observed predecessor and current values, a selector responsibility is proportional to psi(mu) a_mu(i_t|i_(t-mu)); parameter re-estimation can use these expected selector-weighted counts. For a vector state I_t with components nu, the first-order multicomponent form is p(I_t|I_(t-1))=product_nu sum_mu psi_nu(mu) a_nu,mu(i_t^nu|i_(t-1)^mu). Thus current components are conditionally independent given the complete previous vector, despite possible marginal dependence. The hidden extension uses this transition and an emission p(J_t|I_t)=product_nu sum_mu phi_nu(mu) b_nu,mu(j_t^nu|i_t^mu). Fully observed parameter counts scale quadratically rather than exponentially in the number of components for fixed alphabet size; exact hidden posterior inference can still be exponential. The paper instead illustrates a coordinate Viterbi approximation that fixes other hidden chains while optimizing one chain path.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A convex sum of single-predecessor effects cannot represent every higher-order interaction; for example an unrestricted XOR dependence on two binary predecessors is not additive in this way. Compact parameter counts do not establish universal expressiveness.
  • The displayed vector transition explicitly assumes conditional independence of current components given the previous vector. The source’s broad wording about avoiding strong conditional-independence assumptions must not override its product factorization.
  • Selector weights are properties of a fitted parameterization, not uniquely identified causal influence or a generally valid measure of correlation. Initial histories and observed-window boundaries must be specified when counting transitions.
  • In the hidden extension, exact EM needs joint posterior expectations of selectors and hidden-state indicator products. Equations (20) and (22) display hidden indicators outside the expectation, which is not a general full-posterior EM formula; clamping a path changes the calculation. Equation (17) also reverses emission arguments relative to Equation (16).
  • Coordinate Viterbi improves or preserves the joint path score under an exact single-chain update, but need not reach global MAP or optimize the marginal observed likelihood. Ties and stopping rules require care.
  • The Bach example illustrates the authors’ approximate procedure. Figure 2 uses joint path log probability, not exact marginal likelihood. No accuracy, causal discovery, training reproduction or general scaling guarantee for hidden inference is curated.
