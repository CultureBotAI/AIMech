Iterative conditional estimation

Algorithms AIMech:IterativeConditionalEstimation DRAFT
Aliases: ICE
An incomplete-data parameter-estimation procedure that repeatedly replaces a chosen complete-data estimator by its conditional expectation given observed data under the current parameter estimate.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope ICE specifies expectation of an estimator, not maximization of an expected complete-data log likelihood as in EM. The estimator may be maximum likelihood, least squares or another supported complete-data estimator. Shared applications to hidden Markov models do not make ICE a model family, an ICM label optimizer or a synonym for every stochastic EM method. Its behavior depends on the selected estimator and parameterization. The short acronym is scoped to this estimation procedure. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Let observed data be y and hidden data h, and choose a complete-data estimator T(h,y) of a parameter theta. From a valid current theta_k, set theta_(k+1)=E_(theta_k)[T(H,y)|Y=y], when this expectation exists and remains admissible. A finite hidden space permits a posterior-weighted sum; otherwise average T over appropriate posterior samples as a Monte Carlo approximation. This average is taken after evaluating the estimator on each completed datum. It generally differs from applying a nonlinear estimator to expected sufficient statistics. With a complete-data maximum-likelihood estimator, ICE averages an argmax, whereas EM takes an argmax after averaging the log likelihood. For a regular full identifiable exponential family with a valid finite interior complete-data MLE and an invertible mean-parameter map, the expectation coordinate psi=E_theta[s(H,Y)] has complete-data estimator s(H,Y), so ICE and EM coincide in that coordinate. Nonlinear reparameterization need not preserve equality of their updates.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The complete-data estimator must be defined on posterior-supported completed data and have the required moments. Empty mixture components, boundary or infinite MLEs, constrained parameter spaces and nonidentifiability need explicit conventions; a conditional mean can leave a nonconvex parameter space.
  • Conditional expectation is the least-squares approximation to an integrable squared-error target under a fixed law. That fact does not prove decreasing true parameter error, observed-likelihood monotonicity or global convergence across ICE iterations with changing laws.
  • The Delmas equivalence uses the mean/expectation parameter psi, although the source calls it canonical; its natural parameter phi is distinct. The claim is not automatic for arbitrary coordinates, curved/constrained exponential families or boundary estimates. A nonlinear transformation does not generally commute with conditional expectation.
  • For Gaussian mixtures, expectation of a complete-data class mean or variance containing a random class count differs in general from a ratio of expected sufficient statistics. The usual Gaussian-parameter ICE update therefore cannot simply be replaced by an EM update without checking the conditions.
  • Monte Carlo approximation requires valid posterior sampling and appropriate convergence conditions. Correlated Gibbs samples, finite sample counts and apparently steady parameters do not by themselves certify exact expectations or convergence. A one-sample version can resemble stochastic EM for a specific complete-data estimator; it is not every stochastic-approximation EM algorithm.
  • The inspected examples in Delmas contain scaling/covariance notation issues, and its introductory suggestion that mixed discrete-continuous data make likelihood irrelevant is not adopted. The definition and qualified coordinate comparison do not certify all printed example formulas. Neither inspected paper provides a universal empirical performance guarantee; no fitting, benchmark or author-code run was performed.
