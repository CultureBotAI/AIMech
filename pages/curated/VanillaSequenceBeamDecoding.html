Vanilla sequence beam decoding

Algorithms AIMech:VanillaSequenceBeamDecoding DRAFT
Aliases: Vanilla beam decoding
A layer-based sequence beam decoder in which completed and unfinished hypotheses compete for the same k beam slots: finished hypotheses are carried forward unchanged, unfinished hypotheses are extended, and the top-scoring candidates form the next beam.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the vanilla sequence-decoding policy contrasted with FCFS by Kasai and colleagues. It specializes generic breadth-first beam search by specifying EOS retention and competition for active slots. Finished outputs are not accumulated in a separate permanent set, and the procedure is not the score-prioritized per-length best-first decoder. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #search-procedure
At each sequence length, construct a fresh candidate pool from carried-forward EOS-complete hypotheses and one-token extensions of unfinished beam members. Select up to k highest-scoring candidates for the next beam. The 2024 description stops when all retained hypotheses are complete, or at the maximum length. The 2020 standard-beam pseudocode explicitly resets the candidate pool at each layer and carries finished hypotheses unchanged.

  • supports Source: Best-First Beam Search · Locator: Algorithm 1 and footnotes, printed p. 797 / PDF p. 3
Mechanism #fresh-layer-pool
The standard sequence-beam algorithm initializes an empty candidate pool for each time step, carries EOS-complete hypotheses unchanged, extends unfinished hypotheses and selects the next top-k beam. This supplies an explicit layer-pool scope for the later vanilla-policy description.
  • supports Source: Best-First Beam Search · Locator: Algorithm 1 and footnotes, printed p. 797 / PDF p. 3

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A completed hypothesis can later fall off the beam because it competes with unfinished candidates. Its discovery alone does not reserve an output slot.
  • Finite beam pruning is not exact global MAP decoding. Different tie, length-score and maximum-length policies can change outputs.
  • At the length limit, an implementation must specify what to return if no retained hypothesis is EOS-complete; the simplified figures do not provide a universal failure policy.
  • Figure 2 in the 2024 paper omits initialization/reset of its temporary H pool. The fresh-per-layer interpretation is supported by the layer-based prose and the explicit reset in Meister et al. 2020 Algorithm 1; the record does not certify the 2024 figure as executable code.
  • The name identifies the source-defined policy, not a claim that all libraries or all uses of beam search implement it.
