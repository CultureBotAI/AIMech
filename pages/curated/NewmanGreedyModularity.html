Newman greedy modularity algorithm

Aliases: Newman fast community detection, Newman agglomerative modularity
Newman’s direct edge-fraction-matrix algorithm for greedy agglomerative community detection, which starts with singleton vertices, joins adjacent communities using modularity changes, updates the matrix by combining their rows and columns, and selects a maximum-modularity cut along the resulting merge history.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

7 Claims
Mechanism #edge-fraction-convention
Using the symmetric accounting convention in the source, each edge between different communities contributes half of its normalized weight to each orientation of the edge-fraction matrix, while an internal edge contributes wholly to its diagonal entry. Row sums give community degree fractions. Modularity is the sum of diagonal edge fractions minus squared row sums, and joining two groups changes it by twice their cross fraction minus twice the product of their row sums.
  • supports Source: Fast algorithm for detecting community structure in networks · Locator: Section II, Equation 1 and merge-gain expression, PDF pp. 1–2; reference note 19, p. 5
Mechanism #direct-matrix-agglomeration
The procedure evaluates adjacent community pairs and chooses the greatest modularity increase or smallest decrease, then combines the corresponding matrix rows and columns. The dendrogram records the joins; a best encountered cut is selected by modularity. Vertices in different connected components are not joined, as the paper explicitly notes. Sparse candidate restriction therefore does not imply that disconnected data acquire a single root.
  • supports Source: Fast algorithm for detecting community structure in networks · Locator: Section II, PDF p. 2; collaboration-network discussion, p. 3
Scope #cnm-and-runtime-boundary
The direct matrix-update method has the stated worst-case bound O((m+n)n), with m edges and n vertices; the quadratic specialization assumes sparsity. The later Clauset–Newman–Moore method retains the modularity objective and greedy merge structure while maintaining sparse modularity-change rows and maximum-tracking data structures. This record identifies the earlier matrix-update method, not every greedy modularity implementation or a new objective distinct from CNM.
  • supports Source: Fast algorithm for detecting community structure in networks · Locator: Section II, PDF p. 2, candidate scan and matrix-update runtime
  • supports Source: Finding community structure in very large networks · Locator: Section II, PDF pp. 2–4, comparison with Reference 32 and sparse data structures
Limitation #greedy-optimality-boundary
Maximal modularity along the greedy trajectory is not guaranteed to be maximal over all graph partitions. In an exact symmetric-fraction reference calculation on a six-vertex path with fixed lexicographic ties, the best greedy cut scores thirteen fiftieths, while the feasible partition into two consecutive triples scores three tenths. The calculation is a finite example and does not execute author software or identify every possible tie outcome.
  • supports Source: Fast algorithm for detecting community structure in networks · Locator: Section II, PDF pp. 1–2, exhaustive-search discussion and approximate greedy procedure; curator calculation in research/discovery/newman-greedy-boundaries.py
Scope #recursive-subgraph-boundary
The collaboration example reruns the method on selected communities to reveal finer structure. Such a rerun recalculates edge fractions and degree terms on the selected subgraph; it is not merely a different cut of one fixed full-network dendrogram. In the constructed path example, the gain for joining the same first two vertices is four twenty-fifths in the full six-vertex graph and one quarter in its induced three-vertex path.
  • supports Source: Fast algorithm for detecting community structure in networks · Locator: Section III, PDF pp. 3–5, recursive analysis and Figure 4; Equation 1; curator calculation in research/discovery/newman-greedy-boundaries.py
Scope #weighted-extension-boundary
The paper proposes a weighted generalization but evaluates unweighted networks. Interpreting the objective through normalized edge fractions preserves it under multiplication of all nonnegative edge weights by the same positive constant. Raw edge strengths without normalization are not interchangeable with those fractions. This record does not establish signed-weight conventions or performance for the untested weighted extension.
  • supports Source: Fast algorithm for detecting community structure in networks · Locator: Section II, PDF pp. 1–2, edge-fraction definition and weighted-extension paragraph; reference note 19; curator calculation in research/discovery/newman-greedy-boundaries.py
Scope #evaluation-scope
The reported accuracy and speed tradeoff depends on the test. The four-group synthetic benchmark compares recovery at varying external degree; the football example merges several known conferences, while the karate-club and collaboration examples provide other forms of comparison. The large collaboration analysis uses the largest connected component, and its multi-year Girvan–Newman timing comparison is an estimate. These cases do not establish universal recovery, a universal modularity significance threshold or a hardware-independent speed ratio.
  • supports Source: Fast algorithm for detecting community structure in networks · Locator: Section III, PDF pp. 2–5 and Figures 1–4; Section II significance discussion, p. 1

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The retained identity is the original direct matrix-update procedure in arXiv cond-mat/0309508v1, later cited by CNM. Its shared objective and merge structure are explicit; sparse cached-gain acceleration, edge-betweenness removal and hierarchical random-graph modeling have separate identities.
  • The symmetric edge accounting in reference note 19 is used consistently. Its alternative one-sided representation is not silently combined with symmetric row sums or the symmetric gain formula. Author source code and journal-version equations were not compared.
  • Positive total edge weight is needed for normalized fractions. Disconnected components remain separate under adjacent-only merging; ties, isolates and exceptional input handling require implementation conventions that were not audited.
  • The six-path example fixes one tie policy and shows a better feasible partition, not an exhaustive solution or a failure claim about every tie policy. The induced-subgraph example demonstrates changed scores, not a reproduced physicist-network hierarchy.
  • Power-law community-size observations are descriptive and distinguish maximum-modularity cuts from pooling every dendrogram level. Neither the reported 0.3 modularity heuristic nor empirical group interpretations supply a general significance test.
  • No author program, dataset, model training or paid service was executed. Constructed arithmetic is not benchmark replication. Source-version and input-domain limits remain explicit, and the record remains DRAFT.
