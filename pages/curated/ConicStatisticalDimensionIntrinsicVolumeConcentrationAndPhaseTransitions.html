Conic Statistical Dimension, Intrinsic Volume Concentration, and Approximate Kinematic Phase Transitions in Random Convex Programs

Mathematical Methods AIMech:ConicStatisticalDimensionIntrinsicVolumeConcentrationAndPhaseTransitions DRAFT
Aliases: Amelunxen-Lotz-McCoy-Tropp Conic Statistical Dimension and Phase Transitions, Statistical Dimension of Convex Cones and Approximate Conic Kinematic Formula, Conic Intrinsic Volume Concentration and Random Convex Optimization Phase Transitions, Descent Cone Subdifferential Recipe with Nonasymptotic Variance Error Bound
Conic Statistical Dimension, Intrinsic Volume Concentration, and Approximate Kinematic Phase Transitions in Random Convex Programs is a conic integral geometry and convex optimization framework established by Amelunxen, Lotz, McCoy, and Tropp (2014, Information and Inference: A Journal of the IMA 3(3):224-294, DOI:10.1093/imaiai/iau005, arXiv:1303.6672v2) that defines the statistical dimension $\delta(C) := \sum_{k=0}^d k v_k(C) = \mathbb{E}[\|\Pi_C(g)\|^2] = \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{dist}^2(g, C^\circ)]$ of a closed convex cone $C \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ as the mean of its conic intrinsic volume distribution $(v_0(C), \dots, v_d(C))$ and proves that $v_k(C)$ concentrates exponentially around $\delta(C)$ on scale $\omega(C) := \sqrt{\delta(C) \wedge \delta(C^\circ)}$ via $p_C(\lambda) = 4\exp\!\left(\frac{-\lambda^2/8}{\omega^2(C)+ \lambda}\right)$ (Theorem 6.1). Combining intrinsic-volume concentration with the exact conic kinematic and Crofton formulas $\mathbb{P}\{C \cap Q K \ne \{0\}\} = 2 h_{d+1}(C \times K)$ yields the Approximate Kinematic Formula (Theorem I and Theorem 7.1), which proves sharp two-sided phase transitions of width $O(\omega(C)) \le O(\sqrt{d})$ for regularized linear inverse problems at $m = \delta(\mathcal{D}(f, x_0))$ (Theorem II), convex demixing under a random orthogonal basis $U \in O(d)$ at $\delta(\mathcal{D}(f, x_0)) + \delta(\mathcal{D}(g, y_0)) = d$ (Theorem III), and random cone programs between unboundedness ($m \le \delta(C) - \lambda$) and infeasibility ($m \ge \delta(C) + \lambda$, Theorem 8.1), together with a nonasymptotic error bound $0 \le \inf_{\tau \ge 0} J(\tau; \partial f(x)) - \delta(\mathcal{D}(f, x)) \le \frac{2\sup\{\|s\| : s \in \partial f(x)\}}{f(x/\|x\|)}$ for the subdifferential recipe $J(\tau) = \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{dist}^2(g, \tau \partial f(x))]$ (Theorem 4.3).

Distinct from `AIMech:AtomicNormConvexGeometryAndTangentConeGaussianWidthForLinearInverseProblems` (Chandrasekaran, Recht, Parrilo, & Willsky 2012, FoCM 12(6):805-849), which establishes only a one-sided Gaussian recovery sufficiency bound $m \ge w(\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{A}}(x^*) \cap \mathbb{S}^{p-1})^2 + O(\sqrt{p})$ via Gordon's escape-through-a-mesh inequality and an upper-bound polarity recipe without a lower-bound tightness guarantee, whereas Amelunxen, Lotz, McCoy, & Tropp (2014) prove via the spherical Steiner formula and Gaussian log-Sobolev inequalities that conic intrinsic volumes concentrate around the statistical dimension $\delta(C)$ (Theorem 6.1), derive two-sided approximate kinematic bounds for Haar-random orthogonal rotations $Q \in O(d)$ (Theorems I and 7.1), establish universal two-sided phase transitions for linear inverse problems (Theorem II), convex demixing (Theorem III), and random cone programs (Theorem 8.1), prove the nonasymptotic error bound for the subdifferential recipe (Theorem 4.3), and compute exact Weyl-chamber harmonic number statistical dimensions $\delta(\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{P}(x), x)) = H_d$ for permutahedra (Propositions 3.5 and 9.1).

Mechanism #canonical-statistical-dimension-equivalent-characterizations-and-intrinsic-volume-concentration
For any closed convex cone $C \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ with conic intrinsic volumes $v_k(C)$ ($k = 0, \dots, d$ satisfying $\sum_{k=0}^d v_k(C) = 1$, polarity reversal $v_k(C) = v_{d-k}(C^\circ)$, and Gauss-Bonnet $\sum_{k\text{ even}} v_k(C) = \sum_{k\text{ odd}} v_k(C) = 1/2$ when $C$ is not a subspace, Fact 5.5, Eqs. 5.1-5.3), the spherical Steiner formula $\mathbb{P}\{\|\Pi_C(\theta)\|^2 \ge \varepsilon\} = \sum_{k=0}^d v_k(C) I_k^d(\varepsilon)$ (Fact 5.7, Eq. 5.6) implies that the intrinsic definition $\delta(C) := \sum_{k=0}^d k v_k(C)$ coincides with the Gaussian metric projection $\mathbb{E}[\|\Pi_C(g)\|^2] = d\mathbb{E}[\|\Pi_C(\theta)\|^2] = \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{dist}^2(g, C^\circ)] = \mathbb{E}[(\sup_{y \in C \cap \mathbb{B}^d} \langle y, g\rangle)^2]$ (Propositions 2.4, 3.1, and 5.12), satisfies complementarity $\delta(C) + \delta(C^\circ) = d$ (Eq. 3.7), direct-product additivity $\delta(C \times K) = \delta(C) + \delta(K)$ (Eq. 3.8), Gaussian-width sandwiching $w^2(C) \le \delta(C) \le w^2(C) + 1$ (Proposition 10.2, Eq. 10.1), and is the unique continuous, rotation-invariant, localizable valuation on closed convex cones extending $\delta(L) = \operatorname{dim}(L)$ (Section 5.6, p. 23). Moreover, with transition width $\omega(C) := \sqrt{\delta(C) \wedge \delta(C^\circ)}$ and $p_C(\lambda) := 4\exp\!\left(\frac{-\lambda^2/8}{\omega^2(C) + \lambda}\right)$, the tail functional $t_k(C) = \sum_{j=k}^d v_j(C)$ satisfies $t_{k_-}(C) \ge 1 - p_C(\lambda)$ for $k_- \le \delta(C) - \lambda + 1$ and $t_{k_+}(C) \le p_C(\lambda)$ for $k_+ \ge \delta(C) + \lambda$ (Theorem 6.1, Eqs. 6.1-6.3, Lemmas 6.2-6.3, Sublemma E.3).

Mechanism #approximate-conic-kinematic-formula-and-universal-phase-transitions-in-random-convex-programs
Combining the exact conic kinematic and Crofton formulas $\mathbb{P}\{C \cap Q K \ne \{0\}\} = 2 h_{d+1}(C \times K)$ and $\mathbb{P}\{C \cap Q L_{d-m} \ne \{0\}\} = 2 h_{m+1}(C)$ (Facts 2.1 and 5.10, Eqs. 5.9-5.10) with half-tail interlacing $2 h_k(C) \ge t_k(C) \ge 2 h_{k+1}(C)$ (Proposition 5.9, p. 22) and intrinsic-volume concentration yields the Approximate Kinematic Formula (Theorem 7.1, Eqs. 7.1-7.2; Theorem I, p. 5): for uniformly random $Q \in O(d)$ and $\lambda \ge 0$, $m \ge \delta(C) + \lambda \Rightarrow \mathbb{P}\{C \cap Q L_{d-m} \ne \{0\}\} \le p_C(\lambda)$, $m \le \delta(C) - \lambda \Rightarrow \mathbb{P}\{C \cap Q L_{d-m} \ne \{0\}\} \ge 1 - p_C(\lambda)$, and for any convex cone $K \subset \mathbb{R}^d$, $\delta(C) + \delta(K) \le d - 2\lambda \Rightarrow \mathbb{P}\{C \cap Q K \ne \{0\}\} \le p_C(\lambda) + p_K(\lambda)$ while $\delta(C) + \delta(K) \ge d + 2\lambda \Rightarrow \mathbb{P}\{C \cap Q K \ne \{0\}\} \ge 1 - (p_C(\lambda) + p_K(\lambda))$. This immediately proves sharp two-sided phase transitions with $a_\eta = \sqrt{8\log(4/\eta)}$ for: (1) regularized linear inverse problems $\min f(x) \text{ s.t. } A x = A x_0$ at $m = \delta(\mathcal{D}(f, x_0)) \pm a_\eta\sqrt{d}$ (Theorem II, p. 8); (2) convex demixing $\min f(x) \text{ s.t. } g(y) \le g(y_0),\, x + U y = x_0 + U y_0$ at $\delta(\mathcal{D}(f, x_0)) + \delta(\mathcal{D}(g, y_0)) = d \pm a_\eta\sqrt{d}$ (Theorem III, p. 11); and (3) random cone programs $\min \langle u, x\rangle \text{ s.t. } A x = b,\, x \in C$ between unboundedness ($m \le \delta(C) - \lambda$) and infeasibility ($m \ge \delta(C) + \lambda$) each with probability $\ge 1 - p_C(\lambda)$ (Theorem 8.1, p. 27).

Scope #subdifferential-descent-cone-recipe-nonasymptotic-error-bound-and-exact-cone-dimensions
For any proper convex function $f: \mathbb{R}^d \to \overline{\mathbb{R}}$ whose subdifferential $\partial f(x)$ is nonempty, compact, and does not contain $0$, the expected squared distance $J(\tau; \partial f(x)) := \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{dist}^2(g, \tau \partial f(x))]$ is strictly convex and differentiable on $\tau \ge 0$ with unique minimizer $\tau_*$ satisfying $\delta(\mathcal{D}(f, x)) \le \inf_{\tau \ge 0} J(\tau; \partial f(x))$ (Proposition 4.1, Recipe 4.1, Lemmas C.1-C.2, pp. 16-17, 37-40), and when $f$ is a norm on $\mathbb{R}^d$, the Gaussian Poincaré inequality yields the nonasymptotic reverse error bound $0 \le \left(\inf_{\tau \ge 0} J(\tau; \partial f(x))\right) - \delta(\mathcal{D}(f, x)) \le \frac{2\sup\{\|s\| : s \in \partial f(x)\}}{f(x/\|x\|)}$ (Theorem 4.3, Eq. 4.3, p. 17; Appendix C.2, pp. 40-41). Specializing yields the two-sided $\ell_1$ descent-cone bound $\psi(s/d) - \frac{2}{\sqrt{sd}} \le \frac{\delta(\mathcal{D}(\|\cdot\|_1, x))}{d} \le \psi(s/d)$ (Proposition 4.5, Eqs. 4.4-4.6), the Marčenko-Pastur Schatten 1-norm limit $\frac{\delta(\mathcal{D}(\|\cdot\|_{S_1}, X(r,m,n)))}{mn} \to \psi(\rho, \nu)$ (Proposition 4.7, Eqs. 4.7-4.9), and closed-form statistical dimensions for self-dual cones ($\delta(C) = d/2$, Proposition 3.2), $s$-saturated $\ell_\infty$ descent cones ($d - s/2$, Proposition 3.3), circular cones ($d\sin^2\alpha + O(1)$, Proposition 3.4), and Weyl-chamber permutahedron normal and descent cones $\delta(\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{P}(x), x)) = H_d = \sum_{i=1}^d i^{-1}$ and $\delta(\mathcal{D}(\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{P}(y_0)}, x_0)) = d - H_d$ (Propositions 3.5 and 9.1, Appendix D.4).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The exact conic kinematic formula and Theorem I/II/III/8.1 phase transition bounds require rotational invariance (Haar distribution on $O(d)$ or i.i.d. standard Gaussian matrices $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d}$) and noiseless affine constraints $z_0 = A x_0$ or $z_0 = x_0 + U y_0$.
  • For the $\ell_1$ descent cone at an $s$-sparse vector in $\mathbb{R}^d$, the Theorem 4.3 / Proposition 4.5 error estimate $\frac{2}{\sqrt{sd}}$ for the normalized statistical dimension $\delta(\mathcal{D}(\|\cdot\|_1, x))/d$ is tight when $s \ge \sqrt{d} + 1$ but becomes loose in the ultrasparse regime $s \le \sqrt{d}$ (Section 4.3, p. 18; Section 10.1.2, p. 32).
  • Primary-source typographical audit of `arXiv:1303.6672v2`: (1) Proposition 3.3 (p. 14) writes $\mathcal{D}(\|\cdot\|_\infty, x) = d - \frac{1}{2}s$ in the theorem display, omitting the statistical dimension symbol $\delta(\cdot)$ on the left-hand side (which is written as $\delta(\mathcal{D}(\|\cdot\|_\infty, x)) = d - \frac{1}{2}s$ in the proof sketch five lines below); (2) Figure 5.1 caption (p. 20, line 3) writes 'projection $\Pi_C(g)$ of a standard normal vector $g$ onto $K$' instead of 'onto $C$'; (3) Appendix D.4 Equation (D.12) text (p. 45) writes $\mathrm{H}_d := \sum_{k=1}^d i^{-1}$ with summation index $k$ and summand $i^{-1}$; and (4) Appendix F Proof of Proposition 10.2 (p. 49) writes $\mathbb{S}^d$ instead of $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ in line 3, $\mathbb{B}^{d-1}$ instead of $\mathbb{B}^d$ in the final three displays, and cites (3.3) instead of (3.5) in the concluding line.
