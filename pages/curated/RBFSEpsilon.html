RBFS-epsilon

Algorithms AIMech:RBFSEpsilon DRAFT
Aliases: RBFSϵ
A recursive best-first search variant that adds a finite nonnegative epsilon tolerance to the competitive expansion threshold, retaining the baseline regeneration and backup structure while using additional incumbent stopping conditions when a solution-quality certificate is required.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the 2015 additive competitive-threshold RBFS variant. A finite nonnegative epsilon changes continuation eligibility relative to the alternative-derived bound while retaining the baseline recursive organization. Its separately qualified incumbent certificate is not an unqualified first-goal guarantee. Ordinary RBFS, histogram-controlled RBFS_CR and the kth-root split are distinct procedure scopes; epsilon=0 is an algebraic comparison endpoint, not a global alias. The source-local RBFSϵ label is retained, with no generic epsilon-search alias. Later 2024 use of RBFS_CR for an epsilon relaxation remains a source terminology question. Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #additive-threshold-relaxation
The variant replaces the competitive comparison F(best)<=B in RBFS with F(best)<=B+epsilon. Interpreting epsilon as a finite nonnegative cost tolerance lets search continue within a subtree beyond the unrelaxed competing bound; this changes traversal eligibility rather than the static heuristic formula.
  • supports Source: Recursive Best-First Search with Bounded Overhead · Locator: Author PDF p.3 / official p.1153, RBFS-epsilon paragraph; Figure 1 on author p.2 / official p.1152
Mechanism #baseline-regeneration-and-backup
The proposed change is to the upper-bound comparison of the baseline procedure. Conditional inheritance of previously backed-up values, selection of the best two children, recursive competition with the alternative branch, and return-value backup retain their baseline roles. The explicit empty-successor return and finite-F guard remain relevant; the additive relaxation does not replace them.
  • supports Source: Recursive Best-First Search with Bounded Overhead · Locator: Author PDF p.2 / official p.1152, Figure 1 and explanation; author p.3 / official p.1153, first variant paragraph
Scope #incumbent-quality-stopping
The source requires continued branch-and-bound after finding an incumbent when optimality is required, and gives f(goal)<=w*B as a bounded-suboptimal stopping condition. Here that certificate is interpreted with a sound uninflated lower bound B, w>=1 and h(goal)=0 so f(goal) is the solution cost; the relaxed traversal threshold B+epsilon is not substituted for the certificate.
  • supports Source: Recursive Best-First Search with Bounded Overhead · Locator: Author PDF p.3 / official p.1153, RBFS-epsilon paragraph and quality stopping condition
Scope #parameter-and-overhead-scope
The paper identifies the useful epsilon choice as potentially domain- or instance-dependent and explicitly says this modification does not improve its theoretical O(N^2) total-expansion upper bound. This additive variant is presented separately from both the multiplicative kth-root split and the later histogram-controlled RBFS_CR method.
  • supports Source: Recursive Best-First Search with Bounded Overhead · Locator: Author PDF p.3 / official p.1153, first two variant paragraphs and RBFS_CR subsection

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The variant is specified as a modification of baseline Figure 1, not a complete separate executable listing. Root/caller initialization, tie handling, numeric infinities and complete incumbent-management conventions require explicit implementation decisions; the finite-F guard and empty-successor return must not be silently dropped.
  • A first goal reached under an additive relaxed threshold need not be optimal. The source’s incumbent certificate requires a sound uninflated competitive lower bound; arbitrary weighted or relaxed values cannot be reused as that lower bound without justification. An unqualified first-goal or optimality guarantee is not claimed.
  • Finite epsilon>=0 is the bounded interpretation used here; negative or infinite tolerance behavior is not certified. Epsilon has the units of f and is not itself a multiplicative quality factor. At epsilon=0 the upper-bound comparison reduces algebraically to the baseline comparison, without proving that all unspecified implementation choices coincide.
  • No automatic parameter-selection rule or universal reduction in work follows. Larger epsilon can change ordering and the quality-stopping work, and the source retains an O(N^2) expansion bound. The conditional O(N) histogram-overhead theorem and its additional assumptions concern RBFS_CR, not this variant.
  • Depth-linear path/sibling storage presumes bounded branching and node data; optional transposition tables have separate costs. The source description does not establish completeness or termination for arbitrary cyclic, infinite, negative-cost or unsolvable graph inputs.
  • Reported performance depends on domain, heuristic, implementation and tolerance. The square-root puzzle’s epsilon=16 is a particular setting, and Dockyard uses an unlimited transposition table. Historical Java/hardware results and the anytime RBFS-versus-RBFS_CR curves are not portable speedups or evidence about this variant’s total memory; no experiment is reproduced here.
  • The author and official 2015 PDFs are two byte artifacts of one work with matching normalized page bodies, not independent studies. The later 2024 ILBFS source labels an epsilon relaxation RBFS_CR, whereas the defining 2015 source separates them. This terminology difference and the retained source/version/code questions do not justify merging their identities.
