Subsampled Randomized Hadamard Transform Subspace Embedding

Aliases: SRHT Oblivious Subspace Embedding, Subsampled Randomized Walsh-Hadamard Transform (SRHT), Tropp Matrix-Chernoff SRHT Subspace Embedding
Subsampled Randomized Hadamard Transform Subspace Embedding is a structured fast oblivious dimension-reduction operator Phi = sqrt(n / l) R H D in R^{l x n} (for n = 2^q) analyzed via matrix Chernoff bounds by Tropp (2011) that embeds any fixed k-dimensional subspace of R^n spanned by an n x k orthonormal basis matrix V (V^T V = I_k) into R^l in O(n k log l) arithmetic operations instead of the O(n l k) cost of dense Gaussian projections. Here D in R^{n x n} is a diagonal Rademacher sign matrix with i.i.d. +-1 diagonal entries, H in R^{n x n} is the normalized Walsh-Hadamard matrix with entries +-1/sqrt(n), and R in R^{l x n} uniformly samples l rows without replacement. The proof decouples into two stages: (1) Lemma 3.3 uses availability of convex Lipschitz concentration for Rademacher series (Lemma 2.1) to prove that W = H D V equalizes row norms (leverage scores) across all n rows so that max_{j=1..n} ||e_j^T (H D V)||_2 <= sqrt(k / n) + sqrt(8 log(beta n) / n) with probability at least 1 - 1/beta, and (2) Lemma 3.4 applies the matrix Chernoff bound for sampling without replacement (Lemma 2.2) to the rank-1 decomposition X = sum_{j in T} x_j x_j^T of R W to guarantee that l = Omega((k + log n) log k) sampled rows preserve all k singular values of Phi V within [sqrt(1 - delta), sqrt(1 + delta)] (Theorem 3.1).

Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #claim-01
For any n x k matrix V with orthonormal columns (V^T V = I_k), Lemma 3.3 proves by applying Rademacher convex Lipschitz concentration (Lemma 2.1) to the Euclidean norm f(epsilon) = ||e_j^T H diag(epsilon) V||_2 (which has mean <= sqrt(k/n) and Lipschitz constant <= 1/sqrt(n)) that the randomized Walsh-Hadamard transform W = H D V flattens all n row norms simultaneously to satisfy max_{j=1..n} ||e_j^T (H D V)||_2 <= sqrt(k/n) + sqrt(8 log(beta n) / n) with probability at least 1 - 1/beta.
  • supports Source: Improved Analysis of the Subsampled Randomized Hadamard Transform · Locator: Section 2.1, Lemma 2.1, p. 3; Section 3.1, Lemma 3.3 and Proof, pp. 5-6
  • supports Source: Improved Analysis of the Subsampled Randomized Hadamard Transform (Crossref metadata) · Locator: Crossref metadata for Advances in Adaptive Data Analysis 3(1-2):115-126
Scope #claim-02
Combining Lemma 3.3 with the matrix Chernoff bound for uniform row sampling without replacement (Lemma 2.2 / Corollary 2.3 and Lemma 3.4), Theorems 1.1 and 3.1 establish that for embedding dimension l >= 4 [sqrt(k) + sqrt(8 log(beta n))]^2 log(beta k) = Omega((k + log n) log k), the SRHT matrix Phi = sqrt(n/l) R H D preserves all k singular values of Phi V within [1/sqrt(2), sqrt(3/2)] (or more generally [sqrt(1 - delta), sqrt(1 + delta)]) with failure probability at most O(1/beta).
  • supports Source: Improved Analysis of the Subsampled Randomized Hadamard Transform · Locator: Section 1, Theorem 1.1, p. 2; Section 3.1-3.2, Theorem 3.1, Lemma 3.4, and Proof of Theorem 3.1, pp. 5-7
Limitation #claim-03
Section 3.3 demonstrates via a block-Hadamard adversarial basis V that the logarithmic factor k log k in the embedding dimension l cannot be eliminated because uniform row sampling must solve a k-bin coupon collector problem to preserve rank(Phi V) = k, while the additive log(n) log(k) term is required to prevent maximal row-norm spikes.
  • supports Source: Improved Analysis of the Subsampled Randomized Hadamard Transform · Locator: Section 3.3, Optimality of Theorem 3.1, pp. 7-8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Requires padding the ambient dimension n to a power of 2 for the Walsh-Hadamard transform and incurs an unavoidable O((k + log n) log k) logarithmic oversampling factor relative to dense Gaussian embeddings.
