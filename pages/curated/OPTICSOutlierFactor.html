OPTICS outlier factor

Algorithms AIMech:OPTICSOutlierFactor DRAFT
Aliases: OPTICS-OF, OPTICS with outlier factors
An unsupervised local outlier-scoring procedure that uses self-inclusive, tie-inclusive nearest neighborhoods to compute inverse mean neighbor-radius reachability and averages neighbor-to-query density ratios.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes OPTICS-OF with the self-inclusive convention corroborated by the inspected ELKI implementation. It is distinct from OPTICS ordering, DBSCAN noise labels, original self-excluded LOF, neighbor-label voting and raw distance ranking. Mechanism #self-inclusive-locality
For a finite reference dataset D of n observations, a finite metric and 2<=m<=n, core_m(p) is the mth ordered distance to observations in D, including p itself. N_m(p) retains every observation at distance at most that boundary, including all ties. The inspected exact linear scans and heap include the indexed query and boundary ties; a fixed-count truncation is not the same convention. The m range describes the well-defined distinct-point metric case, not a claim that every constructor enforces it.

Mechanism #reachability-and-density-ratios
For o in N_m(p), reach_m(p,o)=max(core_m(o),d(p,o)), using the neighbor radius. Set lrd_m(p)=|N_m(p)| divided by the sum of these reaches, and OF_m(p) to the mean of lrd_m(o)/lrd_m(p) over N_m(p). Thus self reachability is core_m(p), not zero, and the self term contributes a ratio of one when defined. The formulas require positive finite mean reaches for all densities used. These inverse-distance quantities are not normalized probability densities, and the resulting relative score is not an outlier probability or an automatic binary decision.
  • supports Source: ELKI OPTICSOF-0.8.0.java · Locator: OPTICSOF.run, passes 1-3
Mechanism #standalone-three-pass-computation
The inspected implementation first stores neighborhoods and core radii, then computes all local reachability densities, then averages their neighbor-to-query ratios. It does not run an OPTICS ordering or extract a clustering. Pairwise reachability in the score must not be replaced with a minimum reachability accumulated during an OPTICS traversal.
  • supports Source: ELKI OPTICSOF-0.8.0.java · Locator: OPTICSOF.run, passes 1-3
  • context Source: OPTICS-OF: Identifying Local Outliers · Locator: Parsed Definitions 4-7 and computational discussion, PDF pp5-6 and8; not visually verified
Scope #distinct-from-self-excluded-lof
OPTICS-OF and original LOF are retained separately because including the query changes both density and score averaging. Under absolute distance, D={0,1,3} gives OPTICS-OF scores {1,1,3/2} at m=2, whereas self-excluded LOF gives {1,1,2} at k=1, despite identical core radii. At m=3, OPTICS-OF gives {1,1,1}, while LOF at k=2 gives {11/12,6/5,11/12}. These exact curator calculations rule out identifying the two procedures merely by shifting the neighbor-count parameter.
  • supports Source: ELKI OPTICSOF-0.8.0.java · Locator: OPTICSOF.run, passes 1-3
  • supports Source: LOF: Identifying Density-Based Local Outliers · Locator: Definitions 3-7, printed/PDF pp3-4; exact comparisons in research/discovery/optics-outlier-factor-boundaries.py
Limitation #ties-and-scale-boundaries
Boundary ties can change scores: for D={0,2,4,5} and m=2, point 2 has three retained neighbors and score 4/3; selecting only itself and neighbor 0 instead gives score 1. Positive uniform scaling of every distance cancels from defined density ratios, but feature-specific scaling can change neighborhoods. When m=n, every neighborhood is D and reach_m(p,o)=core_m(o), so all densities are equal and all defined scores are one. A score of one therefore does not by itself establish domain normality.
  • supports Source: ELKI OPTICSOF-0.8.0.java · Locator: OPTICSOF.run, passes 1-3
  • supports Source: ELKI DoubleIntegerDBIDKNNHeap.java · Locator: Boundary-tie retention; exact curator calculations in research/discovery/optics-outlier-factor-boundaries.py
Limitation #zero-distance-and-parameter-limits
With m=1 and a unique query location, the self-only neighborhood has zero mean reachability; duplicates can also produce zero means at larger m. The mathematical ratios are then undefined. The inspected parameterizer requires m>1 and defaults to Euclidean distance, but its constructor does not enforce m<=n. KNNList may report an infinite boundary when fewer than m neighbors exist. The scoring loop adds no epsilon; its lrd>0 guard does not resolve infinity/infinity from coincident neighborhoods. A fallback zero or nonfinite output outside the stated finite domain is not evidence of normality.

Limitation #implementation-and-computation-scope
The evidence establishes the inspected release source and exact linear-query/tie contracts, not every index backend, historical implementation or runtime result. Stored all-ties neighborhoods can contain every observation even for small m, requiring quadratic neighbor incidences. All-pairs neighbor search can require quadratic distance evaluations, and selection/index costs need separate analysis; no universal near-linear or n-log-n complexity follows. The historical paper text and versioned test fixture provide context, but no figure-derived claim, numerical performance advantage, library benchmark or probability calibration is curated.
