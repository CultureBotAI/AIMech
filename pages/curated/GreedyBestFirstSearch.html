Greedy best-first search

Algorithms AIMech:GreedyBestFirstSearch DRAFT
Aliases: GBFS
A best-first search algorithm that prioritizes open nodes by a heuristic estimate of remaining cost to a goal, without adding the cost already incurred to that priority.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The identity is heuristic-only global best-first selection, not every algorithm described as greedy. Hill climbing and beam search restrict the available candidates differently. The accumulated cost may still be stored for reconstructing or measuring a solution; it is omitted from the primary priority key. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #search-procedure
Select a node with minimum heuristic value h from the open frontier, expand it, and place eligible successors on that frontier until a selected goal or another stopping condition is reached. The cost-to-date g does not contribute to this heuristic-only primary ranking.
  • supports Source: A Comparison of Greedy Search Algorithms · Locator: Best-First Search: Greedy best-first search and Ignoring Duplicates, PDF p. 2; family distinctions on PDF p. 1
Limitation #speed-qualification
Heuristic-only ordering is the greedy endpoint discussed in weighted-search studies. The cited study cautions that moving toward this endpoint need not reduce search effort; its empirical explanations are not universal runtime guarantees.
  • supports Source: When Does Weighted A* Fail? · Locator: Abstract and Introduction, PDF p. 1; Discussion and Conclusion, PDF p. 8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The priority rule supplies no user-chosen fixed bound on solution suboptimality. Any post-hoc bound requires additional lower-bound information and assumptions.
  • Tie-breaking and duplicate handling remain consequential implementation choices. Completeness is not asserted for arbitrary infinite spaces or memory-pruned variants.
  • A strong cost-to-go heuristic does not by itself establish universally fast search; no empirical correlation threshold is treated as a general theorem.
