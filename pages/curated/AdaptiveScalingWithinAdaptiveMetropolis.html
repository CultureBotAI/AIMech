Adaptive scaling within adaptive Metropolis

Algorithms AIMech:AdaptiveScalingWithinAdaptiveMetropolis DRAFT
Aliases: ASWAM
An adaptive Metropolis algorithm that combines acceptance-rate-driven scalar proposal scaling with recursive mean and covariance-shape adaptation from retained samples.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record gives the bounded mean-and-shape formulation of Vihola Definition 5. Its log scale is unbounded, while the proposed mean/shape pair is accepted only inside a specified compact constraint set. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Choose alpha_star in (0,1) and initialize x with pi(x)>0, mu=x and feasible positive-definite Sigma. Propose Y=x+exp(S)Sigma^(1/2)W with spherical W, use alpha=min(1,pi(Y)/pi(x)), and retain X_new after the accept/reject test. Update S_new=S+eta_n(alpha−alpha_star). With the old mean mu and shape Sigma, form mu_hat=(1−eta_n)mu+eta_n X_new and Sigma_hat=(1−eta_n)Sigma+eta_n(X_new−mu)(X_new−mu)^T. For fixed zeta≥1, accept the entire proposed mean/shape pair only if ||mu_hat||≤zeta and every eigenvalue of Sigma_hat lies in [zeta^−1,zeta]; otherwise keep both old mu and old Sigma. Start with a feasible mean/shape pair and keep the scalar update separately. Use eta_n in (0,1), with divergent sum and finite squared sum under the source assumptions.
  • supports Source: On the stability and ergodicity of adaptive scaling Metropolis algorithms · Locator: Section 2, Definitions 4–5 and constraint set S_zeta, PDF pp. 4–5; Theorems 7 and 9, Remarks 11–13, pp. 5–7

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The covariance update uses the old mean and the retained new state, including repeated states after rejection. Substituting the new mean or only accepted proposals changes the specified recursion.
  • The mean and shape constraints are part of this source’s proved formulation; only the scalar scale lacks a fixed bound. Omitting constraints in practice does not inherit the same theorem.
  • The source’s strong-law results restrict alpha_star to (0,1/2) and require the corresponding target/proposal regularity conditions. Algorithm definition alone does not prove ergodicity.
  • Shape learning is a recursive stochastic-approximation update, not exactly the original unbiased full-history covariance at every finite iteration.
  • No universal efficiency advantage, marginal-distribution convergence or executed benchmark is asserted.
