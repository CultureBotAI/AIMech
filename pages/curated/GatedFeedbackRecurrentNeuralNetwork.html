Gated-feedback recurrent neural network

Aliases: GF-RNN
A layered recurrent architecture in which learned input-dependent scalar gates modulate recurrent projections from every previous layer state to each destination layer, including delayed top-down feedback, alongside the current bottom-up computation.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #global-layer-pair-gate
The displayed gate for source layer i and destination j is g_t^(i->j)=sigmoid(w_g^(i->j)*h_t^(j-1)+u_g^(i->j)*h*_(t-1)), where h* concatenates all previous layer states and the current input x_t replaces h_t^(j-1) at the first layer. Each gate is a scalar controlling a whole source-to-destination recurrent projection, rather than a separate gate for each coordinate of the destination state. The tanh form adds sum_i g_t^(i->j)*U^(i->j)*h_(t-1)^i to the current bottom-up input before applying tanh.
  • supports Source: Gated Feedback Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 3 global reset gate and Section 3.1 tanh update, PDF p. 4
Scope #delayed-feedback-and-order
The top-down recurrent signals in the displayed architecture come from time t-1, while bottom-up input uses the already computed current lower-layer state. Thus the displayed update can be evaluated bottom-to-top within a timestep; it does not require solving an implicit same-timestep top-down cycle. The fully connected recurrent layer-pair form has L-squared possible projections for L layers. The paper figure omits skip connections for clarity, so the illustration is not treated as an exhaustive implementation specification.
  • supports Source: Gated Feedback Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 3 and Figure 1 caption, PDF pp. 3–4; curator dependency/count consequence
Mechanism #placement-in-lstm-and-gru
For LSTM and GRU versions, the source applies global layer-pair gates when computing candidate new content. It explicitly leaves the ordinary LSTM input, forget and output gate equations and GRU reset and update gate equations unmodified. In its displayed GRU convention, the destination reset vector multiplies the sum of globally gated recurrent projections after projection and aggregation. Global pair gates therefore do not replace unit-wise gates or directly gate every cell-state carry path.
  • supports Source: Gated Feedback Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 3.1 Long Short-Term Memory and Gated Recurrent Unit, PDF p. 4; referenced background Equations 2–10, pp. 2–3
Limitation #soft-connectivity-and-gradient-limits
Finite real sigmoid logits give independent pair weights strictly between zero and one; their weights need not sum to one and need not be exactly zero. This is adaptive weighting of recurrent signals, not a guaranteed discrete routing or clock schedule. Unlike the cited clockwork design, the proposed model does not assign explicit fixed rates to its modules. Gates bounded by one do not bound learned projection matrices or the full transition Jacobian, which also includes derivatives through the gates and current lower layers; no automatic contraction or permanent gradient preservation follows.
  • supports Source: Gated Feedback Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 3, PDF pp. 3–4; curator gate and derivative consequences
Limitation #unit-dependent-language-evidence
The reported character-language experiments improve the selected GRU and LSTM comparisons but deteriorate with tanh units. Experiments distinguish roughly matched parameter counts from larger variants matched by hidden-unit counts; these are different capacity comparisons. A fixed-global-gate ablation, validation learning-time curves and a larger language model provide conditional evidence rather than universal superiority or lower computational complexity. The paper explicitly notes that one earlier large-model comparator uses a different vocabulary and is not directly comparable.
  • supports Source: Gated Feedback Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Sections 4.2 and 5.1, Tables 1–4 and Figure 2, PDF pp. 5–7; conclusion, p. 8
Limitation #program-task-and-metric
The program experiment trains an encoder-decoder on generated Python-script input/output sequences with specified nesting, output lengths, curriculum and capacities. Its stated accuracy is next-symbol prediction conditioned on correct preceding output symbols, rather than a guarantee of exact autonomous program execution. Figure 3 mostly favors gated feedback but includes a GRU test setting with lower accuracy. The record preserves those selected-task and metric boundaries and makes no numerical benchmark or independent reproduction claim.
  • supports Source: Gated Feedback Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 4.1.2, PDF p. 5; Section 4.2.2, p. 6; Section 5.2 and Figure 3, pp. 7–8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Global layer-pair scalar gates and ordinary unit-wise LSTM/GRU gates have distinct placement and scope in the displayed formulation.
  • Data-dependent soft weighting does not imply hard routing, normalized attention, a fixed module clock, sparse execution or guaranteed separation of timescales.
  • Learned matrices and gate derivatives prevent a contraction or gradient-preservation guarantee based solely on gate values.
  • Tanh deterioration and the negative GRU program-test comparison are retained alongside the reported improvements; capacities, metrics and vocabulary affect comparisons.
  • All nine primary conference-paper pages were read. No author implementation, supplement, training, runtime audit or empirical replication was inspected.
  • Displayed equations and a figure that omits skip connections do not certify every implementation detail. Constructed scalar checks are not trained-model evidence.
