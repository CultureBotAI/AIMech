Maximum candidates per node in sequence beam pruning

Algorithms AIMech:PerNodeCandidateBeamPruning DRAFT
A sequence-beam pruning procedure that limits how many retained candidate extensions may come from the same predecessor hypothesis, discarding a candidate when the allowed number of better-scoring siblings is already retained.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the Maximum Candidates per Node rule of Freitag and Al-Onaizan. Same history means the common predecessor prefix, not arbitrary semantic similarity between full sequences. It differs from an additive diversity penalty, grouping beams into separate searches or imposing a global frontier capacity. Mechanism #method
Apply the current beam-size cap, then keep at most m candidate continuations for each predecessor prefix by score. A candidate is discarded once m better-scoring candidates with that same predecessor are in the retained list. This is extra pruning after the initial top-beam selection; it does not specify refilling with candidates outside that initial list.
  • supports Source: Beam Search Strategies for Neural Machine Translation · Locator: Section 4, introductory paragraph and Maximum Candidates per Node, printed p. 57 / PDF p. 2
Limitation #diversity-limit
The source introduced a per-history candidate cap to diversify predecessor coverage, but its conclusion states that adding diversity did not improve translation quality in the evaluated settings.
  • supports Source: Beam Search Strategies for Neural Machine Translation · Locator: Introduction and Section 6, printed pp. 56 and 58 / PDF pp. 1 and 3

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A positive integer per-parent cap and deterministic tie handling must be specified. The source’s prose refers to better-scoring candidates without a complete tie policy.
  • Limiting sibling extensions can remove the prefix of the best eventual sequence; this is not an exact or complete search guarantee.
  • More varied parent prefixes do not guarantee semantic diversity or improved translation quality. The paper’s conclusion reports no translation-quality improvement from this diversity intervention.
  • The inherited decoder determines EOS processing, generation length and changing active-beam width. The pruning rule alone is not a complete decoder implementation.
  • No specific current library implementation, numeric quality gain or universal runtime benefit is asserted.
