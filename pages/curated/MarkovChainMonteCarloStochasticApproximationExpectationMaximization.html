Markov Chain Monte Carlo Stochastic Approximation Expectation Maximization

Algorithms AIMech:MarkovChainMonteCarloStochasticApproximationExpectationMaximization DRAFT
Aliases: MCMC-SAEM, SAEM-MCMC, Markovian SAEM
A stochastic approximation EM extension that advances a persistent latent-state Markov chain targeting the conditional distribution at the current parameter, updates an averaged sufficient statistic from that state, and maximizes the resulting complete-data objective.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope MCMC-SAEM replaces exact conditional latent draws in base SAEM by transitions from a carried state, often a fixed number of Metropolis-Hastings steps per parameter update. This differs from running an independent full equilibrium sampler at every iteration, fresh-batch MCEM and posterior sampling of the parameter itself. Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #persistent-markov-step
From the retained latent state z_(k-1), apply a transition Pi_(theta_k) with invariant target p(z|y;theta_k), obtaining z_k. Use it in s_k=s_(k-1)+gamma_k(S(y,z_k)-s_(k-1)), then set theta_(k+1)=theta_hat(s_k). The kernel can comprise M Metropolis-Hastings steps. Retain rejected states and accepted updates; restart or discarded-rejection variants define different simulation schemes.

  • context Source: Convergence of a stochastic approximation version of the EM algorithm · Locator: Section 6 Eq30 and exact conditional-draw assumption, pp.107-109 / PDF14-16; baseline statistic recursion and simulation assumption replaced by the 2004 Markovian extension
Scope #proposal-and-equilibration
Metropolis-Hastings acceptance includes the reverse-to-forward proposal ratio. When an independence proposal is the current latent prior, prior factors cancel and the observed conditional likelihood ratio remains. Invariance of the target does not mean one or finitely many transitions from an arbitrary state produce that target distribution. The finite two-state example preserves its target yet has a different first-step distribution and nonzero conditional sampling error.
  • supports Source: Coupling a stochastic approximation version of EM with an MCMC procedure · Locator: Section 2.2 Eq5-6, p.119 / PDF5; Section 3 discussion of Markovian noise, p.120 / PDF6; finite kernel calculation in discovery ledger
Limitation #conditional-convergence-scope
The published analysis imposes the curved-exponential model and smooth maximizer assumptions, stochastic-approximation step sizes, compact latent states, bounded sufficient statistics, parameter regularity of transitions, uniform ergodicity and compact statistic iterates. Its stationary-set conclusion is conditional on these hypotheses. A fixed number of arbitrary MCMC moves, irreducibility alone or bounded latent support alone does not supply them.
  • supports Source: Coupling a stochastic approximation version of EM with an MCMC procedure · Locator: Section 3 SAEM3-prime and Theorem 1, pp.120-123 / PDF6-9; Appendix pp.129-130 / PDF15-16
Limitation #source-proof-boundaries
The stated Lemma 1 uses exponents 1/alpha for alpha in (0,1), whereas its appendix derives Holder exponents below one. The subsequent lambda=1 conclusion does not directly follow from the displayed weaker estimate. The beta deconvolution example also has log(z) and log(1-z) statistics unbounded near the endpoints, despite bounded latent support. These discrepancies limit what is certified from the printed proof and example; the record does not supply a repaired general convergence proof.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A target-invariant transition can mix poorly or be periodic. A finite irreducible periodic flip chain demonstrates why irreducibility alone does not imply convergence to equilibrium.
  • Markovian simulation errors are not generally conditionally mean-zero at each iteration. The exact-independent-draw martingale argument cannot be transplanted without the additional Markovian-noise analysis.
  • The theorem’s bounded-statistic condition is separate from compact support. Truncating a latent law may change the statistical model and requires an explicit choice; no automatic equivalence to an unbounded original model is asserted.
  • The deconvolution coordinate pseudocode ambiguously refers to old coordinates within its sweep, and its stated uniform beta initialization is inconsistent with ordinary shape parameters. The record specifies persistent transitions and does not reproduce those ambiguous implementation details.
  • The source’s simulations can reach different local solutions. Its truth-centered empirical squared-error matrix is not generally covariance about the estimator mean when bias is present; no finite-sample interval coverage or universal speed guarantee is asserted.
  • All 52 pages of the two primary papers were read. Finite kernel and algebra checks are curator examples, not source software, a historical model fit or independent validation of the general proof.
