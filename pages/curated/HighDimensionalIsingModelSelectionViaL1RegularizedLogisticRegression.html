High-Dimensional Ising Model Selection via L1-Regularized Logistic Regression

Mathematical Methods AIMech:HighDimensionalIsingModelSelectionViaL1RegularizedLogisticRegression DRAFT
Aliases: High-Dimensional Ising Model Selection Using l_1-Regularized Logistic Regression (Ravikumar, Wainwright, & Lafferty 2010), High-Dimensional Graphical Model Selection Using l_1-Regularized Logistic Regression, Nodewise L1-Regularized Logistic Regression Pseudo-Likelihood for Ising Markov Random Field Structure Recovery
High-Dimensional Ising Model Selection via L1-Regularized Logistic Regression is a nodewise convex pseudo-likelihood graphical model structure learning method and primal-dual witness sparsistency framework introduced by Ravikumar, Wainwright, and Lafferty (2010, Annals of Statistics 38(3):1287-1319, arXiv:0804.4202v1, DOI:10.1214/09-AOS691) for recovering the signed edge set E*_{st} = sign(theta*_{st}) of a p-vertex binary Ising Markov random field P_{theta*}(x) = (1/Z(theta*)) exp(sum_{(s,t) in E} theta*_{st} x_s x_t) on {-1, +1}^p with maximum degree d without computing the #P-hard partition function Z(theta*). Exploiting the logistic conditional distribution P_{theta*}(x_r | x_{\r}) = 1 / (1 + exp(-2 x_r sum_{t in V \ r} theta*_{rt} x_t)) (Equation 4), the method solves for each node r in V the convex program min_{theta_{\r} in R^{p-1}} {ell(theta; {x^{(i)}}) + lambda_n ||theta_{\r}||_1} (Equations 5-7) and estimates the signed neighborhood N_hat_pm(r) = {sign(theta_hat_{ru}) u | u in V \ r, theta_hat_{ru} != 0} (Equation 8). Under dependency conditions Lambda_min(Q*_{SS}) >= C_min > 0, Lambda_max(E_{theta*}[X_{\r} X_{\r}^T]) <= D_max (Equation 12) and mutual incoherence |||Q*_{S^c S} (Q*_{SS})^{-1}|||_infty <= 1 - alpha (Equation 13) on the population Fisher information matrix Q*_r = E_{theta*}[eta(X; theta*) X_{\r} X_{\r}^T] with conditional variance weight eta(u; theta) = 4 exp(2 u_r sum_{t != r} theta_{rt} u_t) / (exp(2 u_r sum_{t != r} theta_{rt} u_t) + 1)^2 (Equations 9-11), Theorem 1 (Equations 15-16) proves simultaneous signed graph recovery across all p nodes with sample complexity n = Omega(d^3 log p) and theta*_min = Omega(1/d) for lambda_n = 2 sqrt((log p)/n), whereas Proposition 1 (Equation 26) proves that n = Omega(d^2 log p) and theta*_min = Omega(1/sqrt(d)) suffice when Assumptions A1-A2 hold directly on the sample Fisher matrix Q^n.

3 Claims
Mechanism #nodewise-l1-logistic-pseudo-likelihood-and-primal-dual-witness-shared-sparsity
For a binary Ising Markov random field P_{theta*}(x) = (1/Z(theta*)) exp(sum_{(s,t) in E} theta*_{st} x_s x_t) on {-1, +1}^p (Equation 1), the conditional distribution of node X_r given X_{\r} takes the logistic form P_{theta*}(x_r | x_{\r}) = exp(2 x_r sum_{t in V \ r} theta*_{rt} x_t) / (exp(2 x_r sum_{t in V \ r} theta*_{rt} x_t) + 1) (Equation 4), yielding the rescaled negative conditional log-likelihood ell(theta; {x^{(i)}}) = (1/n) sum_{i=1}^n f(theta; x^{(i)}) - sum_{u in V \ r} theta_{ru} mu_hat_{ru} where f(theta; x) = log(exp(sum_{t in V \ r} theta_{rt} x_t) + exp(-sum_{t in V \ r} theta_{rt} x_t)) and mu_hat_{ru} = (1/n) sum_{i=1}^n x_r^{(i)} x_u^{(i)} (Equations 5-7). Constructing a primal-dual witness pair (theta_hat, z_hat) by setting theta_hat_S = argmin_{(theta_S, 0)} {ell(theta; {x^{(i)}}) + lambda_n ||theta_S||_1} (Equation 24), theta_hat_{S^c} = 0, z_hat_S = sign(theta_hat_S), and lambda_n z_hat_{S^c} = -[nabla ell(theta_hat)]_{S^c} from the KKT stationarity equation nabla ell(theta_hat) + lambda_n z_hat = 0 (Equation 20), Lemma 1 (Section 3.3, p. 10) proves via Lagrangian duality and complementary slackness on the conic sign representation lambda_n z_hat = sum_{v in {-1, +1}^{p-1}} alpha*_v v (Equation 22) that if ||z_hat_{S^c}||_infty < 1, every optimal solution theta_tilde satisfies theta_tilde_{S^c} = 0, and if [nabla^2 ell(theta_hat)]_{SS} > 0, theta_hat is the unique optimal solution.

Mechanism #sample-fisher-consistency-via-boundary-convexity-and-quadratic-taylor-remainder-control
Expanding the zero-subgradient KKT condition around theta* via the coordinate-wise mean-value theorem gives Q^n (theta_hat - theta*) = W^n - lambda_n z_hat + R^n (Equations 27-29), where W^n := -nabla ell(theta*; {x^{(i)}}), Q^n := (1/n) sum_{i=1}^n eta(x^{(i)}; theta*) x_{\r}^{(i)} (x_{\r}^{(i)})^T is the sample Fisher information matrix (Equation 17), and R^n_j = [nabla^2 ell(theta_bar^{(j)}) - nabla^2 ell(theta*)]_j^T (theta_hat - theta*). Under the sample Fisher good event M({x^{(i)}}) where Q^n satisfies Assumptions A1 and A2 (Equation 25): (1) Lemma 2 (p. 12, Equation 30; Appendix A.1, p. 20) applies the Azuma-Hoeffding inequality to bounded score increments |Z_u^{(i)}| <= 2 to bound P((2 - alpha)/lambda_n ||W^n||_infty >= alpha/4) = O(exp(-K n lambda_n^2 + log p)); (2) Lemma 3 (p. 12, Equation 31; Appendix A.2, pp. 20-22, Equations 50-54) proves strict positivity of G(u_S) on the sphere ||u_S||_2 = M lambda_n sqrt(d) with M = 5 / C_min whenever lambda_n d <= C_min^2 / (10 D_max), establishing ||theta_hat_S - theta*_S||_2 = O_p(sqrt(d) lambda_n); and (3) Lemma 4 (p. 12, Equation 32; Appendix A.3, pp. 22-23) uses |g'(t)| <= 1 for g(t) = 4 exp(2t) / (1 + exp(2t))^2 to bound ||R^n||_infty <= D_max ||theta_hat_S - theta*_S||_2^2 = O_p(d lambda_n^2), proving Proposition 1 (p. 11, Equation 26): n > L d^2 log p and theta*_min = Omega(1/sqrt(d)) suffice for ||z_hat_{S^c}||_infty <= 1 - alpha/2 < 1 and exact signed graph recovery.

Scope #population-to-sample-fisher-concentration-and-d3-log-p-sparsistency-scaling
When dependency (Assumption A1: Lambda_min(Q*_{SS}) >= C_min > 0, Lambda_max(E_{theta*}[X_{\r} X_{\r}^T]) <= D_max, Equation 12) and mutual incoherence (Assumption A2: |||Q*_{S^c S} (Q*_{SS})^{-1}|||_infty <= 1 - alpha, Equation 13) are imposed on the population Fisher information matrix Q* = E_{theta*}[eta(X; theta*) X_{\r} X_{\r}^T] (Equations 9-11), Lemma 5 (Section 5, p. 13, Equations 41a-41b) and Lemma 7 (p. 15, Equations 46a-46c; Appendix B, pp. 23-24) apply entrywise Hoeffding bounds |Z_{jk}^{(i)}| <= 4 (Equation 43) and the four-term decomposition Q^n_{S^c S} (Q^n_{SS})^{-1} = T_1 + T_2 + T_3 + T_4 (Equations 45a-45d) to control |||T_1|||_infty <= (1 - alpha) |||Q^n_{SS} - Q*_{SS}|||_infty sqrt(d) |||(Q^n_{SS})^{-1}|||_2 and |||T_2|||_infty <= (sqrt(d) / C_min) |||Q^n_{S^c S} - Q*_{S^c S}|||_infty at threshold delta = O(1/sqrt(d)), proving Lemma 6 (p. 14, Equation 42): P[|||Q^n_{S^c S} (Q^n_{SS})^{-1}|||_infty >= 1 - alpha/2] <= exp(-K n / d^3 + log p). Combining Lemma 6 with Proposition 1 and a union bound over all p nodes establishes Theorem 1 (Section 3.2, p. 8, Equations 15-16): if n > L d^3 log p and theta*_min = Omega(1/d), then for lambda_n = 2 sqrt((log p)/n), the probability of failing to recover the exact signed graph G(p, d) decays as O(exp(-K n / d^3 + 3 log p)) -> 0, with overall convex-programming computational complexity O(max{n, p} p^3).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Population Fisher Mutual Incoherence and Degree-Cubed Scaling: Theorem 1 requires the population Fisher information matrix Q*_r = E_{theta*}[eta(X; theta*) X_{\r} X_{\r}^T] to satisfy the l_infinity mutual incoherence bound |||Q*_{S^c S} (Q*_{SS})^{-1}|||_infty <= 1 - alpha (Equation 13) and requires n = Omega(d^3 log p) samples and theta*_min = Omega(1/d) when transferring incoherence from Q* to the sample Fisher matrix Q^n (whereas n = Omega(d^2 log p) suffices under sample Fisher incoherence in Proposition 1).
  • Binary Pairwise Ising Model Scope: The explicit analysis in Theorems 1 and Proposition 1 is formulated for zero-field pairwise binary Ising models X_s in {-1, +1} (Section 2.1, Equation 1), with extensions to general multi-state discrete Markov random fields discussed only at a high level.
  • Primary-Source Typographical Notes in Ravikumar, Wainwright, & Lafferty (2010, arXiv:0804.4202v1): Verified six self-correcting typographical details during full-text inspection and numerical verification: (1) Section 2.3 (p. 5, Equation 3) defines N_pm(r) := {sign(theta*_{rt}) t | t in N(s)} with s in place of r, while p. 6 writes theta_{\s} in line 15 and theta_hat_{su} != 0 in Equation (8) in place of theta_{\r} and theta_hat_{ru} != 0; (2) Section 3.1 (p. 7, Equation 9) writes Q*_r := E_{theta*}{nabla^2 log P_{theta*}[X_r | X_{\r}]} omitting the minus sign of the negative log-likelihood ell(theta) (whereas Equation 10 gives the positive-definite Fisher information matrix E_{theta*}[eta(X; theta*) X_{\r} X_{\r}^T]); (3) Section 3.2 (p. 8, Theorem 1, Equation 16) and Section 4 (p. 11, Proposition 1, Equation 26) write the success event P[G_hat(lambda_n) = G(p, d)] on the LHS with the failure-rate upper bound O(exp(-K n / d^3 - 3 log p)) -> 0 and O(exp(-n lambda_n^2 - log p)) -> 0 on the RHS (where the union-bound failure probability P[G_hat != G] has +3 log p / +log p in the exponent, as in Lemma 2 Eq. 30, Lemma 4 Eq. 32, and Lemma 6 Eq. 42, so P[G_hat = G] = 1 - O(exp(-K n / d^3 + 3 log p)) -> 1); (4) Section 3.3 (p. 9, Equation 21) writes 'if theta_hat_i != 0' in place of 'if theta_hat_{rt} != 0'; (5) Section 4.2 (p. 12, Equation 35) writes [W^n_{S^c} - R^n_{S^c}] - Q^n_{S^c S} (Q^n_{SS})^{-1} [W^n_S - R^n_S] with minus signs before R^n_{S^c} and R^n_S when eliminating (theta_hat_S - theta*_S) from Equations (33a)-(33b) (whereas substituting (Q^n_{SS})^{-1}[W^n_S - lambda_n z_hat_S + R^n_S] gives +R^n_{S^c} and +R^n_S, leaving the triangle-inequality bound in Equations 36-37 identical); and (6) Section 5.2 (p. 15, bottom display) writes T_1 = Q*_{S^c S} (Q*_{SS})^{-1} [Q^n_{SS} - Q*_{SS}] (Q^n_{SS})^{-1} with [Q^n_{SS} - Q*_{SS}] in place of [Q*_{SS} - Q^n_{SS}] (which flips only the overall sign of T_1 and leaves |||T_1|||_infty unchanged).
