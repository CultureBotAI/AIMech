Exponentiated-gradient reduction for fair classification

Algorithms AIMech:ExponentiatedGradientFairClassification DRAFT
Aliases: Agarwal exponentiated-gradient fairness reduction
A cost-sensitive classification reduction that alternates classifier best responses with exponentiated-gradient updates of bounded Lagrange multipliers to approximate an error–fairness saddle point and return a randomized classifier mixture.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is Algorithm 1 of Agarwal et al., not generic exponentiated-gradient optimization. It returns a distribution over classifiers and is distinct from the paper’s deterministic-candidate grid-search reduction and from postprocessing a fixed classifier. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method-scope
Express fairness as linear inequalities on conditional prediction moments whose conditioning events do not depend on the classifier. Given multiplier values, call a cost-sensitive classification oracle with modified per-example label costs. Update multipliers from constraint violations using exponentiated gradient, average the classifiers and multipliers, and stop when the computed saddle-point gap reaches the chosen tolerance.
  • supports Source: A Reductions Approach to Fair Classification · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.2 and 3.1–3.3, equations (1)–(6), Algorithm 1, BESTh/BESTlambda constructions, Theorems 1–3, PDF pp. 2–6

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The optimization analysis assumes a cost-sensitive best-response oracle; heuristic base learners need not realize the exact oracle guarantees.
  • Bounds retain optimization tolerance, the multiplier-norm cap and statistical error, including dependence on the number of observations in each conditioning event.
  • The constraint representation excludes conditioning events that depend on the learned predictor; the paper identifies predictive parity and calibration as outside this formulation.
  • Protected attributes are required for training constraints. No separate protected-attribute argument is needed at prediction time only when the chosen feature vector and base classifier do not require it.
