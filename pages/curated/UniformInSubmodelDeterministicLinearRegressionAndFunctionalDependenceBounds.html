Uniform-in-submodel deterministic linear regression and functional dependence bounds

Mathematical Methods AIMech:UniformInSubmodelDeterministicLinearRegressionAndFunctionalDependenceBounds DRAFT
Aliases: Model-Free Uniform-in-Submodel OLS Consistency and Bahadur Linear Representation Bounds, General Linear Regression Map beta_M(Sigma, Gamma) Deterministic Submodel Inequalities, Functional Dependence Sub-Weibull Concentration for Uniform-in-Submodel Regression
Uniform-in-submodel deterministic linear regression and functional dependence bounds are a model-free high-dimensional statistical methodology introduced by Kuchibhotla, Brown, Buja, George, and Zhao (2021, arXiv:1802.05801v3) for simultaneously controlling estimation error and first-order Bahadur linear representation remainders across all covariate subsets M in M(k) := {M subseteq {1,...,p} : 1 <= |M| <= k}. Defining the general linear regression map beta_M(Sigma, Gamma) := [Sigma(M)]^{-1} Gamma(M) for any symmetric positive-definite p x p matrix Sigma and p-vector Gamma (encompassing ordinary least squares, Catoni/Minsker heavy-tailed Gram estimators, outlier-robust estimators, and Loh-Wainwright noisy or missing-data surrogate estimators, Section 3.3), the framework introduces the k-sparse submatrix operator norm RIP(k, Sigma_1 - Sigma_2) := sup_{M in M(k)} ||Sigma_1(M) - Sigma_2(M)||_{op}, the k-sparse vector norm D(k, Gamma_1 - Gamma_2) := sup_{M in M(k)} ||Gamma_1(M) - Gamma_2(M)||_2, and the k-sparse minimum eigenvalue Lambda(k; Sigma_2) := inf_{||theta||_0<=k} ||Sigma_2 theta||_2 / ||theta||_2 (Equations 13-14). Whenever RIP(k, Sigma_1 - Sigma_2) < Lambda(k; Sigma_2), Theorem 1 proves the assumption-free deterministic uniform l2 bound ||beta_M(Sigma_1, Gamma_1) - beta_M(Sigma_2, Gamma_2)||_2 <= [D(k, Gamma_1 - Gamma_2) + RIP(k, Sigma_1 - Sigma_2) ||beta_M(Sigma_2, Gamma_2)||_2] / [Lambda(k; Sigma_2) - RIP(k, Sigma_1 - Sigma_2)] simultaneously for all M in M(k), and Theorem 3 proves the deterministic uniform Bahadur linear representation bound ||beta_M(Sigma_1, Gamma_1) - beta_M(Sigma_2, Gamma_2) - [Sigma_2(M)]^{-1}{Gamma_1(M) - Sigma_1(M) beta_M(Sigma_2, Gamma_2)}||_2 <= [RIP(k, Sigma_1 - Sigma_2) / Lambda(k; Sigma_2)] ||beta_M(Sigma_1, Gamma_1) - beta_M(Sigma_2, Gamma_2)||_2 with matching two-sided sandwiching (Remarks 3.2-3.3). Combining these deterministic inequalities with epsilon-net covering of Theta_k (Section 4) and a dyadic martingale plus blocking decomposition under Wu's (2005) functional dependence measure with dependence-adjusted sub-Weibull norms ||{W}||_{psi_alpha,nu} (Section 5, Theorem 12) yields uniform O_p(sqrt(k log(ep/k)/n)) consistency and O_p(k log(ep/k)/n) linear representation remainders for independent and causal time-series data.

Distinct from AIMech:SimultaneousNormalEquationPostSelectionConfidenceRegions (Kuchibhotla et al. 2020, arXiv:1806.04119v1, DOI:10.1214/19-AOS1917), which avoids inverting Sigma_hat_n(M) by working directly with the normal-equation residual ||Sigma_hat_n(M)(beta_{n,M} - beta_hat_{n,M})||_infty and bivariate max-norm quantiles (D_n^Gamma, D_n^Sigma) over M_p(1) and M_p(2) to construct simultaneous post-selection confidence regions R_hat_{n,M} and R_hat_{n,M}^dagger, whereas the present concept establishes deterministic uniform-in-submodel l2 and l1 estimation error bounds and first-order Bahadur influence-function expansions for the inverted submodel map beta_M(Sigma, Gamma) = [Sigma(M)]^{-1} Gamma(M) under k-sparse operator norm RIP(k) and develops the dyadic martingale/blocking concentration theory (Theorem 12, Lemmas 1-4) for dependence-adjusted sub-Weibull norms ||{W}||_{psi_alpha,nu}. Distinct from AIMech:GeneralizedBernsteinOrliczSubWeibullConcentrationAndHighDimensionalLasso (Kuchibhotla & Chakrabortty 2022, arXiv:1804.02605v4), which studies independent observations via the GBO norm ||.||_{Psi_{alpha,L}}, free-s RSC/RE optimization, and L1-penalized misspecified Lasso rather than unregularized uniform-in-submodel Bahadur expansions and functional dependence time-series concentration.

Mechanism #deterministic-uniform-in-submodel-l2-and-l1-consistency-bounds
Sections 3.1-3.3 (pp. 8-13 and 15-16, Theorems 1-2, Remarks 3.1 and 3.4, Equations 11-17 and 23-24) define the general linear regression map beta_M(Sigma, Gamma) := [Sigma(M)]^{-1} Gamma(M) over M in M(k) and prove purely deterministically that whenever RIP(k, Sigma_1 - Sigma_2) < Lambda(k; Sigma_2), every M in M(k) satisfies ||beta_M(Sigma_1, Gamma_1) - beta_M(Sigma_2, Gamma_2)||_2 <= [D(k, Gamma_1 - Gamma_2) + RIP(k, Sigma_1 - Sigma_2) ||beta_M(Sigma_2, Gamma_2)||_2] / [Lambda(k; Sigma_2) - RIP(k, Sigma_1 - Sigma_2)] (Theorem 1, Equation 15), while Remark 3.1 proves the model-free population moving-target bound S_{2,k}(Sigma_n, Gamma_n) := sup_{M in M(k)} ||beta_{n,M}||_2 <= (n^{-1} Lambda(k; Sigma_n)^{-1} sum_{i=1}^n E[Y_i^2])^{1/2} (Equation 24) and Theorem 2 establishes the coordinatewise/L1 analogue under Infinity-norm Gram and cross-product errors.

Mechanism #deterministic-uniform-in-submodel-bahadur-linear-representation-and-sandwiching
Section 3.2 (pp. 12-15, Theorems 3-4, Corollary 1, Remarks 3.2-3.3, Equations 18-22) proves that for every M in M(k), the first-order Bahadur linear representation remainder satisfies ||beta_M(Sigma_1, Gamma_1) - beta_M(Sigma_2, Gamma_2) - [Sigma_2(M)]^{-1}{Gamma_1(M) - Sigma_1(M) beta_M(Sigma_2, Gamma_2)}||_2 <= [RIP(k, Sigma_1 - Sigma_2) / Lambda(k; Sigma_2)] ||beta_M(Sigma_1, Gamma_1) - beta_M(Sigma_2, Gamma_2)||_2 (Theorem 3, Equation 19) with a matching lower bound C_*(k, Sigma_2) Lambda(k; Sigma_1 - Sigma_2) ||beta_M(Sigma_1, Gamma_1) - beta_M(Sigma_2, Gamma_2)||_2 showing worst-case optimality (Remark 3.2), and proves that whenever RIP(k, Sigma_1 - Sigma_2) <= Lambda(k; Sigma_2)/2, the estimation error is sandwiched within a factor of 2 of the linear term: (1/2) ||beta_M(Sigma_1, Gamma_1) - beta_M(Sigma_2, Gamma_2)||_2 <= ||[Sigma_2(M)]^{-1}{Gamma_1(M) - Sigma_1(M) beta_M(Sigma_2, Gamma_2)}||_2 <= (3/2) ||beta_M(Sigma_1, Gamma_1) - beta_M(Sigma_2, Gamma_2)||_2 (Remark 3.3, Equation 22).

Mechanism #epsilon-net-sparse-sphere-discretization-and-fixed-design-exact-linearity
Section 4 and Appendix A (pp. 16-20 and 27-29, Theorems 5-6, Remarks 4.1-4.5, Equations 25-27) discretize the k-sparse unit sphere Theta_k := {theta in R^p : ||theta||_0 <= k, ||theta||_2 = 1} using an epsilon-net N(epsilon, Theta_k) of cardinality at most ((1 + epsilon^{-1}) e p / k)^k (Equation 26) so that D(k, Gamma_hat_n - Gamma_n) <= (1 - epsilon)^{-1} max_{theta in N(epsilon, Theta_k)} theta^T(Gamma_hat_n - Gamma_n) and RIP_n(k) <= (1 - 2 epsilon)^{-1} max_{theta in N(epsilon, Theta_k)} |theta^T(Sigma_hat_n - Sigma_n)theta| (Equation 27), proving under marginal (MExp) or joint (JExp) sub-Weibull(alpha) tails that sup_{M in M(k)} ||beta_hat_{n,M} - beta_{n,M}||_2 = O_p(sqrt(k log(ep/k)/n)) and the uniform Bahadur remainder is O_p(k log(ep/k)/n) (matching second-order degenerate U-statistics, Remark 4.4), while under fixed covariates (Sigma_hat_n = Sigma_n) RIP_n(k) = 0 so the Bahadur remainder is identically zero across all M in M(k) (Remark 4.3).

Scope #functional-dependence-adjusted-sub-weibull-concentration-for-time-series
Section 5 and Appendix B (pp. 20-26 and 30-37, Theorems 7-12, Lemmas 1-4, Equations 28-45) extend the uniform-in-submodel bounds to causal stationary and non-stationary time series W_i = G_i(..., varepsilon_{i-1}, varepsilon_i) using Wu's (2005) physical functional dependence measure delta_{s,r,j} = max_i ||W_i(j) - W_{i,s}(j)||_r, cumulative tail Delta_{m,r,j} = sum_{s=m}^infty delta_{s,r,j}, and dependence-adjusted sub-Weibull norm ||{W}||_{psi_alpha,nu} := sup_{r>=2} sup_{m>=0} r^{-1/alpha}(m+1)^nu Delta_{m,r} (Equations 28-30). Via a dyadic martingale difference plus xi_ell-dependent blocking decomposition and Rio's (2009) Burkholder inequality (Lemma 1, Equations 36-45), Theorem 12 proves part-Gaussian tail bounds with effective tail exponent s(alpha) = (1/2 + 1/alpha)^{-1} and dependence factor Omega_n(nu) = 1 (nu > 1/2), (log n)^{3/2} (nu = 1/2), or n^{1/2 - nu} (nu < 1/2) (Equation 31), while Lemmas 3-4 establish linear-combination and product persistence ||{W^{(1)} W^{(2)}}||_{psi_alpha, min(nu_1,nu_2)} <= 2^{1+1/alpha} ||{W^{(1)}}||_{psi_{alpha_1},nu_1} ||{W^{(2)}}||_{psi_{alpha_2},nu_2} for 1/alpha = 1/alpha_1 + 1/alpha_2.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Minimum k-Sparse Eigenvalue Condition RIP(k, Sigma_1 - Sigma_2) < Lambda(k; Sigma_2): Unlike the normal-equation residual formulation in Kuchibhotla et al. (2020, arXiv:1806.04119v1), the deterministic uniform l2 and Bahadur bounds in Theorems 1 and 3 require positive k-sparse minimum eigenvalue Lambda(k; Sigma_2) > 0 and RIP(k, Sigma_1 - Sigma_2) < Lambda(k; Sigma_2) to invert Sigma_1(M) and Sigma_2(M) across all M in M(k).
  • Effective Tail Exponent s(alpha) = (1/2 + 1/alpha)^{-1} < 2 and Slow-Decay Penalty Omega_n(nu) Under Functional Dependence: In Theorem 12 (Equation 35), blocking dependent summands yields an effective heavy-tail exponent s(alpha) = (1/2 + 1/alpha)^{-1} strictly smaller than min{alpha, 2}, and when the functional dependence decay parameter satisfies nu < 1/2, the variance-like factor scales with Omega_n(nu) = n^{1/2 - nu}, slowing the convergence rate below n^{-1/2}.
  • Primary-Source Typographical Notes in arXiv:1802.05801v3: Verified five self-correcting typographical slips in the primary manuscript: (1) Proof of Theorem 1 (p. 10, display 3) prints 'Lambda(k; Sigma_2) - RIP(k; Sigma_2)' in the denominator instead of 'Lambda(k; Sigma_2) - RIP(k, Sigma_1 - Sigma_2)'; (2) Theorem 6 (p. 19) labels both parts '(a)' ('(a) under Assumption (MExp)' and '(a) under Assumption (JExp)') instead of '(a)' and '(b)'; (3) Theorem 8 (p. 25, second display for RIP_n(k)) prints 'Upsilon_{n,k}^Gamma' under the square root instead of 'Upsilon_{n,k}^Sigma'; (4) Proof of Lemma 2(b) (p. 31, line 1) prints 'If beta > 1/2, then' at the start of the third case where it should read 'If beta < 1/2, then'; and (5) Theorem 12 (p. 31, Equation 35) and p. 36 (first display after Equation 45) print '||{Z}||_{psi_alpha,nu}' in the first (Gaussian) term instead of '||{Z}||_{2,nu}' (cf. Equations 37 and 45), and Proof of Lemma 4 (p. 37, line 1) defines 'delta_{s,r}^{(j)} := ||W_i^{(1)} - W_{i,s}^{(1)}||_r' with superscript '(1)' instead of '(j)'.
  • All 41 pages of the full-text PDF of Kuchibhotla, Brown, Buja, George, & Zhao (2021, arXiv:1802.05801v3) were read.
