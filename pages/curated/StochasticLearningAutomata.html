Stochastic learning automata

Learning Paradigms AIMech:StochasticLearningAutomata DRAFT
A family of adaptive stochastic decision procedures that selects an action from a maintained finite action-probability vector, receives evaluative environmental feedback on that action, and updates the vector through a specified reinforcement rule. The classical framework accommodates different linear, nonlinear and hybrid rules; it is not one universally fixed optimizer, value-learning algorithm or neural-network architecture.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the classical finite-action probability-update learning family; LRI is one member. It is not a universal fixed optimizer, neural architecture, Q-learning, REINFORCE, temporal-difference learning, ACO, AntNet or QRouting. Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #finite-action-feedback-loop
In the inspected classical formalism, a stochastic automaton selects among finitely many actions according to its current probabilities. Its action becomes an input to the environment; the environmental response returns to the automaton and influences the next probability vector. The paper uses a deterministic one-to-one state-to-action map for its exposition. Those automaton states are not automatically observed states of an external Markov decision process.
  • supports Source: Learning Automata—A Survey · Locator: Section II, PDF p. 3 / printed p. 325, Figure 3 and the stochastic automaton, environment and learning-automaton definitions; Equation 7, PDF p. 4 / printed p. 326.
Scope #feedback-alphabet-and-penalty-model
The source distinguishes P-model binary feedback, Q-model feedback drawn from a finite set, and S-model feedback in [0,1]. For its binary example, zero denotes nonpenalty and one denotes penalty; c_i is the initially unknown probability of a penalty for action i, and the environment is called stationary when these probabilities do not change with the trial index. Q-model names a feedback alphabet, not Q-learning, and S-model feedback does not imply a continuous action space.
  • supports Source: Learning Automata—A Survey · Locator: Section II, PDF p. 3 / printed p. 325, Environment and the P-, Q- and S-model distinction.
Scope #alternative-reinforcement-rules
The probability update is specified by a reinforcement rule that receives the current probabilities, selected action and environmental response. The source treats linear, nonlinear and hybrid schemes, and reward, penalty or inaction choices, as different constructions within this framework. Linear reward-inaction and linear reward-penalty are named members rather than aliases of the entire family; no one of these rules is mandatory for every stochastic learning automaton.
  • supports Source: Learning Automata—A Survey · Locator: Section IV, PDF pp. 4–5 / printed pp. 326–327, Equation 7, reinforcement-rule classification, Equations 8 and 10, and the named linear schemes. The displayed Equation 9 formula is not adopted.
Mechanism #linear-reward-inaction-member
For the P-model linear reward-inaction member, let p be a probability vector, i the selected action and 0<a<1. A nonpenalty response gives p_i_new=p_i+a(1-p_i) and p_j_new=(1-a)p_j for j≠i; a penalty response leaves the vector unchanged. Equivalently, with R=1 for nonpenalty and R=0 for penalty, p_new=p+a*R*(e_i-p). This is a member-specific rule, and a finite update from an interior probability vector remains in the interior.
  • supports Source: Learning Automata—A Survey · Locator: Section IV, PDF p. 5 / printed p. 327, Equations 8 and 10 and the following explanation that LRI ignores penalty inputs; algebraic restatement and simplex consequence of the displayed rule.
Mechanism #conditional-lri-penalty-drift
For that LRI rule, assume that, conditional on the current history, action I is drawn from p and its penalty probability is a fixed c_I. Define M(p)=sum_i p_i*c_i. Directly from the update, E[p_k_new-p_k | p]=a*p_k*(M(p)-c_k), and E[M(p_new)-M(p) | p]=-a*(sum_i p_i*c_i^2-M(p)^2)≤0. The inequality is strict only when the c_i differ on the positive-probability support. This is a one-step conditional expectation under the stated response law, not pathwise improvement or a guarantee of eventual best-action selection.
  • supports Source: Learning Automata—A Survey · Locator: Section III, PDF p. 4 / printed p. 326, Equations 1 and 6; Section IV, PDF p. 5 / printed p. 327, Equations 8 and 10. The displayed drift is an explicitly conditional algebraic consequence of these definitions and the LRI rule, not a separately quoted theorem.
Scope #distinct-performance-criteria
For the stationary binary model, the source compares limiting expected penalty with the uniform-action reference and the minimum action penalty. Expediency improves on the uniform reference; optimality reaches the minimum in the stated limiting expectation; epsilon-optimality allows the limiting expectation to approach that minimum within any prescribed positive tolerance by a suitable parameter choice. These criteria differ from a finite-budget bound and do not by themselves identify a probability-one limit for every fixed reinforcement parameter.
  • supports Source: Learning Automata—A Survey · Locator: Section III, PDF p. 4 / printed p. 326, Equations 1–6 and Definitions 1–4; Section IV, PDF pp. 6–7 / printed pp. 328–329, Convergence of Action Probabilities and Comments on Convergence Results.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This record covers the classical finite-action probability-update family. The paper’s one-to-one identification of its finite internal states and actions is an expository choice, not a general environment-state model. Specific fixed-structure, continuous-action, contextual, estimator and hybrid constructions require their own assumptions; they are not automatically aliases or interchangeable implementations of one rule.
  • The inspected archive copy displays g_j(p)=b*p_j+b/r−1 in Equation 9. Under the literal ordinary-precedence reading and Equation 8, r=2, p=(1/2,1/2), b=1/4, selection of action 1 and a penalty produce p_new=(5/4,−1/4), outside the probability simplex. Footnote 4 allowing negative g_j does not justify negative resulting probabilities. The intended sign or denominator correction is unresolved; no conventional LRP formula is silently substituted, and this is not an asserted author erratum.
  • The displayed Equation 14 requires the common ratio mu to be strictly positive, whereas LRI has g_j=0 and hence mu=0 in the interior, despite the adjoining discussion including LRI among the relevant schemes. Footnote 5 refers to an earlier paper for a precise theorem. This record does not certify that printed necessary-and-sufficient statement, and it does not adopt the paper’s treatment of epsilon-optimality and absolute expediency as a general equivalence after its acknowledgment of an unproved reverse implication.
  • Expected one-step drift, convergence of expected penalty, convergence in distribution and almost-sure convergence of the probability sequence are different statements. A worse action can receive nonpenalty and reduce the better action’s probability on a realized LRI trial. Increasing bounded expected action probability does not alone prove that its limit is one. No universal best-action convergence, finite-time regret, rate or global-optimization guarantee is asserted for this family.
  • LRI preserves interior probabilities at every finite step with 0<a<1, but it supplies no uniform exploration floor. An initially zero action probability cannot recover under that rule, and limiting concentration can impair adaptation when the environment changes. Strict expected improvement also fails when all active actions have equal penalty probabilities. Resetting, probability bounds and tracking modifications are separate choices; stationary analyses do not automatically establish nonstationary tracking.
  • The stationary finite-action setting can overlap a bandit problem, and particular probability updates can resemble components of AntNet or other reinforced decision systems. Such relationships do not make the family identical to a specific bandit strategy, ACO, AntNet, REINFORCE, Q-learning or temporal-difference learning. Its feedback loop also does not require softmax, a recurrent neural network or the pinned AIO Markov-chain network structure.
  • Primary evidence is the inspected twelve-page academic-archive copy of the 1974 author survey and mathematical exposition. Equality with an IEEE- or author-hosted PDF was not established, and later PDF build timestamps are not its publication date. Earlier cited proofs, priority claims, author implementations and empirical replications were not independently verified. The record uses the clear LRI member and qualified framework definitions while retaining the displayed-formula and theorem-boundary gaps.
