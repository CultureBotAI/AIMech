Rejection sampling

Algorithms AIMech:RejectionSampling DRAFT
Aliases: Accept-reject sampling
A random-variate generation algorithm that draws proposals from a normalized envelope dominating an unnormalized target and accepts each with the target-to-envelope ratio, yielding independent target draws with fresh independent random trials.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is ordinary accept-reject generation with a valid global envelope. A rejected candidate is discarded, not recorded as a repeated chain state. The method differs from Metropolis acceptance, rejection-driven Metropolis proposals, importance weighting and selective filtering of model answers. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a nonnegative target g with finite positive integral, choose an integrable envelope g_u with g_u(x)≥g(x) everywhere on the target support and sample x from g_u/integral(g_u). Independently draw w uniformly on (0,1); accept x if w≤g(x)/g_u(x), otherwise discard the trial and repeat. An optional lower bound 0≤g_l≤g allows early acceptance when the same w≤g_l(x)/g_u(x), avoiding evaluation of g. Fresh independent trials produce independent accepted draws from normalized g. The target normalizing constant is not required for the acceptance ratio.
  • supports Source: Adaptive Rejection Sampling for Gibbs Sampling · Locator: Section 2.1, Rejection Sampling, printed pp. 338–339 / PDF pp. 3–4

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A valid dominating envelope and an exact sampler for its normalized distribution are required. Clipping an invalid envelope ratio at one does not preserve the intended independent-sample law.
  • An envelope with excessive mass can make acceptance rare. No universal efficiency, dimensional scaling or bound on the number of trials is claimed.
  • The optional squeezing bound must be valid everywhere it is used; it is an evaluation shortcut, not a replacement target.
  • Numerical approximation, finite precision and inaccurate envelope sampling can change the output distribution. Log concavity is not required by ordinary rejection sampling itself.
