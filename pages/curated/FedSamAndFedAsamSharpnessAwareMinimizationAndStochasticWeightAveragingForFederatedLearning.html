FedSAM and FedASAM (Sharpness-Aware Minimization and Server Stochastic Weight Averaging for Federated Learning)

Algorithms AIMech:FedSamAndFedAsamSharpnessAwareMinimizationAndStochasticWeightAveragingForFederatedLearning DRAFT
Aliases: FedSAM, FedASAM, Federated Sharpness-Aware Minimization, Federated Adaptive Sharpness-Aware Minimization
A sharpness-aware federated optimization framework (Caldarola, Caputo, & Ciccone 2022, Sections 3-4, Equations 1-5, Algorithm 1) that mitigates sharp-landscape misalignment across statistically heterogeneous clients by replacing local client SGD with Sharpness-Aware Minimization (FedSAM, perturbing local weights by epsilon^hat_k(w) = rho * grad L_k(w) / ||grad L_k(w)||_2, Equation 3) or Adaptive Sharpness-Aware Minimization (FedASAM, using scale-invariant normalization operator T_w^i = diag(|w|) so epsilon^tilde_k(w) = rho * T_w^2 grad L_k(w) / ||T_w grad L_k(w)||_2, Equation 5), combined on the server side with cyclic-learning-rate Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA, w_SWA <- (w_SWA * n_models + w^{t+1}) / (n_models + 1) every c rounds starting at fraction s of total rounds, Algorithm 1).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from centralized SAM (AIMech:SharpnessAwareMinimization, Foret et al. 2021) and centralized SWA (AIMech:StochasticWeightAveraging, Izmailov et al. 2018), which operate on a single centralized model without cross-client parameter averaging or heterogeneous client drift; and distinct from FedSpeed (AIMech:FedSpeedProxCorrectionAndGradientPerturbationForFederatedLearning, Sun et al. 2023), which combines a local proximal regularizer and prox-correction momentum variable g^hat_i^t with unnormalized extra-gradient perturbation rather than scale-invariant ASAM and server-side cyclic SWA.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #r454-local-sam-and-scale-invariant-asam-perturbation-mechanism
Drives each participating client k toward wider, lower-curvature basins (measured by reduced maximum Hessian eigenvalue lambda_max and trace Tr(H)) by evaluating local gradients at the adversarially perturbed point w + epsilon^hat_k(w) under Euclidean ball radius rho (FedSAM, Equation 3) or parameter-scale-normalized radius ||T_w^{-1} epsilon||_2 <= rho (FedASAM, Equations 4-5), reducing the loss barrier when heterogeneous client models are averaged on the server.
  • supports Source: Improving Generalization in Federated Learning by Seeking Flat Minima · Locator: Section 3.3 Loss Surfaces & Section 4.1 SAM and ASAM on the Client Side (Equations 2-5, Table 1, Figures 1-3), pp. 4-7
Scope #r454-server-side-cyclic-swa-aggregation-scope
Integrates server-side Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) after an initial warmup fraction s of total communication rounds by cycling the server-broadcast learning rate between alpha_1 and alpha_2 over cycle length c and updating the running average w_SWA <- (w_SWA * n_models + w^{t+1}) / (n_models + 1) at the minimum of each cycle without increasing client-server communication cost.
  • supports Source: Improving Generalization in Federated Learning by Seeking Flat Minima · Locator: Section 4.2 SWA on the Server Side & Algorithm 1, pp. 7-8
Limitation #r454-two-forward-backward-passes-per-local-step-limitation
Doubles the local per-minibatch backpropagation cost on participating clients because each local step requires one gradient evaluation to compute the ascent perturbation epsilon^hat_k(w) and a second gradient evaluation at w + epsilon^hat_k(w).
  • supports Source: Improving Generalization in Federated Learning by Seeking Flat Minima · Locator: Section 4.1 Equation 3 & Algorithm 1 Client Update, pp. 6-8
Application #r454-heterogeneous-federated-vision-generalization-application
Applied to heterogeneous cross-device federated vision benchmarks including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Federated Extended MNIST (FEMNIST), and Landmarks-User-160k using GroupNorm-equipped ResNet and MobileNetV2 architectures.
  • supports Source: Improving Generalization in Federated Learning by Seeking Flat Minima · Locator: Section 5 Experiments (Tables 1-4, Figures 3-5), pp. 8-14

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Requires two forward-backward gradient evaluations per local minibatch step on each active client, doubling local compute cost.
  • Does not eliminate client drift when local objectives have disjoint optima without additional drift-correction or control variates.
