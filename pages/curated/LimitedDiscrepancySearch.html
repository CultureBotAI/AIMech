Limited discrepancy search

Algorithms AIMech:LimitedDiscrepancySearch DRAFT
Aliases: LDS
A backtracking tree-search strategy that repeatedly explores paths under an increasing upper limit on deviations from a successor-ordering heuristic; in the original binary formulation, taking the nonpreferred child consumes one discrepancy.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The original LDS searches paths with at most k discrepancies in each pass. It differs from Korf’s improved LDS, which avoids revisiting leaves at the supplied maximum depth by allocating an exact discrepancy count there, and from depth-bounded discrepancy search, which bounds where deviations may occur. The original 1995 Figure 1 explores the nonpreferred child first when discrepancy budget remains; Korf’s 1996 presentation of original LDS uses the opposite within-pass order. The names do not establish identical visit sequences. Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #search-procedure
Run a depth-first probe with budget k, starting with k=0 and increasing through a sufficient maximum depth for a finite binary tree. When the budget is zero, follow the heuristic-preferred child. With positive budget, the original Figure 1 first probes the nonpreferred child with k−1 and then the preferred child with k. Return when a goal is found. Each pass permits at most k deviations, so earlier paths may be revisited in later passes.
  • supports Source: Limited Discrepancy Search · Locator: Section 3 and Figure 1, printed p. 608 / PDF p. 2; Section 5.5, printed p. 611 / PDF p. 5
Scope #original-versus-improved
Korf distinguishes original at-most-k passes from an exact-count-at-maximum-depth modification, and presents a left-child-first original probe. This differs from the right-child-first positive-budget probe printed in the 1995 paper; the two presentations share a budget principle, not necessarily a visit order.
  • supports Source: Improved Limited Discrepancy Search · Locator: Printed pp. 286–287 / PDF pp. 1–2; Figures 2–3
  • supports Source: Limited Discrepancy Search · Locator: Figure 1, printed p. 608 / PDF p. 2
Limitation #problem-dependence
Discrepancy search depends on the successor heuristic and search-tree structure. The 1996 study includes negative results against DFS, so the original method is not characterized by universal runtime superiority.
  • supports Source: Improved Limited Discrepancy Search · Locator: A Simpler Algorithm and Negative Result and Conclusions, printed pp. 290–291 / PDF pp. 5–6

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Completeness applies to exhaustive passes on a finite tree with a sufficient discrepancy/depth limit and adequate resources. A fixed early-pass cutoff is not a decision procedure for unsolvability.
  • The first goal found is not generally a minimum-cost or shortest-path goal. Successor preference is a search-order heuristic, not an admissible cost bound.
  • The curated original mechanism is binary. Counting any alternate child as one discrepancy and assigning rank-based discrepancy costs are distinct multiway extensions requiring an explicit policy.
  • Repeated at-most-k passes revisit paths. The subsequent ILDS paper qualifies the original redundancy argument and supplies cases where discrepancy search has no advantage over ordinary DFS.
  • The original paper references an LDS-BBS appendix that is not present in the inspected seven-page download. The bounded-backtracking hybrid is not asserted as part of this record.
