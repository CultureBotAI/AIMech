Information-Theoretic Limits of High-Dimensional Binary Graphical Model Selection

Mathematical Methods AIMech:InformationTheoreticLimitsOfHighDimensionalBinaryGraphicalModelSelection DRAFT
Aliases: Information-Theoretic Limits of Selecting Binary Graphical Models in High Dimensions, Santhanam-Wainwright Ising Graphical Model Selection Capacity, Information-Theoretic Sample Complexity of High-Dimensional Ising Model Selection
Information-Theoretic Limits of High-Dimensional Binary Graphical Model Selection is a minimax information-theoretic framework (Santhanam & Wainwright 2012, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 58(7):4117-4134, arXiv:0905.2639v1) that characterizes necessary and sufficient sample sizes n for exact structure recovery of p-vertex pairwise binary Markov random fields (zero-field Ising models P_theta(x) = (1/Z(theta)) exp(sum_{(s,t) in E} theta_{st} x_s x_t) on {-1, +1}^p) over bounded-degree graph families G_{p,d}(lambda, omega) (maximum degree at most d) and bounded-edge graph families G_{p,k}(lambda, omega) (at most k edges) parameterized by minimum edge weight lambda*(theta(G)) := min_{(s,t) in E} |theta_{st}| >= lambda and maximum neighborhood weight omega*(theta(G)) := max_{s in V} sum_{t in N(s)} |theta_{st}| <= omega. By combining Fano's inequality over single-edge, clique-minus-single-edge (K_m \ {(s,t)}), and full-graph packing ensembles with explicit maximum-likelihood and mean-parameter l_infty-projection graph decoders analyzed via symmetric-difference edge matchings and a spin-flipping separation lemma, the framework establishes that sample complexity grows exponentially in omega and that under non-saturation scalings lambda = O(1/d) (for G_{p,d}) or lambda = O(k^{-1/2}) (for G_{p,k}), n > c max{d^2, lambda^{-2}} log p (respectively n > c max{k, lambda^{-2}} log p) samples are necessary for any decoder to achieve worst-case error below 1/2 while n > c' max{d^2, lambda^{-2}} d log p (respectively n > c' k^2 log p for lambda ~ k^{-1/2}) samples suffice.

Distinct from AIMech:HighDimensionalIsingModelSelectionViaL1RegularizedLogisticRegression (Ravikumar, Wainwright, & Lafferty 2010), which analyzes a polynomial-time nodewise l_1-regularized logistic regression estimator under sample/population Fisher mutual incoherence and proves sufficient sample complexity n = Omega(d^3 log p) for theta*_min = Omega(1/d) without establishing algorithm-independent Fano lower bounds, maximum-neighborhood-weight exponential barriers exp(omega/2), or information-theoretic ML/mean-projection decoders without incoherence assumptions. Distinct from AIMech:InformationTheoreticLimitsOfHighDimensionalNoisySparsityRecovery (Wainwright 2009), which studies vector support recovery in linear Gaussian regression Y = X beta + W rather than undirected binary graphical model selection on {-1, +1}^p. Distinct from AIMech:MinimaxInformationTheoreticLowerBoundsViaPackingsAndFanoInequality (Yu 1997; Yang & Barron 1999), which provides general metric-entropy Fano/Le Cam/Assouad machinery rather than Ising-specific clique-minus-edge mean-parameter saturation bounds (Lemmas 1-3), FKG likelihood ratio bounds, symmetric-difference matching divergence bounds (Lemma 4), and spin-flipping separation lemmas (Lemmas 6-8).

Mechanism #clique-minus-edge-mean-saturation-and-fano-necessary-sample-complexity-bounds
For two Ising parameter vectors theta, theta' in R^{C(p,2)}, the symmetrized Kullback-Leibler divergence satisfies S(theta || theta') := D(theta || theta') + D(theta' || theta) = sum_{s != t} (theta_{st} - theta'_{st})(mu_{st} - mu'_{st}) where mu_{st} = E_theta[X_s X_t] (Equations 18, 21-22). On the edge-deleted complete graph G^{st} = K_m \ {(s,t)} with uniform edge weight lambda > 0 and omega = lambda m >= 2, Lemma 1 (Section 4.2, pp. 12-13, Equations 29-31) proves that the likelihood ratio on the missing edge (s, t) satisfies q_{st}/(1 - q_{st}) := P_{theta(G^{st})}[X_s X_t = +1] / P_{theta(G^{st})}[X_s X_t = -1] >= exp(omega/2 - 3 lambda/2)/(m + 1) and therefore E_{theta(G^{st})}[X_s X_t] >= 1 - 2(m + 1) exp(3 lambda/2)/(exp(omega/2) + (m + 1) exp(3 lambda/2)). Applying Fano's inequality (Lemma 2, pp. 11-12, Equations 27-28) to Ensemble A (M = C(p, 2) single-edge graphs with S = 2 lambda tanh(lambda)), Ensemble B (floor(p/(d+1)) cliques of size d+1 minus one edge, where Lemma 2 on p. 14 bounds S(theta(G^{st}) || theta(G^{uv})) <= 8 lambda d exp(3 lambda/2)/exp(lambda d/2)), and Ensemble C (all graphs in G_{p,d}, where Lemma 1(b) on p. 10 bounds log |G_{p,d}| >= (d p/4) log(p/(8d)) and I(X_1^n; G) <= n p) establishes Theorem 1 (p. 6, Equations 8-9): any graph decoder phi has worst-case error >= 1/2 over G_{p,d}(lambda, omega) if n <= max{ log p / (2 lambda tanh(lambda)), exp(omega/4) d lambda log(p d/4 - 1) / (128 exp(3 lambda/2)), (d/8) log(p/(8d))}, yielding n > c max{d^2, lambda^{-2}} log p when lambda = O(1/d) (Corollary 1).

Mechanism #fkg-likelihood-ratio-bound-and-bounded-edge-necessary-conditions
For the bounded-edge class G_{p,k}(lambda, omega), choosing the largest integer m with k + 1 >= C(m, 2) >= floor(sqrt(k)) >= sqrt(k)/2 and forming the family of C(m, 2) subgraphs G^{st} = K_m \ {(s,t)} with uniform edge weight lambda > 0, Lemma 3 (Section 4.4, pp. 15-17, Equations 33-34) applies the Fortuin-Kasteleyn-Ginibre (FKG) inequality to show that for (s, t) != (u, v), P_{theta(G^{uv})}[X_s X_t = +1] / P_{theta(G^{uv})}[X_s X_t = -1] <= P_{theta(K_m)}[X_s X_t = +1] / P_{theta(K_m)}[X_s X_t = -1] = exp(2 lambda) q_{st}/(1 - q_{st}), which combined with Lemma 1 (Equation 29) bounds the symmetrized KL divergence by S(theta(G^{st}) || theta(G^{uv})) <= 4 lambda (exp(2 lambda) - 1)(m + 1)/exp(omega/2 - 3 lambda/2) <= 16 omega exp(5 lambda/2) sinh(lambda) / exp(omega/2). Combining this with Ensemble A via Fano's inequality proves Theorem 2 (Section 3.1, p. 7, Equations 10-11): any decoder has worst-case error >= 1/2 over G_{p,k}(lambda, omega) if n <= max{log p / (2 lambda tanh(lambda)), exp(omega/2) log(k/8) / (64 omega exp(5 lambda/2) sinh(lambda))}, implying n > c max{k, lambda^{-2}} log p when lambda = O(k^{-1/2}) (Corollary 2).

Mechanism #matching-number-chernoff-achievability-and-mean-parameter-projection-decoding
For known edge weights, the maximum likelihood decoder phi*(X_1^n) = argmax_{G in G} (1/n) sum_{i=1}^n log P_{theta(G)}[X^{(i)}] (Equations 35-36) satisfies the pairwise Chernoff bound P[ell_{theta(G')}(X_1^n) >= ell_{theta(G)}(X_1^n)] <= exp(-(n/2) J(theta(G) || theta(G'))) where J(theta || theta') := D((theta + theta')/2 || theta) + D((theta + theta')/2 || theta') = log(Z(theta) Z(theta') / Z^2((theta + theta')/2)) (Lemma 3, p. 18, Equations 19-20, 37). Using the spin-flipping lemma (Lemma 8, Appendix B, pp. 26-27, Equation 56, proving that for any (s, t) in E \ E' flipping x_s, x_t, or both changes Delta(x) by at least |theta_{st}|) and quadratic root contradiction (Lemma 7, Appendix A, pp. 24-26, Equation 50), Lemma 4 (Section 5.2, p. 18, Equation 38) lower bounds J(theta(G) || theta(G')) >= (m(G, G') / (3 exp(2 omega) + 1)) sinh^2(lambda/4) where m(G, G') is the matching number of the symmetric difference E Delta E' (with m(G, G') >= |E Delta E'|/(4d) on G_{p,d}), proving Theorem 3(a) (n = O(max{d^2, lambda^{-2}} d log p) for lambda = O(1/d), Corollary 3) and Theorem 4(a) (n = O(k^2 log p) for lambda ~ k^{-1/2}, Corollary 4). For unknown edge weights, the mean-parameter projection decoder phi^dagger(X_1^n) = argmin_{G in G} min_{theta in Theta_{lambda,omega}(G)} ||mu_hat - mu(theta)||_infty with mu_hat_{st} = (1/n) sum_{i=1}^n X_s^{(i)} X_t^{(i)} (Equations 41-42) combines Hoeffding concentration (Lemma 5, p. 21) with pairwise mean separation max_{u in {s,t}, v in V} |E_{theta(G)}[X_u X_v] - E_{theta(G')}[X_u X_v]| >= sinh^2(lambda/4) / (2 omega (3 exp(2 omega) + 1)) (Lemma 6, pp. 21-23) to achieve error <= delta for n > [omega(3 exp(2 omega) + 1) / sinh^2(lambda/4)]^2 (16 log p + 4 log(2/delta)) (Theorems 3(b) and 4(b), Equation 14).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The analysis is restricted to zero-field pairwise binary Ising Markov random fields on {-1, +1}^p without external node potentials or higher-order clique interactions (Section 2.1, p. 3, Equation 2; Section 6, p. 24).
  • Both the maximum-likelihood graph decoder phi* (Equation 36) and the mean-parameter l_infty-projection decoder phi^dagger (Equation 42) search exhaustively over the combinatorial graph class G_{p,d} or G_{p,k} and are therefore computationally intractable, serving strictly to establish information-theoretic achievability rather than polynomial-time algorithms (Section 1, p. 1; Section 5.1, p. 17; Section 5.5.1, p. 20).
  • For growing maximum degree d under lambda = O(1/d) (and growing edge count k under lambda ~ k^{-1/2}), a multiplicative gap of order d (respectively order k) remains between the necessary sample complexity Omega(d^2 log p) (respectively Omega(k log p)) and the sufficient sample complexity O(d^3 log p) (respectively O(k^2 log p)); moreover, for unknown edge weights, the mean-parameter projection bound scales as lambda^{-4} log p rather than lambda^{-2} log p (Section 3.2, pp. 8-9, Corollaries 3-4; Section 6, p. 24).
  • Primary-source typographical audit of arXiv:0905.2639v1: (1) Equation (3) (p. 3) writes Z(theta) := log[sum_x exp(...)] with an extra logarithm, whereas Equations (2) and (20) and pp. 16 and 18 use Z(theta) = sum_x exp(...) as the partition function; (2) Lemma numbers 1, 2, and 3 are each assigned twice (pp. 10 & 12, pp. 11 & 14, and pp. 15 & 18); (3) Section 4.1.1 (p. 10) writes 'D(theta || theta') != D(theta || theta')' for 'D(theta || theta') != D(theta' || theta)', and Equation (31) (p. 13) indexes the binomial sum over m with exponent (2j - m + 1)^2 - 4 in place of the m - 2 vertices outside {s, t} with exponent (2j - (m - 2) + 2)^2 - 4; (4) Equation (14) (p. 8) and p. 21 print an unclosed parenthesis in '3 exp(2 omega + 1' for '3 exp(2 omega) + 1' and write J_G(G_hat) <= ||mu_hat - theta(G)|| in place of J_G(mu_hat) <= ||mu_hat - mu(theta(G))||_infty; (5) Lemma 4 (p. 18) writes E' \ E' for E' \ E in E Delta E', and Sections 5.3-5.4 (pp. 19-20) write sinh^2(lambda/2) when substituting Lemma 4's sinh^2(lambda/4); and (6) Appendix A (p. 25, line 9) writes alpha(y)/beta(y) in place of beta(y)/alpha(y) in the second factor of Equation (53).
