Author-persona-topic model

Model Families AIMech:AuthorPersonaTopicModel DRAFT
Aliases: APT
A probabilistic topic-model family in which each author has a mixture of personas, each persona has a topic distribution, and one persona shared across a document generates its token topics.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Scoped to the author-persona-topic model in Mimno and McCallum's KDD2007 expertise paper. Author-specific persona mixtures and one persona per whole document distinguish it from the author-topic model, which selects a coauthor per token, and from message-role models with sender/recipient role pairs. Persona counts are fixed by a document-count heuristic in the reported experiment, not inferred by a nonparametric prior. APT is the source acronym in this scope. Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #document-persona-generation
Corpus-shared topic word vectors have Dirichlet priors. Each author has a Dirichlet persona vector and each author/persona pair has a Dirichlet topic vector. A single-author document chooses one persona, then each token independently chooses a topic from that persona and a word from that topic conditional on the parameters. Multi-author publications are replicated once per relevant author and treated as single-author copies; this preprocessing does not infer which coauthor contributed each token.
  • supports Source: Expertise Modeling for Matching Papers with Reviewers · Locator: Section 3, printed pp.501-502 / PDF pp.2-3; Section 3.2.2, printed p.503 / PDF p.4 and Figure 1
Mechanism #collapsed-document-update
With fixed hyperparameters, a collapsed topic update combines leave-token-out persona-topic and shared topic-word predictive factors. A persona update removes the entire document, combines the author-persona document-count factor with a Dirichlet-multinomial ratio for the document's whole topic histogram, and preserves within-document count reinforcement. Topic-word factors cancel when those topic assignments are fixed. The reported persona count is the ceiling of author document count divided by twenty, and each persona coordinate has prior value ten, so total persona concentration grows with the chosen count.
  • supports Source: Expertise Modeling for Matching Papers with Reviewers · Locator: Section 3.2.2, printed pp.503-504 / PDF pp.4-5, Equations 5-6 and parameter discussion
Mechanism #query-persona-sharing
For a query treated as a new document and fixed parameters, the generative law sums over a shared persona outside the product over query tokens. Moving that sum inside the product instead gives independent token personas and a different law. With two equiprobable personas assigning word probability 0.9 and 0.1, a two-copy query has probability 0.41 under a shared persona versus 0.25 under independent token personas. The paper prints plug-in query scoring for its single-author AT comparator but does not provide a complete separate APT ranking formula; this model-law distinction does not certify the historical ranking implementation.
  • supports Source: Expertise Modeling for Matching Papers with Reviewers · Locator: Section 3.2.2, printed p.503 / PDF p.4, generative process; Section 3.3, printed pp.504-505 / PDF pp.5-6, Equations 7-9

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Persona counts and topic counts are fixed heuristics in the evaluated construction. The displayed persona rule needs an additional policy for an author with no documents; none is invented here. Mentioned logarithmic and Dirichlet-process variants are not reported complete methods in this evidence.
  • The study uses 148 accepted NIPS2006 papers and 364 reviewers drawn from NIPS2005 according to its footnote. The 650 pooled judgments cover candidates retrieved by the compared systems, not every paper/reviewer pair. Authors and conflicts are not excluded, so this is relevance ranking rather than a complete constrained reviewer-assignment algorithm.
  • Reported comparisons depend on relevance thresholds, disagreement handling, 75 or 200 topics and averaging ten chains after specified sampling. The single-document language model leads at the first five ranks, while APT200 improves several later ranks; no universal superiority, convergence or objectively correct persona interpretation follows.
  • Finite calculations verify collapsed assignment probabilities and document-level sharing on synthetic examples. They do not reproduce author software, ranking scores or the benchmark.
