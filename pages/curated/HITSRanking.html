HITS hub and authority ranking

Algorithms AIMech:HITSRanking DRAFT
Aliases: HITS
A link-analysis ranking algorithm that constructs a query-focused directed page graph and alternately propagates authority scores from incoming hub scores and hub scores from outgoing authority scores, normalizing the vectors and selecting their largest coordinates.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #query-graph
Start from the top t text-query results, then add every outgoing neighbor and at most d incoming neighbors of each root page, taking an arbitrary subset when the latter bound is exceeded. This is one expansion from the root set, not recursive crawl closure. Form the induced graph on the base set and remove links between pages on the same domain. The source uses typical t=200 and d=50. A proposed cap on referrals from one domain was not used in the reported experiments.
  • supports Source: Authoritative Sources in a Hyperlinked Environment · Locator: Section 2, Subgraph and Figure 1, printed pp. 4–6 / PDF pp. 5–7
Mechanism #sequential-propagation
Let A[u,v]=1 for a retained link u to v. Initialize authority x and hub y to all ones. Each Iterate step computes x_new=A^T y_old, then y_new=A x_new using the newly computed authority vector, and separately normalizes both by their Euclidean norms. Filter selects the c largest coordinates of each vector. Ranking after graph construction uses links alone in this original procedure. It is not indegree ranking, simultaneous updates with both old vectors, or relevance-weighted propagation.
  • supports Source: Authoritative Sources in a Hyperlinked Environment · Locator: Section 3, Figures 2–3, Iterate and Filter, printed pp. 7–11 / PDF pp. 8–12; finite graph arithmetic in research/discovery/hits-boundaries.py
Limitation #spectral-boundary
The convergence argument assumes a strict leading eigenvalue gap for the nonnegative Gram matrices A^T A and A A^T, with all-ones initialization supplying a nonzero leading projection. Authority and hub directions approach their principal eigendirections under these assumptions. The reported stabilization after approximately 20 iterations is an empirical observation, not a universal stopping rule. The printed algorithm does not specify a zero-norm policy for an edgeless graph.
  • supports Source: Authoritative Sources in a Hyperlinked Environment · Locator: Section 3, Theorems 3.1–3.2 and following discussion, printed pp. 10–11 / PDF pp. 11–12
Scope #query-and-topic-scope
For a similar-page query, the source replaces the text-query root with pages linking to the supplied page and applies the same expansion and ranking machinery. Broad-topic authority is a graph-based relevance heuristic. For narrow queries, the strongest linked theme can diffuse into a broader or different topic; high link authority does not establish factual correctness, exhaustive retrieval or a human community boundary.
  • supports Source: Authoritative Sources in a Hyperlinked Environment · Locator: Sections 4 and 7, printed pp. 12–14,24–25 / PDF pp. 13–15,25–26
Limitation #evaluation-boundary
The primary manuscript presents historical ranked URL lists for broad and similar-page queries. Section 8 separately summarizes user studies of systems augmented with text and link heuristics and explicitly distinguishes these from direct evaluation of the pure algorithm. Those enhanced-system results are not recorded here as HITS accuracy, benchmark wins or contemporary web effectiveness.
  • supports Source: Authoritative Sources in a Hyperlinked Environment · Locator: Sections 4 and 8, printed pp. 12–14,25–27 / PDF pp. 13–15,26–28

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The concept covers the original query-focused principal ranking algorithm. The Section 6 nonprincipal extraction and Chakrabarti relevance-weighted distiller are distinct procedures.
  • Graph acquisition, domain filtering, initialization and selection ties affect the result. The source does not give a universal tie, degeneracy or zero-score policy, nor an arbitrary-graph convergence-rate guarantee.
  • A constructed eleven-vertex graph has one authority of indegree four and three authorities of indegree three; mutual reinforcement favors the latter component. This is a finite algebraic example, not a web experiment or benchmark replication.
  • All 34 manuscript pages, references and relevant rendered equations, algorithms and result lists were inspected. Author code, historical graph snapshots and primary enhanced-system user studies were not inspected or executed.
  • The author copy has different pagination from journal metadata. Source locators identify that copy. NetworkX documentation corroborates the HITS name but its modern implementation options are outside this record; DRAFT remains.

Artificial Intelligence Ontology (AIO) Grounding

Explicit AIO Snapshot Assessment: No Match

Rationale: No suitable AIO counterpart was found for the original hub/authority ranking procedure in this snapshot. Its graph propagation and query selection do not make it a classifier, language model, teacher–student distiller, centered PCA or clustering task. The separate nonprincipal extension has an implicit-clustering relationship; that relationship is not transferred to this principal ranking record.
