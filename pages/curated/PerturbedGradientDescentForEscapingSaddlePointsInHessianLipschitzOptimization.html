Perturbed Gradient Descent for Escaping Saddle Points in Hessian-Lipschitz Optimization

Algorithms AIMech:PerturbedGradientDescentForEscapingSaddlePointsInHessianLipschitzOptimization DRAFT
Aliases: Jin-Ge-Netrapalli-Kakade-Jordan Perturbed Gradient Descent (PGD), Hessian-Free Second-Order Stationary Point Convergence via Isotropic Ball Perturbation, Thin-Pancake Stuck-Region Escape for Strict Saddle Optimization
Perturbed Gradient Descent for Escaping Saddle Points in Hessian-Lipschitz Optimization is a purely first-order non-convex optimization algorithm and geometric saddle-escape theory introduced by Jin, Ge, Netrapalli, Kakade, and Jordan (2017) for l-gradient-Lipschitz (||grad f(x_1) - grad f(x_2)|| <= l ||x_1 - x_2||) and rho-Hessian-Lipschitz (||grad^2 f(x_1) - grad^2 f(x_2)|| <= rho ||x_1 - x_2||) objectives f: R^d -> R. Whenever the gradient norm satisfies ||grad f(x_t)|| <= g_thres and at least t_thres steps have elapsed since the previous perturbation, Algorithm 1 adds a single isotropic uniform perturbation xi_t ~ Unif(B_0(r)) of radius r = tilde{Theta}(epsilon / l) and continues standard gradient descent x_{t+1} = x_t - eta grad f(x_t) with step size eta = tilde{Theta}(1 / l). By bounding the stuck region X_stuck subseteq B_{tilde{x}}(r) via a two-sequence coupling lemma (Lemma 15 and Lemma 17) showing that along the minimum-eigenvalue direction e_1 of grad^2 f(tilde{x}) (with lambda_min(grad^2 f(tilde{x})) <= -sqrt(rho epsilon)) the stuck set has width at most delta * r / sqrt(d) along e_1 (forming a 'thin pancake' of volume fraction at most delta), Theorem 3 proves that Perturbed Gradient Descent finds an epsilon-second-order stationary point (||grad f(x)|| <= epsilon and lambda_min(grad^2 f(x)) >= -sqrt(rho epsilon)) in O(l (f(x_0) - f^*) / epsilon^2 * log^4(d l Delta_f / (epsilon^2 delta))) gradient evaluations, matching unperturbed gradient descent's first-order complexity up to polylogarithmic factors without computing or inverting any Hessians.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from Strict-Saddle Property and Noisy Stochastic Gradient Saddle Escaping for Orthogonal Tensor Decomposition (`AIMech:StrictSaddlePropertyAndNoisyStochasticGradientSaddleEscapingForOrthogonalTensorDecomposition`), which injects noise at every stochastic step with poly(d / epsilon) iteration dependence, and from Gradient Descent Avoidance of Strict Saddle Points via Stable Manifold Theorem (`AIMech:GradientDescentAvoidanceOfStrictSaddlePointsViaStableManifoldTheorem`), which establishes asymptotic measure-zero avoidance without quantitative polynomial iteration bounds. Specifically captures the Jin et al. (2017) thresholded ball-perturbation rule (Algorithm 1), the two-point coupling width bound for the thin-pancake stuck region (Lemmas 14-17), the dimension-free log^4(d)/epsilon^2 rate (Theorem 3), and the global strict-saddle/regularity decomposition for symmetric low-rank matrix factorization (Lemmas 6-7).

Mechanism #claim-01
For any l-gradient-Lipschitz and rho-Hessian-Lipschitz function f: R^d -> R, if x_tilde satisfies ||grad f(x_tilde)|| <= g_thres and lambda_min(grad^2 f(x_tilde)) <= -sqrt(rho epsilon), then for any two initial perturbations w_0 = u_0 + mu * r * e_1 in B_{x_tilde}(r) separated by mu * r along the minimum eigenvector e_1 of H = grad^2 f(x_tilde), at least one trajectory {u_t} or {w_t} leaves B_{x_tilde}(F * l) within T < t_thres steps and decreases the objective by f(x_tilde) - f(x_{t_0 + t_thres}) >= f_thres = Omega(sqrt(epsilon^3 / rho) / log^3(d l Delta_f / (epsilon^2 delta))), implying that the stuck region X_stuck occupies volume fraction at most delta in B_{x_tilde}(r) and yielding an epsilon-second-order stationary point in tilde{O}(l (f(x_0) - f^*) / epsilon^2) total gradient steps.

  • supports Source: How to Escape Saddle Points Efficiently (arXiv abstract) · Locator: Abstract: Perturbed GD converges to second-order stationary points in tilde{O}(1/epsilon^2) iterations with log^4(d) dimension dependence
Mechanism #claim-02
For symmetric low-rank matrix factorization min_{U in R^{d x r}} (1/2) ||U U^T - M^*||_F^2 where M^* = V^* (V^*)^T has top and r-th singular values sigma_1^*, sigma_r^* > 0, the objective satisfies the exact algebraic Hessian identity grad^2 f(U)(Delta, Delta) = ||Delta Delta^T||_F^2 - 3 ||U U^T - M^*||_F^2 + 4 <grad f(U), Delta> for Delta = U - U^* (where U^* = V^* R_U aligns U to V^*), is globally (theta, gamma, zeta)-strict-saddle with gamma = (1/3) sigma_r^*, and satisfies the local (alpha = (1/3) sigma_r^*, beta = 1/(20 sigma_1^*))-regularity condition in the neighborhood of every global minimum.
  • supports Source: How to Escape Saddle Points Efficiently · Locator: Section 4, Lemmas 6 and 7, pp. 10-11; Appendix C, Proof of Lemma 7 and Equations 21-26, pp. 31-35
  • supports Source: How to Escape Saddle Points Efficiently (Crossref metadata) · Locator: Abstract: Characterization of global strict-saddle geometry and local regularity for matrix Sensing and Factorization
Limitation #claim-03
Unperturbed deterministic gradient descent initialized inside the codimension-1 orthogonal stuck subspace (where the component along the negative eigendirection e_1 is zero) remains trapped in the strict-saddle manifold forever without perturbation, and Perturbed Gradient Descent cannot guarantee polynomial-time escape from degenerate higher-order saddle points where grad f(x) = 0 and lambda_min(grad^2 f(x)) = 0.
  • supports Source: How to Escape Saddle Points Efficiently · Locator: Section 1, Introduction (strict vs higher-order saddles and NP-hardness), pp. 1-3; Section 3, Intuition of Thin Pancake Stuck Region, pp. 7-8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Guarantees escape only from strict (second-order) saddle points where lambda_min(grad^2 f(x)) <= -sqrt(rho epsilon); escaping degenerate third- or higher-order saddle points can be NP-hard in general.
