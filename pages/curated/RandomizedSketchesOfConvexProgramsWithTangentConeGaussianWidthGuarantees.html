Aliases: Randomized Sketches of Convex Programs with Sharp Guarantees (Pilanci & Wainwright 2015), Tangent-Cone Gaussian-Width Sketched Convex Programming, S-Gaussian and Rademacher Width Randomized Orthonormal System Convex Sketching, Privacy-Preserving Sketched Convex Optimization with Vanishing Mutual Information Rate, Second-Order Set-Difference Covering Reduction for Sketched Convex Cones
Randomized Sketches of Convex Programs with Tangent-Cone Gaussian-Width Cost-Approximation and Privacy Mutual-Information Guarantees is a convex-analytic dimensionality-reduction and privacy-preserving optimization framework introduced by Pilanci and Wainwright (2015, IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 61(9):5096-5115, arXiv:1404.7203v1, DOI:10.1109/TIT.2015.2450722) for approximating constrained convex quadratic programs x^* in argmin_{x in C} f(x) := ||A x - y||_2^2 over an arbitrary convex constraint set C subseteq R^d by solving the projected program x_hat in argmin_{x in C} g(x) := ||S(A x - y)||_2^2 with a sketching matrix S in R^{m x n} (m < n). Let K := cl conv {Delta = t(x - x^*) : t >= 0, x in C} denote the tangent cone of C at x^* (Equation 4) and AK = {A Delta : Delta in K} its linear image with Gaussian width W(AK) := E_g[sup_{z in AK cap S^{n-1}} |<g, z>|] (Equation 5). Lemma 1 (p. 17, Equations 41a-45) proves that for any deterministic matrix S, the relative cost ratio satisfies f(x_hat) <= {1 + 2 Z_2(AK) / Z_1(AK)}^2 f(x^*), where Z_1(AK) := inf_{v in AK cap S^{n-1}} (1/m)||S v||_2^2 and Z_2(AK) := sup_{v in AK cap S^{n-1}} |<u, (S^T S / m - I) v>| for u = (A x^* - y)/||A x^* - y||_2. For sigma-sub-Gaussian sketches (Theorem 1, Equation 7), sketch size m >= (c_0 / delta^2) W^2(AK) guarantees multiplicative delta-optimality f(x_hat) <= (1 + delta)^2 f(x^*) with probability at least 1 - c_1 e^{-c_2 m delta^2}, while Corollary 1 (Equation 8) proves that Gaussian sketching of i.i.d. data matrices with entry variance gamma^2 bounds the mutual information per symbol by I(S A; A)/(n d) <= (c_0 / delta^2) (W^2(AK)/n) log(2 pi e gamma^2), which vanishes whenever W^2(AK) = o(n). For randomized orthonormal system (ROS) sketches s_i = sqrt(n) D H^T p_i (Theorem 2, Equation 11) and non-convex set-difference covering reductions (Section 5, Lemmas 8-13), the framework establishes sharp sketch-dimension bounds for unconstrained least squares, l_1-constrained Lasso, compressed sensing with folded noise, simplex-constrained dual SVMs and Markowitz portfolios, weighted nuclear-norm matrix approximation, and group-sparse second-order cone programs (Corollaries 2-7).

Mechanism #tangent-cone-cost-approximation-lemma-and-privacy-mutual-information-rate
For the convex program x^* in argmin_{x in C} f(x) := ||A x - y||_2^2 and its sketched counterpart x_hat in argmin_{x in C} g(x) := ||S(A x - y)||_2^2 with tangent cone K = cl conv{t(x - x^*) : t >= 0, x in C} (Equations 1-4), let Z_1(AK) := inf_{v in AK cap S^{n-1}} (1/m) ||S v||_2^2 and Z_2(AK) := sup_{v in AK cap S^{n-1}} |<u, (S^T S / m - I) v>| with fixed unit residual u = (A x^* - y)/||A x^* - y||_2 (Equations 41a-41b). Using the convex optimality condition <A^T(A x^* - y), x_hat - x^*> >= 0 and g(x_hat) <= g(x^*), Lemma 1 (pp. 17-18, Equations 42-45) proves the deterministic inequalities ||A(x_hat - x^*)||_2 / ||A x^* - y||_2 <= 2 Z_2(AK) / Z_1(AK) and f(x_hat) <= {1 + 2 Z_2(AK) / Z_1(AK)}^2 f(x^*). For sigma-sub-Gaussian sketches, Lemmas 2 and 3 (pp. 19-21, Equations 46-55, using orthant splitting V_+ cup V_- and Sudakov-Fernique projection comparison) show that m >= (c_0 / delta^2) W^2(AK) guarantees Z_1(AK) >= 1 - delta and Z_2(AK) <= delta, establishing Theorem 1 (Equation 7): f(x_hat) <= (1 + delta)^2 f(x^*) with probability at least 1 - c_1 e^{-c_2 m delta^2}. When A in R^{n x d} has i.i.d. entries of finite variance gamma^2, combining Theorem 1 with the Gaussian mutual-information upper bound I(S A; A)/(n d) <= (m / (2n)) log(2 pi e gamma^2) yields Corollary 1 (p. 4, Equation 8): m = (c_0 / delta^2) W^2(AK) achieves delta-optimality while bounding per-symbol mutual information by (c_0 / delta^2) (W^2(AK)/n) log(2 pi e gamma^2) -> 0 whenever W^2(AK) = o(n).

Mechanism #ros-s-gaussian-rademacher-width-bounds-and-set-difference-covering-sharpening
For a randomized orthonormal system (ROS) sketch S in R^{m x n} with rows s_i^T = sqrt(n) p_i^T H diag(nu) formed in O(d n log m) time from a Hadamard or Fourier matrix H and Rademacher signs nu in {-1, +1}^n (Section 2.3, p. 5), define the S-Gaussian width W_S(AK) := E_{g,S}[sup_{z in AK cap S^{n-1}} |<g, S z / sqrt(m)>|] and Rademacher width R(AK) := E_eps[sup_{z in AK cap S^{n-1}} |<z, eps>|] (Equations 9-10). By conditioning on the high-probability Rademacher truncation events G_1 cap G_2 (Equations 60-63) and applying Talagrand's empirical-process concentration and Ledoux-Talagrand contraction (Lemmas 4-7, Proposition 2, pp. 21-24 and Appendix B, pp. 31-32), Theorem 2 (Equation 11) proves that m / log m > (c_0 / delta^2) (R^2(AK) + log n) W_S^2(AK) guarantees delta-optimality with probability at least 1 - c_1/(mn)^2 - c_1 exp(-c_2 m delta^2 / (R^2(AK) + log(mn))). Furthermore, to eliminate the extra R^2(AK) factor for structured cones, Section 5.2 (p. 26, Lemma 8, Equations 66-68; proved in Appendix C, pp. 32-33) shows that if 0 in Y_0, Y_1 is convex, Y_1 subseteq cl conv(Y_0), partial^2[Y_0] subseteq alpha Y_1, and Pi(partial^2[Y_0]) subseteq alpha Y_1, then any eps-cover {z^1, ..., z^M} of partial[Y_0] = Y_0 - Y_0 with eps in (0, 1/(27 alpha^2)] satisfies sup_{z in Y_1} |z^T Q z| <= 3 max_{j in [M]} |(z^j)^T Q z^j| for any symmetric matrix Q, which combined with Lemma 13 (Appendix D, p. 34, Equation 86: B_F(1) cap B_nuc(2 sqrt(r)) subseteq cl conv(B_F(3) cap B_rank(4r)) via support functions phi_1(X) <= (1 + sqrt(r/floor(r))) phi_0(X) <= 3 phi_0(X)) and Krahmer-Ward RIP-to-JL embeddings yields sharpened ROS bounds (Lemmas 9-12).

Scope #concrete-sketch-dimension-instantiations-across-convex-programs
Specializing Theorems 1-2 and Lemmas 8-13 yields concrete sketch-dimension bounds for delta-optimal cost approximation across six problem families in Section 3: (1) Unconstrained least squares (Corollary 2, p. 6): m > c_0 rank(A) / delta^2 for sub-Gaussian sketches and m > c_0' (rank(A) / delta^2) log^4(n) for ROS sketches; (2) l_1-constrained least squares / Lasso (Corollary 3, p. 8, Equations 13-20) with k-sparse optimum x^*: m >= (c_0 / delta^2) min{rank(A), (max_j ||a_j||_2^2 / gamma_k^-(A)) k log d} for sub-Gaussian sketches and m > (c_0' / delta^2) log^4(n) min{rank(A), ((max_j ||a_j||_2^2 / gamma_k^-(A)) k log d)^2 / log^4(n), (gamma_k^+(A) / gamma_k^-(A))^2 k log d} for ROS sketches; (3) Compressed sensing and noise-folded denoising (Corollary 4, pp. 10-12, Equations 21-25, setting A = I_n in min_{||x||_1 <= R} ||x - y||_2^2): exact recovery x_hat = x_bar in the noiseless case f(x^*) = 0 and ||x_hat - y||_2^2 <= (1 + delta)^2 ||x^* - y||_2^2 under folded noise S y = S x_bar + S w using m >= (c_0 / delta^2) ||x^*||_0 log d sub-Gaussian or (c_0 / delta^2) min{||x^*||_0 log^5 d, ||x^*||_0^2 log d} ROS projections; (4) Dual squared-hinge support vector machines and Markowitz portfolio allocation over the simplex (Corollary 5, pp. 12-14, Equations 26-30): m >= (c_0 / delta^2) ||x^*||_0 log(d) max_j ||a_j||_2^2 / gamma_k^-(A) scaling with the number of support vectors ||x^*||_0 on the margin; (5) Weighted low-rank nuclear-norm matrix approximation (Corollary 6, pp. 14-16, Equations 31-37): m >= (c_0 / delta^2) kappa^2(omega) r (d_1 + d_2) (sub-Gaussian) and with an additional log^4(d_1 d_2) factor for ROS; and (6) Group-sparse SOCP least squares (Corollary 7, pp. 16-17, Equations 38-40) with k active groups and max group size M: O((k log |G| + k M) / delta^2).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Cost-Value Multiplicative Approximation vs. Statistical Parameter Estimation Under Noise: As analyzed in the companion paper Pilanci & Wainwright (2016, JMLR 17(53):1-38), Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 of Pilanci & Wainwright (2015) bound the worst-case optimization cost ratio f(x_hat) <= (1 + delta)^2 f(x^*) for a single joint sketch ||S(A x - y)||_2^2; when y = A x_0 + w contains additive noise with f(x^*) = O(n sigma^2), a single joint sketch of dimension m << n incurs O(delta ||A x^* - y||_2) residual-to-parameter cross-term error unless f(x^*) is small (as in noiseless compressed sensing, Corollary 4) or iterative Hessian sketching is used.
  • Optimization Dimension in Vectorized Nuclear-Norm Matrix Sketching: As explicitly noted in Section 3.5 (p. 16), for weighted low-rank matrix approximation min_{|||X|||_nuc <= R} ||S y - S A vec(X)||_2^2 (Corollary 6), sketching reduces the measurement dimension from D = d_1 d_2 to m = O(r(d_1 + d_2)) for storage and privacy-sensitive optimization, but the matrix variable X itself remains d_1 x d_2 dimensional.
  • Primary-Source Typographical Notes in Pilanci & Wainwright (2015, arXiv:1404.7203v1): Verified five self-correcting typographical details across the 37-page manuscript: (1) Section 2.2 (p. 4, Equation 6) writes the sub-Gaussian tail exponent as -n t^2 / (2 sigma^2) whereas Proposition 1 (p. 19) uses isotropic rows cov(s_i) = I_n (so that the 1/sqrt(m) normalization is applied inside S^T S / m); (2) Section 4.1 (p. 18, Equations 44-45) and Section 4.2 (p. 19, Lemma 3, Equation 47) omit the 1/m normalization factor on S^T S in (1/2)||S A e_hat||_2^2 and <u, (S^T S - I) v> (which is explicitly included in Lemma 1's definitions of Z_1(AK) and Z_2(AK) in Equations 41a-41b on p. 17); (3) In Section 4.2.2 (p. 20), Equation (51a) prints W(V) instead of W(U_+), the text below (51c) omits 1/sqrt(m) on 4 c_1 W(U_+), Equation (52) prints |u^T Q u| on the left-hand side instead of sup_{v in V_+} |u^T Q v| (and omits 1/sqrt(m), which is restored in Equation 55 on p. 21), and Equation (53) prints W(V) instead of W(U_+); (4) Section 3.6 (p. 17, Corollary 7, Equation 40) prints the unsquared operator norm |||A_g|||_op in the numerator instead of |||A_g|||_op^2 (needed to match the quadratic scaling of gamma_{k,G}^-(A) and to reduce to max_j ||a_j||_2^2 / gamma_k^-(A) in Equation 15 when |G| = d, M = 1); and (5) Section 5.2 (p. 25, Equation 65) writes Z(Y) = sup_{y in Y} |y^T (A^T S^T S A / m - I) y| with -I instead of -A^T A (corrected to R = A^T S^T S A / m - A^T A in the proof of Lemma 9 on pp. 26-27).
