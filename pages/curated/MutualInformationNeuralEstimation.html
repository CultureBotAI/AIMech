Mutual information neural estimation

Algorithms AIMech:MutualInformationNeuralEstimation DRAFT
Aliases: MINE, Mutual information neural estimator
A mutual-information estimation procedure that trains a neural statistics function to maximize a Donsker–Varadhan objective contrasting joint samples with product-of-marginals samples, using stochastic optimization and an optional moving-average denominator for gradient estimation.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope MINE denotes the original neural Donsker–Varadhan estimator and its moving-average training heuristic. The paper’s alternative MINE-f objective is a different variational bound and is not silently equated with this record. MINE estimates dependence; DIM is a representation-learning framework that can use dependence critics, and InfoNCE is a different finite-candidate objective. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a neural statistics function T_theta(x,z), optimize J(theta)=E_joint[T_theta]−log(E_product[exp(T_theta)]). Under the variational representation’s integrability assumptions, each population J(theta) is a lower bound on I(X;Z); optimizing within a neural family yields its restricted supremum. Estimate expectations from observed joint pairs and samples approximating the product of marginals, such as mismatched or shuffled pairs. The minibatch gradient contains a ratio of product-sample means and is generally biased. The original training heuristic replaces its exponential-moment denominator by an exponential moving average to reduce this bias. Poole et al. show that, when a positive running denominator is held fixed for an update, the resulting update has an interpretation as the gradient of a different unnormalized variational bound. This interpretation does not make the moving-average update a generally unbiased gradient estimator of the population Donsker–Varadhan objective. After fitting a critic, evaluating sample means in the Donsker–Varadhan expression gives a finite estimate, not a certified mutual-information lower bound for that sample.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Finite-sample log-moment estimation is biased, can be unstable for large critic scores, and need not remain below true mutual information. Reusing samples for critic fitting adds selection effects.
  • The moving-average denominator is a bias-reduction heuristic. Its fixed-denominator alternative-bound interpretation does not prove that a same-batch updated running statistic is independent of the current samples or that the original Donsker–Varadhan gradient is unbiased.
  • Strong-consistency arguments separate neural approximation from empirical estimation under compactness, continuity and measure assumptions. The fixed-family uniform-convergence result is not a proof that stochastic gradient optimization reaches the required supremum. Bounded/Lipschitz critic sample-complexity assumptions do not hold automatically for arbitrary networks and distributions.
  • Shuffling one finite batch can include fixed points and dependent pairs; it is not literally independent sampling from the population product distribution. Sampling construction must be stated when interpreting estimates.
  • As a mathematical consequence of a loose lower bound, reducing a poorly optimized estimated lower bound does not certify a reduction in true mutual information. No author implementation or numerical estimator experiment was audited.
