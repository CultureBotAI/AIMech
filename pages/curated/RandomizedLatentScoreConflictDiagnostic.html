Randomized latent score conflict diagnostic

Algorithms AIMech:RandomizedLatentScoreConflictDiagnostic DRAFT
Aliases: Randomised latent score conflict diagnostic
A Bayesian hierarchical-model diagnostic that freezes draws from a parent posterior excluding selected child data, draws child latent parameters conditionally, and compares derivatives of an expanded conditional log prior with scores of conditional-prior replicates. The resulting randomized tail values are examined and optionally combined as a model-conflict summary, with calibration depending on the actual sampling law, score direction and aggregation.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

8 Claims
Scope #latent-expansion-scope
Select a child component and a parent/remaining-data component of a Bayesian hierarchical model. Expand a specified conditional latent prior through a scalar parameter alpha, with the original model at alpha0. The derivative of its log density supplies a discrepancy aimed at that expansion direction. This checks a chosen latent relationship; it is not a generic observed-data residual, a contrast between two independently fitted separator copies, or a method for learning a neural score function.
  • supports Source: Detecting Conflicts in Evidence Synthesis Models Using Score Discrepancies · Locator: Sections 2.5 and 3.1–3.3, PDF pp. 10–18; Algorithm 1, p. 16
Mechanism #frozen-parent-conditional-sampling
Algorithm 1 draws theta1 from the parent posterior q_parent(theta1) fitted without the selected child data. For each such draw it fixes theta1 and draws theta2 from the normalized conditional child posterior p(theta2|y_child,theta1). Thus its ideal sampling law is q_parent(theta1)*p(theta2|y_child,theta1), retaining the parent weights. An ordinary full update would additionally weight theta1 by the child marginal likelihood. The two laws generally differ; the algorithm is read by its fixed-parent steps rather than treating sequential updating as automatic equivalence to the full posterior. Proper parent and conditional distributions are required.
  • supports Source: Detecting Conflicts in Evidence Synthesis Models Using Score Discrepancies · Locator: Sections 2.5 and 3.1, PDF pp. 13–15; Algorithm 1 steps 1–2, p. 16
Mechanism #randomized-score-not-posterior-average
For each sampled pair compute s(theta1,theta2)=d log p(theta2|theta1,alpha)/d alpha at alpha0, including parameter-dependent normalizers. Under differentiation/integration regularity, the marginal-likelihood score discussed in the background is a posterior expectation of a log-prior score. Algorithm 1 instead retains randomized latent scores. A tail probability evaluated at one score, an average of such tails and a tail evaluated at the mean score are different quantities and must not be substituted for one another.
  • supports Source: Detecting Conflicts in Evidence Synthesis Models Using Score Discrepancies · Locator: Section 2.5, Equations 2–8, PDF pp. 11–14; Algorithm 1 step 4, p. 16; Appendix B, p. 37
Mechanism #conditional-reference-and-monte-carlo
For each fixed parent draw, sample G latent reference values from p(theta2|theta1,alpha0) and compute their scores. The source estimates an upper score-tail probability by the fraction of reference scores at least as large as the child-posterior score. These are latent-prior replicates, not new observed datasets. The estimate can be zero and depends on G, ties and Monte Carlo dependence. Exact Monte Carlo test validity requires an appropriate exchangeability/rank argument; modifying the count with a finite-sample correction alone does not repair a miscalibrated underlying reference law.
  • supports Source: Detecting Conflicts in Evidence Synthesis Models Using Score Discrepancies · Locator: Algorithm 1 steps 4–5, PDF p. 16; Section 3.3, p. 18
Limitation #score-direction-matters
Specify both the expansion parameterization and the tail used to flag conflict. A decreasing reparameterization reverses the score sign and exchanges upper and lower ordering. For a normal conditional prior, expanding variance gives a score increasing with squared standardized distance, whereas expanding precision gives the opposite ordering. Appendix B uses a precision-style expansion whose deviance contribution decreases with deviance. An upper raw-score tail therefore cannot be assumed to detect large deviations for every expansion. A normalizing constant near one does not itself justify dropping its derivative.
  • supports Source: Detecting Conflicts in Evidence Synthesis Models Using Score Discrepancies · Locator: Section 2.5, PDF pp. 11–14; Section 3.2, p. 17; Algorithm 1, p. 16; Appendix B, p. 37
Limitation #marginal-calibration-boundary
A draw from the full joint posterior, averaged over data from the matching proper prior predictive, has the joint prior as its marginal law. This identity does not automatically apply to Algorithm 1 after parent feedback is cut. Uniform score-tail values additionally require the stated reference law and continuous score, or a justified treatment of ties. Hierarchical conditioning, retained parent uncertainty and the sampling ensemble matter. The accompanying constructed Gaussian calculation shows a frozen-parent standardized latent residual X with variance greater than one even under a proper model; its variance-expansion score s=(X^2-1)/2 therefore need not have uniform reference-tail values; it is a mathematical boundary example, not a reproduction of the source experiments.
  • supports Source: Detecting Conflicts in Evidence Synthesis Models Using Score Discrepancies · Locator: Section 2.4, PDF pp. 8–9; Section 2.5, pp. 13–14; Algorithm 1, p. 16; Appendix A, p. 36; Appendix D, pp. 38–44
Mechanism #dependent-score-aggregation
The source summarizes the dependent randomized tail values using either a minimum over order-statistic upper bounds or a weighted half-Cauchy transformation sum w_m*cot(pi*p_m/2), with a Landau approximation described for the latter. A valid bound at a fixed rank does not by itself validate minimizing across ranks. Uniform marginals alone do not guarantee validity of that adaptive minimum under arbitrary dependence. Likewise the half-Cauchy reference needs its own dependence, tail and approximation conditions, and zero estimates require explicit handling. These summaries are not asserted here to provide general finite-sample error control or a posterior probability that conflict is true.
  • supports Source: Detecting Conflicts in Evidence Synthesis Models Using Score Discrepancies · Locator: Section 2.4, PDF pp. 9–10; Algorithm 1 step 6, p. 16; Section 3.4, p. 18; discussion, pp. 34–35
Limitation #interpretation-and-computation
The diagnostic depends on the selected split, latent parameterization, expansion direction, prior, approximation and aggregation. It can suggest where to investigate model inconsistency, but it does not establish which source is wrong. Repeated conditional child fits and nested reference sampling can be costly; MCMC convergence and effective sample size matter. The paper recommends examining the distribution of randomized tail values alongside a combined summary and warns that conventional thresholds can be uninformative. Its examples do not establish universal power, calibration or superiority.
  • supports Source: Detecting Conflicts in Evidence Synthesis Models Using Score Discrepancies · Locator: Sections 3–6, PDF pp. 15–35, especially discussion pp. 34–35

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This descriptive local identity denotes the randomized latent score procedure in Yang, Nott and Presanis Algorithm 1, not all prior-expansion checks, pivotal diagnostics, node splitting, cut inference or half-Cauchy combination tests.
  • The source is the 2026-09-12 arXiv v2 preprint. Its full 52-page PDF, all figures and tables and references were inspected; this is not a claim of peer-review status or external empirical validation.
  • Fixed-parent conditional sampling differs from full posterior sampling. Neither the prior-posterior marginal identity nor uniform marginal scores can be assumed after cutting feedback. Constructed calculations qualify these claims without fitting the source models.
  • Score sign, normalizing derivatives, continuity/ties, Monte Carlo zeros and dependent aggregation must be specified. The separate primary theory for half-Cauchy combination has not yet been fully inspected; no general error-control guarantee is imported from a secondary description.
  • Source application inconsistencies are retained in the discovery report: a Table 2 threshold marker, GP versus HOS interpretation around Table 3, and a beta-binomial marginalization that does not preserve the latent probability joint law when replaced by a count ratio. No application outcome or joint-model equivalence is imported.
  • No MCMC fitting, source implementation execution, biomedical inference, benchmark or repeated-data empirical calibration was performed. Constructed Gaussian and uniform-interval examples check mathematical limits only.
