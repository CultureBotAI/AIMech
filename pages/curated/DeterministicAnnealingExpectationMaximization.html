Deterministic Annealing Expectation Maximization

Algorithms AIMech:DeterministicAnnealingExpectationMaximization DRAFT
Aliases: Deterministic Annealing EM, DAEM
A latent-variable likelihood-fitting procedure that performs deterministic powered-posterior EM updates along a warm-started cooling schedule from a small positive inverse temperature to one, including an ordinary-likelihood fit at the endpoint.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope DAEM denotes the Ueda-Nakano outer cooling procedure, whose finite-state fixed-temperature update overlaps the existing tempered-EM framework. It is retained as a named schedule-defined specialization, not a disjoint update family. Hofmann’s held-out inverse-annealing realization starts at one and decreases inverse temperature, whereas this procedure increases it to one for likelihood fitting. CAEM samples hard assignments; DAEM uses fractional expectations. Neither deterministic procedure is Metropolis simulated annealing. Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #powered-joint-stages
At fixed beta>0 define f_beta(z|x,theta)=p(x,z|theta)^beta/Z_beta(x,theta), where Z is a positive finite latent sum or integral. The power applies to the whole joint density, including mixing weights. Compute expectations under f_beta at the current parameters, then maximize the expected complete log likelihood with that distribution held fixed. Repeat the deterministic stage updates to the chosen stopping condition, increase beta, and warm-start the next stage. Explicitly fit at beta=1 to recover the ordinary EM endpoint.
  • supports Source: Deterministic Annealing Variant of the EM Algorithm · Locator: Sections3.1-3.2 Eq7-14, pp547-549 / PDF3-5
Scope #stage-objective
At fixed beta, the entropy-weighted variational objective profiles to (1/beta)*sum_i log Z_beta(x_i,theta), the negative of the paper’s free energy. Exact admissible coordinate updates increase that stage objective; beta=1 gives observed log likelihood. For beta different from one, stage improvement need not increase ordinary likelihood. Changing beta changes the optimized objective, so a cooling path is not ascent on a single unchanged likelihood surface.
  • supports Source: Deterministic Annealing Variant of the EM Algorithm · Locator: Eq11-13, p548 / PDF4; Eq15-17, p551 / PDF7
Limitation #normalization-and-global-claims
The source’s high-temperature uniqueness and global-tracking argument is conditional, not a general convergence guarantee. On an unbounded continuous latent domain, the beta=0 uniform law need not exist and positive powers of a heavy-tailed density can have infinite normalizers. Negative log density also need not be nonnegative: normalized continuous densities can exceed one. Local warm-started optimization need not follow the global optimum through temperature changes or bifurcations.
  • supports Source: Deterministic Annealing Variant of the EM Algorithm · Locator: Section3.1 energy note, p548 / PDF4; Section3.2 global-minimum discussion, p549 / PDF5; curator continuous-density counterexamples
Limitation #endpoint-rule
The printed pseudocode increases beta and repeats only while beta<beta_max, which can stop before fitting at the desired beta_max=1. The endpoint solve is specified explicitly here; a literal transcription of that test is not certified. The experimental multiplicative rule beta_new=1.4*beta also requires an endpoint policy. The selected two-Gaussian demonstrations do not establish initialization independence or a universally successful cooling schedule.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The continuous extension requires a specified base measure, support and finite powered-density integrals at every scheduled beta. Finite mixtures avoid some integrability issues but can still have singular or inadmissible parameter fits.
  • At beta=1 the update is ordinary EM, not necessarily a global maximum. The beta-to-infinity hard-assignment discussion is a different limit from this cooling-to-one likelihood procedure.
  • The fully inspected1994proceedings version is distinct from the1998journal article. No unseen journal correction, proof or author implementation is claimed.
  • No author software or historical empirical study was reproduced. Finite curator calculations illustrate stated objective and schedule boundaries; they are not a general convergence proof or an empirical speed comparison.
