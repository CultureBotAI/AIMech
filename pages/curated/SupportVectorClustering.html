Support Vector Clustering

Algorithms AIMech:SupportVectorClustering DRAFT
Aliases: SVC
An unsupervised clustering procedure that fits a soft enclosing sphere to observations in Gaussian-kernel feature space, then labels retained observations by connected components of a graph whose input-space segments remain within the sphere preimage.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record covers the Ben-Hur-Horn-Siegelmann-Vapnik sphere-and-connectivity procedure in the exact inspected JMLR PDF. The support description alone does not assign component labels. This is distinct from supervised support-vector classification, label-margin clustering, kernel k-means, density-connected neighborhoods and iterative mode seeking. Low- and high-outlier regimes are parameter settings within the method. Claims & Grounded Evidence

7 Claims
Mechanism #sphere-fit
For N input observations, Gaussian kernel K(x,y)=exp(-q*||x-y||²), q>0, and slack penalty C>0, fit a center a and radius R by minimizing R²+C*sum_i xi_i subject to ||Phi(x_i)-a||²<=R²+xi_i and xi_i>=0. The displayed dual maximizes sum_i beta_i*K_ii-beta^T*K*beta with sum_i beta_i=1 and 0<=beta_i<=C. Its feasibility requires N*C>=1. The center is sum_i beta_i*Phi(x_i); kernel evaluation avoids explicitly constructing those features. The convex quadratic dual concerns sphere fitting, not globally optimal clustering or parameter selection.
  • supports Source: Support Vector Clustering · Locator: Section2.1, printed pp.126-127 / PDF pp.2-3, Equations1-11; feasibility follows by summing the box bounds
Limitation #radius-and-bound-vectors
The squared distance of a new input is K(x,x)-2*sum_i beta_i*K(x_i,x)+beta^T*K*beta. A free support vector with 0<beta_i<C lies on the sphere and supplies R. If no free support vector exists, the stated radius rule needs an additional convention. A coefficient beta_i=C permits distance equal to R as well as greater than R; bounded coefficients alone do not prove strict exclusion. With N=2, C=1/2 and Gaussian off-diagonal kernel value 1/2, beta=(1/2,1/2), and every squared radius from 0 to 1/4 with equal slacks 1/4-R² attains primal and dual value 1/4. At R²=1/4 both bounded vectors are on the boundary and no free vector exists.
  • supports Source: Support Vector Clustering · Locator: Section2.1, printed pp.127-128 / PDF pp.3-4, Equations3-14; curator exact two-observation KKT and primal-dual calculation
Mechanism #component-assignment
For observations retained inside or on the sphere, form an edge when every point on their straight line segment in input space also maps inside or onto the sphere. Assign clusters as connected components of this observation graph. The reported implementation approximates each continuous segment test with 20 sampled points. Points treated as outliers are initially unassigned; leaving them unassigned or assigning them to a nearest cluster is an additional policy, and the paper uses the latter in its examples.
  • supports Source: Support Vector Clustering · Locator: Section2.2, printed p.128 / PDF p.4, Equation16 and following paragraphs
Limitation #finite-connectivity
An exact accepted segment gives a path inside the sphere preimage, but a graph on finitely many observations need not capture every path-connected component of that continuous set. Finite segment sampling can miss an excursion outside and introduce a false connection. The 20-sample implementation therefore has no stated exact topological recovery guarantee. Restricting adjacency tests to support vectors is a heuristic described in Section 5 reported to agree on the tried datasets, not a general equivalence proof.
  • supports Source: Support Vector Clustering · Locator: Section2.2, printed p.128 / PDF p.4; Section5, printed p.135 / PDF p.11; curator distinction between continuous paths and finite sampled edges
Mechanism #scale-and-outlier-control
The parameters q and p=1/(N*C) explore spatial scale and allowable slack. Larger q means a narrower Gaussian; larger p means a smaller penalty C. The box and sum constraints imply that the fraction of coefficients at their upper bound is at most p; the paper’s stricter inequality requires remaining positive coefficient mass. High-p fits can describe cluster cores rather than the envelope of all data. The weighted kernel score sum_i beta_i*K(x_i,x) approaches the equal-weight Parzen score as p approaches 1, up to density normalization; this resemblance does not make the component procedure identical to mode seeking.
  • supports Source: Support Vector Clustering · Locator: Sections3.2-4, printed pp.130-133 / PDF pp.6-9, Equations17-22; bound qualification follows from Equations3,9
Limitation #selection-and-hierarchy
The source proposes increasing q and adjusting p while monitoring support-vector fractions and stability. It explicitly reports counterexamples to a strict hierarchy when bounded support vectors are used. These choices do not provide a universal cluster-count selector or nested partition guarantee. Strong overlap may leave small cluster cores; the finite experiments also show sensitivity to retained PCA dimensions.
  • supports Source: Support Vector Clustering · Locator: Sections4.1-4.2 and 6, printed pp.134-136 / PDF pp.10-12
Limitation #computational-scope
The source uses an adapted sequential minimal optimization fit and sampled pairwise connectivity checks. With a fixed number of segment samples, straightforward labeling scales with retained-point pairs, nonzero-coefficient kernel evaluations and input dimension. The stated overall quadratic scaling assumes a bounded support-vector count and empirical solver behavior; it is not an unconditional complexity guarantee for all kernels or datasets.
