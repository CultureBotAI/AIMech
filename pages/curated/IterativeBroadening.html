Iterative broadening

Algorithms AIMech:IterativeBroadening DRAFT
A tree-search strategy that repeats depth-first search with increasing limits on the number of ordered successors considered at each parent, widening the allowed search tree after an unsuccessful pass.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #per-parent-breadth-passes
The inspected conference formulation first searches with a successor cutoff of two, then three and so on until a goal is found. Each parent contributes at most that many ordered children during a pass. This bounds local branching rather than the total width of a depth layer or the entire open frontier, and earlier search work may be repeated.
  • supports Source: Iterative Broadening · Locator: Section1, PDF pp.1–2 / printed pp.216–217; Figure1
Limitation #coverage-and-solution-boundaries
Coverage requires each restricted depth-first pass to finish and the cutoff eventually to admit a path to a goal. A finite tree permits this as the cutoff reaches its necessary branching width; a depth-unbounded infinite branch can prevent widening. A first-found goal is not guaranteed minimum path cost. Combining breadth growth with depth limits requires an adequate terminating schedule.
  • supports Source: Iterative Broadening · Locator: Section1, PDF pp.1–2 / printed pp.216–217; Section5, PDF p.5 / printed p.220
Scope #special-random-tree-model
The analysis uses a uniform branching factor b and depth d, with exactly s successful children at each successful node, randomly positioned among its b children; the basic derivation takes integer s greater than one. Failure at the next level averages the previous failure probability raised to the number of selected successful children, using a hypergeometric distribution. This correlated family is not an arbitrary tree or a uniform placement of a fixed number of fringe goals.
  • supports Source: Iterative Broadening · Locator: Section2, PDF pp.2–3 / printed pp.217–218; Equation1
Limitation #approximation-and-printed-boundary
The conditional failure ratio for wider after narrower unsuccessful passes requires nested retained child sets and a nonzero conditioning probability. The authors explicitly approximate successful-pass work as unchanged by earlier failure, so the resulting total expected-time calculation is qualified. A cutoff b−s+1 retains a successful child at every successful node in the stated model; later prose prints b+s−1 despite using b−(s−1) in its ensuing sum. The large-depth performance proposition is not asserted here as a universal deterministic speedup.
  • supports Source: Iterative Broadening · Locator: Sections2–3, PDF pp.3–4 / printed pp.218–219; Equations5–6, large-depth discussion and Proposition3.1
Limitation #finite-empirical-boundaries
The random-tree comparisons use finite configured samples and include cases where iterative broadening is slower than ordinary depth-first search. Heuristic ordering changes performance, while the crossword examples combine broadening with other inference and search choices. Those experiments do not isolate a universal runtime improvement, and a binary tree is already fully admitted by the stated initial cutoff of two.
  • supports Source: Iterative Broadening · Locator: Sections3–4, PDF pp.4–5 / printed pp.219–220; Figures4–5 and crossword discussion

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This is the1990 conference method; the1992 journal full text is not assumed identical.
  • The cutoff is per parent. It is neither a global beam width nor a bound on all program memory.
  • Completeness statements require terminating passes and eventual inclusion of an adequate search path. No shortest-path or arbitrary infinite-tree guarantee is asserted.
  • The random-tree analysis has specific distributional assumptions and an explicit approximation. Changing child order invalidates its nested-event argument unless nesting is otherwise preserved.
  • All five conference pages were visually inspected. Author software and empirical replication were not executed. Constructed examples assess mathematical boundaries only.
