Variational information maximization IM algorithm

Algorithms AIMech:VariationalInformationMaximizationIMAlgorithm DRAFT
Aliases: IM algorithm, IM algorithm (Barber and Agakov)
An alternating optimization procedure that adjusts a noisy encoder and a variational decoder to increase the Barber–Agakov lower bound on transmitted information for a fixed input distribution.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the named IM algorithm in Barber and Agakov’s 2003 paper, not every algorithm that maximizes information. It is separate from its lower-bound objective and from RIM’s regularized discrete clustering, MINE’s dependence estimation and DIM’s feature-matching framework. The analogy to expectation maximization does not make the optimized target an observed-data likelihood. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Choose a fixed input distribution p(x), a parameterized encoder p_theta(y|x), and a class Q of normalized decoders q(x|y) for which the expected decoder log probability is tractable or can be approximated. Starting from an encoder and decoder, hold q fixed and maximize H(X)+E_p(x)p_theta(y|x)[log q(X|Y)] over theta. Then hold theta fixed and fit q within Q by maximizing the same expectation. Repeat the two updates. H(X) is constant in these updates. Exact coordinate maximizations cannot decrease the chosen lower bound; approximate updates require their own improvement check. The paper develops a noisy linear projection with Gaussian decoding as one feature-extraction configuration and also describes richer encoder/decoder choices.
  • supports Source: The IM Algorithm: A variational approach to Information Maximization · Locator: Section 2, PDF pp. 2–3, Equation (3) and The IM algorithm numbered updates; Section 3, pp. 3–4, linear Gaussian channel and constrained projection context

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Restricted decoders can leave a positive variational gap. Monotonic improvement of the bound does not generally imply monotonic true mutual information or a global optimum.
  • The coordinate subproblems may be nonconvex or expensive, and exact maximization is an idealization. The paper’s instruction to iterate to convergence is not a general finite-iteration convergence guarantee.
  • Encoder noise, capacity and constraints matter. In the linear noisy example, unconstrained encoder scaling can diverge; the procedure does not prescribe one universal norm or power constraint for every application.
  • The normalized decoder and fixed source distribution distinguish this method from generic contrastive loss minimization. No IM implementation or empirical performance claim was reproduced.
