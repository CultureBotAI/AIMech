Derivative-observation Gaussian process regression

Algorithms AIMech:DerivativeObservationGaussianProcessRegression DRAFT
Aliases: Gaussian process regression with derivative observations
A Gaussian-process regression procedure that jointly conditions a sufficiently differentiable scalar-function prior on function values and partial-derivative values using differentiated kernel covariance blocks and a specified Gaussian observation-error model.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #joint-kernel-derivatives
For a sufficiently mean-square differentiable scalar GP f, form a joint Gaussian vector of function values and selected partial derivative values. Its covariance blocks are k(x,x_prime), derivatives with respect to the appropriate first or second kernel argument, and mixed derivatives with respect to both arguments. Differentiate the latent kernel, then add the observation-error covariance. The inspected squared-exponential construction gives cov(f_prime(x),f(x_prime))=-(x-x_prime)*exp(-(x-x_prime)²/2) in the one-dimensional unit-kernel case. Modeling all derivatives through the same scalar function enforces mutual integrability of the latent derivative field; it does not force noisy observed gradients to be matched exactly.
  • supports Source: Derivative Observations in Gaussian Process Models of Dynamic Systems · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.2, Equations 1–6 and Figure 1, PDF pp. 2–3; Section 4, PDF pp. 6–7
Mechanism #gaussian-conditioning
With fixed kernel parameters and additive jointly Gaussian observation errors independent of the latent function, combine the value and derivative observations in one Gaussian conditioning calculation. A full error covariance can retain correlations among observations. Posterior means and covariances are available for both values and derivatives. The paper fits kernel hyperparameters by joint marginal likelihood; conditional exactness under the specified Gaussian model does not integrate hyperparameter uncertainty or guarantee a globally optimal fit.
  • supports Source: Derivative Observations in Gaussian Process Models of Dynamic Systems · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.3, Equations 2–3 and covariance construction, PDF pp. 2–4
Mechanism #local-linearization-summaries
An optional system-identification construction fits a local linear model to perturbation data around an operating point. Its intercept supplies a function-value observation and its D slopes supply D partial-derivative observations, with the estimated full (D+1)-dimensional coefficient covariance added as an error block. Treating different blocks as independent requires the corresponding error assumption. These summaries can reduce the dense GP system size when many locally linear observations are replaced by fewer coefficients; they are not generally a lossless representation of arbitrary nonlinear data.
  • supports Source: Derivative Observations in Gaussian Process Models of Dynamic Systems · Locator: Section 2.3, Equations 7–9, PDF pp. 3–4; Section 4, PDF pp. 6–7
Mechanism #mean-and-uncertainty
Derivative observations can change both the posterior mean and its uncertainty. For a zero-mean unit squared-exponential GP conditioned on exact f(0)=1 and f_prime(0)=d, the mean at x is exp(-x²/2)*(1+x*d) and latent variance is 1-exp(-x²)*(1+x²). The zero-derivative symmetric example leaves the value-only mean unchanged, but d=1 changes it. At x=1 the variance is 1-2/e, about 0.2642. These are fixed-parameter curator conditioning calculations, not a reproduction of the paper’s fitted examples.
  • supports Source: Derivative Observations in Gaussian Process Models of Dynamic Systems · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.2 and 2.4, Equations 2–6 and Figure 2, PDF pp. 2–5; analytic checks in research/discovery/derivative-gp-boundaries.py
Limitation #finite-difference-and-dependence
Two exact function values at distinct locations are not literally the same observations as an exact derivative and a function value. With the unit kernel, f(0) and f(1) fix the variance at x=1 to zero, whereas f(0) and f_prime(0) leave variance 1-2/e. The source’s exact-equivalence wording therefore requires a derivative-limit qualification. Estimated slopes and reused raw data can also share errors: treating them as independent evidence can misstate uncertainty. A curator scalar Gaussian example gives posterior variance 1/2 from one noisy observation, but 1/3 when a copy is incorrectly counted as independent.
  • supports Source: Derivative Observations in Gaussian Process Models of Dynamic Systems · Locator: Section 2.3 discussion following Equation 9, PDF p. 4; fixed-parameter finite-separation and dependence checks in research/discovery/derivative-gp-boundaries.py

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This record scopes direct Gaussian conditioning on derivative values, including noisy estimates, with function values. Virtual derivative signs with EP, finite-difference pseudo-observation augmentation, a separately fitted GP for every gradient component, and particular nonlinear controllers are separate constructions. No universal gradient-enhanced-kriging alias is assigned.
  • The latent kernel must support the required mean-square derivatives. Gaussian conditional formulas assume a valid covariance and specified error model; nonsmooth kernels, correlated errors treated as independent, ill-conditioning and duplicate constraints need appropriate handling. Smooth latent integrability is not a proof of dynamical stability, controllability or globally accurate identification.
  • Local-linearization compression needs a justified local approximation and error covariance. Equations 7–9 give a particular regularized fit and covariance estimate; they do not establish exact uncertainty for every prior scaling or every regularized estimator. Correlation within coefficient blocks and with any retained original data matters. Local equilibrium models alone do not identify general off-equilibrium dynamics.
  • At fixed Gaussian hyperparameters, adding observations cannot increase conditional covariance, but refitting hyperparameters changes the comparison. The source’s near-equivalence of 150 raw observations and a summary is a particular illustration, not a sufficiency theorem or universal accuracy/computational gain. The general cubic dense-system cost uses the total value and derivative observation count.
  • The source’s two-nearby-values equivalence and zero-derivative mean wording are qualified by explicit curator calculations. The nonlinear demonstration’s output-time indexing and noise notation need resolution before any exact numerical reproduction. No benchmark, dynamical simulation, fitted GP or author implementation was executed; the record remains DRAFT after author self-review.
