Population Based Augmentation

Algorithms AIMech:PopulationBasedAugmentation DRAFT
Aliases: PBA
An augmentation-schedule learning procedure that jointly trains a population of child classifiers and evolves their augmentation hyperparameters by copying successful members and perturbing their policies over training time.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope PBA learns a time-dependent augmentation schedule using population-based training; it is not identical to general PBT, fixed-policy AutoAugment, fixed-model Fast AutoAugment scoring, or a population ensemble used at inference. The transferred artifact is the schedule, not the child model checkpoint. 2 Claims
Mechanism #method
Train a population of randomly initialized child classifiers and periodically compare their validation performance. In the published truncation-selection configuration, a bottom-quartile member copies the weights and augmentation hyperparameters of a top-quartile member, then perturbs those hyperparameters and continues training without reinitializing the copied weights. Each policy has two entries for each of 15 operation types, excluding SamplePairing, with one magnitude and one application probability per entry: 60 scalar hyperparameters. The probability grid is 0 to 1 in tenths and magnitude indices are 0 through 9. To augment an image, shuffle the 30 entries, draw an operation-count cap of 0,1 or 2 with probabilities 0.2,0.3 or 0.5, then scan entries and probabilistically apply each until that many operations have actually been applied or the list is exhausted. Each hyperparameter is reset uniformly with probability 0.2; otherwise its integer grid index is shifted up or down by a uniformly sampled amount in {0,1,2,3} and clipped to its domain. The source uses an epoch per step, an exploit/explore interval of three epochs and initially zero probabilities/magnitudes. Retain the selected schedule along the winning training lineage and discard the child weights when training the final model; schedules can be stretched to a different training duration. These numerical settings are the published configuration, not universal PBT requirements.

Mechanism #integer-grid-and-cap
The pinned exploration code stores probability indices as integers0..10 and magnitude indices as integers0..9, applies reset or signed local perturbations and clips to the matching bounds. The augmentation function decrements its sampled cap only after an operation passes its probability test, stopping at zero or the end of the shuffled list. Repeated operation types are possible because the policy contains two entries per type; applying the same type twice does not require sampling with replacement from a one-entry-per-type list.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The count distribution sets a maximum number of successful applications, not a guarantee of exactly that many operations. At zero probabilities no operation is applied even when the cap is positive; this is distinct from sampling exactly two operations.
  • Each probability perturbation operates on an integer tenths-grid index, not by adding an unscaled integer to a probability in[0,1]. Some operations ignore magnitude.
  • The retained schedule can change non-monotonically; a hypothesis that augmentation should strengthen through training is not a monotonicity constraint.
  • Validation-guided population selection and schedule transfer can depend on population size, search data, model, duration and transformation choices. No guaranteed schedule quality, universal transfer, cost ratio or benchmark advantage is asserted.
  • The source PDF and arXiv author list place Xi Chen last; the PMLR landing metadata orders the authors differently. Bibliographic order here follows the inspected primary PDF.
