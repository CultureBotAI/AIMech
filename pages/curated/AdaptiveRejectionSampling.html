Adaptive rejection sampling

Algorithms AIMech:AdaptiveRejectionSampling DRAFT
Aliases: ARS
The Gilks–Wild univariate rejection sampler that uses tangents to a concave log density as a piecewise-linear upper hull and chords as a lower hull, updating these bounds from evaluated proposals.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record scopes ARS to the derivative-based tangent construction in Gilks and Wild (1992), for a fixed log-concave scalar target on a connected domain. Derivative-free envelope constructions and adaptive rejection Metropolis sampling are distinct variants requiring their own evidence. Exact conditional draws do not make successive joint Gibbs states independent. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Write h=log g for a differentiable concave log density. At ordered support points evaluate h and h prime; tangent intersections define an upper hull u, and chords define a lower hull l, with l=−infinity outside the extreme support points. Initialize points so exp(u) is integrable; on unbounded tails this requires a positive leftmost slope and a negative rightmost slope. Draw x from normalized exp(u) and w uniformly on (0,1). Accept immediately if w≤exp(l(x)−u(x)); otherwise evaluate h(x) and h prime(x), accept if w≤exp(h(x)−u(x)), and incorporate the evaluated point into the hulls whether that full test accepted or rejected it. Repeat rejected trials. The conditional-on-history rejection argument gives the fixed target law for each output despite adaptation of the valid bounds.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Concavity of log g is essential for the tangent and chord bounds. Unimodality alone is insufficient, and non-log-concave targets are not covered by this construction.
  • The displayed baseline assumes usable derivatives and connected scalar support. It does not assert validity for every nonsmooth density or a direct multivariate joint draw.
  • Tail slopes and support endpoints must make the proposal integrable. Concentrated or skewed densities require numerical care.
  • When a Gibbs conditional changes, hull values must be computed for that conditional; reusing stale density bounds is not justified.
  • No universal evaluation savings or benchmark result is asserted. ARMS and its 1997 correction remain separate uninspected-method leads.
