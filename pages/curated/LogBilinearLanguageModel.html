Log-bilinear language model

Architectures AIMech:LogBilinearLanguageModel DRAFT
Aliases: LBL
A next-word language architecture that linearly combines position-specific transforms of context-word embeddings and normalizes their bilinear compatibility with candidate-word embeddings over the vocabulary.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the original Mnih–Hinton LBL construction. It uses the same word-embedding matrix for context and candidate words, has no stochastic hidden variables, and retains a flat vocabulary normalizer. HLBL replaces that output parameterization with tree-node decisions. The FRBM and temporal FRBM in the same 2007 paper are separate latent-variable architectures, not aliases for LBL. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
In the 2007 row-vector convention, let r_w be a word’s embedding written as a column, C_i the transform for context position i, b_r a feature bias and b_w a candidate-word bias. The candidate score is sum_i r_context_i^T C_i r_w+b_r^T r_w+b_w. Equivalently, form r_hat=b_r+sum_i C_i^T r_context_i once, then take score_w=r_hat^T r_w+b_w and apply the vocabulary softmax. The original energy is the negative of this score. Maximum-likelihood gradients are observed sufficient statistics minus their exact finite-vocabulary conditional expectations. Because the embedding matrix is shared, its gradient includes both input-context and candidate-output roles. The 2008 exposition writes r_hat=sum_i C_i r_context_i with the opposite matrix orientation and without an explicit feature bias; this is a notation/parameterization distinction to preserve when comparing formulas.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The context feature computation is linear, while normalization makes word probabilities nonlinear. Position-specific transforms preserve context order; the construction is not generally a bag-of-words model.
  • Without an additional exact computational method, the original flat normalizer requires summing candidate scores over the vocabulary. Absence of stochastic hidden variables makes this expectation explicit, not independent of vocabulary size.
  • Bilinear scores and shared learned embeddings do not make joint parameter fitting a convex optimization problem. Exact likelihood gradients do not guarantee a global optimum or calibrated predictions.
  • The energy scores one candidate conditional on its context; it is not the unnormalized energy of a whole sequence used by the pinned ImplicitLanguageModel or EnergyBasedLLM classes.
  • The record covers a fixed-context word model, without asserting long-context capability, benchmark superiority, author-code behavior or the mechanisms of the neighboring FRBM variants.
