Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Scope #domain-and-resistance
The maintained domain is a connected simple undirected graph with n>=3 and positive edge conductances. Ground one vertex and embed the inverse of the reduced weighted Laplacian in C with a zero ground row and column. Effective resistance between v and w is R_vw=C_vv+C_ww-2C_vw, the potential drop for unit current from v to w. It is positive for distinct vertices and independent of the chosen ground.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Section 2, Equation 1, PDF p. 3 / printed p. 535; Definition 3 and proof, PDF pp. 5–6 / printed pp. 537–538
Mechanism #column-accumulation
Initialize q(v)=0. For each inverse column indexed by v and every vertex w, add C_vv-2C_wv to q(v) and C_vv to q(w), including w=v. After all columns, q(v)=n C_vv-2 sum_w C_wv+trace(C)=sum_w R_vw. Algorithm 2 returns 1/q(v). Including the diagonal terms in both updates cancels them; the ground column is zero. Column order does not change the exact final sum.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Section 4, Algorithm 2, PDF p. 9 / printed p. 541; explicit accumulator algebra in research/discovery/grounded-closeness-boundaries.py
Limitation #normalization-discrepancy
The source defines n_C=n-1 and uses n_C divided by the resistance sum in Definition 3, but its proof and Algorithm 2 omit that factor. This record preserves the explicit Algorithm 2 output 1/sum_w R_vw; multiplying it by n-1 yields the stated normalized definition. On the unit triangle, these values are 3/4 and 3/2 respectively. No unqualified numerical identity with every convention called information centrality is asserted.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Section 3, PDF p. 4 / printed p. 536; Definition 3 and proof, PDF pp. 5–6 / printed pp. 537–538; Algorithm 2, PDF p. 9 / printed p. 541; curator triangle example
Scope #computation-and-numerics
With a dense reduced inverse, forming and accumulating all n columns costs O(n^2) beyond inversion and uses O(n^2+m) storage. The source also describes obtaining columns as needed from reduced linear solves, allowing the column accumulator to avoid storing the whole inverse. Numerical solve time, accuracy and preconditioner storage require implementation-specific assumptions; the chapter’s rough conjugate-gradient and condition-number discussion is not an unconditional exact floating-point guarantee.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Section 4, matrix and streaming-column discussion, PDF pp. 8–9 / printed pp. 540–541; explicit dense and accumulator accounting is curator interpretation
Limitation #distance-and-weight-boundaries
This uses effective resistance rather than shortest-path length in general. On a tree, effective resistance equals the unique-path sum of reciprocal conductances, so matched-normalization closeness values agree. On a unit triangle, inverse resistance-sum closeness is 3/4 but inverse shortest-path-sum closeness is 1/2. Multiplying all conductances by a positive factor multiplies this closeness by the same factor. These finite graph examples and scaling consequences do not make the score dimensionless or bounded by one.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Definition 3, PDF p. 5 / printed p. 537; Section 6 tree comparison, PDF p. 11 / printed p. 543; explicit curator examples in research/discovery/grounded-closeness-boundaries.py
Limitation #method-and-evidence-scope
Algorithm 2 computes reciprocal resistance sums; it is distinct from the chapter’s exact and sampled betweenness algorithms and from the earlier information-centrality construction using an inverse of L plus the all-ones matrix. The inspected chapter supplies a mathematical mechanism; its Appendix A experiment concerns betweenness. No empirical closeness speedup, replicated author implementation, causal interpretation or learned predictive performance is asserted.
  • supports Source: Centrality Measures Based on Current Flow · Locator: Section 3.2, Equation 2, PDF p. 5 / printed p. 537; Section 4 and Algorithm 2, PDF pp. 8–9 / printed pp. 540–541; Appendix A/Figure 1, PDF p. 12 / printed p. 544

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Connected simple undirected positive-conductance graphs with n>=3 only. Disconnected components, singleton cases, signed conductances and directed networks require other definitions.
  • Algorithm 2 and Definition 3 differ by n-1 in the inspected source. The maintained output follows the explicit algorithm, and the alternative normalized quantity is labeled separately. Information-centrality naming is not an unqualified normalization identity.
  • The chapter’s literal cycle equation for currents is inconsistent with its Ohm law for unequal conductances. The maintained construction uses the explicit weighted Laplacian and potential drops; no corrected source edition is claimed.
  • Dense inverses and streaming numerical solves have different storage and precision requirements. Finite rational graphs verify selected equalities and scaling, not general floating-point stability or the performance of historical software.
  • All twelve chapter pages and references were read, with method equations and Figure 1 visually inspected. No author code, raw data or closeness benchmark was run. This structural score does not establish causality or trained-model performance. DRAFT remains.
