Aliases: ICM
A deterministic conditional-mode optimization algorithm that repeatedly updates one variable to a mode of its conditional distribution given the current values of the remaining variables, seeking a coordinatewise mode of a fixed joint distribution.

Identity Notes & Concept Scope ICM is a label or state optimization procedure, not the hidden Markov random field it can optimize and not iterative conditional estimation of model parameters. It is a probability-based coordinate-ascent instance related to hill climbing; Gibbs sampling instead draws a conditional random value. The classical image implementation uses local Markov neighbors and site emissions, but the conditional-mode principle also applies when an observation affects several sites or when the posterior neighborhood is wider. Single-site ICM is the defining scope; joint block updates are a stated extension. The paper credits earlier work and does not establish a 1986 first-invention claim. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method
For fixed observed y and model parameters theta, start at a configuration x with a defined positive posterior. In a sequential sweep, replace x_i by an element of argmax_u p(x_i=u | x_(-i),y,theta), using already updated values at earlier sites. Equivalently maximize the joint posterior with all other coordinates held fixed. For an MRF prior and independent local emissions the score is b_i(y_i|u)*p_i(u|x_neighbors); shared observation factors require every factor affected by the coordinate. Each exact update cannot decrease the fixed joint objective. On a finite configuration space, requiring strict improvement for a changed value, or retaining the current value on a tie, yields finite termination at a coordinatewise mode. This need not be a global joint MAP solution. Joint updates on conditionally independent coding sets can be parallelized; simultaneously updating interacting sites from stale neighbors has no such monotonicity guarantee. A block extension maximizes over a selected block given its complement.

Limitation #local-optimality-limits
ICM can remain at an initialization-dependent local mode and does not generally quantify uncertainty or guarantee the globally optimal segmentation; the source’s illustrative parameter settings are not universal prescriptions.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Initialization and update order can change the resulting mode. A coordinatewise optimum can have lower posterior probability than another configuration. ICM does not provide posterior uncertainty or the sitewise marginal posterior mode merely by returning a joint labeling.
  • The monotonicity and finite-state termination statements require a fixed objective, valid exact conditionals and the stated tie policy. Arbitrary switching among tied states can cycle; continuous-valued coordinate ascent need not terminate in finitely many steps.
  • The source’s simultaneous image updates and its changing interaction parameter are practical variants, not a proof for fully synchronous updates or a changing posterior. Alternating estimated labels and fitted parameters, especially pseudolikelihood fitting, does not inherit exact EM monotonicity.
  • For independent site observations, the local emission factor is sufficient for a coordinate update. Correlated observations, blur, tomography and shared likelihood factors can enlarge the required conditional neighborhood. A product containing only the updated site’s emission would then generally be wrong.
  • Strong coupling and fine structures can produce initialization dependence and artifacts. The illustrative interaction strength 1.5 and a few reported sweeps are not universal defaults or convergence bounds. The author’s reply explicitly limits optimality and empirical claims.
  • The paper’s main image examples and discussion establish method context, not universal segmentation superiority. No author code, image benchmark or parameter fitting was reproduced. A separate finite probability witness tests update order, synchronous oscillation and tie behavior.
