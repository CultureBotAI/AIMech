Holland bucket-brigade credit assignment

Algorithms AIMech:HollandBucketBrigade DRAFT
A classifier-system credit-assignment procedure in which satisfied condition/action rules compete using strength-and-specificity bids, winning rules pay the suppliers of their triggering messages, and consumer payments together with external payoff adapt strengths along chains of rule activation.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #rule-strength-credit-assignment
In the inspected classifier-system formulation, condition/action rules communicate by posting messages, and each rule has a numerical strength representing its usefulness in the contexts where it acts. The bucket-brigade procedure modifies these strengths to allocate credit to rules that provide conditions for later useful activity. It is the credit-assignment mechanism within the rule system, not the whole classifier-system framework.
  • supports Source: Properties of the Bucket Brigade Algorithm · Locator: Printed/PDF p. 1, opening two paragraphs on the credit-assignment problem, rule strengths and message-passing conditions.
Mechanism #specificity-and-strength-bidding
Rules whose conditions are satisfied compete for activation. In the stated classifier-system version, rule C bids b(C,t)=c*r(C)*s(C,t), where s is its strength and r is the number of specified condition positions, computed as total defining positions minus don’t-care positions. Selected highest bidders become active and post their action messages. Both strength and specificity enter the bid; greater specificity alone does not determine the winning rule.
  • supports Source: Properties of the Bucket Brigade Algorithm · Locator: Printed/PDF pp. 1–2, auction description, bid formula and definitions of specificity, strength and c; p. 5, qualified exception/default competition illustration.
Mechanism #winner-debit-and-supplier-credit
A winning rule pays its bid from its strength, giving the displayed debit s(C,t+1)=s(C,t)-b(C,t). In the simplest formulation, that bid is divided equally among the classifiers whose messages satisfy the winner’s conditions: a supplier C1 receives the additive credit b(C,t)/n(C,t), where n counts the supplying classifiers. This states the payment prose and the additive first equality of the supplier display; its inconsistent final expression is retained as a source limitation.
  • supports Source: Properties of the Bucket Brigade Algorithm · Locator: Printed/PDF p. 2, winner debit, equal supplier-payment prose and the first equality in the supplier-strength display.
Mechanism #consumer-payments-and-external-payoff
After a winning rule posts a message, it can become a supplier to rules activated on the next step and receive shares of their bids. When the environment provides payoff, all classifiers active on that step receive equal fractions of it, in addition to payments from their next-step consumers. Credit therefore passes through local message-supplying transactions and externally rewarded activity.
  • supports Source: Properties of the Bucket Brigade Algorithm · Locator: Printed/PDF p. 2, consumer-to-supplier transition and final paragraph on environmental payoff; p. 3, opening discussion of coupling to payoff sequences.
Mechanism #persistent-rule-bridge
The source illustrates a bridge across a longer sequence using an epoch-marking rule that remains active through the sequence and is active when payoff arrives. If an initiating rule supplies a message that activates this persistent rule, the latter’s increased strength can increase the payment to that initiator on a subsequent use. This construction depends on the persistent rule and the stated supplier coupling; it does not itself discover the required rules.
  • supports Source: Properties of the Bucket Brigade Algorithm · Locator: Printed/PDF pp. 5–7, local propagation discussion and persistent epoch-marking bridge, especially the initiating-rule example on p. 6 and separate rule discovery on p. 7.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The supplier-strength display on p. 2 ends with (1-c*r(C)/n(C,t))*s(C,t), which is generally inconsistent with its preceding additive expression s(C1,t)+b(C,t)/n(C,t) and the supplier-payment prose. The additive statement is used here and the conflicting printed expression is preserved explicitly. No official erratum, silently repaired full specification or author-implementation behavior is asserted.
  • Specificity is a raw count, and describing c as small does not ensure c*r(C)<=1. For the isolated debit, nonnegative strength is preserved under nonnegative starting strength and 0<=c*r(C)<=1; adding nonnegative payments preserves that elementary boundary. These are explicit algebraic conditions, not a source-wide invariant for arbitrary specificity, signed rewards or simultaneous transactions. The equal-share formula also requires n(C,t)>0; the selected description does not resolve every zero-supplier case.
  • The article describes the mechanism rather than complete executable pseudocode. It does not fully specify winner cardinality, ties, initial strengths, exploration, no-supplier behavior, overlapping or self-supplier transactions, or a complete order for concurrent debits and credits. These choices cannot be reconstructed as one mandatory implementation from the stated local equations.
  • Credit recipients are message suppliers whose outputs satisfy a consumer’s conditions. This is not automatically credit to every temporally preceding rule, a generic eligibility trace, or proof that every paid supplier was counterfactually necessary for the environmental outcome. Restricted temporal-difference analogies do not identify the whole rule-credit procedure with TD, Q-learning or Sarsa.
  • The parasite and default/exception hierarchy discussions are conditional illustrations, not general convergence, monotone-improvement or extinction theorems. A more specific exception can still bid less than a stronger default because the bid depends on both factors. The source leaves simultaneous parasite and useful stage-setting activity outside its simple illustration.
  • The bridge example requires suitable persistent activation, a message link from the initiator, later payoff and subsequent reuse. It does not guarantee one-shot learning for arbitrary rule sets, immediate propagation to every earlier rule, or automatic discovery of the bridge. Genetic rule discovery and construction of further hierarchy components are additional mechanisms.
  • The declared evidence is the inspected seven-page 1985 chapter. The historical discovery source cites a 1986 Holland chapter that was not obtained in this research, and a later book draft’s simplified Sarsa comparison points to unread Wilson 1994 details. Those later forms are not exact aliases of the selected version. No first-use priority, universal benchmark advantage, full later-version equivalence, author-code inspection or experiment reproduction is claimed.
