Covariate-powered cross-weighted multiple testing

Mathematical Methods AIMech:CovariatePoweredCrossWeightedMultipleTesting DRAFT
Aliases: Independent Hypothesis Weighting (IHW), Cross-Weighted Benjamini-Hochberg Procedure (IHW-BH), Cross-Weighted Storey Benjamini-Hochberg Procedure (IHW-Storey), IHW-Grenander Covariate-Weighted Multiple Testing
A covariate-assisted multiple-testing methodology (Ignatiadis & Huber 2021, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series B 83(4):720-751, arXiv:1701.05179) that learns data-driven hypothesis weights W_i >= 0 from side information X_i via K-fold sample splitting across hypotheses (cross-weighting) to maximize discovery power while controlling the false discovery rate (FDR) or k-familywise error rate (k-FWER). Hypotheses [m] are partitioned into K disjoint folds I_1, ..., I_K; for each i in I_ell, an out-of-fold weight function W_hat^{-ell} is fit using only (P_j, X_j)_{j notin I_ell}, evaluated at X_i, and normalized within fold I_ell so that sum_{i in I_ell} W_i = |I_ell| (Specification 1, honest weighting). Combined with tau-censoring (Specification 2, where weights depend on out-of-fold p-values only through P_j * 1{P_j > tau}) and weighted Storey fold null-proportion adjustments, cross-weighting guarantees finite-sample FDR control under independence (Theorems 1-2), asymptotic FDR control without tau-censoring and zero asymptotic power loss from fold splitting under the conditional two-groups model (Proposition 1, Corollary 2), and finite-sample k-FWER and Benjamini-Yekutieli FDR control under block dependence when folds align with independent dependence blocks (Theorems 3-4).

Distinct from AIMech:AdaptivePValueThresholding (Lei & Fithian 2018, AdaPT), which iteratively shrinks a coordinatewise rejection threshold s_t(x_i) using full-sample mirror-masked p-values min(P_i, 1 - P_i) rather than learning out-of-fold cross-weights W_i = W_hat^{-ell}(X_i) for weighted Benjamini-Hochberg, weighted Storey, weighted k-Bonferroni, or weighted Benjamini-Yekutieli procedures via Grenander least-concave-majorant linear programming; distinct from AIMech:SelectivelyTraversedAccumulationRulesForStructuredFDRControl (Lei, Ramdas, & Fithian 2021, STAR), which enforces combinatorial structural constraints R_t in K by interactive filtration peeling with accumulation functions h(p); and distinct from AIMech:ConditionalCalibrationForFalseDiscoveryRateControl (Fithian & Lei 2022, dBH), which calibrates hypothesis-specific thresholds under known conditional null distributions rather than learning out-of-fold covariate weights from side information X_i.

Mechanism #honest-tau-censored-cross-weighting-finite-sample-fdr-control-and-uncensored-counterexample
Under Assumption 1 (null p-values independent of each other and of non-null p-values given covariates X and super-uniform on [0, 1]), partitioning [m] into K disjoint folds I_1, ..., I_K and learning honest weights W_i = W_hat^{-ell}(X_i) >= 0 from (P_j, X_j)_{j notin I_ell} normalized within each fold (sum_{i in I_ell} W_i = |I_ell|, Specification 1) that are tau-censored (depending on out-of-fold p-values only through P_j * 1{P_j > tau}, Specification 2) guarantees finite-sample FDR <= alpha for tau-censored weighted Benjamini-Hochberg (Theorem 1, Corollary 1) and for null-proportion-adaptive weights W_i / pi_hat'_{0,W,ell} where pi_hat'_{0,W,ell} = (max_{i in I_ell} W_i + sum_{i in I_ell} W_i 1{P_i > tau}) / (|I_ell| (1 - tau)) via the Inverse Binomial Lemma (Theorem 2, Supplement S1.2 and S1.4); in contrast, naive full-sample Grouped BH (Algorithm 1) overfits under the global null as the number of groups G grows (Section 2.2, Fig. 2), and omitting tau-censoring allows an m = 4 adversarial honest cross-weighting rule to achieve exact FDR = alpha * (1 + alpha * (1 - alpha) / 2) > alpha (Supplement S1.3).

Mechanism #asymptotic-consistency-without-tau-censoring-and-zero-power-loss-from-fold-splitting
Under the conditional two-groups model (Assumption 3, (X_i, H_i, P_i) i.i.d. with strictly positive null proportion pi_0 and continuous conditional CDF F(t | x)), a naive full-sample weighting rule taking values in a bounded function class (Specification 7) can overfit small p-values and fail to control FDR asymptotically (liminf_{m -> infinity} FDR >= 1 - 2.2 delta / alpha > alpha, Proposition 1(a), Supplement S2.1), whereas cross-weighted IHW-BH with any Specification-7 weight learner controls FDR asymptotically without tau-censoring (tau = 1) via conditional Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz concentration (Proposition 1(b), Supplement S2.2), and achieves the exact same asymptotic rejection threshold t^*(W^*) and asymptotic power as full-sample or oracle W^* weighting whenever ||W_hat^{([m])} - W^*||_infinity -> 0 in probability (Proposition 1(c), Corollary 2, Supplement S2.3–S2.4).

Mechanism #block-dependent-fold-partitioning-for-finite-sample-kfwer-and-ihw-by-fdr-control
Under Assumption 2 (where hypotheses are partitioned into K folds I_1, ..., I_K such that p-values and covariates in different folds are mutually independent while arbitrary dependence is permitted within each fold, e.g., linkage disequilibrium blocks or chromosomes in genomics), honest cross-weighting ensures each null p-value P_i (i in H_0 intersect I_ell) is independent of the entire in-fold weight vector (W_k)_{k in I_ell} without requiring tau-censoring (Lemma 1(a)), guaranteeing finite-sample k-FWER <= alpha for weighted k-Bonferroni (Theorem 3, Supplement S1.6) and finite-sample FDR <= alpha for weighted Benjamini-Yekutieli (Theorem 4, Supplement S1.5); conversely, under the same block-dependence assumption, dishonest data-driven weighted BY can suffer FDR = 1 (Example 1) and AdaPT with BY correction and tau-censoring (tau = 0.45) suffers FDR >= 0.2925 at alpha = 0.10 (Example 2, Section 3.3).

Mechanism #grenander-least-concave-majorant-convex-weight-optimization-and-sparse-superuniform-robustness
To learn informative weights in each out-of-fold training set [m] \ I_ell, IHW discretizes X_i into G bins, estimates the conditional p-value CDF F_hat^{-ell}(t | g) in each bin via the nonparametric Grenander estimator (the least concave majorant of the empirical CDF, corresponding to the non-increasing density NPMLE), and solves a convex linear program (or exact O(m log m) KKT slope-sorting procedure, Algorithm 5) with optional total-variation or uniform-deviation regularization to maximize expected discoveries subject to the fold budget constraint (Section 4, Equations 8–12; Supplement S4.1–S4.2); unlike mirror-reflection masking methods such as AdaPT—which require at least ceil(1 / alpha) rejections to make any discovery and lose power when true alternatives are sparse (m_1 < 1 / alpha) or null p-values are strictly super-uniform near 1—IHW-Grenander maintains high discovery power in both regimes (Section 5.2, 'Breaking AdaPT').

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Finite-sample FDR control in Theorems 1 and 2 requires mutual independence of null p-values and independence between null and non-null p-values conditional on covariates (Assumption 1), together with both within-fold weight normalization (Specification 1) and tau-censoring of out-of-fold p-values at a pre-specified threshold tau (Specification 2); omitting tau-censoring allows adversarial honest weighting rules to exceed alpha in finite samples (Supplement S1.3).
  • Under dependence (Assumption 2, Theorems 3 and 4), finite-sample k-FWER and IHW-BY FDR guarantees require that the fold partition I_1, ..., I_K be constructed from domain knowledge so that hypotheses in different folds are genuinely independent (such as separate chromosomes or distant genomic blocks); random fold assignment does not decouple dependent p-values across folds (Section 3.3, Section 6.2).
  • Out-of-fold Grenander least-concave-majorant weight learning requires partitioning the covariate space X into G discrete bins or trees with sufficient hypotheses per bin to estimate the conditional p-value distribution accurately, making direct nonparametric estimation challenging for high-dimensional covariates without tree or regression-based binning (Section 4, Section 7).
