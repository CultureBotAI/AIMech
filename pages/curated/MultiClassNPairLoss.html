Multi-class N-pair loss

Mathematical Methods AIMech:MultiClassNPairLoss DRAFT
Aliases: N-pair-mc loss
A supervised metric-learning objective that averages a joint positive-versus-multiple-negative log-softmax loss across a batch of positive pairs drawn from distinct classes.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is Sohn’s N-pair-mc objective, Equation (7), together with its distinct-class positive-pair batching. The source also uses unqualified N-pair loss for a family including N-pair-ovo, so that family name is not an exact alias of this member. Its per-anchor categorical form is shared with generic InfoNCE at dot-product scores, but the named supervised batching configuration is retained explicitly. It is not the full SimCLR procedure or NT-Xent’s cosine-temperature two-view construction. N-pair-ovo instead sums individual pairwise softplus terms. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Take N>=2 classes and two examples (x_i,x_i+) from each class. Encode 2N examples to f_i and f_i+. The Equation (7) objective is L_mc=(1/N) sum_i log[1+sum_(j!=i) exp(f_i^T f_j+−f_i^T f_i+)]. Each anchor has its paired positive and N−1 negative candidates from the positive side of other classes. Sharing these candidates across anchors forms N tuplets using 2N forward embeddings. Equation (7) specifies one orientation; footnote 2 also mentions a symmetric loss that swaps the anchor/positive roles, without specifying its aggregation in that footnote. No explicit temperature or cosine normalization appears in this expression.
  • supports Source: Improved Deep Metric Learning with Multi-class N-pair Loss Objective · Locator: Sections 3.1–3.2.2, PDF pp. 3–5; Equations (3) and (7), footnotes 1–2, embedding regularization discussion

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Use N positive pairs from N distinct classes; other positive-side examples must represent different classes for the stated objective. Pair sampling and class selection are part of this configuration, not interchangeable with arbitrary augmented-view negatives.
  • The main paper uses embedding-norm regularization instead of a hard unit-length constraint. This record specifies the loss term; it does not prescribe a regularization coefficient, encoder, optimizer or full training schedule.
  • No unconditional equivalence to a margin triplet hinge or an information lower bound is asserted. Objective shape and label-based batching do not establish downstream accuracy. No training or benchmark was reproduced.
  • The proceedings Supplemental.zip inspected during retrieval contains a different paper by Steinhardt and Liang. It is not supporting evidence; this record relies on the inspected nine-page N-pair main paper.
