Backpropagation through time

Algorithms AIMech:BackpropagationThroughTime DRAFT
Aliases: BPTT
A reverse differentiation algorithm that computes derivatives of a finite dynamic-system objective by propagating adjoints backward through its time-unfolded computation and accumulating contributions for shared parameters.

Matches are source mentions, existing architecture training descriptions, prior derivative reviews and the pending nested lead. BPTT differentiates a temporally unfolded computation in reverse order. RTRL propagates parameter sensitivities forward; the same fixed objective can have the same gradient without making the algorithms identical. SGD and Rprop consume gradient information to update parameters and do not define its temporal computation. Full finite-horizon BPTT is distinguished from detached-boundary approximations. Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #temporal-reverse-differentiation
Evaluate the dynamic system forward over the selected finite interval, then apply the chain rule in reverse computational order, including reverse time. A recurrent parameter reused at many steps receives the sum of contributions from those uses. Within-time computations must also respect their dependency order. Unfolding is a derivative representation of a shared-parameter dynamic system; it does not create independently trainable parameters for every time position.
  • supports Source: Backpropagation Through Time: What It Does and How to Do It · Locator: Section II.D, ordered derivatives, Equations7–8, PDF pp.3–4; Sections III.B–D, Equations15–18 and pseudocode, pp.5–7
Mechanism #adjoint-and-direct-terms
For the first-order specialization h_t=F_t(h_(t-1),theta) and L=sum_(t=1..T) ell_t(h_t,theta), use column-vector adjoints lambda_t=partial_h ell_t+(partial_h F_(t+1))^T*lambda_(t+1), with the future term omitted at T. The shared-parameter gradient is sum_t[partial_theta ell_t+(partial_theta F_t)^T*lambda_t], plus (partial_theta h_0)^T*lambda_0 when initialization depends on theta; lambda_0=(partial_h F_1)^T*lambda_1. Partial derivatives hold the displayed state arguments fixed. This is a chain-rule restatement of the source for a first-order recurrence, including direct loss and initialization terms, not a verbatim transcription of its lag-two example.
  • supports Source: Backpropagation Through Time: What It Does and How to Do It · Locator: Sections II.C–E, Equations6–12, PDF pp.3–4; Section III.C, Equations16–18 and initial-state discussion, p.6; curator forward/reverse and finite-difference check
Scope #objective-and-boundary-conditions
The complete finite-trajectory derivative assumes the same parameter values define its forward trajectory and backward calculation. Fixed external inputs and targets are treated as constants; parameter-dependent supplied signals require their additional derivative paths. A zero future adjoint expresses that the chosen objective has no later terms, not that an ongoing physical process ends. A missing direct loss at one time does not remove gradients arriving through later states. Initial states may be specified or learned and must use the corresponding derivative boundary.
  • supports Source: Backpropagation Through Time: What It Does and How to Do It · Locator: Section III.C–D, finite interval, zero future adjoints and initial states, PDF pp.6–7; selective output errors, p.7; chain-rule conditions, Section II.D, pp.3–4
Limitation #storage-and-detached-boundary
The displayed full reverse pass retains intermediate trajectory information and processes complete strings before their weight updates. In a constructed recurrence, carrying an intermediate state numerically while treating it as independent of parameters drops its earlier derivative contribution. Such a detached-boundary derivative need not equal the full-trajectory gradient, even when the forward states match. This boundary observation does not prescribe or validate a particular truncated-BPTT schedule. Storage and work depend on the computation and its implementation; no fixed hardware cost or universal memory lower bound is asserted.
  • supports Source: Backpropagation Through Time: What It Does and How to Do It · Locator: Sections III.D and IV.C, PDF pp.7,9–10; full chain rule in Section II.D, pp.3–4; curator terminal-loss detached-boundary example
Scope #gradient-versus-optimization
BPTT supplies derivatives that an optimizer can use. The source illustrates gradient descent and discusses alternative optimization methods, but a particular learning rate, update rule, minimum or convergence rate is not part of the derivative algorithm identity. Exact differentiation of the stated smooth finite objective does not imply exact floating-point arithmetic, successful optimization or generalization.
  • supports Source: Backpropagation Through Time: What It Does and How to Do It · Locator: Section III.D, PDF p.7; Section IV.D–E, optimization and local-minimum discussion with Table1, p.10
Scope #applications-and-implementation-scope
The treatment discusses recurrent pattern recognition, dynamic-system identification and control objectives, including differentiation through model and action networks. These application descriptions do not establish a reproduced benchmark, universal control performance or correct identification under arbitrary noise. The author explicitly says the printed code was not tested and presents it to explain the equations; this record does not certify that pseudocode or a modern library implementation.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The record covers full differentiation of a specified finite differentiable trajectory. Nonsmooth choices require a derivative convention, and stochastic expectation gradients require additional assumptions and estimators.
  • A detached intermediate boundary changes the derivative problem unless its omitted contribution is zero. Particular truncation, checkpointing, reversible or online implementations require separate evidence.
  • The initial-state and direct-parameter terms in the generalized formula are required by the chain rule. The source’s simple pseudocode uses fixed zero initial states and must not be generalized by silently dropping those terms.
  • All eleven scanned journal pages were read. Earlier historical-priority sources, executable author code, modern library code and empirical performance were not inspected or reproduced.
  • Constructed rational and finite-difference calculations check stated derivative boundaries only. No model training, paid service, hardware measurement or numerical benchmark was run.
