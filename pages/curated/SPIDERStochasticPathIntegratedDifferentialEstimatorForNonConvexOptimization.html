SPIDER Stochastic Path-Integrated Differential Estimator for Non-Convex Optimization

Algorithms AIMech:SPIDERStochasticPathIntegratedDifferentialEstimatorForNonConvexOptimization DRAFT
Aliases: Stochastic Path-Integrated Differential EstimatoR (SPIDER), SPIDER-SFO and SPIDER-SFO+ Non-Convex Stochastic Optimization, SPIDER-SZO Stochastic Zeroth-Order Path-Integrated Optimization
SPIDER Stochastic Path-Integrated Differential Estimator for Non-Convex Optimization is a martingale variance-reduction tracking framework and family of stochastic first-order (SPIDER-SFO, SPIDER-SFO+) and zeroth-order (SPIDER-SZO) non-convex optimization algorithms introduced by Fang, Li, Lin, and Zhang (2018) for minimizing smooth possibly non-convex objectives f(x) = E[F(x; zeta)] or f(x) = (1/n) sum_{i=1}^n f_i(x) with averaged L-Lipschitz gradients E||grad f_i(x) - grad f_i(y)||^2 <= L^2 ||x - y||^2. To dynamically track a deterministic quantity Q(x^k) along an iterate sequence x^{0:K}, SPIDER integrates unbiased differential increments xi_k(x^{0:k}) as Q_tilde(x^{0:K}) = Q_tilde(x^0) + sum_{k=1}^K xi_k(x^{0:k}), achieving the exact L^2-martingale variance identity E||Q_tilde(x^{0:K}) - Q(x^K)||^2 = E||Q_tilde(x^0) - Q(x^0)||^2 + sum_{k=1}^K E||xi_k(x^{0:k}) - (Q(x^k) - Q(x^{k-1}))||^2 (Proposition 1) and error bound E||V^k - B(x^k)||^2 <= k L_B^2 epsilon_1^2 / S_* + E||V^0 - B(x^0)||^2 whenever ||x^k - x^{k-1}|| <= epsilon_1 (Lemma 1). SPIDER-SFO (Algorithm 1) hybridizes this path-integrated gradient estimator v^k = grad f_{S_2}(x^k) - grad f_{S_2}(x^{k-1}) + v^{k-1} with Normalized Gradient Descent (NGD), updating x^{k+1} = x^k - eta^k v^k with stepsize eta^k = min(epsilon/(L n_0 ||v^k||), 1/(2 L n_0)) so that the step length is strictly capped at ||x^{k+1} - x^k|| <= epsilon/(L n_0), maintaining E[||v^k - grad f(x^k)||^2] <= epsilon^2 across epochs of length q (Lemma 2) and finding an epsilon-first-order stationary point in O(min(n + n^{1/2} epsilon^{-2}, epsilon^{-3})) stochastic gradient evaluations (Theorems 1-2), matching the Omega(L Delta n^{1/2} epsilon^{-2}) algorithmic lower bound (Theorem 3).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from SARAH (`AIMech:SARAHStochasticRecursiveGradientForFiniteSumOptimization`), which uses unnormalized gradient descent steps x_{t+1} = x_t - eta v_t with constant stepsize eta = Theta(1/L) for convex/strongly-convex objectives rather than Normalized Gradient Descent stepsizes proportional to epsilon / ||v^k||, and from Perturbed Gradient Descent (`AIMech:PerturbedGradientDescentForEscapingSaddlePointsInHessianLipschitzOptimization`), which uses full-batch gradients with isotropic ball noise in O(n epsilon^{-2}) evaluations. Specifically captures the general SPIDER martingale integration framework and Pinelis vector-martingale concentration bound (Proposition 1 and Lemma 1), the NGD-coupled SPIDER-SFO algorithm and matching finite-sum lower bound (Algorithm 1, Theorems 1-5), the NEON2-fused second-order stationary point solver SPIDER-SFO+ with mini-step negative-curvature maintenance (Algorithm 2, Theorem 6), and the two-point Gaussian zeroth-order solver SPIDER-SZO (Algorithm 3, Theorem 8).

Mechanism #claim-spider-martingale-variance-and-normalized-step-bound
For any sequence x^{0:K} with step displacements ||x^k - x^{k-1}|| <= epsilon_1 and mini-batch estimator B_{S_*}(x) satisfying E||B_i(x) - B_i(y)||^2 <= L_B^2 ||x - y||^2, the SPIDER recursion V^k = B_{S_*}(x^k) - B_{S_*}(x^{k-1}) + V^{k-1} forms a square-integrable martingale difference sum whose variance satisfies E||V^k - B(x^k)||^2 <= k L_B^2 epsilon_1^2 / S_* + E||V^0 - B(x^0)||^2 (Proposition 1(i) and Lemma 1) and dimension-free Pinelis high-probability concentration ||Q_tilde(x^{0:k}) - Q(x^k)|| <= 2 sqrt(sum_{s=0}^k b_s^2 log(1/gamma)) (Proposition 1(ii) and Proposition 2); coupling V^k with the Normalized Gradient Descent step x^{k+1} = x^k - eta^k v^k where eta^k = min(epsilon/(L n_0 ||v^k||), 1/(2 L n_0)) enforces ||x^{k+1} - x^k|| <= epsilon/(L n_0) and guarantees E[||v^k - grad f(x^k)||^2 | x^{0:k_0}] <= epsilon^2 across the entire epoch of length q (Lemma 2).

Mechanism #claim-spider-sfo-near-optimal-first-and-second-order-stationarity
Under averaged L-Lipschitz gradients (Assumption 1), SPIDER-SFO finds an epsilon-approximate first-order stationary point E[||grad f(x_tilde)||] <= 5*epsilon in at most 16 L Delta sigma epsilon^{-3} + 2 sigma^2 epsilon^{-2} + 4 sigma n_0^{-1} epsilon^{-1} = O(epsilon^{-3}) stochastic gradient evaluations in the online setting (Theorem 1) and n + 8 L Delta n^{1/2} epsilon^{-2} + 2 n_0^{-1} n^{1/2} = O(n + n^{1/2} epsilon^{-2}) evaluations in the finite-sum setting (Theorem 2), matching the algorithmic lower bound Omega(L Delta n^{1/2} epsilon^{-2}) for any randomized first-order algorithm when n <= O(Delta^2 L^2 epsilon^{-4}) (Theorem 3); furthermore, under rho-Lipschitz Hessians (Assumption 3), SPIDER-SFO+ (Algorithm 2) splits each delta/rho negative-curvature escape step into K = delta L n_0 / (rho epsilon) equal-length mini-steps of size eta = epsilon/(L n_0) to maintain the SPIDER estimator without full-gradient restart, finding an (epsilon, O(epsilon^{0.5}))-approximate second-order stationary point in O_tilde(min(n^{1/2} epsilon^{-2} + epsilon^{-2.5}, epsilon^{-3})) gradient evaluations (Theorem 6 and Corollary 7).

Limitation #claim-spider-small-n-regime-and-zeroth-order-dimension-overhead
Because SPIDER-SFO requires small normalized step sizes proportional to epsilon / (L n_0) to control martingale error accumulation along the path, in the finite-sum second-order regime when n <= O(epsilon^{-1}) unaccelerated SPIDER-SFO+ has complexity O_tilde(epsilon^{-2.5}), which is slower than momentum-accelerated NEON+FastCubic/CDHS methods achieving O_tilde(n^{3/4} epsilon^{-1.75}) (Footnote 1 and Section 3.3, p. 16); additionally, in stochastic zeroth-order optimization (SPIDER-SZO, Algorithm 3), two-point Gaussian smoothing incurs an O(d) variance factor E||g_hat_i(x) - g_hat_i(y)||^2 <= 2(d+4)L^2 ||x - y||^2 + 2 mu^2 (d+6)^3 L^2 (Lemma 3), scaling the Incremental Zeroth-Order Oracle complexity by d to O(d min(n^{1/2} epsilon^{-2}, epsilon^{-3})) (Theorem 8).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Requires small normalized stepsizes ||x^{k+1} - x^k|| = O(epsilon / L) to prevent martingale variance blowup, and is outperformed by momentum-accelerated methods in the small-sample second-order regime n <= O(epsilon^{-1}).
