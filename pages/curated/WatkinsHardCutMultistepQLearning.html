Watkins hard-cut multistep Q-learning

Algorithms AIMech:WatkinsHardCutMultistepQLearning DRAFT
The original hard-cut multistep Q-learning construction that estimates action returns using maximum-value bootstraps and continuation through policy actions, cuts subsequent-return contributions at non-policy actions, and updates action values from the delayed estimates.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This identity selects Watkins1989 hard-cut forward-return and delayed action-value updating, preserving the original policy-cut coefficient, displayed U timing gap and corrigenda boundaries. It is not an alias for one-step Q-learning, generic Q(lambda), MCQ-L/Sarsa, Peng–Williams control or the replacing-trace component. The soft-cut alternative and later backward/true-online implementations remain separate. Existing owner264 is used for scoped trace/control progress; owner267 remains the distinct routing lead. Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #action-values-and-implicit-greedy-policy
Represent action values Q_t(s,a) and derive the state estimate U_t^Q(s)=max_a Q_t(s,a). The implicit current policy chooses an action attaining that maximum. Actions actually taken may differ from that policy; learning action values does not itself specify how exploration is selected.
  • supports Source: Learning from Delayed Rewards · Locator: Chapter 7, Section 3, printed pp. 95–96 / PDF pp. 102–103, state-value and implicit-policy definitions and the action-selection discussion.
Mechanism #hard-cut-forward-return
Choose lambda_star in [0,1] and set lambda_t=lambda_star when a_t is classified as a policy action, and lambda_t=0 otherwise. With R_t denoting the source’s variable-lambda action-return estimate, its printed recursion is R_t=r_t+gamma*((1-lambda_(t+1))*U_t(s_(t+1))+lambda_(t+1)*R_(t+1)). In the action-value construction U is the maximum-based state estimate. This retains the literal U_t indexing of the displayed recursion; the source’s following prose uses a different time index, as recorded in the limitations.
  • supports Source: Learning from Delayed Rewards · Locator: Chapter 7, Section 2.3, printed pp. 93–95 / PDF pp. 100–102, especially the hard-cut rule and recursion on printed p. 94; Section 3.1, printed p. 97 / PDF p. 104.
Mechanism #cut-boundary-and-experimental-action-credit
When action a_(t+1) is non-policy, lambda_(t+1)=0 and the current action’s return reduces to r_t+gamma*U_t(s_(t+1)). The next-state bootstrap remains, while rewards generated by that non-policy action and subsequent steps do not propagate into earlier returns through this recursion. The estimate R_t still concerns the action actually taken at t, including when that action is itself experimental; the cut does not mean that experimental actions are excluded from action-value learning.
  • supports Source: Learning from Delayed Rewards · Locator: Chapter 7, Section 2.3, printed p. 94 / PDF p. 101, recursive cut explanation and distinction between action-value and state-value estimation; Section 3.1, printed p. 97 / PDF p. 104.
Mechanism #delayed-action-value-update
In the displayed delayed realization, once the return estimate for the state-action pair at time t is available at a later time t+T, update Q_(t+T+1)(s_t,a_t)=(1-alpha)*Q_(t+T)(s_t,a_t)+alpha*R_t. The value being blended is the pair’s value at the update time, not necessarily its value when the action was taken. The source also discusses incremental prediction-difference implementations, but this selected construction does not substitute a later online backward-trace loop for the displayed delayed update.
  • supports Source: Learning from Delayed Rewards · Locator: Chapter 7, Section 3.1, printed pp. 97–98 / PDF pp. 104–105, delayed update equation and changing-estimate discussion.
Scope #one-step-and-unit-continuation-boundaries
At lambda_star=0, all continuation coefficients vanish and the action-return estimate has the one-step reward-plus-maximum bootstrap target. At lambda_star=1, subsequent policy actions retain continuation, but a non-policy action still causes a cut and a bootstrap. Thus unit continuation is not an unconditional full-trajectory Monte Carlo target under arbitrary behavior, and equality of a one-step target does not establish equality of differently timed update trajectories.
  • supports Source: Learning from Delayed Rewards · Locator: Chapter 7, Section 2, printed p. 88 / PDF p. 95, constant-lambda endpoints; Section 2.3, printed pp. 93–95 / PDF pp. 100–102, variable-lambda cut; Section 3.1, printed p. 97 / PDF p. 104.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The selected scope is the original hard-cut forward-return and delayed-update construction, not every method called Q(lambda), the whole Q-learning family, selected-next-action MCQ-L/Sarsa, Peng–Williams two-error propagation, or a replacing-trace component. The thesis separately proposes a soft exponential continuation rule. That alternative is not a defining component or an exact alias of this hard-cut method.
  • The inspected return-estimation development assumes bounded rewards and discount gamma<1 in a Markov setting. Continuing undiscounted returns, unbounded rewards or arbitrary approximation require additional conditions. Lambda_star=1 does not remove non-policy cuts and does not itself establish existence of a return, stable learning or convergence.
  • The displayed recursion on printed p. 94 / PDF p. 101 uses U_t(s_(t+1)), while the immediately following explanatory prose invokes U_(t+1)(s_(t+1)). The draft preserves the displayed formula and records this timing gap instead of silently selecting a modern convention. Return construction, delayed updates and changing value estimates must use an explicitly declared timing convention in an implementation.
  • The initial corrigenda amend the prediction-difference development on printed pp. 90–91, retaining changing-value correction terms. The original printed p. 91 update also uses C(x,t+1) after defining C(x,t). These passages do not establish exact equality between arbitrary finite-step online updates and the delayed forward construction. Small-step remarks are not a uniform error bound or a true-online equivalence theorem.
  • The preliminary policy-classification discussion assumes a unique policy action per state, while the maximum-based policy can have ties. Tie breaking, the classification of equal-valued alternatives, initialization, terminal treatment and episode resets are not fully specified by this selected construction. An exploratory sampling branch can still select a maximizing action; its label alone does not determine whether the selected action is non-policy.
  • The thesis explicitly does not prove convergence of this multistep control family and discusses possible interactions between changing values and policies. Its one-step result is not a general proof for this method, nonlinear approximators, arbitrary behavior or arbitrary learning rates. This is a boundary of the inspected original source, not a claim that no later theorem exists.
  • Evidence is a 241-page Cambridge-hosted scan of the May 1989 thesis, including its initial corrigenda. The source’s PDF build date and repository modification date are not publication dates. Selected methods, qualifications and formula images were inspected; no full-thesis proof certification, author-code inspection or experiment reproduction is claimed. Historical demonstrations do not establish universal speed, variance reduction or benchmark superiority.
