GSAT clause-weighting strategy

Algorithms AIMech:GSATClauseWeighting DRAFT
A GSAT extension that starts each clause with weight one, uses weighted clause counts to select flips, and increases the weights of clauses unsatisfied by the current assignment at the end of each search attempt.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is Strategy I of Selman and Kautz’s 1993 GSAT extensions. The adaptive weights guide solving the original SAT formula; they are not externally supplied weighted MAX-SAT preferences. The update occurs between attempts and uses the current end-of-attempt assignment, unlike Breakout’s reweighting at non-solution local minima within a run or Fill’s state-specific cost lifts. The paper credits Morris with an independently proposed similar approach rather than claiming exclusive invention. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Initialize all clause weights to 1. During a GSAT attempt, count a clause according to its positive integer weight when evaluating the change in satisfied-clause score from a variable flip. Choose a best weighted-score flip with the underlying GSAT tie convention. A clause of weight L therefore contributes as if it occurred L times. At the end of an unsuccessful attempt, increment by K the weights of every clause unsatisfied by the current assignment, retain the resulting weights and begin the next attempt under the selected initialization rule. The paper normally uses K=1. Satisfaction of the original formula remains the stopping goal; adaptive weights alter move ranking but do not change which assignments satisfy all clauses. The source’s update names the current assignment, not the best assignment seen earlier in that attempt.
  • supports Source: Domain-Independent Extensions to GSAT: Solving Large Structured Satisfiability Problems · Locator: Section 2 and Figure 1, PDF1–2, base GSAT; Section 3.1, PDF2–3, positive-integer clause interpretation and Strategy I; Figure 3 and discussion PDF4

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The source reports an empirical search extension, not a completeness or polynomial-time guarantee. Unsuccessful finite search does not certify UNSAT, and Fill’s separate completeness theorem must not be transferred to these shared clause weights.
  • The update schedule matters: raising weights at every local minimum, at every flip, or according to a stored best assignment implements a different rule. The source does not prescribe a decay, cap or automatic normalization of these retained weights.
  • K=1 is a reported usual choice, not a universal optimum. Positive integer weights preserve the source’s repeated-clause interpretation; arbitrary real, negative or externally fixed weights require a separate formulation.
  • Attempt budgets, initialization, tie handling and the original GSAT final-flip test boundary remain implementation choices to declare. Combining the extension with other strategies does not justify inventing their interaction or reset semantics. No source solver or numerical experiment was run.
