PaCMAP

Algorithms AIMech:PaCMAP DRAFT
Aliases: Pairwise Controlled Manifold Approximation Projection
A nonlinear dimensionality-reduction algorithm that embeds neighbor, mid-near and farther point pairs using separate attractive and repulsive losses whose relative weights change through three optimization phases.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope PaCMAP uses three classes of pair losses and a changing phase schedule, rather than TriMap’s coupled triplet loss or UMAP’s fuzzy graph construction. The graph-sampling rules and phase weights are defining components, not a proof of universal local/global preservation. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method-scope
Choose neighbors from a Euclidean-neighbor candidate pool using locally scaled distances, choose mid-near partners as the second closest among six sampled observations, and sample non-neighbors for farther pairs. With d̃=||y_a−y_b||²+1, use d̃/(10+d̃) for neighbors, d̃/(10000+d̃) for mid-near pairs and 1/(1+d̃) for farther pairs. Optimize with Adam, ordinarily from PCA initialization, first emphasizing mid-near attraction, then reducing it and finally setting its weight to zero while refining local structure.
  • supports Source: Understanding How Dimension Reduction Tools Work: An Empirical Approach to Deciphering t-SNE, UMAP, TriMap, and PaCMAP for Data Visualization · Locator: Sections 3.4 and 7, PDF pp. 11–12 and 36–39; Algorithm 1, p. 37; initialization qualifications, Section 6.2, pp. 35–36

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Random sampling and the initial coordinates affect the result. The authors explicitly state that robustness to initialization is incomplete and that initialization scale matters.
  • A mid-near partner is selected by a six-point sample rule, not an exact global distance quantile; the method does not guarantee that every such partner is outside the nearest-neighbor set.
  • Locally scaled distances select neighbor pairs, while optimization uses embedded distances and pair-class weights; the original distances are not fitted as an all-pairs metric.
  • The three-phase procedure seeks a useful nonconvex layout. No global optimum, exact topology preservation, density preservation or universal performance advantage is asserted.
