Matching pursuit

Algorithms AIMech:MatchingPursuit DRAFT

A greedy fixed-dictionary approximation algorithm that repeatedly selects a unit-norm atom by its absolute correlation with the current residual and subtracts the residual projection onto that atom.
Definition Evidence
  • supports Source: Matching Pursuits With Time-Frequency Dictionaries · Locator: Section III, Equations 9–17, PDF pp. 3–4 / printed pp. 3399–3400
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The record covers the finite real-dictionary algorithm and identifies the paper’s fixed-fraction weak-selection generalization. MP is used only as a contextual source abbreviation and is not an identity alias. It differs from orthogonal matching pursuit, which refits the whole selected span at every step. Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #single-atom-update
For supplied unit real atoms g_j and r_0=y, exact matching chooses an index maximizing abs(g_j^T*r_k), sets c_k=g_j^T*r_k and r_(k+1)=r_k-c_k*g_j, and adds c_k*g_j to the approximation. Ties require a choice rule. The source also permits a fixed weak fraction 0<alpha<=1 with selected correlation at least alpha times the supremum; alpha=1 is attainable for a finite dictionary. The dictionary remains fixed, and selected atoms can recur.
  • supports Source: Matching Pursuits With Time-Frequency Dictionaries · Locator: Section III, Equations 9–17, PDF pp. 3–4 / printed pp. 3399–3400
Mechanism #residual-and-energy
Each residual is orthogonal to the atom just selected, and ||r_k||^2=abs(c_k)^2+||r_(k+1)||^2. Summing gives ||y||^2=sum_k abs(c_k)^2+||r_m||^2 for the performed steps. This sum uses per-step coefficients; combining repeated occurrences of an atom does not preserve that coefficient-energy formula. Earlier selected atoms need not remain orthogonal to the residual.
  • supports Source: Matching Pursuits With Time-Frequency Dictionaries · Locator: Section III, Equations 9–17, PDF pp. 3–4 / printed pp. 3399–3400
Limitation #projection-convergence-boundary
The ideal weak-selection sequence with a fixed positive fraction converges to the signal component orthogonal to the closed dictionary span. Thus zero residual requires the signal to belong to that span. The complete finite-dimensional case has a dictionary-dependent geometric residual bound. Neither statement guarantees finite termination, minimum support, a unique decomposition, a practical uniform rate, or the same behavior after early stopping and finite-precision errors.
  • supports Source: Matching Pursuits With Time-Frequency Dictionaries · Locator: Section III Theorem 1, Section IV Lemmas 1–2, Appendices A–B, PDF pp. 4,6,14–15 / printed pp. 3400,3402,3410–3411
Scope #orthogonal-refit-distinction
An exact curator example uses D=[e1,b], b=(3/5,4/5), y=e2. MP chooses b then e1, with step coefficients 4/5 and -12/25, leaving residual (0,9/25), whose correlation with b is 36/125. It selects b again next. OMP instead refits those two atoms to coefficients 5/4 on b and -3/4 on e1, giving zero residual. The original MP paper’s optional final backprojection onto the selected span is a separate correction; it does not refit before each subsequent selection as OMP does.
  • supports Source: Matching Pursuits With Time-Frequency Dictionaries · Locator: Section III, Equations 18–29, PDF pp. 4–5 / printed pp. 3400–3401; curator-derived exact examples in research/discovery/pursuit-boundaries.py
  • supports Source: Orthogonal Matching Pursuit: Recursive Function Approximation with Applications to Wavelet Decomposition · Locator: Sections 2–2.2, PDF pp. 2–3 / printed pp. 41–42; curator-derived exact examples in research/discovery/pursuit-boundaries.py
Scope #structured-implementation-scope
The paper’s Gabor implementation uses a structured coarse dictionary, local refinement and recursive correlation updates. Its O(N log N) per-iteration analysis uses those dictionary and numerical approximation assumptions, not an arbitrary dense dictionary. The time-frequency representation and denoising examples are applications of the algorithm; the supplied atoms are not learned by the basic pursuit recursion.
  • supports Source: Matching Pursuits With Time-Frequency Dictionaries · Locator: Sections IV–VIII and Appendix E, PDF pp. 5–14,17–18 / printed pp. 3401–3410,3413–3414

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The maintained definition is scoped to supplied finite real unit-norm dictionaries. Complex or infinite dictionaries, learned dictionaries, batch methods and recovery theorems need their own conditions and evidence.
  • Greedy representation is not a universal minimum-support or minimum-L1 solver. Ties, normalization, conditioning and stopping criteria can affect the selected representation.
  • Both original papers were visually read in full. No author implementation, model training or empirical benchmark was run; the rational examples are curator calculations. The record remains DRAFT and this work provides no independent scientific approval.
  • The scan appears to omit squares in Equation 35; the consistent squared energy identity is used. Speech sample and selected-atom counts also differ within the paper, so no quantitative performance claim is curated from those ambiguous examples.
