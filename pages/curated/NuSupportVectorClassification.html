Nu support vector classification

Algorithms AIMech:NuSupportVectorClassification DRAFT
Aliases: Nu-SVC, ν-SVC, Nu-SV classification
A supervised binary-classification algorithm that jointly fits a regularized kernel hyperplane and a nonnegative margin level, using nu to reward that level relative to linearly penalized margin shortfalls.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The original binary nu-SVC formulation in Scholkopf, Smola, Williamson and Bartlett (2000) is the selected identity. It differs from a particular decomposition solver, multiclass reduction, nu-SVR and the one-class estimator; source-specific equivalence to C-SVC requires fitted positive rho and parameter conversion. Claims & Grounded Evidence

7 Claims
Mechanism #learned-margin-objective
For n observations with both binary labels y_i in{-1,+1} represented and a positive-semidefinite kernel, minimize (1/2)||w||^2-nu*rho+sum_i xi_i/n, subject to y_i*(w.Phi(x_i)+b)>=rho-xi_i, xi_i>=0 and rho>=0. A positive feasible nu is at most2*min(n_positive,n_negative)/n, as follows directly from the balanced dual masses and coefficient caps. Values beyond this class-balance limit make the stated dual infeasible and primal unbounded. For three positive and one negative label, nu=3/4 exceeds the limit1/2; w=0,b=rho=t and negative slack2t give objective -t/4.
  • supports Source: New Support Vector Algorithms · Locator: Section 7, PDF pp17-19 / printed pp1223-1225, Equations 7.3-7.12; feasibility implication and exact example in research/discovery/nu-svm-boundaries.py
Mechanism #dual-and-decision
The dual minimizes (1/2)*alpha^T*Y*K*Y*alpha, with0<=alpha_i<=1/n, sum_i alpha_i*y_i=0 and sum_i alpha_i>=nu. The score is g(x)=sum_i alpha_i*y_i*k(x_i,x)+b and the classifier takes its sign. The source does not specify how an exactly zero score is assigned to one binary label. When free coefficients exist in both classes, KKT equalities give g(x_positive)=rho and g(x_negative)=-rho and permit recovery of b and rho. Such free coefficients need not exist; general recovery must respect the remaining KKT inequalities.
  • supports Source: New Support Vector Algorithms · Locator: Section 7, PDF pp18-19 / printed pp1224-1225, Equations 7.7-7.15
Scope #margin-error-counts
For an exact optimum with rho>0, sum_i alpha_i=nu. Thus nu bounds the fraction of strict margin errors y_i*g(x_i)<rho from above and the positive-coefficient fraction from below. A margin error can still be correctly classified; it is not the same event as misclassification. Finite counts need not equal nu, and capped coefficients can occur on the margin. Four linear-kernel inputs -1,-1,1,1 with matching labels and nu=1/2 admit alpha_i=1/8,w=1/2,b=0,rho=1/2: all points are support vectors and none is a strict margin error. The source states the finite result under an analytic-kernel assumption; its additional asymptotic distribution and boundary-mass arguments are not promoted to unconditional calibration.
  • supports Source: New Support Vector Algorithms · Locator: Section 7 Proposition 5, PDF pp20-21 / printed pp1226-1227; exact finite certificate in research/discovery/nu-svm-boundaries.py
Scope #c-svc-correspondence
If the fitted rho is positive, dividing w,b and slacks by rho yields a C-SVC optimum with C=1/rho in the paper’s C/n slack-penalty convention, preserving the sign decision. The numerical conversion differs when software uses C times the unaveraged slack sum. A global positive multiplier on the nu-SVC loss terms scales the solution without changing its boundary, explaining removal of that separate C parameter. This does not remove kernel selection or make arbitrary C and nu settings equivalent, and the conversion cannot divide by rho=0.
  • supports Source: New Support Vector Algorithms · Locator: Section 7, PDF p19 / printed p1225, homogeneity discussion; Proposition 6, PDF p21 / printed p1227
Limitation #degenerate-and-nonunique-offsets
Feasibility does not guarantee positive rho or a useful binary separator. Balanced opposite labels at identical zero inputs admit w=b=rho=0. Even at rho>0, b and rho can be nonunique when no coefficient is free: for linear-kernel inputs -1 and1 with matching labels and nu=1, both coefficients are1/2 and w=1, while (b,rho)=(0,1) and(3,4) attain the same objective -1/2 and classify the first training point differently. Strict convexity fixes the optimal normal w, not necessarily the intercept, margin variable or singular-Gram coefficient representation.
  • supports Source: New Support Vector Algorithms · Locator: Section 7, PDF pp18-21 / printed pp1224-1227, primal/dual constraints, free-coefficient formulas and positive-rho condition; exact certificates in research/discovery/nu-svm-boundaries.py
Limitation #generalization-and-robustness-limits
The generalization analysis adds sample-size, confidence, margin and function-class complexity terms to empirical margin error; test risk is not bounded by nu alone. Its displayed normalized radial-kernel bound omits the intercept b, and the authors explicitly note that treating b requires a modification. Their resistance statement is conditional on a representation using free support vectors and local movements in feature space parallel to w; it is not arbitrary input-space or label-noise robustness. External proof dependencies were not independently audited.
  • supports Source: New Support Vector Algorithms · Locator: Section 7 Propositions 7-10, PDF pp21-24 / printed pp1227-1230, including the paragraph following Proposition 10
Limitation #classification-evaluation-scope
The classification study shows a two-dimensional circles-versus-disks example with a Gaussian kernel and nu values from0.1 to0.8, reporting support-vector and margin-error fractions plus geometric margins. The paper leaves real-world nu-SVC evaluation and efficient chunking methods to future work. It supplies no universal accuracy or runtime advantage over C-SVC, and later decomposition solvers are not identified with this original optimization formulation.
  • supports Source: New Support Vector Algorithms · Locator: Section 8.2 and Table 4/Figure 11, PDF pp32 and35 / printed pp1238 and1241; Section 9, PDF p36 / printed p1242
