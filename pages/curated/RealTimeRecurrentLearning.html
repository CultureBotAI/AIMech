Real-time recurrent learning

Algorithms AIMech:RealTimeRecurrentLearning DRAFT
Aliases: RTRL
A recurrent-network training algorithm that propagates state sensitivities to adjustable weights forward in time and contracts those sensitivities with current supervised output errors to form weight updates.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #forward-state-sensitivities
For the inspected synchronous network y_k(t+1)=f_k(sum_l w_kl*z_l(t)), z concatenates recurrent outputs and external inputs, including a constant bias input when needed. The sensitivity p_ij^k(t+1)=f_k'(s_k(t))*(sum_l w_kl*p_ij^l(t)+delta_ik*z_j(t)) propagates through every recurrent unit. Initial sensitivities are zero when the initial state is independent of the weights. Weight-dependent initialization or externally supplied signals require their additional derivatives; zero initialization is not universally valid.
  • supports Source: A Learning Algorithm for Continually Running Fully Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 2.1, Equations 1–3 and 10–14, PDF pp. 2–4
Mechanism #fixed-trajectory-gradient
With fixed weights and parameter-independent inputs, targets and initial state, contracting p with target-minus-output discrepancies gives the negative gradient of the current half-squared error. Summing these negative-gradient contributions gives the negative trajectory-loss gradient without storing the entire trajectory. Units lacking a target at a time contribute zero direct error, yet their sensitivities must generally still be propagated. Forward sensitivity propagation and reverse backpropagation through time agree for the same differentiable fixed-weight objective; they organize the derivative computation differently.
  • supports Source: A Learning Algorithm for Continually Running Fully Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Sections 1 and 2.1, Equations 4–15, PDF pp. 2–4; Section 2.4, p. 6; curator fixed-trajectory forward/reverse and finite-difference checks
Limitation #online-weight-drift
The real-time variant applies each update while the recurrent network continues running, avoiding a required epoch boundary. The source explicitly states that this no longer follows the exact negative gradient of the total error along a fixed-weight trajectory. Weight changes also change the generated trajectory; a sufficiently slow learning timescale is a heuristic control, not a general convergence guarantee. In a constructed linear recurrence with initial state 1 and successive weights 1/2 and 3/5, the propagated sensitivity is 11/10, whereas rerunning both steps at the current fixed weight gives derivative 6/5. Exactness of the fixed-parameter recursion must not be transferred unqualified to changing weights.
  • supports Source: A Learning Algorithm for Continually Running Fully Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 2.2, PDF pp. 4–5; curator two-step changing-weight calculation
Mechanism #teacher-forced-variant
Teacher forcing replaces a unit’s output by its supplied target in subsequent state computation whenever that target exists. Assuming those targets are independent of the weights, the sensitivity recurrence omits propagation through the forced outputs. Their sensitivities still contribute to the current weight update before being treated as zero for future propagation. This changes the training dynamics and derivative paths; it is an optional RTRL configuration, not the definition of all RTRL or an assurance of autonomous free-running performance.
  • supports Source: A Learning Algorithm for Continually Running Fully Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 2.3, Equations 17–19 and implementation explanation, PDF pp. 5–6
Limitation #storage-and-nonlocality
For n recurrent units and r adjustable weights the direct method retains n*r sensitivities, including sensitivities of units without immediate targets. A fully connected n-unit network with m external inputs has r=n*(n+m), hence n^2*(n+m) sensitivity values. Directly evaluating the displayed recurrent sums takes order n^2*r arithmetic per time step, or order n^3*(n+m) for that dense parameterization. Storage does not grow with elapsed sequence length at fixed network size; this is not constant cost in network size or a guaranteed wall-clock deadline. The method needs nonlocal weight and error information and is not established as a biologically local learning rule.
  • supports Source: A Learning Algorithm for Continually Running Fully Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 2.4, PDF p. 6; Discussion, p. 8; direct count from Equation 12, p. 4
Scope #experimental-scope
Reported demonstrations use small fully interconnected networks on delayed XOR, sequence recognition, delayed nonmatch, finite-state-controller imitation and oscillation tasks. The Turing-machine example is described as imitation of finite-state-controller actions; it does not demonstrate that a finite recurrent state alone supplies an unbounded tape or arbitrary-program learning. The oscillation section reports that teacher forcing was needed for general success in the tested setting and discusses near-zero gradients around initially settling dynamics. These selected demonstrations do not establish universal convergence, arbitrary memory fidelity, computational efficiency or success without teacher forcing.
  • supports Source: A Learning Algorithm for Continually Running Fully Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Sections 3.1–3.5 and Discussion, PDF pp. 6–8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The mechanism is scoped to the inspected ten-page manuscript copy of Williams and Zipser (1989). It acknowledges earlier related work; no sole-inventor or historical-priority claim is made. Final publisher layout and later implementations were not inspected.
  • Fixed-weight derivative exactness assumes the stated differentiable dynamics and parameter-independent supplied signals and initialization. The source’s online weight updates are approximate with respect to the fixed-trajectory gradient; numerical floating-point exactness is not asserted.
  • The complete sensitivity tensor can be expensive even though its dimensions do not grow with sequence length. Restricted, approximate, sparse or low-rank algorithms require their own evidence and identity assessment.
  • Teacher forcing changes state evolution and cuts specified derivative paths. Reported training success does not establish matching free-running behavior or biologically local learning.
  • The small task demonstrations and qualitative results are source reports, not a reproduced benchmark or a guarantee of arbitrary-duration memory and convergence.
  • All ten manuscript PDF pages and references were read. Constructed differentiation and algebra checks use fixed small examples without fitting; no author code, model training, hardware benchmark or empirical replication was run.
