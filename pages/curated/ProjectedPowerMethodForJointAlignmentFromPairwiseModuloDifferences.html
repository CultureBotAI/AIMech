Projected Power Method for Joint Alignment from Pairwise Modulo Differences

Algorithms AIMech:ProjectedPowerMethodForJointAlignmentFromPairwiseModuloDifferences DRAFT
Aliases: Projected Power Method (PPM) for Joint Alignment, Chen-Candes Projected Power Iteration for Cyclic Group Synchronization, Block-Simplex Projected Power Method from Pairwise Modulo Differences
A nonconvex iterative discrete synchronization and joint alignment algorithm (introduced by Yuxin Chen and Emmanuel J. Candès in 2018) for recovering $n$ latent discrete shifts or group elements $x_1,\dots,x_n \in \{0,1,\dots,m-1\}$ (up to a global modular shift $\bmod\ m$) from corrupted pairwise difference measurements. Representing each state $x_i$ as standard basis vector $\boldsymbol{e}_{x_i} \in \mathbb{R}^m$ and stacking $\boldsymbol{z} = [\boldsymbol{z}_1^\top,\dots,\boldsymbol{z}_n^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{nm}$ along with the $nm \times nm$ block cyclic likelihood matrix $\boldsymbol{L}$ whose $(i,j)$ block encodes the cyclic log-likelihood scores of $x_i - x_j \equiv s \pmod m$, maximum likelihood joint alignment reduces to the nonconvex quadratic program $\max_{\boldsymbol{z}} \boldsymbol{z}^\top\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{z}$ subject to $\boldsymbol{z}_i \in \{\boldsymbol{e}_0,\dots,\boldsymbol{e}_{m-1}\}$ (Equations 2.2–2.5). The Projected Power Method (PPM, Algorithm 1) relaxes the discrete vertices to the scaled probability simplex $\Delta_m = \{\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{R}_+^m : \mathbf{1}^\top\boldsymbol{w} \le 1\}$ and iterates $\boldsymbol{z}^{t+1} = \mathcal{P}_{\Delta}(\mu_t\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{z}^t)$ (Equations 2.7–2.10), where $\mathcal{P}_{\Delta}$ applies blockwise Euclidean projection $\mathcal{P}_{\Delta_m}(\boldsymbol{v}_i) = \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{w}\in\Delta_m} \|\boldsymbol{w} - \boldsymbol{v}_i\|_2^2$ computable in $O(m\log m)$ sorting time (or $O(m)$ expected randomized pivot time) and $\mu_t$ is an amplification factor pushing iterates onto the extreme vertices $\{\boldsymbol{e}_s\}$. Theorems 1–3 prove that under a randomized corruption model with $p_{\mathrm{obs}} \gtrsim \frac{\log^2(nm)}{n \cdot \mathrm{KL}^{\min}}$ (where $\mathrm{KL}^{\min}$ is the minimum Kullback–Leibler divergence between shifted pairwise likelihoods), spectral initialization followed by $O(\log n)$ PPM iterations recovers all $n$ shifts exactly with high probability, matching the information-theoretic Fano/minimax threshold up to logarithmic factors (Theorem 4).

Distinct from `AIMech:ImplicitRegularizationInNonconvexStatisticalEstimationByLeaveOneOutTrajectoryAnalysis` (Ma, Wang, Chi, & Chen 2020, which analyzes continuous-phase $U(1)$ synchronization and unprojected quadratic forms via leave-one-out trajectories, whereas Chen & Candès 2018 formulate the lifted $nm \times nm$ block-circulant discrete $\mathbb{Z}_m$ synchronization objective, the scaled simplex projection operator $\mathcal{P}_{\Delta}(\mu_t \boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{z}^t)$ that snaps continuous relaxations onto discrete indicator vertices in finitely many steps, and the $\mathrm{KL}^{\min}$ minimax threshold).

Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #claim-block-circulant-lifting-and-scaled-simplex-projection-contraction
Lifting discrete states $x_i \in \mathbb{Z}_m$ to indicator blocks $\boldsymbol{z}_i = \boldsymbol{e}_{x_i} \in \mathbb{R}^m$ turns non-concave multi-frequency pairwise likelihoods into the bilinear block-circulant quadratic form $\boldsymbol{z}^\top\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{z}$ (Equations 2.2–2.5), and Lemma 1 proves that the scaled simplex projection $\mathcal{P}_{\Delta_m}(\mu\boldsymbol{v}_i)$ is firmly non-expansive ($\|\mathcal{P}_{\Delta_m}(\boldsymbol{a}) - \mathcal{P}_{\Delta_m}(\boldsymbol{b})\|_2 \le \|\boldsymbol{a} - \boldsymbol{b}\|_2$) while mapping any vector with a strict top-coordinate separation $v_{i,(1)} - v_{i,(2)} \ge 1/\mu$ exactly onto the discrete vertex $\boldsymbol{e}_{\arg\max_s v_{i,s}}$ (Section 2.2, Equations 2.7–2.10, Section 6.1, Lemmas 1–4).
  • supports Source: The Projected Power Method: An Efficient Algorithm for Joint Alignment from Pairwise Differences · Locator: Section 2.2 (Eqs. 2.7-2.10, Algorithm 1, pp. 7-10), Section 6.1 (Lemmas 1-4, pp. 24-27)
Mechanism #claim-two-stage-exact-discrete-alignment-in-logarithmic-iterations
Within the contraction basin $\{\boldsymbol{z} \in \Delta : \|\boldsymbol{z} - \boldsymbol{z}^*\|_2 \le \delta\sqrt{n}\}$, Theorem 1 proves that PPM iterates $\boldsymbol{z}^{t+1} = \mathcal{P}_{\Delta}(\mu_t\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{z}^t)$ first contract geometrically in $\ell_2$ norm ($\|\boldsymbol{z}^t - \boldsymbol{z}^*\|_2 \le \rho^t\|\boldsymbol{z}^0 - \boldsymbol{z}^*\|_2$) and then, by entrywise $\ell_\infty$ separation of the true coordinate score $(\boldsymbol{L}\boldsymbol{z}^t)_{i,x_i^*}$ above all $s \ne x_i^*$, snap to the exact ground-truth discrete configuration $\boldsymbol{z}^t = \boldsymbol{z}^*$ in at most $T = O(\log n)$ iterations when $p_{\mathrm{obs}} \gtrsim \frac{\log^2(nm)}{n \cdot \mathrm{KL}^{\min}}$ (Section 3, Theorems 1–3, Sections 6.2–6.4, Lemmas 5–10).
  • supports Source: The Projected Power Method: An Efficient Algorithm for Joint Alignment from Pairwise Differences · Locator: Section 3 (Theorems 1-3, pp. 11-16), Sections 6.2-6.4 (Lemmas 5-10, pp. 28-38)
Application #claim-information-theoretic-minimax-kl-divergence-threshold
Theorem 4 establishes that if $p_{\mathrm{obs}} < \frac{(1-\varepsilon)\log n}{n \cdot \max_{l \ne 0}\mathrm{KL}(P_0 \| P_l)}$, then any estimator fails to recover the exact joint alignment with probability approaching 1, proving that PPM achieves the information-theoretic sample complexity threshold up to logarithmic factors while running in $O(|\mathcal{E}|m\log m)$ time per iteration via Fast Fourier Transforms on the circulant blocks (Sections 2.2 and 3.2, Theorem 4, Appendix D).
  • supports Source: The Projected Power Method: An Efficient Algorithm for Joint Alignment from Pairwise Differences · Locator: Section 2.2 (p. 9), Section 3.2 (Theorem 4, pp. 15-16), Appendix D (pp. 51-53)

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Because each node is lifted to an m-dimensional indicator vector z_i in R^m, the memory footprint of storing the iterate z is O(nm) and the per-edge FFT multiplication costs O(m log m), which requires moderately sized discretization grids m.
  • Theorem 2's spectral initialization requires the spectral gap of the lifted matrix L to exceed the random corruption perturbation, which can require warm-start anchor nodes or multi-frequency initialization when m is large and the clean observation fraction is very small.
