MSDA and SSDA (Multi-Step and Single-Step Dual Accelerated Decentralized Optimization)

Algorithms AIMech:MsdaMultiStepDualAcceleratedDecentralizedOptimization DRAFT
Aliases: MSDA and SSDA, Multi-Step Dual Accelerated Decentralized Optimization, Single-Step Dual Accelerated Decentralized Optimization
Rate-optimal decentralized optimization algorithms for alpha-strongly convex and beta-smooth objectives min_{theta_1=...=theta_n} sum_{i=1}^n f_i(theta_i) over a network with gossip Laplacian W (Scaman, Bach, Bubeck, Lee, & Massoulie 2017, Section 3, Algorithms 1-2): Single-Step Dual Accelerated (SSDA, Algorithm 1) reformulates the consensus problem into the unconstrained dual max_{lambda} -F^*(lambda sqrt(W)) and applies Nesterov's accelerated gradient descent using local dual conjugate gradients nabla f_i^*(y_{i,t}) gossiped by W; Multi-Step Dual Accelerated (MSDA, Algorithm 2) replaces W with the K-step Chebyshev polynomial gossip operator P_K(W) = I - T_K(c_2(I - c_1 W)) / T_K(c_2) where K = floor(1 / sqrt(gamma(W))) and gamma(W) = lambda_{n-1}(W) / lambda_1(W).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from primal-dual consensus algorithms such as EXTRA (AIMech:ExtraExactFirstOrderDecentralizedConsensusOptimization, Shi et al. 2015) and DIGing (AIMech:DigingAndPushDigingDistributedInexactGradientTrackingOverTimeVaryingGraphs, Nedic et al. 2017), which evaluate primal gradients nabla f_i(x_i) without Nesterov dual acceleration or Chebyshev polynomial gossip and have non-accelerated O(kappa_l / gamma) dependence on the condition number kappa_l = beta / alpha and spectral gap gamma(W).

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #r435-decentralized-and-centralized-lower-bounds-theorems-1-and-2
For black-box distributed optimization with local computation time 1 and per-link communication delay tau, Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 establish a centralized lower bound Omega(sqrt(kappa_g)(1 + Delta tau) ln(1 / varepsilon)) in terms of the global condition number kappa_g = beta_g / alpha_g and network diameter Delta, while Theorem 2 and Corollary 2 construct adversarially split chain/grid graphs to prove a decentralized gossip lower bound Omega(sqrt(kappa_l)(1 + tau / sqrt(gamma)) ln(1 / varepsilon)) in terms of the local condition number kappa_l = beta / alpha and normalized Laplacian eigengap gamma = lambda_{n-1}(W) / lambda_1(W).
  • supports Source: Optimal Algorithms for Smooth and Strongly Convex Distributed Optimization in Networks · Locator: Section 2 Optimal Convergence Rates (Theorems 1-2, Corollaries 1-2)
Mechanism #r435-chebyshev-gossip-eigengap-boost-and-msda-optimal-time-theorem
Whereas SSDA (Theorem 3) requires O(sqrt(kappa_l / gamma)(1 + tau) ln(1 / varepsilon)) time, MSDA's Chebyshev polynomial operator P_K(W) with c_1 = 2 / (lambda_1(W) + lambda_{n-1}(W)), c_2 = (1 + gamma) / (1 - gamma), and K = floor(1 / sqrt(gamma)) satisfies 1 / sqrt(gamma(P_K(W))) <= 2 (Proposition 1), decoupling computation and communication so MSDA reaches varepsilon-precision in O(sqrt(kappa_l) ln(1 / varepsilon)) local conjugate-gradient steps and O(sqrt(kappa_l / gamma) ln(1 / varepsilon)) gossip steps, matching the optimal decentralized time bound O(sqrt(kappa_l)(1 + tau / sqrt(gamma)) ln(1 / varepsilon)) (Theorem 4).
  • supports Source: Optimal Algorithms for Smooth and Strongly Convex Distributed Optimization in Networks · Locator: Section 3.2 SSDA (Theorem 3) & Section 3.3 MSDA (Proposition 1, Algorithm 2, Theorem 4)
Limitation #r435-fenchel-conjugate-oracle-and-spectral-parameter-knowledge-limitation
Requires each node i to evaluate the Fenchel conjugate gradient nabla f_i^*(y) = argmax_theta (<y, theta> - f_i(theta)) (an exact local inner minimization at every outer step) and requires prior knowledge of the spectral bounds lambda_1(W) and lambda_{n-1}(W) as well as alpha and beta.
  • supports Source: Optimal Algorithms for Smooth and Strongly Convex Distributed Optimization in Networks · Locator: Section 3.1 Dual Formulation & Section 3.2-3.3 Algorithms 1-2
Application #r435-decentralized-regression-and-regularized-classification-application
Applied to decentralized l_2-regularized linear regression and logistic classification across grid, cycle, and Erdos-Renyi communication networks.
  • supports Source: Optimal Algorithms for Smooth and Strongly Convex Distributed Optimization in Networks · Locator: Section 3.3 Practical Remarks & Appendix Proof of Theorem 4

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Each outer step requires evaluating the exact Fenchel conjugate gradient nabla f_i^*(y_{i,t}) on every node.
  • Requires a static symmetric gossip Laplacian W with known eigengap gamma(W).
