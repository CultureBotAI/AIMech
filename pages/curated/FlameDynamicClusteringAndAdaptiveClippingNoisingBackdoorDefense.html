FLAME Dynamic Clustering and Adaptive Clipping-Noising Backdoor Defense

Algorithms AIMech:FlameDynamicClusteringAndAdaptiveClippingNoisingBackdoorDefense DRAFT
Aliases: FLAME Taming Backdoors in Federated Learning, FLAME Federated Backdoor Defense Framework, FLAME HDBSCAN Cosine Clustering and Adaptive Clipping-Noising
A three-stage federated learning backdoor defense framework that (1) computes pairwise cosine distances c_ij = 1 - (W_i . W_j)/(||W_i||_2 ||W_j||_2) across all n client models and applies HDBSCAN density-based clustering with minimum cluster size floor(n/2) + 1 to admit the majority benign cluster while rejecting angularly deviating backdoor outliers, (2) clips admitted model updates W_bl^c = G_{t-1}^* + (W_bl - G_{t-1}^*) * min(1, S_t / e_bl) to the median Euclidean update norm S_t = MEDIAN(e_1, ..., e_n) across all n clients, and (3) injects adaptive Gaussian parameter noise N(0, sigma^2 I) with sigma = lambda * S_t and lambda = (1/varepsilon) * sqrt(2 ln(1.25/delta)) into the averaged model.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Introduced by Nguyen et al. (USENIX Security 2022, pp. 1415-1432, arXiv:2101.02281). Distinct from fixed-two-cluster defenses such as Auror (Shen et al. 2016) and Krum, which fail when an attacker simultaneously injects multiple backdoors or honeypot outliers (pigeonhole principle), and from CRFL (AIMech:CrflCertifiablyRobustFederatedLearningViaParameterClippingAndSmoothing), which clips and perturbs the aggregated global model for randomized parameter smoothing rather than combining HDBSCAN pairwise cosine filtering with median-update-norm adaptive clipping before noise injection.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #c1-mechanism-hdbscan-pairwise-cosine-dynamic-clustering
FLAME computes the n x n pairwise cosine distance matrix c_ij = 1 - (W_i . W_j)/(||W_i|| ||W_j||) (Eq. 2) and runs HDBSCAN with min_cluster_size = floor(n/2) + 1 and min_samples = 1 to extract the single dense majority cluster (b_1, ..., b_L) while labeling all remaining models as individual outliers, preventing scale-boosted updates from altering angular distances and defeating simultaneous multi-backdoor or honeypot clustering attacks.
  • supports Source: FLAME: Taming Backdoors in Federated Learning · Locator: Section 3 Equation (2), Section 4.3.1, Algorithm 1 Lines 6-7, Figure 5, and Appendix E, pp. 4, 6, 19
Mechanism #c2-mechanism-median-euclidean-clipping-and-adaptive-dp-noise
FLAME computes Euclidean distances e_i = ||W_i - G_{t-1}^*||_2 across all n submitted models (including clustering-rejected models), sets the dynamic clipping bound S_t = MEDIAN(e_1, ..., e_n) so that S_t lies within the benign update norm range whenever k < n/2, scales each admitted model via W_bl^c = G_{t-1}^* + (W_bl - G_{t-1}^*) * min(1, S_t / e_bl), and adds Gaussian noise N(0, sigma_G^2 I) with sigma_G = (S_t / varepsilon) * sqrt(2 ln(1.25 / delta)) to the averaged model G_t = (1/L) sum_{l=1}^L W_bl^c.
  • supports Source: FLAME: Taming Backdoors in Federated Learning · Locator: Section 4.3.2, Algorithm 1 Lines 8-14, and Section 5.1 Theorem 1 & Equations (5)-(7), pp. 6-9
Application #c3-application-backdoor-mitigation-and-stpc-private-aggregation
FLAME eliminates constrain-and-scale, Distributed Backdoor Attack (DBA), Edge-Case, and PGD backdoors across Reddit LSTM word prediction, CIFAR-10/MNIST/Tiny-ImageNet image classification, and DIoT GRU network intrusion detection with less than 0.4% main-task accuracy drop, and supports Secure Two-Party Computation (STPC) over arithmetic shares between two non-colluding servers.
  • supports Source: FLAME: Taming Backdoors in Federated Learning · Locator: Section 7.1 Tables 3-4 and Section 8 Figure 10, pp. 10-14
Limitation #c4-limitation-honest-majority-assumption-and-stpc-overhead
FLAME requires a strict honest majority (Poisoned Model Rate PMR = k/n < 50%) so that the HDBSCAN minimum cluster size floor(n/2) + 1 and the median clipping bound S_t are dominated by benign updates, and its cryptographic STPC realization incurs up to three orders of magnitude server runtime overhead compared to plaintext FLAME.
  • supports Source: FLAME: Taming Backdoors in Federated Learning · Locator: Section 7.3 Figure 7 and Appendix G, pp. 12, 19-20

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Fails when the Poisoned Model Rate reaches or exceeds 50% (PMR >= 50%), because the majority cluster and median clipping norm can be controlled by malicious clients.
  • Realizing FLAME privately over Secure Two-Party Computation (STPC) increases server-side computation time by up to three orders of magnitude.
