Nearest-neighbor-chain clustering

Algorithms AIMech:NearestNeighborChainClustering DRAFT
Aliases: Nearest neighbor chain algorithm, NN-chain clustering
An agglomerative clustering algorithm that follows a chain of nearest clusters until its last pair are reciprocal nearest neighbors, merges that pair, and reuses the remaining chain; for suitable distance updates, sorting and relabeling the recorded merges produces a valid stepwise hierarchy.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #reciprocal-chain
Begin with a current cluster and repeatedly find a nearest distinct cluster. Prefer the preceding chain member when it is tied for nearest, so a reciprocal pair terminates the chain. Merge that pair, update distances and cluster sizes, and continue from the retained prefix or restart when it is exhausted. The chain avoids restarting a full global pair search after every merge.

Scope #update-law-conditions
The cited sufficient conditions require reducibility and invariance of the distance between two disjoint merged pairs to their merge order. Reducibility means d(A,B)≤min(d(A,C),d(B,C)) implies min(d(A,C),d(B,C))≤d(A∪B,C). Reducibility alone is insufficient for arbitrary history-dependent updates. The paper establishes the conditions for single, complete, average, weighted and Ward linkage; centroid and median updates are outside this guarantee.
  • supports Source: Modern hierarchical, agglomerative clustering algorithms · Locator: Section3.2, Equation1, Theorem 1, Proposition2 and footnote1, PDF pp.12–16
Mechanism #stepwise-output-and-ties
A reciprocal pair need not be the globally closest current pair, so discovery-order merge heights can be unsorted. To obtain the stated stepwise dendrogram, stably sort the recorded merges by height and relabel their cluster representatives while preserving the dependency order of tied merges. Under the stated conditions, this yields a valid greedy stepwise hierarchy; it need not reproduce one particular arbitrary global tie convention or a unique tree.

Scope #matrix-complexity
In the stored-dissimilarity setting with constant-time scalar distance updates, the chain algorithm takes quadratic time in the number of observations and linear auxiliary space beyond the writable quadratic-size distance matrix. This is not a linear-total-memory claim or a guarantee for arbitrary high-dimensional distance evaluation; vector implementations require a separate analysis.

Scope #procedure-versus-linkage
The chain specifies how merge candidates are found; the chosen linkage defines cluster dissimilarity. It can implement the Ward ESS criterion but is not identical to Ward clustering, a nearest-neighbor classifier, or the earlier procedure that repeatedly finds and merges batches of reciprocal pairs. The cited correctness result establishes valid stepwise output; it does not assert globally optimal fixed-count partitions.

Limitation #implementation-boundaries
Both Figure 4 of the 2011 paper and the later pinned fastcluster matrix implementation restart at a chain length or tip count of at most three. The pinned code retains premerge cluster sizes, uses explicit root handling and stable output sorting, and its Ward matrix wrapper squares inputs and square-roots output heights. These inspected details clarify the implemented conventions without certifying every printed listing or floating-point variant. The same repository uses a generic algorithm for vector Ward input and documents removal of its earlier vector chain implementation.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Scope is finite symmetric nonnegative dissimilarities and appropriate linkage updates. General dissimilarity matrices need not be metric, while Ward’s Euclidean ESS interpretation requires its geometry and distance-scale conventions. No automatic cluster-count selection or class-label prediction is implied.
  • Theorem 1 supplies sufficient update-law conditions; this record does not claim they are necessary or certify every proof. A five-point sum-of-three-distances update satisfies reducibility but fails the disjoint-merge condition and yields a different invalid stepwise result.
  • Figure 4 prints length(chain) ≤3, consistent with the pinned implementation; its three-entry restart does not access chain[-4]. An earlier curator reading incorrectly substituted <3 and is explicitly rejected in the discovery correction. Separate Figure 5 root/union notation and Figure 2 Ward/centroid/median scaling still require care. No claim is made that the printed pseudocode was executed unchanged.
  • The original French batch and chain sources have different output and tie conventions. De Rham’s displayed equality of nearest-distance minima is not itself a sufficient definition of reciprocity, and its all-pairs batch instruction needs tie handling. These are retained as source boundaries, not adopted as chain instructions.
  • All 29 pages of Müllner 2011 v1, 11 of Benzécri 1982, 8 of Juan 1982 and 11 of de Rham 1980 were visually read. Only named ranges of the pinned C++ files and the complete README, NEWS and license were statically inspected. No author program, package or historical benchmark was executed.
  • Finite curator calculations check 11,664 four-object matrix/start/linkage cases and 324 one-dimensional Ward/start cases, predecessor tie handling, discovery-order sorting, the published reducibility counterexample and the actual ≤3 restart guard and safe longer-chain indexing. They are not a universal proof, floating-point audit or benchmark replication.
