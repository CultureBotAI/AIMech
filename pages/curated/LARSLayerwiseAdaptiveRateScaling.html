LARS (Layer-wise Adaptive Rate Scaling)

Algorithms AIMech:LARSLayerwiseAdaptiveRateScaling DRAFT
Aliases: LARS Optimizer, Layer-wise Adaptive Learning Rate Scaling, SGD with LARS
A layerwise adaptive large-batch optimization algorithm (Algorithm 1) that assigns each network layer l (weights and biases separately) a local learning rate lambda^l = eta * ||w_t^l||_2 / (||g_t^l||_2 + beta ||w_t^l||_2) proportional to the ratio of the l2 norm of the layer's parameters ||w_t^l||_2 to the l2 norm of its stochastic gradient ||g_t^l||_2 plus weight-decay term beta ||w_t^l||_2, scaled by a layerwise trust coefficient eta < 1 and combined with global learning rate gamma_t and heavy-ball momentum v_{t+1}^l = m v_t^l + gamma_{t+1} lambda^l (g_t^l + beta w_t^l).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from coordinatewise adaptive optimizers such as Adam, RMSProp, and AdaGrad in that LARS computes a single scalar learning rate lambda^l per layer rather than per weight and scales the update magnitude proportionally to the layer's weight norm ||w_t^l||_2. Distinct from LAMB in that LARS normalizes raw or momentum gradients by their layerwise l2 norm without coordinatewise second-moment preconditioning 1 / sqrt(v_hat_t).

Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #c-r594-layerwise-weight-to-gradient-trust-scaling
Because the ratio ||w^l||_2 / ||nabla L(w^l)||_2 varies by orders of magnitude across layers and between weights and biases (e.g., 5.76 for conv1.w vs 1345 for fc6.w in AlexNet-BN at iteration 1, Table 2), LARS defines a local learning rate lambda^l = eta ||w_t^l||_2 / (||g_t^l||_2 + beta ||w_t^l||_2) (Eqs. 5-6) so that the layer update norm is proportional to ||w_t^l||_2 and independent of gradient magnitude, mitigating both vanishing and exploding layer gradients.
  • supports Source: Large Batch Training of Convolutional Networks · Locator: Section 4, Table 2, Equations (4)-(6), & Algorithm 1, p. 4
Application #c-r594-alexnet-and-resnet50-32k-batch-scaling
Coupled with Batch Normalization and polynomial decay, LARS scales ImageNet training of AlexNet-BN and ResNet-50 to mini-batch sizes up to B = 32K (matching the B = 512/256 baseline accuracies up to B = 8K/16K and within 0.7% on ResNet-50 at B = 32K), whereas standard linear scaling with warmup diverges on AlexNet above B = 2K.
  • supports Source: Large Batch Training of Convolutional Networks · Locator: Section 3, Table 1, p. 3; Section 5, Tables 3-4 & Figures 3-5, pp. 5-7
Limitation #c-r594-extreme-batch-step-count-and-attention-limitation
At B = 32K on AlexNet-BN, fixed-epoch training still loses 2.6% accuracy (57.8% vs 60.2% at 100 epochs) because very large mini-batches perform too few parameter updates when stochastic gradients are already close to true gradients, requiring 200 epochs to recover 59.9% accuracy (Table 5).
  • supports Source: Large Batch Training of Convolutional Networks · Locator: Section 5, Table 3(b), p. 5; Section 6, Table 5, p. 7

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • At batch sizes of 32K and above, fixed-epoch training suffers accuracy degradation due to too few parameter update steps unless the total number of training epochs is increased (Section 6, Table 5).
  • Lacks coordinatewise second-moment normalization, causing inferior scaling on attention-based models such as BERT where it diverges at batch size 32K (You et al., 2020, Table 2).
