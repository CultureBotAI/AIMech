Temporal-difference learning

Learning Paradigms AIMech:TemporalDifferenceLearning DRAFT
Aliases: TD learning
A family of prediction-learning methods that assign temporal credit using discrepancies between successive, recursively related predictions of future outcomes, with intervening costs or rewards included when the target is cumulative.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This family-level record covers temporal-difference prediction and the original TD(lambda) construction, including TD(0) and TD(1) endpoints. It is not a policy optimizer, the GAE advantage estimator, backpropagation as a way to compute derivatives, a Markov-chain model, or every sequential prediction problem. TD(lambda) is treated here as a specified family member rather than a synonym for the whole paradigm. Mechanism #method
For the original terminal-outcome problem, predictions P_t=P(x_t,w) estimate the same eventual scalar outcome z and P_(m+1)=z. With weights held fixed within a sequence, compute increments Delta w_t=alpha*(P_(t+1)−P_t)*e_t, where e_t=sum_(k=1..t) lambda^(t−k)*grad_w P_k and 0<=lambda<=1; apply their sum after the sequence. The trace can be maintained recursively as e_t=grad_w P_t+lambda*e_(t−1). TD(0) uses only the current prediction gradient, whereas TD(1) gives the same per-sequence changes as the corresponding supervised outcome-error update under the fixed-weight convention. In the cumulative-outcome extension, replace the difference with c_(t+1)+P_(t+1)−P_t and set the terminal remaining prediction to zero. This is the source’s undiscounted cumulative formulation, not an implicit insertion of a discount factor. Intra-sequence weight updates are a separate extension requiring consistent parameter timing; simple algebraic equivalence at fixed weights does not establish equality of online trajectories.

Scope #control-application
TD prediction can supply value estimates in a reinforcement-learning control procedure. The author exposition estimates the current behavior policy’s action values using TD updates while improving that policy toward greedy choices. Prediction alone does not specify the action-selection and policy-improvement components of a complete control algorithm.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The prediction targets must obey the declared temporal recursion. Consecutive forecasts of different future events cannot simply be subtracted; Section 5.3 embeds fixed-horizon forecasts in a larger set of related predictions. Terminal-outcome and cumulative-outcome formulations use different terminal conventions.
  • The original linear TD(0) theorem is convergence in expected value for an absorbing Markov setting, linearly independent state observation vectors, and sufficiently small constant step size, with weight changes after each sequence. It is not an almost-sure convergence theorem for arbitrary online nonlinear networks or a finite-data guarantee; the proof appendix was not independently audited.
  • TD(1)’s fixed-weight per-sequence equivalence does not make all TD methods identical to Monte Carlo or supervised learning. Updating weights within a sequence changes predictions and traces; Section 5.2 warns against mixing predictions computed with inconsistent parameter versions.
  • Temporal credit assignment and structural backpropagation are compatible but distinct. The method family alone supplies neither a control policy nor an exploration strategy. No claim of universal accuracy, lower computation, or benchmark superiority is curated.
