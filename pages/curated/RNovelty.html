R-Novelty SAT heuristic

Aliases: R-Novelty
A Novelty-derived SAT heuristic whose choice between the best and second-best flips depends on their objective-score gap when the best variable was most recently flipped; its original 1997 search strategy also includes a periodic random clause-variable move.

Mechanism #method
Use Novelty’s randomly selected unsatisfied clause, post-flip total-violation ranking and oldest-variable tie break. If the best candidate is not the most recently flipped variable in that clause, select it. Otherwise let d be the second-best post-flip violation count minus the best count; the 1997 rule assumes d>=1. If p<0.5 and d>1, choose the best. If p<0.5 and d=1, choose the second-best with probability 2p and otherwise the best. If p>=0.5 and d=1, choose the second-best. If p>=0.5 and d>1, choose the second-best with probability 2(p−0.5) and otherwise the best. Independently of this ordinary selection rule, the 1997 strategy specifies that every 100 flips it chooses a random variable from the selected clause instead. Update the chosen variable’s flip timestamp. At p=0.5 the recency-triggered rule picks second-best for gap one and best for a larger gap; that special case is not the full parameterized method.

Scope #incompleteness-and-version-boundary
Hoos1999 proves essential incompleteness of unrestarted R-Novelty even with scheduled random flips every 100 steps, then defines R-Novelty+ by replacing that periodic escape with a positive per-step random-walk branch. The 2004 competition report uses the plus name for a periodic configuration and claims cycling prevention. This record preserves the discrepancy and follows the explicit 1997 method and 1999 theorem, without endorsing the conflicting guarantee.

  • context Source: Walksat in the 2004 SAT Competition · Locator: PDF/printed2, Rnovelty p=0.5 and Rnovelty+ naming/cycling statement

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Hoos1999 Theorem 4 and its continuation show that the unrestarted R-Novelty strategy is essentially incomplete for arbitrary noise settings even with its every-100-step override. A periodic random move can occur at phases that never expose a needed variable; its mere presence does not establish asymptotic success.
  • The 1999 R-Novelty+ method removes the periodic override and adds a positive random-walk probability at each step. Its guarantee relies on possible arbitrarily long random-walk sequences, not a deterministic schedule. The 2004 source uses the plus name differently and its cycling-prevention statement is not adopted as a guarantee.
  • The d>=1 branch formulation uses age-based score tie breaking. Initial timestamp ties, unit clauses with no second candidate, empty clauses and exact counter/reset conventions need explicit implementation decisions; a d=0 probability rule is not invented here.
  • Parameters p and the periodic schedule have different roles. Score gaps are in unweighted clause counts; changing them to weighted or real-valued objectives requires a new specification. Finite failure is not an UNSAT proof, and no author solver or benchmark was executed.
