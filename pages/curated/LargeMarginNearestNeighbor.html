Large margin nearest neighbor

Algorithms AIMech:LargeMarginNearestNeighbor DRAFT
Aliases: LMNN, Large-margin nearest-neighbor classification, Large margin nearest neighbour
A supervised metric-learning method that fits a positive-semidefinite distance matrix by pulling fixed same-class target neighbors together while penalizing margin violations by differently labeled observations.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The core single-metric LMNN formulation fixes directed target-neighbor assignments before fitting and uses a convex objective in a positive-semidefinite matrix. It is not NCA’s stochastic classification objective, a generic triplet loss, or a deep embedding network. Iteratively reassigning targets, learning local metrics, kernelization and rank constraints are distinct extensions; their behavior is not inferred from the core convex program. The energy-based prediction rule in Section 3.5 is separate from ordinary k-nearest-neighbor voting. Mechanism #method
For labeled vectors choose k other same-class target neighbors T_i for each training point, usually using Euclidean distance before fitting; these directed sets remain fixed in the core method. Let d_M(i,j)=(x_i-x_j)^T M(x_i-x_j), with M positive semidefinite. Minimize (1-mu)sum_i sum_(j in T_i)d_M(i,j)+mu sum_i sum_(j in T_i)sum_(l:y_l!=y_i)max(0,1+d_M(i,j)-d_M(i,l)), with mu in [0,1]. The pull term concerns target neighbors, not all same-class pairs, and the hinge is not squared. Introducing nonnegative slack variables for the margin violations yields a semidefinite program. After learning, factor M=L^T L and use the learned distances for k-nearest-neighbor classification. The matrix-variable objective is convex for fixed target sets; optimizing directly in L is not generally convex.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Each point needs enough other same-class observations for its chosen target count. Target selection, labels, weighting, feature scale and the solver affect the learned representation; training margins do not certify held-out accuracy.
  • The source uses metric terminology for quadratic distances. The displayed d_M is a squared distance and does not itself generally satisfy the triangle inequality; its square root is a pseudometric when M is positive semidefinite and may collapse distinct points if M is rank deficient.
  • Convexity applies to the fixed-target optimization over M, not to arbitrary factorized, rank-constrained or target-reassignment extensions. Convexity also does not imply a unique optimum, perfect training separation or a certified result from every numerical solver.
  • The endpoints of the stated weight interval can remove one training term; mu=0 permits the collapsed M=0 solution. The paper’s unit margin is a scale convention. No benchmark, special-purpose solver implementation or extension was reproduced.
