Simultaneous normal-equation post-selection confidence regions in model-free linear regression

Mathematical Methods AIMech:SimultaneousNormalEquationPostSelectionConfidenceRegions DRAFT
Aliases: Model-Free Normal-Equation PoSI Confidence Regions, Assumption-Lean Simultaneous Normal-Equation Confidence Regions (R_hat_{n,M}, R_hat_{n,M}^dagger), Polynomial-Time Multiplier-Bootstrap Post-Selection Confidence Regions
Simultaneous normal-equation post-selection confidence regions in model-free linear regression are an assumption-lean statistical inference methodology introduced by Kuchibhotla, Brown, Buja, Cai, George, and Zhao (2020, The Annals of Statistics 48(5):2953-2981, arXiv:1806.04119v1, DOI:10.1214/19-AOS1917) for constructing universally valid post-selection confidence regions across arbitrary data-dependent submodel selectors M_hat in M_p subseteq M_p(p) := {M subseteq {1,...,p} : 1 <= |M| <= p} for independent (possibly non-identically distributed) or functionally dependent observations (X_i^T, Y_i)^T in R^p x R. For each submodel M, the submodel ordinary least squares estimator beta_hat_{n,M} and the model-free submodel moving target beta_{n,M} solve the submodel normal equations Sigma_hat_n(M) beta_hat_{n,M} = Gamma_hat_n(M) and Sigma_n(M) beta_{n,M} = Gamma_n(M), where Sigma_hat_n := 1/n sum_{i=1}^n X_i X_i^T, Gamma_hat_n := 1/n sum_{i=1}^n X_i Y_i, Sigma_n := E[Sigma_hat_n], and Gamma_n := E[Gamma_hat_n] (Section 2.2, Equations 4-6). After proving that universal post-selection validity P(beta_{n,M_hat} in R_hat_{n,M_hat}) >= 1 - alpha over all selectors M_hat with P(M_hat in M_p) = 1 is strictly equivalent to simultaneous family-wise validity P(bigcap_{M in M_p} {beta_{n,M} in R_hat_{n,M}}) >= 1 - alpha (Section 3, Theorem 3.1, Equation 9), the method exploits the assumption-free deterministic normal-equation inequality ||Sigma_hat_n(M) {beta_{n,M} - beta_hat_{n,M}}||_infty <= D_n^Gamma + D_n^Sigma ||beta_{n,M}||_1 for all M in M_p(p) (Equations 17-18), where D_n^Sigma := |||Sigma_hat_n - Sigma_n|||_infty = max_{M in M_p(2)} |||Sigma_hat_n(M) - Sigma_n(M)|||_infty and D_n^Gamma := ||Gamma_hat_n - Gamma_n||_infty = max_{M in M_p(1)} ||Gamma_hat_n(M) - Gamma_n(M)||_infty depend only on marginal and bivariate moments (Equation 12). Using bivariate joint (1-alpha)-quantiles (C_n^Gamma(alpha), C_n^Sigma(alpha)) of (D_n^Gamma, D_n^Sigma) estimated in polynomial O(n p^2) time via a high-dimensional Gaussian multiplier bootstrap on the q = p + p(p+1)/2 unique cross-product and Gram coordinates W_i = ({X_i(j) Y_i}, {X_i(l) X_i(m)}_{l <= m}) (Section 5, Theorem 5.1), the method defines the exact finite-sample simultaneous confidence region R_hat_{n,M} := {theta in R^{|M|} : ||Sigma_hat_n(M)(beta_hat_{n,M} - theta)||_infty <= C_n^Gamma(alpha) + C_n^Sigma(alpha) ||theta||_1} (Equation 13, Theorem 4.1) and its asymptotically valid 2|M|-constraint parallelepiped approximation R_hat_{n,M}^dagger := {theta in R^{|M|} : ||Sigma_hat_n(M)(beta_hat_{n,M} - theta)||_infty <= C_n^Gamma(alpha) + C_n^Sigma(alpha) ||beta_hat_{n,M}||_1} (Equation 14, Theorem 4.2), whose Lebesgue volume O_p(sqrt(|M| log(p)/n))^{|M|} adapts to the selected model cardinality |M| rather than the maximum search size k (Proposition 4.1, Equations 21 and 28).

Distinct from AIMech:UniformlyValidPostModelSelectionConfidenceIntervals (Berk, Brown, Buja, Zhang, & Zhao 2013, PoSI), which constructs coordinatewise max-|t| Scheffe-type confidence intervals under fixed covariates X and homoscedastic Gaussian errors Y = mu + epsilon by computing a PoSI constant K(X, M_p, alpha) over the selection collection M_p in exponential O(sum_{M in M_p} |M|^2) time with volume O_p(sqrt(k log(p)/n))^{|M|} scaling with the maximum search size k rather than the selected model size |M|. Distinct from AIMech:UnifiedAssumptionLeanMovingTargetLinearRegressionAndScoreBootstrap (Kuchibhotla, Brown, & Buja 2018, arXiv:1809.10538v1), which analyzes a single fixed model (p = o(n^{1/5})) via multidimensional Berry-Esseen bounds and score bootstraps without post-selection simultaneous inference over 2^p submodels. Distinct from AIMech:ExactPolyhedralPostSelectionInference (Lee, Sun, Sun, & Taylor 2016), which conditions on a specific polyhedral selection event {A y <= b} (such as Lasso at a fixed lambda) under exact Gaussianity rather than providing universal selection-agnostic simultaneous validity over all M_hat in M_p via the normal equations.

Mechanism #equivalence-of-universal-post-selection-and-simultaneous-inference
Section 3 (pp. 10-13, Theorem 3.1, Equations 7-10) proves purely set-theoretically without any distributional, independence, or dimensionality assumptions that for any family of confidence regions {R_hat_{n,M} : M in M_p} and any level alpha in [0, 1], universal post-selection coverage P(beta_{n,M_hat} in R_hat_{n,M_hat}) >= 1 - alpha for all data-dependent model selection procedures M_hat satisfying P(M_hat in M_p) = 1 is strictly equivalent to simultaneous family-wise coverage P(bigcap_{M in M_p} {beta_{n,M} in R_hat_{n,M}}) >= 1 - alpha, because any selector M_hat satisfies {beta_{n,M_hat} in R_hat_{n,M_hat}} supseteq bigcap_{M in M_p} {beta_{n,M} in R_hat_{n,M}} while the adversarial selector M_hat in argmin_{M in M_p} 1{beta_{n,M} in R_hat_{n,M}} achieves exact equality.

Mechanism #deterministic-normal-equation-inequality-and-simultaneous-regions
Sections 4.1-4.4 (pp. 14-21, Equations 11-19, Theorems 4.1-4.2, Lemma 4.1) define the bivariate max-norm estimation errors D_n^Sigma := |||Sigma_hat_n - Sigma_n|||_infty = max_{M in M_p(2)} |||Sigma_hat_n(M) - Sigma_n(M)|||_infty and D_n^Gamma := ||Gamma_hat_n - Gamma_n||_infty = max_{M in M_p(1)} ||Gamma_hat_n(M) - Gamma_n(M)||_infty (Equation 12) and prove via the submodel normal equations that ||Sigma_hat_n(M){beta_{n,M} - beta_hat_{n,M}}||_infty <= D_n^Gamma + D_n^Sigma ||beta_{n,M}||_1 holds deterministically for all M in M_p(p) with probability 1 (Equations 17-18). Given bivariate (1-alpha)-quantiles (C_n^Gamma(alpha), C_n^Sigma(alpha)) satisfying P(D_n^Gamma <= C_n^Gamma(alpha), D_n^Sigma <= C_n^Sigma(alpha)) >= 1 - alpha (Equation 15), Theorem 4.1 proves exact finite-sample simultaneous coverage P(bigcap_{M in M_p(p)} {beta_{n,M} in R_hat_{n,M}}) >= 1 - alpha for all n, p >= 1 (including p >> n and singular Sigma_hat_n(M)), and Lemma 4.1 and Theorem 4.2 prove uniform-in-model L1 consistency max_{M in M_p(k)} ||beta_hat_{n,M} - beta_{n,M}||_1 <= k (D_n^Gamma + D_n^Sigma max_{M in M_p(k)} ||beta_{n,M}||_1) / (Lambda_n(k) - k D_n^Sigma)_+ and asymptotic simultaneous validity of the 2|M|-constraint parallelepiped R_hat_{n,M}^dagger over M_p(k) under Condition (A1)(k): k D_n^Sigma = o_p(Lambda_n(k)).

Mechanism #model-cardinality-adaptive-volume-and-fixed-design-tightness
Sections 4.5-4.6 and Appendix B (pp. 21-25 and 38-39, Lemma 4.2, Propositions 4.1-4.2, Equations 20-31 and 40) prove that under marginal sub-Weibull(gamma) tail bounds max_{i,j} ||Z_i(j)||_{psi_gamma} <= K_{n,p}, max{C_n^Gamma(alpha), C_n^Sigma(alpha)} = O(sqrt(log(p)/n)) and the Lebesgue volume of R_hat_{n,M}^dagger satisfies Leb(R_hat_{n,M}^dagger) = |Sigma_hat_n(M)|^{-1} {2 C_n^Gamma(alpha) + 2 C_n^Sigma(alpha) ||beta_hat_{n,M}||_1}^{|M|} = O_p(sqrt(|M| log(p)/n))^{|M|} uniformly over M in M_p(k) (Proposition 4.1, Equation 21), adapting to the selected model cardinality |M| rather than the maximum search size k in contrast to the Berk et al. (2013) max-|t| confidence region volume O_p(sqrt(k log(p)/n))^{|M|} (Equation 28). Under fixed covariates (Sigma_hat_n = Sigma_n, D_n^Sigma = 0, C_n^Sigma(alpha) = 0), R_hat_{n,M} = R_hat_{n,M}^dagger has volume O_p(|Sigma_n(M)|^{-1} (sqrt(log(p)/n))^{|M|}) (Equation 25) and is tight: sup_{M in M_p(p)} ||Sigma_n(M)(beta_hat_{n,M} - beta_{n,M})||_infty = D_n^Gamma, achieved by the greedy marginal screening selector M_hat = {j_hat} with j_hat in argmax_{1 <= j <= p} |1/n sum_{i=1}^n X_i(j)(Y_i - E[Y_i])|.

Mechanism #polynomial-time-multiplier-bootstrap-surrogate-and-ellipsoidal-extensions
Sections 5-7 and Appendices D-F (pp. 27-33 and 41-49, Theorems 5.1, 6.1, 7.1, E.1, E.2, F.1, Equations 32-39 and 41-46) construct a polynomial-time O(n p^2) Gaussian multiplier bootstrap S_{n,j}^star = 1/sqrt(n) sum_{i=1}^n e_{i,j} (W_i - W_bar_n) on the q = 2p + p(p-1)/2 = p + p(p+1)/2 unique coordinates of W_i = ({X_i(j) Y_i}, {X_i(l) X_i(m)}_{l <= m}), proving via high-dimensional central limit theorems (Chernozhukov et al. 2017) and Anderson's Lemma that the bootstrap quantiles are asymptotically exact under i.i.d. observations and asymptotically conservative under non-identically distributed observations (Theorem 5.1, Appendix E). Section 6 generalizes the confidence regions to arbitrary surrogate matrices and vectors (Sigma_hat_n^star, Gamma_hat_n^star) for errors-in-variables, missing data, or outlier-robust estimation (Theorem 6.1); Section 7 derives valid Lasso-type (R_ring_{n,M}, R_ring_{n,M}^dagger) and sqrt-Lasso-type (R_breve_{n,M}, R_breve_{n,M}^dagger) ellipsoidal confidence regions whose intersection R_hat_{n,M} cap R_hat_{n,M}^dagger cap R_ring_{n,M} remains simultaneously valid with smaller volume (Theorem 7.1, Remark 7.1, Equation 43); and Appendix F proves concentration under Wu's (2005) functional dependence measure (Theorem F.1).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Coordinatewise Projections Versus Joint Parallelepiped Shape: Remark 4.6 (p. 20) and Section 4.5 (pp. 22-23, Equation 29) show that while R_hat_{n,M}^dagger is a 2|M|-constraint parallelepiped whose joint Lebesgue volume scales as O_p(sqrt(|M| log(p)/n))^{|M|}, projecting R_hat_{n,M}^dagger onto individual coordinates yields coordinatewise confidence intervals of width 2(C_n^Gamma(alpha) + C_n^Sigma(alpha) ||beta_hat_{n,M}||_1) ||Sigma_hat_n^{-1}(M) e_j||_1 = O_p(sqrt(|M| log(p)/n) ||beta_hat_{n,M}||_1), which can be wider than Berk et al. (2013) coordinatewise intervals when |M| is close to k and ||beta_hat_{n,M}||_1 grows with sqrt(|M|).
  • Sparse-Eigenvalue Condition (A1)(k) for R_hat_{n,M}^dagger and Null-Testing Power Loss: While R_hat_{n,M} (Equation 13) is finite-sample valid for any n, p >= 1 without eigenvalue conditions, the plug-in parallelepiped R_hat_{n,M}^dagger (Equation 14) requires Condition (A1)(k): k D_n^Sigma = o_p(Lambda_n(k)) with Lambda_n(k) = min_{M in M_p(k)} lambda_min(Sigma_n(M)) > 0 (Theorem 4.2), and Remark 4.7 (p. 20) proves that 0 in R_hat_{n,M} implies 0 in R_hat_{n,M}^dagger, so R_hat_{n,M}^dagger has strictly lower power than R_hat_{n,M} for testing H_{0,M}: beta_{n,M} = 0.
  • Primary-Source Typographical Notes in arXiv:1806.04119v1: Verified five typographical slips in the primary manuscript: (1) Section 2.2 (p. 7, last line) prints 'beta_{n,M'}(1) != beta_{n,M'}(1)' where the second term should be 'beta_{n,M}(1)'; (2) the proofs of Theorem 4.1 (p. 17, Equation 18), Theorem 4.2 (p. 19, penultimate display), and Proposition 4.1 (Appendix B, p. 39, Equation 40) print population 'Sigma_n(M)' in place of empirical 'Sigma_hat_n(M)'; (3) Section 4.5 (p. 22, Equation 22) prints '(log p)^{2/alpha} (log n)^{2/alpha - 1/2} = o(n^{1/2})' with 'alpha' in the exponent instead of the sub-Weibull tail parameter 'gamma'; (4) Section 5 (p. 29, Step 2 of the multiplier bootstrap algorithm) prints '1/n sum_{i=1}^n e_{i,j}(W_i - W_bar_n)' instead of '1/sqrt(n) sum_{i=1}^n e_{i,j}(W_i - W_bar_n)' (matching S_n^{eW} on p. 28) and Step 3 sums 'i=1' to B_n while indexing 'j'; and (5) Section 6 (p. 30, line 13) prints 'Gamma_hat_n^star, Gamma_hat^star' for 'Gamma_hat_n^star, Gamma_n^star' and Theorem 6.1 (p. 31) prints 'k D_{2n}^star' for 'k D_n^{Sigma star}'.
