Beam decoding with a monotone-score stopping certificate

Algorithms AIMech:OptimalStoppingBeamDecoding DRAFT
Aliases: Optimal-ending beam search
A layer-based sequence beam decoder that retains the best completed hypothesis seen so far and stops when the highest current prefix score cannot exceed that incumbent under a score that never increases with extension.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is Huang, Zhao and Ma’s 2017 optimal-ending procedure, with optimality explicitly limited by the retained beam. It differs from stopping on a completed top-ranked item without remembering earlier completions, from counting completed outputs, and from score-prioritized best-first expansion with per-length budgets. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method
Expand the current beam by one token and retain the highest-scoring candidates under a fixed beam policy. Do not extend EOS-complete hypotheses. Maintain the best completed hypothesis encountered in retained beams. Once that incumbent exists, stop when the maximum current prefix score is at most its score. Products of conditional probabilities in [0,1], or their cumulative log probabilities, cannot increase with extension, so descendants of retained active prefixes cannot improve the incumbent when the inequality holds.
  • supports Source: When to Finish? Optimal Beam Search for Neural Text Generation (modulo beam size) · Locator: Sections 2–3, Equations (1)–(2), Theorems 1–2, printed p. 2135 / PDF p. 2
Limitation #score-versus-quality
The unmodified probability objective can favor short translations; the source therefore introduces bounded length rewards. The stopping certificate is a search-score statement, not a quality guarantee.
  • supports Source: When to Finish? Optimal Beam Search for Neural Text Generation (modulo beam size) · Locator: Section 4 and Section 5.2 discussion, printed pp. 2136 and 2138 / PDF pp. 3 and 5

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The certificate concerns descendants of retained beam prefixes. It does not recover prefixes pruned by finite width or guarantee global MAP decoding.
  • A non-increasing extension score and a stored completed incumbent are required. Length normalization or positive rewards can invalidate the raw-prefix bound and need a different certificate.
  • The paper compares stopping times under the same beam evolution and a specific historical top-completed baseline. It does not establish universally fastest decoding or current software defaults.
  • Better model score does not imply better translation quality; the source reports short-output problems under pure probability scoring.
  • Finite resource limits, absent EOS completions, empty beams and tie conventions need explicit implementation policies. No unconditional finite-time termination claim is made.
