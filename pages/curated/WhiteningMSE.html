Aliases: W-MSE, Whitening MSE
A self-supervised representation-learning procedure that differentiably whitens projected image-view embeddings and minimizes the average squared distance between normalized whitened embeddings of views from the same image.

Identity Notes & Concept Scope W-MSE refers to Ermolov et al.’s normalized whitening mean-squared-error method. It is not generic MSE, a standalone whitening transform, Barlow Twins cross-correlation matching or VICReg soft covariance regularization. The unnormalized Euclidean ablation is distinguished from the normalized default. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Generate d≥2 augmented views of each of N images and pass them through a shared encoder and projection head. For a whitening group of m projected vectors in R^p, center by the group mean and compute sample covariance Sigma=sum_i(v_i−mean)(v_i−mean)^T/(m−1). When Sigma is positive definite, factor Sigma=L L^T and transform each vector to z_i=L^−1(v_i−mean). For nonzero z normalize u_i=z_i/||z_i|| and minimize the average ||u_i−u_j||^2 over all N d(d−1)/2 unordered same-image view pairs. Backpropagate through both views and whitening, including its batch statistics. In batch slicing, place each image’s views in separate view partitions, apply the same random image-index permutation to every partition, split each into groups, and whiten each group separately; compare corresponding positive views afterward. Repeat slicing and average losses if configured. The procedure requires no negative-pair loss, prediction head, momentum teacher or stopped target.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Exact Cholesky whitening requires positive-definite covariance. A centered group has rank at most m−1, so m>p is necessary but not sufficient. The paper’s group-size heuristic is not a rank or numerical-stability guarantee.
  • The pinned code optionally replaces Sigma by (1−epsilon)Sigma+epsilon I. Whitening that regularized matrix does not generally give the original centered samples identity covariance. A collapsed group is outside the exact unregularized construction; numerical regularization does not prove every optimizer avoids collapse.
  • Identity covariance expresses second-order decorrelation and unit variances. It does not prove statistical independence, Gaussianity or a rotationally symmetric distribution. Subsequent L2 normalization generally changes covariance again.
  • Whitening is group-dependent: absence of explicit negatives does not mean independent per-example training. Group composition, common view permutations, embedding dimension and repeated slicing affect the computation. The pinned slicing implementation requires the image batch size to be divisible by its group size.
  • Zero-norm handling and covariance regularization matter. The main record states normalized squared Euclidean distance; the supplement’s unnormalized variant and any element-averaged MSE convention are not silently identified. No runtime, gradient implementation or benchmark was reproduced.
