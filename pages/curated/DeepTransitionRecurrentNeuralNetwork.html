Deep Transition Recurrent Neural Network

Architectures AIMech:DeepTransitionRecurrentNeuralNetwork DRAFT
Aliases: DT-RNN
A recurrent neural architecture whose state-transition function uses a multilayer perceptron with intermediate nonlinear feedforward layers to compute the current hidden state from the previous hidden state and current input.

DT-RNN is defined by an MLP hidden-state transition. DOT additionally constrains the output map, whereas DO constrains that output map without requiring deep transition. DT(S) is an optional shortcut variant, and stacking is compatible. The record denotes the broader transition-depth architecture criterion, not an exclusive DT-only benchmark configuration.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #within-step-transition
The transition first combines the prior hidden state and current input, then applies additional nonlinear feedforward transformations before producing the current state. The illustrated DT-RNN uses one intermediate layer. This depth is inside one time step and is distinct from the depth obtained by unfolding recurrence through time.
  • supports Source: How to Construct Deep Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Sections 2–3.3.1, PDF pp. 2–5, Equations 1 and 4, Figure 2(b) and unnumbered MLP formula
Scope #combinable-architecture-scope
Transition depth does not require a shallow output map or exclude recurrent stacking. The paper explicitly combines deep transition and deep output as DOT-RNN and describes stacking DT-RNNs as an orthogonal possibility that it does not investigate. Intermediate transition activations are not, by that fact alone, separately recurrent hidden states. DT-RNN is therefore broader than the combined DOT construction, and its shorthand is not an exact synonym for DOT.
  • supports Source: How to Construct Deep Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Sections 3.2.3–3.3.3, PDF pp. 4–6, Figure 2 and formal descriptions
Limitation #shortcut-and-gradient-limits
Additional nonlinear transition layers add operations to temporal backpropagation. The optional DT(S)-RNN variant inserts shortcuts within the transition to create shorter gradient paths. A shortcut is not required by the DT definition and does not guarantee a nonzero full derivative, stable optimization or indefinite memory. The illustrated scalar cancellation is a constructed boundary case, not an evaluation of the authors’ implementation.
  • supports Source: How to Construct Deep Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 3.2.3, PDF p. 4, Figure 2(b*); Discussion, p. 10; constructed transition and shortcut derivatives
Limitation #reported-shortcut-variant-comparisons
The experiments evaluate DT(S), including transition shortcuts, rather than isolating bare DT against the same architecture without shortcuts. Its reported music negative log-probabilities are lower than the selected shallow RNN on all three datasets, while stacked RNN is lower on MuseData. Its Penn Treebank word perplexity is 112.0 versus 117.7 for the shallow RNN and 107.5 for DOT(S). Model sizes, parameter counts and training procedures differ, including pretraining for DOT(S) and stacked models. These historical comparisons do not isolate transition depth or prove universal improvement.
  • supports Source: How to Construct Deep Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Sections 5.1–5.3, PDF pp. 7–9, Tables 1–3 and training details; constructed table comparisons

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The record denotes the transition-depth architecture criterion in the inspected primary paper, including compatible combinations; it is not an exclusive DT-only benchmark configuration.
  • Shortcuts are optional and experiments use the shortcut variant. Gating, separate recurrent stacking and output depth are different design choices.
  • One complete 13-page versioned primary paper was visually inspected and reused with its original download timestamp and hash. No author code, training or empirical replication; DRAFT remains.
