Nonlinear autoregressive model with exogenous inputs

Aliases: NARX model, Nonlinear autoregressive exogenous model
A discrete-time nonlinear input-output model family whose current output is a function of finite lagged outputs and exogenous inputs, plus an additive current error, without explicit dependence on lagged errors.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #finite-lag-predictor
In the strictly causal scalar formulation, y_t=f(y_{t−1},…,y_{t−ny},u_{t−1},…,u_{t−nu})+e_t. The lag orders and function parameterization are model choices. The 1989 OLS paper defines NARX by omitting lagged noise from its general NARMAX form; the current additive noise term remains. Setting that term to zero gives a deterministic recurrence. A neural network is not required by this definition.
  • supports Source: Orthogonal least squares methods and their application to non-linear system identification · Locator: Section 2, Equations 1–6, PDF p. 3 / printed p. 1874
Mechanism #parameter-estimation-boundary
For a fixed polynomial NARX structure, measured lagged input/output values form a fixed design matrix and coefficient fitting can be posed as linear least squares. Structure selection and coefficient estimation are distinct from the model family. Being linear in coefficients does not make the observed dynamics linear, and a least-squares fit alone establishes neither physical causation nor valid generalization.
  • supports Source: Orthogonal least squares methods and their application to non-linear system identification · Locator: Section 2, Equations 6–13 and structure-selection discussion, PDF pp. 3–5 / printed pp. 1874–1876; curator exact examples in research/discovery/narx-narmax-boundaries.py
Limitation #local-representation-boundary
A chosen output-history recurrence must be well-defined on its stated domain. In the source example x_next=x+u, y=x+x², solving for x gives two possible branches unless the state is restricted. States x=0 and x=−1 have the same current output 0; with the same input u=1/4 their next outputs are 5/16 and −3/16. Thus those one-step observed arguments alone do not define a global single-valued update across both branches. This example does not rule out other history lengths or restricted-domain representations.
  • supports Source: Representations of non-linear systems: the NARMAX model · Locator: Section 2.2, Example 1, PDF p. 3 / printed p. 1015; curator exact examples in research/discovery/narx-narmax-boundaries.py
Scope #polynomial-scope
A polynomial parameterization expands finite lag variables into monomials and is linear in the monomial coefficients, while it can be nonlinear in the input and output values. The continuous scalar recurrence function on a specified compact finite-dimensional lag domain can be uniformly approximated by some polynomial; this does not prescribe a fixed degree, identify coefficients from finite observations, justify extrapolation, or guarantee stable recursive trajectories. The general model family is not restricted to polynomial parameterizations.
  • supports Source: Orthogonal least squares methods and their application to non-linear system identification · Locator: Section 2, Equations 6–13, PDF pp. 3–5 / printed pp. 1874–1876
  • supports Source: Representations of non-linear systems: the NARMAX model · Locator: Section 4, Equations 34–43, PDF pp. 9–11 / printed pp. 1021–1023; curator exact examples in research/discovery/narx-narmax-boundaries.py
Limitation #published-approximation-bound
The source supplies the displayed formulas and stated error bound below; the refutation and trajectory calculation are curator derivations. The representation paper’s particular degree-four approximation example claims error below 0.04 on |y|<=1/4, |u|<=1. Exact substitution in its displayed formulas gives error 5/16 at y=−1/4,u=1, and 2336/15625 at y=−6/25,u=1 (the y coordinate is interior; u is on the boundary). These curator counterexamples reject that numerical bound, not the compact-domain polynomial approximation existence statement. A small one-step function error also need not remain small under repeated simulation: comparing f(y)=2y and g(y)=2y+1/100 from zero gives difference (2^t−1)/100 after t steps.
  • supports Source: Representations of non-linear systems: the NARMAX model · Locator: Section 4, displayed recurrence, polynomial and claimed error bound after Equation 43, PDF pp. 11–12 / printed pp. 1023–1024; these source inputs support the explicit curator derivations in research/discovery/narx-narmax-boundaries.py, not an author-reported correction
Application #identification-use
The OLS study uses polynomial NARX structure selection to model measured pilot liquid-level input-output data. This is a documented system-identification use, not evidence that all NARX fits recover a physical mechanism or outperform alternative models. The generic family also permits formulations other than the particular polynomial fit.
  • supports Source: Orthogonal least squares methods and their application to non-linear system identification · Locator: Section 6, Example 1, Figure 2 and Table 1, PDF p. 21 / printed p. 1892

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This record covers finite-lag, real-valued, discrete-time, strictly causal input-output formulations; current-input/feedthrough conventions and broader vector/noise assumptions need explicit specification. The family is not a trained model, dataset or unique estimator.
  • Local realization, uniqueness, stability, identifiability and predictive accuracy are separate questions. No claim is made that every nonlinear or stochastic system has an exact global representation of this form. Original 1984 reports and 1985 journal proofs are follow-up sources, not audited theorem evidence here.
  • The 1989 representation paper contains problematic unrestricted inverse formulas and a numerical approximation bound contradicted by exact substitution. Those statements are not adopted. Its explicit recurrence definitions and scoped polynomial construction are distinguishable from these source defects.
  • No model was trained and no empirical result was replicated. Exact finite curator examples check stated boundaries. The record remains DRAFT; structural validation and author self-review are not independent scientific approval.
