Overlapping hierarchical clustering (OHC)

Aliases: OHC clustering
A graph-based agglomerative clustering algorithm that expands clusters across increasing distance thresholds when the change in induced edge density is within a tolerance, retaining overlapping groups in a cluster-inclusion hierarchy.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #density-controlled-expansion
Increase pairwise-distance thresholds to grow a neighborhood graph. For each newly affected cluster, consider its most-linked neighboring points and accept expansion when the absolute change in induced graph-edge density is at most a supplied tolerance. Continue permitted growth, remove old clusters contained in the expanded ones and retain the new level. The density is 2E/[n(n−1)], with singleton density defined as one.
  • supports Source: Overlapping Hierarchical Clustering (OHC) · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.2, Definitions 1–2 and Algorithm 1, PDF pp. 3–5
Mechanism #overlapping-hierarchy
The intended output is a quasi-dendrogram: a directed acyclic cluster-inclusion structure with singleton leaves, a full-data root and potentially overlapping covers at intermediate levels. Hard membership in several clusters differs from a fuzzy membership probability. Graph-edge density is not the core-neighbor-count criterion of DBSCAN.
  • supports Source: Overlapping Hierarchical Clustering (OHC) · Locator: Section 2.2, Definition 3 and Algorithm 1, PDF pp. 3–5; related work, pp. 10–11
Scope #parameter-extremes
The paper relates zero density tolerance to clique-like clusters and tolerance one to single-linkage-like connected grouping. Its statements about exactly the cliques or connected subgraphs must not be read as enumerating every contained subgraph: Algorithm 1 removes contained clusters. Simultaneous tied-neighbor expansion can also have a different density from each separate expansion. No exhaustive-clique or order-invariance guarantee is asserted here.
  • supports Source: Overlapping Hierarchical Clustering (OHC) · Locator: Properties 2–3 and Algorithm 1, PDF pp. 4–5; Section 3.3, pp. 8–9
Limitation #printed-specification-boundaries
The printed algorithm leaves the initialization and reset of its new-cluster list implicit and does not fully settle tied-neighbor and equal-distance edge processing. The neighborhood definition uses a metric and a less-than-or-equal threshold, while experiments use cosine dissimilarity and the path wording of Definition 3 says strictly less than the threshold. These passages are not adopted as a complete executable or universally metric specification.
  • supports Source: Overlapping Hierarchical Clustering (OHC) · Locator: Definitions 1 and 3, Section 2.2 and Algorithm 1, PDF pp. 3–5
Scope #hierarchy-comparison
The proposed comparison averages best-match Jaccard similarities in both directions between cluster levels. Repeated levels with the same cluster count are paired by a greedy ordered correspondence before averaging across levels. This is a structural comparison of the produced hierarchies; the paper does not supply a proof that the greedy matching globally optimizes the stated correspondence objective.
  • supports Source: Overlapping Hierarchical Clustering (OHC) · Locator: Section 3.2, Equations 1–3 and Figure 2, PDF pp. 6–7
Scope #evaluation-and-runtime
Experiments compare SLINK, Ward and HDBSCAN* on 1000 random two-dimensional points and yearly sets of 1000 word vectors learned from Wiley abstracts. There is no known ground-truth hierarchy for the comparison. A fitted runtime trend of approximately n^2.45 at a particular density tolerance is empirical, not a worst-case complexity theorem or portable speed guarantee.
  • supports Source: Overlapping Hierarchical Clustering (OHC) · Locator: Sections 3.1–3.3 and Figures 3–5, PDF pp. 5–9

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This record denotes the Jeantet, Miklós and Gross-Amblard OHC algorithm, not all overlapping hierarchical clustering methods.
  • Exact graph density, tolerance, tie handling and edge ordering affect the construction. Properties about parameter extremes are retained as qualified source descriptions, not independently certified theorems.
  • Standalone calculations test a static tied expansion and subset removal. They do not establish that the tied state is reached by a complete OHC execution or measure empirical clustering quality.
  • The full 13-page primary chapter, all figures, equations, algorithm and references were read. Author code and alternative revisions were not inspected or executed; no benchmark replication or formal proof certification was performed. This record remains DRAFT.
