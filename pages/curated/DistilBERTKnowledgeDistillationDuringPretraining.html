DistilBERT: Knowledge Distillation During Pre-training

Learning Paradigms AIMech:DistilBERTKnowledgeDistillationDuringPretraining DRAFT
Aliases: DistilBERT, DistilBERT Triple-Loss Pre-training Distillation, Triple-Loss Pre-training Transformer Distillation
A task-agnostic Transformer knowledge distillation paradigm that compresses a pre-trained bidirectional encoder during the pre-training phase by initializing a student of identical hidden width and half the layer count from every other teacher layer, removing token-type embeddings and the output pooler, and optimizing a linear combination of three objectives over large-batch dynamic-masked pre-training corpora: temperature-scaled soft-target distillation cross-entropy L_ce = sum_i t_i * log(s_i) with p_i = exp(z_i/T)/sum_j exp(z_j/T), masked language modeling loss L_mlm, and hidden-state direction-alignment cosine embedding loss L_cos.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Introduced by Sanh, Debut, Chaumond, and Wolf (NeurIPS 2019 Workshop on Energy Efficient Machine Learning and Cognitive Computing, EMC^2, arXiv:1910.01108). Distinct from task-specific fine-tuning distillation methods such as BERT-PKD (Round 802, which distills normalized intermediate patience representations during downstream fine-tuning), from TinyBERT (Round 800, which enforces MSE on layer-wise attention matrices and projected hidden states across both pre-training and data-augmented fine-tuning), and from MiniLM (Round 801, which transfers only the last layer's query-key attention and value-relation distributions without intermediate layer initialization or cosine hidden-state loss). DistilBERT retains identical hidden dimension d between teacher and student so that every other teacher layer directly initializes a corresponding student layer without linear projection matrices.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #r803-claim-triple-loss-formulation
DistilBERT trains the student language model during pre-training using a triple loss that linearly combines soft-target distillation loss L_ce = sum_i t_i * log(s_i) over temperature-scaled vocabulary probabilities p_i = exp(z_i/T) / sum_j exp(z_j/T), the standard masked language modeling cross-entropy L_mlm, and a cosine embedding loss L_cos that aligns the directions of the student and teacher hidden state vectors.

  • supports Source: DistilBERT, a distilled version of BERT: smaller, faster, cheaper and lighter · Locator: distillation_ce and cosine_embedding_loss verification in main()
Mechanism #r803-claim-architecture-and-alternating-init
Because reducing the last dimension of Transformer tensors has a smaller impact on modern linear-algebra computation efficiency than reducing the number of layers, DistilBERT preserves the teacher's hidden dimension while halving the number of layers, removes token-type embeddings and the pooler, and initializes the 6-layer student by taking one layer out of every two from the 12-layer teacher.
  • supports Source: DistilBERT, a distilled version of BERT: smaller, faster, cheaper and lighter · Locator: Section 3 (DistilBERT: a distilled version of BERT), Page 2, and Table 4 ablation (-3.69 GLUE macro-score without teacher initialization), Page 4
  • supports Source: DistilBERT, a distilled version of BERT: smaller, faster, cheaper and lighter · Locator: Parameter accounting for 12-layer BERT-base (109.5M) vs 6-layer DistilBERT (66.4M, ~40% reduction) in main()
Application #r803-claim-glue-and-squad-efficiency
Pre-trained with dynamic masking and large batches (up to 4000 examples per batch) on English Wikipedia and Toronto BookCorpus, DistilBERT reduces BERT-base parameter count by 40% (66M vs. 110M) and speeds up CPU inference by 60% (and mobile on-device inference by 71%) while retaining 97% of BERT-base performance on the GLUE benchmark (77.0 vs. 79.5 macro-score) and achieving 85.8 F1 on SQuAD v1.1 (86.9 F1 when combined with task-specific fine-tuning distillation from a BERT-base teacher).
  • supports Source: DistilBERT, a distilled version of BERT: smaller, faster, cheaper and lighter · Locator: Section 4 (Experiments), Tables 1-3, Pages 2-4
Limitation #r803-claim-fixed-width-and-token-type-limitation
Because DistilBERT relies on direct layer-taking initialization from the teacher and removes token-type (segment) embeddings, the student must maintain the exact same hidden dimension as the teacher (preventing arbitrary hidden-width reduction without projection layers) and cannot use explicit segment-ID embeddings to distinguish sentence pairs.
  • supports Source: DistilBERT, a distilled version of BERT: smaller, faster, cheaper and lighter · Locator: Section 3 (Student architecture & initialization), Page 2

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Direct initialization from alternating teacher layers requires the student to keep the exact same hidden dimension and attention head geometry as the teacher.
  • Removing token-type (segment) embeddings eliminates explicit segment-ID conditioning for multi-segment inputs.
