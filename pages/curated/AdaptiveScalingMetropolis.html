Adaptive scaling Metropolis

Algorithms AIMech:AdaptiveScalingMetropolis DRAFT
Aliases: ASM
A random-walk Metropolis algorithm that adapts its proposal scale through stochastic approximation driven by the proposal acceptance probability relative to a chosen target rate.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The source’s ASM has fixed positive-definite shape and an adaptive scalar scale. This is distinct from covariance-shape adaptation and from RAM’s direction-dependent matrix update. The record uses the exponential-scale specialization of the source’s more general increasing scale map. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Fix shape Sigma positive definite and use a spherical proposal W. From retained x with pi(x)>0 and log scale S, propose Y=x+exp(S)Sigma^(1/2)W. Compute alpha=min(1,pi(Y)/pi(x)), accept Y with that probability or retain x, then update S_new=S+eta_n(alpha−alpha_star). Adapt after either outcome, using the acceptance probability rather than its Bernoulli realization. Choose alpha_star in (0,1) and positive step sizes with divergent sum and convergent sum of squares for the stated stochastic-approximation setting. The shape remains fixed; exp(S) multiplies standard deviation, so the covariance multiplier is exp(2S).
  • supports Source: On the stability and ergodicity of adaptive scaling Metropolis algorithms · Locator: Section 2, Assumptions 1–3 and Definition 4, PDF pp. 3–4; Theorems 7 and 9 and Remarks 11–13, pp. 5–7

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The stability and strong-law theorems in this version restrict alpha_star to (0,1/2), beyond the broader algorithm definition. Larger targets are discussed as expected extensions, not proved here.
  • Compact-support regularity or the stated unbounded-target tail/contour assumptions and admissible proposal conditions are needed for the corresponding results. Positive step sizes alone are insufficient.
  • The strong-law statements concern sample averages. The source explicitly distinguishes them from convergence in distribution of the adaptive sampled states.
  • A target rate such as 0.234 is not a universal optimum, evidence of convergence or guarantee of good multimodal exploration.
  • The inspected arXiv v3 submission date and later PDF footer differ; no later journal PDF inspection or numerical validation is claimed.
