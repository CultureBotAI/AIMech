SimCLR

Algorithms AIMech:SimCLR DRAFT
Aliases: Simple framework for contrastive learning of visual representations
A self-supervised representation-learning procedure that jointly trains an encoder and a nonlinear projection head by contrasting two augmented views of each image against other images’ views in the same minibatch.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the original 2020 SimCLR procedure, not a particular ResNet checkpoint, the general contrastive-learning category or SimCLR v2. It uses current-batch views and a shared gradient-trained encoder/projection head, rather than a queue of historical keys or a momentum target encoder. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a minibatch of N≥2 images, independently augment each image twice to obtain 2N views. Encode each with the same f and nonlinear projection head g, giving z_i=g(f(v_i)). With nonzero projected vectors and temperature tau>0, set s_ik=z_i^T z_k/(||z_i|| ||z_k||). For anchor i and its paired view j, use loss ell(i,j)=−log[exp(s_ij/tau)/sum_(k≠i) exp(s_ik/tau)]. The denominator contains the positive j and the 2N−2 other-image views, while excluding i itself. Average over all 2N directed positive pairs and update both f and g by gradients. After pretraining, discard g and use the representation before projection from f for downstream learning.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The source’s image realization composes crop/resize/flip, color distortion and Gaussian blur. These choices determine the prediction task; an augmentation policy may remove information needed downstream.
  • Views from other images serve as negatives by construction, not by verified semantic dissimilarity. This does not establish class labels or guarantee that semantically related images never oppose one another.
  • The source uses global batch normalization to mitigate same-device information leakage between positive views. Distributed normalization and optimization choices matter to reproducing its experiments.
  • Projection normalization requires meaningful nonzero vectors and numerically stable loss evaluation. Batch size, temperature, architecture and training schedule affect the procedure’s empirical behavior.
  • Reported ImageNet and transfer comparisons are context-specific. No universal performance advantage, mutual-information explanation, trained checkpoint or reproduced benchmark is asserted.
