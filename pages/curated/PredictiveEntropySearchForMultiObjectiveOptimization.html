Predictive entropy search for multi-objective Bayesian optimization

Algorithms AIMech:PredictiveEntropySearchForMultiObjectiveOptimization DRAFT
A Bayesian multi-objective optimization algorithm that selects evaluations using an approximate information gain about the Pareto set, computed from sampled finite Pareto-set approximations and objective-factorized Gaussian expectation-propagation approximations to conditional predictions.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the original PESMO procedure for information about a Pareto set under the stated independent objective models, finite sampled Pareto-set representation and EP approximations. Scalar PES targets one optimizer location; PESC adds feasibility to a scalar objective. Neither defines the multi-objective dominance factors or approximate objective-wise information allocation of PESMO. The proposed PESMOC, PPESMOC and PPES records preserve their different constrained or joint-batch targets.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #pareto-set-information-target
PESMO targets information about the Pareto set in input space for several objectives, using minimization conventions. A point dominates another when it is no worse in every objective and strictly better in at least one; the Pareto set contains the nondominated points. Assume a well-defined posterior over nonempty Pareto sets and finite mutual information. At fixed surrogate hyperparameters, the ideal acquisition is I(X_star; y(x) | D), equivalently the predictive entropy of the objective observations minus their expected conditional entropy given the Pareto set. This is a target for approximation; it is not information about only a scalar optimum value or a chosen scalarization.
  • supports Source: Predictive Entropy Search for Multi-objective Bayesian Optimization · Locator: Section1, PDFp.1 dominance definition; Section2, PDFpp.2–3, Eqs1–2. The later quantified characterization and Eq4 have the separate caveats retained below.
Mechanism #independent-objective-surrogates
The model assigns independent Gaussian-process priors to the objectives and independent zero-mean Gaussian observation noise. At fixed independent model hyperparameters, the ordinary predictive distribution at a candidate factorizes across objectives. The reported experiments average acquisition values over sampled surrogate hyperparameters; such conditional averaging does not by itself compute the entropy of a hyperparameter mixture. Conditioning on a shared Pareto set need not preserve objective independence.
  • supports Source: Predictive Entropy Search for Multi-objective Bayesian Optimization · Locator: Section2, PDFpp.2–3, assumptions before Eq1 and Eq3; Section4, PDFpp.5–6, acquisition averaging over hyperparameter samples.
  • supports Source: Supplementary Material for: Predictive Entropy Search for Multi-objective Bayesian Optimization · Locator: Section1, PDFp.1, stated independence approximation; Section1.3, PDFp.4, tilted distribution and objective-factorized Gaussian projection.
Mechanism #approximate-pareto-set-samples
To approximate the expectation over Pareto sets, sample finite random-feature linear approximations to each objective posterior, solve the resulting multi-objective problem approximately, and retain a finite representative set of nondominated locations. The paper describes a grid search and NSGA-II for higher-dimensional problems. Its choices of ten draws and fifty retained locations are reported numerical settings, not exact posterior sampling or fixed mathematical requirements of the algorithm. Finite search and set compression can omit parts of the true Pareto set.
  • supports Source: Predictive Entropy Search for Multi-objective Bayesian Optimization · Locator: Section2, PDFp.3, random kernel features, grid/NSGA-II search and representative subset; Section2.1, PDFp.4, Monte Carlo average.
Mechanism #finite-domain-ep-projection
For each retained Pareto-set sample, replace the continuous-domain conditioning calculation by a finite set containing previous evaluation locations, the sampled Pareto locations and the candidate. Approximate each non-Gaussian comparison factor by a product of objective-specific bivariate Gaussian factors, fitted through expectation propagation. Reuse the factors independent of the candidate, while the paper updates candidate-dependent factors once. The Gaussian projection matches the retained within-objective moments and discards cross-objective dependence; it does not reproduce all moments of the joint tilted distribution or certify exact whole-domain Pareto-set conditioning.
  • supports Source: Predictive Entropy Search for Multi-objective Bayesian Optimization · Locator: Section2.1, PDFpp.3–4, finite restriction, Eq6 Gaussian projection and candidate-factor reuse/update paragraph; printed comparison signs are qualified below.
  • supports Source: Supplementary Material for: Predictive Entropy Search for Multi-objective Bayesian Optimization · Locator: Sections1.1 and1.3–1.4, PDFpp.2 and4–5, self exclusion, cavity/tilted distributions, objective-factorized moment projection and damping.
Mechanism #noisy-gaussian-variance-acquisition
Within the factorized Gaussian approximation, the contribution of objective k is one half of the difference between log(v_k(x)+sigma_k_squared) and the sample average of log(v_k_conditional(x; X_star_sample)+sigma_k_squared). Here v denotes latent predictive variance and sigma_k_squared is the observation-noise variance; the entire log-variance difference is multiplied by one half. Summing these contributions yields the coupled acquisition. All logarithm arguments must be positive. This additive score follows the Gaussian factorization and finite sample approximation, and is not generally an exact additive decomposition of joint Pareto-set mutual information.
  • supports Source: Predictive Entropy Search for Multi-objective Bayesian Optimization · Locator: Section2, PDFp.3, Eq3; Section2.1, PDFp.4, Eqs7–8 and paragraph adding observation-noise variance.
  • supports Source: Supplementary Material for: Predictive Entropy Search for Multi-objective Bayesian Optimization · Locator: Section1.2, PDFp.3, final paragraph adding Gaussian observation-noise variance.
  • supports Source: PESM author implementation: predictive_entropy_search_multiobjective.py · Locator: Pinned author implementation at commit 9525e612b94a3b76d5510a816d94eb2801219782, evaluate_acquisition_function_given_EP_solution, lines2994–2995 and3014–3026: supports observation-noise addition and the pre-clipping half-log variance terms only. Clipping before averaging is a separate version-specific choice retained in limitations, not asserted equivalence to the paper acquisition.
Mechanism #coupled-and-decoupled-selection
In the coupled setting, maximize the sum of the objective contributions to choose a location for evaluating all objectives. If objectives can be evaluated separately, maximize each objective contribution over location and select the objective/location pair with the largest resulting score. The same sampled Pareto sets link the objective-specific approximations. The displayed decoupled rule compares information scores and does not include a cost divisor; unequal evaluation costs do not turn it into a guaranteed optimal scheduler. Coupled and decoupled use are modes of this record, not separate concept identities.
  • supports Source: Predictive Entropy Search for Multi-objective Bayesian Optimization · Locator: Section2.1, PDFp.4, Eqs7–8 and objective selection following Eq8; Section4.3, PDFp.8, separate evaluation counts and unequal training costs.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The ideal target assumes a well-defined joint posterior over objectives and Pareto sets. A bounded domain alone does not establish existence or regularity. Tied objective vectors, repeated input locations and degenerate covariance matrices require explicit handling; no general tie policy or empty-set fallback is invented here.
  • Main PDFp.1 gives a correct weak-plus-one-strict dominance definition, but its subsequent strict quantified characterization includes self comparisons. Main PDFp.3 Eq4 includes self pairs with Theta(0)=1 and reverses the minimization comparison direction; Eq6 on p.4 and supplement Eq8 on p.3 repeat that orientation. Supplement Eq3 on p.2 excludes self pairs. Even correctly oriented nondominated-membership factors do not require a supplied subset to equal the entire Pareto set. These printed products are not certified normalized densities or exact set-equality events.
  • The supplement has additional literal Gaussian-parameter inconsistencies: PDFp.1 Eq2 cross-term scaling differs from the symmetric precision entries in p.2 Eq4; Eq4 includes an M+M endpoint where the observed block has N+M variables; p.3 Eq7 mixes covariance and precision entries and does not in general construct the precision of the required marginal. The high-level Gaussian projection is curated without certifying these expressions as executable formulas.
  • Selective static inspection of the separately pinned July2016 author implementation supports minimization sign reversal, self-site removal and covariance block inversion. That version also rescales candidate covariances, uses a damped single candidate pass and clips each sampled contribution below zero before averaging. These are version-specific implementation choices, not an official erratum or proof of equivalence to the proceedings equations. Conditioning reduces entropy in expectation, so per-realization clipping is an additional approximation. No author code was executed.
  • Finite random features, approximate multi-objective optimization, finite Pareto representatives, EP factorization, limited candidate updates and hyperparameter averaging all introduce approximation. The original bounded checks in research/discovery/pesmo-boundaries.py illustrate selected source and information boundaries only; they do not validate full PESMO inference or its empirical accuracy.
  • The paper gives linear dependence on the number of objectives only for specified EP/variance linear-algebra operations with other sizes and iteration/sample budgets held fixed. Pareto-set size, input dimension, posterior sampling and acquisition maximization can dominate cost. No universal runtime improvement, convergence guarantee or benchmark reproduction is asserted.
