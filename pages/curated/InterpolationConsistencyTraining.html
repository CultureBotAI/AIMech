Interpolation consistency training

Algorithms AIMech:InterpolationConsistencyTraining DRAFT
Aliases: ICT
A semi-supervised training procedure that encourages a student’s prediction at an interpolated pair of unlabeled inputs to match the same interpolation of predictions from a moving-average teacher, while also fitting labeled examples.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The original IJCAI2019 procedure uses interpolated unlabeled inputs and interpolated EMA-teacher probabilities. Mean Teacher compares perturbed versions of one input; MixUp interpolates observed targets; MixMatch builds sharpened guesses and a combined mixing pool. These are related components and procedures, not equivalent names. This record uses the five-author conference version and does not import theory claims from the later expanded seven-author version. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For two sampled unlabeled minibatches, obtain fixed teacher predictions f_theta_prime(u_j) and f_theta_prime(u_k). Draw lambda from a distribution on [0,1], with Beta(a,a), a>0, used in the reported method. Form u_mix=lambda*u_j+(1−lambda)*u_k and q_mix=lambda*f_theta_prime(u_j)+(1−lambda)*f_theta_prime(u_k). Minimize labeled cross-entropy plus a ramped coefficient times a discrepancy between f_theta(u_mix) and q_mix; the experiments use mean squared error, while Equation1 allows a general discrepancy. Update the student from gradients with respect to theta, holding the teacher targets fixed. Algorithm1 explicitly computes these gradients, updates theta_prime←rho*theta_prime+(1−rho)*theta using the pre-step student, and then takes the student optimization step; this order differs from averaging the already-updated student. The displayed algorithm returns the student parameters. Although Algorithm1 and Figure2 display ordinary labeled cross-entropy, Section3.3 reports applying supervised MixUp to labeled examples in the experiments. This protocol choice is not silently substituted into the displayed core algorithm.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Low-density boundary motivation depends on cluster and interpolation assumptions and is not a universal guarantee that arbitrary input mixtures lie in low-density regions.
  • Incorrect teacher targets can be propagated by interpolation; the EMA and consistency ramp do not establish target correctness.
  • The published pre-step EMA order is recorded explicitly; initialization, normalization and alternative implementation scheduling require version-specific evidence.
  • The paper’s general discrepancy and mixing distribution permit variants, but the cited experimental choices are MSE and a symmetric Beta distribution. No benchmark or implementation reproduction is claimed.
