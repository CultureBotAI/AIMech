Minimin search with alpha pruning

A bounded single-agent lookahead planner that backs up minimum path-cost-plus-heuristic frontier estimates and prunes branches using an incumbent frontier value when the evaluation is nondecreasing along paths.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #bounded-minimum-backup
From the current planning root, simulate successors to a fixed number-of-moves horizon. Evaluate frontier nodes with f(n)=g(n)+h(n), where g is the path cost from that root, and back up minima because the single agent controls every move. This yields bounded-lookahead estimates and a direction toward a minimizing child rather than a minimax opponent response.
  • supports Source: Real-Time Heuristic Search · Locator: Section 2, physical PDF pp. 4–5 / printed 192–193; the clipped p. 4 scan is corroborated by the clean 1988 presentation.
  • supports Source: Real-Time Heuristic Search: New Results · Locator: Section 2, physical PDF p. 1 / printed 139: fixed-depth minimin lookahead and minimum backup.
Mechanism #terminal-evaluation
Terminate a simulated path early at a goal with heuristic value zero, preserving its accumulated path cost. A nongoal dead end before the horizon receives an infinite heuristic value. The intended branch selection avoids such dead ends when a finite alternative is available.
  • supports Source: Real-Time Heuristic Search · Locator: Section 2, physical PDF p. 5 / printed 193: early goal and dead-end evaluation.
Mechanism #monotonic-alpha-cutoff
Maintain alpha as the lowest evaluated frontier cost in the current search. Evaluate interior nodes as well. Under nondecreasing f along each generated path, stop expanding a branch whose interior f equals or exceeds alpha; when a frontier node has a smaller f, lower alpha. This preserves the bounded-tree minimum while potentially avoiding descendants that cannot improve it.
  • supports Source: Real-Time Heuristic Search · Locator: Section 3, physical PDF pp. 5–6 / printed 193–194: monotonicity condition, alpha incumbent and cutoff.
  • supports Source: Real-Time Heuristic Search: New Results · Locator: Section 2, physical PDF p. 1 / printed 139: earlier alpha-pruning presentation; the 1990 edge-consistency formulation defines the qualification.
Mechanism #complete-child-values
The 1990 presentation reinitializes alpha to infinity before searching each immediate child of the current state. This sacrifices some cross-child pruning to return an actual backed-up value for every child, as required by the separate RTA* controller rather than only identifying the overall best direction.
  • supports Source: Real-Time Heuristic Search · Locator: Section 3, physical PDF p. 6 / printed 194: reinitialize alpha to infinity for each child to obtain every child value.
Scope #planning-component-boundary
The source treats minimin lookahead with alpha pruning as a planning procedure that can be encapsulated as a more expensive heuristic evaluator. Executing one recommended move and planning again is distinct from RTA* second-best memory updates, LRTA* repeated-trial learning and the separate 1988 graph-correction sketch.
  • supports Source: Real-Time Heuristic Search · Locator: Sections 4–5 and 7, physical PDF pp. 9–12 and 18–19: planning/execution distinction, RTA* memory and LRTA* learning.
  • supports Source: Real-Time Heuristic Search: New Results · Locator: Sections 3–4, physical PDF pp. 3–5: RTA*, separate graph-correction sketch and learning variants.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The literal alpha cutoff requires nondecreasing f on the generated paths. Admissible but inconsistent h can hide a lower-valued horizon descendant behind a pruned interior node. A learned or altered heuristic must satisfy the same condition; no consistency repair is silently inserted.
  • Equality cutoffs retain a minimum value, not every tied leaf or a prescribed tie distribution. Exact inequality comparisons are the mathematical rule; numerical tolerances and all-infinite/empty alternatives need explicit implementation conventions. Infinite dead-end scores do not certify a solution when no finite alternative exists.
  • Finite-depth heuristic estimates do not certify a globally shortest solution. With nonuniform costs, merely encountering a goal within the horizon does not force the minimum frontier to be a goal; the source’s broad p. 10 statement must be qualified rather than promoted to an unrestricted theorem.
  • Repeated memoryless use can cycle even on a finite positive-cost graph with a reachable goal. The RTA* execution table, LRTA* learning updates and their assumptions are separate mechanisms; minimin pruning alone supplies none of their global guarantees.
  • Fixed depth is a real-time policy only relative to bounded successor generation and evaluation costs. Work may remain exponential in depth, and exploration order changes pruning work even when the final minimum value is unchanged. The paper’s immediate-f ordering experiment reports overhead rather than a universal speedup.
  • The finite sliding-tile observations and independent zero/one-increment analytic model do not prove that larger branching factors always permit deeper search, or that deeper lookahead always improves a particular executed solution. The 1990 article explicitly reports model mismatch and nonmonotone empirical behavior. No benchmark, figure-value reproduction or author-code execution was performed here.
  • The 1990 consistency formulation is the defining operational account; the 1988 metric shorthand and more optimistic model discussion are not interchangeable guarantees. Graph-corrected RTA* remains a separate source-version lead: its 1988 proof is omitted, and the 1990 article states that graph local optimality requires significant modifications. No complete graph-correction implementation, formal proof or family exhaustion is claimed.
