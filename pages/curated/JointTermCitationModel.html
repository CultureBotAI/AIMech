Joint term-citation model

Model Families AIMech:JointTermCitationModel DRAFT
A latent-factor document model that shares each document's fixed topic proportions between categorical term and citation-incidence channels, with topic-specific distributions for each channel.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Scoped to Cohn and Hofmann's joint content/connectivity model in The Missing Link. The maintained name describes its term/citation construction; PHITS names a link-only predecessor and is not used as an alias. Fixed document memberships distinguish this PLSA-style model from the integrated Dirichlet word/reference model of Erosheva and colleagues. Link orientation must be specified from the supplied incidence matrix rather than silently treating cited targets and citing sources as interchangeable. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #shared-fixed-memberships
Each document d has one fixed topic probability vector theta_d. Topic z has a categorical term distribution phi_z and a separate categorical citation-incidence distribution psi_z. The channel mixtures are P(t|d)=sum_z theta_dz*phi_zt and P(c|d)=sum_z theta_dz*psi_zc. Topic choices are made per observation and share the document weights; the construction is not one latent topic for a whole document and does not integrate a Dirichlet membership prior. The source optimizes alpha times the term log score plus 1-alpha times the link log score, separately normalizing each document's counts in each channel. This weighted criterion gives equal document mass per nonempty channel and differs from the raw-count corpus likelihood.
  • supports Source: The Missing Link - A Probabilistic Model of Document Content and Hypertext Connectivity · Locator: Section 3, PDF pp.3-4, channel mixtures and Equation (2)
Mechanism #weighted-responsibility-fitting
An EM-style fit computes topic responsibilities separately for observed terms and citation incidences, then pools their document-normalized expected counts with channel weights alpha and 1-alpha. Document mixtures combine the two channel responsibility averages, and each topic's emission counts must be normalized over its own channel categories. Equation (3) has an inconsistent document subscript in its first numerator, and the displayed emission updates in Equation (4) omit the final categorical normalization; use the conditioning document and normalized vectors for a coherent probability model. Experiments use tempered responsibilities, so ordinary beta=1 EM monotonicity does not certify the whole temperature schedule.
  • supports Source: The Missing Link - A Probabilistic Model of Document Content and Hypertext Connectivity · Locator: Section 3, PDF p.4, Equations (3)-(5); Section 4.1, PDF p.5, tempering settings

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The exposition alternates between citing-source and cited-target language: Section 2 defines an incidence matrix as document i cited by j, while PHITS and Section 3 use differing verbal descriptions. The factor equations apply to the chosen orientation, but transposing the matrix changes the modeling task. No unique historical implementation orientation is inferred.
  • The displayed channel normalization is undefined for a document with an empty channel; a complete implementation needs an explicit missing-channel policy. Alpha endpoints discard one channel. Fixed topic count, categorical incidence modeling and fixed document weights constrain generalization; unseen documents require inference of new mixtures.
  • The reported factor-space nearest-neighbor classification, topic counts tied to known category counts, and source-page ranking among random distractors are specific evaluation setups. They do not establish recovery of true classes, universal advantage, or a demonstrated end-to-end web crawler.
  • Reference-flow scores sum over observed link support and use reverse document-given-topic probabilities requiring prior and normalization choices. They are not automatically a calibrated Markov transition or causal information flow. The proposed continuous-state MDP extension is prospective. No author code or benchmark was executed.
