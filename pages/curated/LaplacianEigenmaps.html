Laplacian Eigenmaps

Algorithms AIMech:LaplacianEigenmaps DRAFT
Aliases: Laplacian Eigenmap
A spectral embedding algorithm that builds a weighted neighborhood graph and uses nonconstant generalized eigenvectors of its graph Laplacian to place strongly connected points close together.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The record denotes the neighborhood-graph Laplacian embedding algorithm, not the Laplace–Beltrami operator itself or every spectral-clustering method. LLE instead uses reconstruction weights; diffusion maps explicitly scale Markov eigenvectors with diffusion time. Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #method-scope
Connect nearby observations by an epsilon or symmetrized nearest-neighbor rule, assign Gaussian heat-kernel or unit edge weights, form D from weighted degrees and L = D − W, and solve Ly = λDy. On a connected component, discard the constant zero mode and use the smallest nonzero modes as coordinates. This minimizes weighted squared separations along graph edges subject to degree-weighted normalization and exclusion of the constant mode.
  • supports Source: Laplacian Eigenmaps and Spectral Techniques for Embedding and Clustering · Locator: Section 1, steps 1–3, and Section 2, equations (1)–(2), PDF pp. 2–4
Limitation #convergence-assumptions
The convergence study assumes i.i.d. uniform sampling on a compact infinitely differentiable embedded manifold without boundary and establishes the existence of a bandwidth sequence tending to zero for spectral convergence in probability. Its analyzed heat-kernel operator does not certify arbitrary finite graphs or noisy data.
  • supports Source: Convergence of Laplacian Eigenmaps · Locator: Sections 1.1–2 and Theorem 2.1, PDF pp. 2–3
Limitation #sampling-density-limit
Coifman and Lafon distinguish ordinary isotropic graph normalization, whose continuum behavior depends on nonuniform sampling density, from alpha-normalized constructions. Their alpha = 1 construction removes density dependence in the stated continuum limit; this is not an unconditional property of every Laplacian embedding.
  • supports Source: Diffusion maps · Locator: Sections 3.1–3.4, printed pp. 14–16 / PDF pp. 10–12

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Graph connectivity, neighborhood size and edge weighting are consequential; the original construction treats disconnected components separately.
  • The local graph objective does not guarantee preservation of all pairwise distances, sampling density or topology.
  • The inspected 2006 convergence result uses a particular heat-kernel point-cloud operator, i.i.d. uniform sampling on a compact smooth boundaryless manifold and a suitable shrinking bandwidth sequence; it is not a finite-sample guarantee for every neighborhood graph.
  • Nonuniform sampling can change the limiting operator under ordinary graph normalization; interpreting an embedding as pure manifold geometry requires additional assumptions or normalization.
