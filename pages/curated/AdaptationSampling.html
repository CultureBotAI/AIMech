Adaptation sampling

Algorithms AIMech:AdaptationSampling DRAFT

An online optimizer-hyperparameter controller that compares sample quality under a current and a hypothetical setting, using either fresh evaluations or importance-weighted reuse of existing observations, to decide how to adapt the setting.
Definition Evidence
  • supports Source: Studies in Continuous Black-box Optimization · Locator: Section 2.4.4, PDF pp. 48–49 / printed pp. 24–25, direct and virtual adaptation sampling.
  • supports Source: Natural Evolution Strategies · Locator: Section 3.2, PDF pp. 11–13 / printed pp. 959–961, controller definition and NES example.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Adaptation sampling denotes the online optimizer-hyperparameter comparison controller, with direct fresh-sample and virtual density-ratio-reuse modes. It differs from baseNES/xNES/SNES/IGO updates, CMAevolutionpaths, Rpropgradient-sign control and population-based training. xNES-as is the composed optimizer, not an alias. Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #direct-hyperparameter-comparison
Given an algorithm setting theta, a sampling law pi_theta and a quality measure psi for its samples, direct adaptation sampling evaluates fresh samples under a nearby hypothetical setting theta_prime. It retains the new setting when the chosen quality comparison indicates sufficient improvement and otherwise reverts. The comparison acts at the algorithm-hyperparameter level, with progress over time as its motivation; it is not itself the base optimizer’s natural-gradient update. The source gives a Mann–Whitney comparison as an example, not a complete universal testing protocol.
  • supports Source: Studies in Continuous Black-box Optimization · Locator: Section 2.4.4, PDF p. 48 / printed p. 24, direct adaptation sampling and meta-level quality comparison.
  • supports Source: Natural Evolution Strategies · Locator: Section 3.2, PDF p. 11 / printed p. 959, direct procedure and progress-versus-fitness distinction.
Mechanism #virtual-density-ratio-reuse
Virtual adaptation sampling reuses samples z_i drawn under pi_theta. It compares their existing quality values once with unit weights and once with weights w_i_prime = pi_theta_prime(z_i) / pi_theta(z_i). The weights represent the hypothetical law while the observed quality values remain unchanged. This can avoid fresh quality evaluations for the comparison, but the two groups reuse the same observations and are not independent samples.
  • supports Source: Studies in Continuous Black-box Optimization · Locator: Section 2.4.4, PDF p. 48 / printed p. 24, two virtual-adaptation bullets.
  • supports Source: Natural Evolution Strategies · Locator: Section 3.2, PDF p. 11 / printed p. 959, importance ratio and weighted copies of the same quality values.
Mechanism #reported-weighted-rank-comparison
For quality values s_i and s_j_prime with positive weights w_i and w_j_prime, the thesis defines U as the sum of w_i*w_j_prime over pairs with s_i greater than s_j_prime, plus half that product for equal values. It sets m = sum_i w_i and m_prime = sum_j w_j_prime, then uses mu = m*m_prime/2 and variance m*m_prime*(m+m_prime+1)/12 in a normal comparison. These are the source’s reported weighted-statistic and approximation formulas. They do not establish nominal independent-sample calibration for the paired, importance-weighted virtual mode.
  • supports Source: Studies in Continuous Black-box Optimization · Locator: Appendix B, PDF p. 121 / printed p. 97, weighted U, weight masses, normal mean/standard deviation and directional comparisons; paired reuse in Section 2.4.4, PDF p. 48. The body’s Appendix A pointer is incorrect in this copy.
Mechanism #nes-counterfactual-scale-learning-rate
In the concrete NES application, the controller adapts the learning rate eta_sigma of the global search scale. It forms the hypothetical distribution that would result from the previous optimizer state using 1.5 times the current eta_sigma, then weights the current population by the hypothetical-to-actual density ratio and compares its fitness ranks with their weighted counterpart. The hypothetical learning-rate change is distinct from directly multiplying the current search scale by 1.5 or adapting every optimizer block learning rate.
  • supports Source: Investigating the Impact of Adaptation Sampling in Natural Evolution Strategies on Black-box Optimization Testbeds · Locator: Section 2.2 and Algorithm 2, PDF p. 2 / printed p. 222, eta_sigma scope, hypothetical distribution from theta_(t-1) and density ratios.
  • supports Source: Natural Evolution Strategies · Locator: Section 3.2, PDF pp. 12–13 / printed pp. 960–961; Algorithm 4 on p. 13, input and hypothetical-update lines.
Mechanism #empirical-increase-or-return-policy
The described NES policy starts from a conservative initial learning rate. When its improvement criterion succeeds, it sets eta_sigma_new = min((1+c_prime)*eta_sigma, 1); otherwise it sets eta_sigma_new = (1-c_prime)*eta_sigma + c_prime*eta_sigma_init. The reported defaults are c_prime = 0.1 and rho = 1/2 - 1/(3*(d+1)) for search dimension d. These are empirical controller settings, not optimality or nominal error-rate guarantees. The unspecified test-return and rank-orientation conventions in the displayed pseudocode remain explicit limitations.
  • supports Source: Studies in Continuous Black-box Optimization · Locator: Section 2.4.4, PDF p. 49 / printed p. 25, hypothetical 1.5 rate, empirical rho, 1.1 increase/cap and return toward the initial rate.
  • supports Source: Investigating the Impact of Adaptation Sampling in Natural Evolution Strategies on Black-box Optimization Testbeds · Locator: Section 2.2 and Algorithm 2, PDF p. 2 / printed p. 222; Table 1, PDF p. 3 / printed p. 223.
Scope #modes-and-composition-boundary
The source distinguishes direct fresh-evaluation adaptation from virtual reuse and also discusses combining reweighted observations with some new hypothetical-law samples of weight one. Fresh sampling and reuse are therefore explicit modes, not interchangeable implementations. The 2012 paper calls the combination of xNES with the controller xNES-as. That combined optimizer, generic importance sampling, importance mixing and the weighted comparison statistic are not exact aliases of the adaptation-sampling controller.
  • supports Source: Studies in Continuous Black-box Optimization · Locator: Section 2.4.4, PDF pp. 48–49 / printed pp. 24–25, direct/virtual distinction and optional interpolation using new samples.
  • supports Source: Natural Evolution Strategies · Locator: Section 3.2, PDF p. 11 / printed p. 959, optional fresh samples accompanying reuse.
  • supports Source: Investigating the Impact of Adaptation Sampling in Natural Evolution Strategies on Black-box Optimization Testbeds · Locator: Section 2.2, PDF p. 2 / printed p. 222, naming of the xNES-as combination.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The weighted normal approximation is not established as calibrated for reused importance-weighted groups. In the equal-law case with finite ratios equal to one, both groups contain the same n quality values. Pair symmetry, including ties, makes U=n²/2 deterministically, whereas the displayed substituted variance is n²*(2*n+1)/12>0 for n>0. This bounded curator derivation refutes that expression as the actual variance in this paired case; it is not an author-issued erratum, a general error-rate calculation or a claim that every independent direct-sampling test is invalid.
  • The thesis Appendix B gives half credit for equal values but displays no tie-adjusted variance. Integer weights as repeated observations explain the pair-count identity, not independence or valid variance. Adaptive choice of the hypothetical law, repeated comparisons and dependence between the reused groups require separate calibration. Empirical rho is not asserted to be a valid nominal significance level.
  • The thesis and journal prose attach weights to unchanged quality values. The 2012 Algorithm 2 and 2014 Algorithm 4 instead write a set containing weight times rank, which must not silently be implemented as altered rank values. Their weighted-Mann-Whitney return value and rank/tail convention are not fully specified; the threshold branch is not certified as a literal implementation of the thesis’s directional normal comparisons. No repaired pseudocode or author-code behavior is claimed.
  • Ratios need finite, positive sampling densities at reused observations and defined finite hypothetical densities. Representing hypothetical-law expectations also requires suitable proposal coverage and integrability; positivity only at observed points does not establish global support coverage or stable weights. Large distribution changes can create large as well as small ratios. A weighted finite batch need not reveal an improvement, and no automatic effective-sample-size or numerical safeguard is supplied.
  • Direct mode spends fresh evaluations, while virtual mode reuses expensive quality observations and still needs hypothetical-density evaluation and comparison work. The optional interpolation using fresh samples is not required in every mode. No cost-free controller, general variance reduction, monotonic progress, convergence, universal noise robustness or benchmark advantage is asserted.
  • The concrete NES rule concerns the global-scale learning rate and empirical defaults. It is distinct from xNES or SNES themselves, natural-gradient geometry, IGO flow, CMA-ES evolution-path control, Rprop gradient-sign updates and population-based model copying. The source’s increase/decrease analogy with Rprop does not establish an exact alias. Underlying full-covariance NES formulas are not re-certified by this controller description.
  • Primary reading is limited to the declared methods and context: the thesis Section 2.4.4 and Appendix B, journal Section 3.2, and the 2012 Section 2.2/Algorithm 2 and defaults. The thesis body incorrectly points to Appendix A; the acquired 134-page author copy places the weighted test in Appendix B, PDF p. 121 / printed p. 97. Its cover dates and the separately inspected TUM repository metadata are retained without claiming byte equivalence to the repository PDF. The full thesis and empirical figures/tables were not audited.
  • The 2012 proceedings paper notes that its use of the known target fitness for restart decisions was outside the BBOB guidelines; its numerical comparisons are not imported here. Its initial sandbox DNS download failure and separate successful retry remain in the research provenance. An author BibTeX fetch was unsupported by the web tool. No author implementation, benchmark, statistical test or calculator was executed for this controller assessment.
