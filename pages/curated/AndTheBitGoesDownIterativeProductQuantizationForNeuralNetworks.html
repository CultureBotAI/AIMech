Iterative Product Quantization for Neural Networks (And the Bit Goes Down / Activation-Weighted PQ)

Algorithms AIMech:AndTheBitGoesDownIterativeProductQuantizationForNeuralNetworks DRAFT
Aliases: Iterative Product Quantization for Neural Networks, And the Bit Goes Down Activation-Weighted Product Quantization, Activation-Weighted Product Quantization with Codebook Distillation
Iterative Product Quantization for Neural Networks (introduced in 'And the Bit Goes Down: Revisiting the Quantization of Neural Networks', often denoted iPQ) is a structured vector quantization method that compresses deep neural networks sequentially from lowest to highest layer by partitioning weight columns (or spatially reshaped K x K convolutional filters) into contiguous d-dimensional subvectors, learning a byte-aligned codebook C via a weighted k-means EM algorithm that minimizes the in-domain output activation reconstruction error ||y - y_hat||_2^2 = sum_j ||x(w_j - q(w_j))||_2^2 on unlabelled input activations rather than Frobenius weight error, and fine-tuning the learned codewords both layer-by-layer and globally by averaging subvector gradients under a teacher-student Kullback-Leibler distillation loss.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from classical weight-space Product Quantization (Gong et al., 2014), which minimizes Frobenius weight error ||W - W_hat||_2^2 (Eq. 1) and suffers catastrophic accuracy drift across deep layers, and distinct from Quant-Noise (AIMech:QuantNoiseTrainingWithQuantizationNoiseForExtremeCompression), which applies quantization noise to random weight blocks during training to preserve unbiased gradient flow.

Mechanism #ipq-activation-weighted-kmeans-em-objective
Instead of minimizing Frobenius weight reconstruction error ||W - W_hat||_2^2 (Eq. 1), iPQ minimizes in-domain output activation reconstruction error ||y - y_hat||_2^2 = sum_j ||x(w_j - q(w_j))||_2^2 (Eq. 2) by unrolling input activations x in R^{B x C_in} into x_tilde in R^{(B*m) x d} and alternating an E-step cluster assignment c_j = argmin_{c in C} ||x_tilde(c - v)||_2^2 (Eq. 3) with a least-squares M-step update c* = argmin_{c in R^d} sum_{p in I_c} ||x_tilde(c - v_p)||_2^2 (Eq. 4).
  • supports Source: And the Bit Goes Down: Revisiting the Quantization of Neural Networks · Locator: Section 3.1 (Quantization of a Fully-Connected Layer), Equations 1-4, pp. 3-4
  • supports Source: And the Bit Goes Down: Revisiting the Quantization of Neural Networks · Locator: frobenius_weight_error_sq and activation_recon_error_sq
Mechanism #ipq-sequential-layer-quantization-and-codeword-distillation
For convolutional layers W in R^{C_out x C_in x K x K}, iPQ reshapes filters spatially into subvectors of dimension d = K x K (or 2*K*K), quantizes layers sequentially using activations forwarded through already-quantized lower layers, and fine-tunes fixed-assignment codewords under teacher-student KL divergence L = KL(y_s, y_t) via averaged subvector gradients c <- c - eta * (1/|I_c|) sum_{p in I_c} dL/db_p (Eq. 5), followed by global centroid fine-tuning with BatchNorm running-statistics adaptation.
  • supports Source: And the Bit Goes Down: Revisiting the Quantization of Neural Networks · Locator: Section 3.2-3.4, Figure 2 and Equation 5, pp. 4-5
  • supports Source: And the Bit Goes Down: Revisiting the Quantization of Neural Networks · Locator: codeword_distillation_step and layer_storage_bytes
Application #ipq-resnet50-and-mask-rcnn-byte-aligned-compression
Using byte-aligned codebooks (k = 256 centroids, 1 byte per subvector index), iPQ compresses a semi-supervised ResNet-50 by 20x to ~5 MB while preserving 76.12% ImageNet top-1 accuracy and compresses Mask R-CNN (ResNet-50 FPN) by 26x from 170 MB to 6.65 MB with 33.9 Box AP and 30.8 Mask AP.
  • supports Source: And the Bit Goes Down: Revisiting the Quantization of Neural Networks · Locator: Section 4.2-4.3, Tables 1-4 and Figure 3, pp. 6-8
Limitation #ipq-sequential-calibration-time-and-depthwise-conv-limitation
Because each layer's codebook depends on the quantized activations of all preceding layers and requires per-layer distillation fine-tuning (~2,500 iterations per layer), quantizing ResNet-50 takes ~1 GPU day on a V100, and architectures dominated by depthwise separable convolutions (such as MobileNets) exhibit lower spatial filter correlation for PQ than ResNets.
  • supports Source: And the Bit Goes Down: Revisiting the Quantization of Neural Networks · Locator: Section 2 (Dedicated architectures) and Section 4.1 (Experimental Setup), pp. 3, 6

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Sequential bottom-up layer quantization and per-layer distillation fine-tuning require ~1 V100 GPU day to compress a ResNet-50 model.
  • Depthwise-separable architectures (e.g., MobileNets, EfficientNets) have less spatial filter redundancy than standard 3x3 ResNet convolutions, reducing PQ gains without joint training.
