Alternating Expectation Conditional Maximization

Aliases: AECM, AECM Algorithm
A likelihood-optimization framework that alternates cycles of expectation and constrained maximization, permitting the admissible data augmentation and constraint functions to change between cycles while collectively covering the parameter directions.
Definition Evidence
  • supports Source: The EM Algorithm—an Old Folk-song Sung to a Fast New Tune · Locator: Section 3.2, Definition 1 and Equations 3.5-3.8, pp.525-527 / PDF 16-18
Identity Notes & Concept Scope AECM generalizes fixed-augmentation ECM and partitioned SAGE through cycle-specific augmentations and general constraints. One cycle contains an E-step and one or more CM steps sharing that expectation; an iteration completes the stated space-filling coverage. Working parameters index augmentation choices without changing the observed model. This is distinct from PX-EM expansion/reduction and from Gibbs sampling, even when particular examples have matching updates. Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #cycle-specific-augmentation
At the current parameter choose an augmentation that yields the same observed-data model and compute its expected augmented log likelihood conditional on the observed data. Perform the ordered CM steps on that fixed expectation, each on a constraint surface containing the most recent intermediate parameter. Before changing augmentation or starting the next cycle, refresh the expectation at the current parameter. Constraints may be more general than disjoint coordinate blocks.
  • supports Source: The EM Algorithm—an Old Folk-song Sung to a Fast New Tune · Locator: Section 3.2, pp.525-527 / PDF 16-18, especially Eq 3.5
Mechanism #cycle-ascent-and-coverage
The fixed expected objective increases within a cycle, so observed likelihood is nondecreasing between completed cycles and consequently completed iterations. This need not hold between every pair of CM substeps within one frozen-expectation cycle. Parameter-space coverage is additional to ascent: the source groups consecutive cycles into iterations when their constraint-gradient spaces have trivial intersection. The stationary-limit result further requires its stated regularity, interior, CM uniqueness or tie-handling, and iteration-mapping conditions.
  • supports Source: The EM Algorithm—an Old Folk-song Sung to a Fast New Tune · Locator: Section 3.2 Eq 3.7-3.8; Section 3.3 Theorems 2-3 and ECME-ordering remark, pp.526-528 / PDF 17-19
Scope #working-parameter-t-example
For a multivariate t model, the source indexes augmentations by q(a)=|Sigma|^(-a)q. With fixed degrees of freedom nu and dimension p, a=1/(nu+p) gives the weighted location and scatter normalized by the sum of latent weights rather than sample count. For unknown nu, location/scatter and nu updates form different cycles, with the design parameter recomputed from the current fixed nu. This family-specific information comparison does not assert optimality over all augmentations or algorithms.
  • supports Source: The EM Algorithm—an Old Folk-song Sung to a Fast New Tune · Locator: Sections 2.2-2.4, pp.515-522 / PDF 6-13; Section 3.4, pp.529-531 / PDF 20-22
Limitation #rate-theorem-limits
The general matrix-rate formula assumes convergence to an interior point and a fixed cycle composition with the specified differentiability conditions. It does not imply AECM is always faster than EM. Improving a local asymptotic rate or an individual block step does not guarantee fewer elapsed seconds, and the theorem does not establish convergence at nonnegative parameter boundaries.
  • supports Source: The EM Algorithm—an Old Folk-song Sung to a Fast New Tune · Locator: Section 3.3 Theorem 4, p.529 / PDF 20; Section 3.5, p.537 / PDF 28; author reply, p.562 / PDF 53

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Monotone bounded likelihood values alone do not establish parameter convergence or global optimality. Incomplete coverage, inexact unconstrained numerical steps or stale expectations after changing augmentation require separate analysis.
  • The main paper’s interior convergence results do not automatically cover its Poisson image example at zero intensities; the discussion explicitly refers to separate SAGE analysis. The Poisson pseudobackground example overlaps SAGE3 and is not assigned a duplicate concept ID.
  • Working-parameter selection may simplify or complicate the E/CM subproblems. A deterministic optimization result is not a posterior-sampling mixing guarantee.
  • All 58 source PDF pages were inspected, including discussions and author corrections. Historical simulations, CPU comparisons, image reconstructions and author software were not reproduced; no universal speed or clinical-performance claim is made.
