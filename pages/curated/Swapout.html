Swapout

Algorithms AIMech:Swapout DRAFT
A stochastic neural-network training and inference procedure that forms elementwise Bernoulli-selected sums of already computed compatible tensors, trains the resulting network family with shared parameters, and predicts using either local mean selectors or an average of sampled network predictions.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the original 2016 stochastic combination procedure over already computed compatible tensors, with shared training and explicit inference choices. It remains distinct from whole-branch stochastic depth, recurrent Zoneout, complementary continuous Shake-Shake and shared-switch ShakeDrop. Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #compatible-stochastic-path-sums
Let F_i be compatible tensors whose values have already been computed in the network, and let Theta_i be Bernoulli selector tensors with configured probabilities theta_i. The general construction forms Y=sum_i Theta_i elementwise-times F_i. Each selector tensor has independently and identically distributed entries. The contributions can include the block input or outputs of earlier layers; they need not all be functions of one common block input. The surrounding computation remains a directed acyclic network.
  • supports Source: Swapout: Learning an ensemble of deep architectures · Locator: Section 3, PDF p. 3, notation paragraph and Equation 2 with its immediately preceding tensor definitions.
Mechanism #unitwise-four-choice-specialization
The studied two-contribution specialization is Y=Theta_1 elementwise-times X + Theta_2 elementwise-times F(X). With independent selectors, each unit can report zero, its input component, its transformed component, or their sum, according to the two binary outcomes. These are four selector patterns, not a guarantee of four distinct numerical values. The unitwise choices can differ within a block, unlike one shared scalar that drops or retains its whole residual transformation.
  • supports Source: Swapout: Learning an ensemble of deep architectures · Locator: Figure 1 and Section 3, PDF p. 3, Equation 3 and the four-item explanation; Section 3.2, PDF p. 5, scalar Layer Dropout comparison.
Mechanism #shared-parameter-stochastic-training
Training samples members of the stochastic network family while retaining shared learned parameters across those members. The sampled selectors change which computed contributions reach each unit; this does not require training a separate parameter set for every possible architecture. The original experiments optimize these shared models using stochastic gradient descent. This description does not establish a general stability or convergence guarantee for the resulting optimization.
  • supports Source: Swapout: Learning an ensemble of deep architectures · Locator: Section 3, PDF pp. 3–4, sampled architecture discussion; Section 3.1, PDF p. 4, tied-parameter family; Section 4, PDF p. 5, Training paragraph.
Mechanism #depth-dependent-selector-configuration
The Bernoulli probabilities can be configured separately for the identity and transformed contributions and for different blocks. The source compares constant choices with Linear(a,b), meaning linear interpolation of the probabilities from a in the first block to b in the last block. This schedule varies across network depth, not over elapsed training iterations. The reported probability choices are experimental configurations rather than a universally optimal setting.
  • supports Source: Swapout: Learning an ensemble of deep architectures · Locator: Section 4, PDF p. 5, stochastic training schedule paragraph; Table 2 and its caption, PDF p. 6.
Mechanism #deterministic-and-stochastic-inference
The paper specifies two inference options for a trained shared-parameter family: replace the selectors by their expectations within each local computation, or sample several network members and average their predictions. The first option is deterministic; the second averages stochastic forward predictions using the same learned parameters. Replacing local coefficients by their means does not generally yield the expected prediction of the full nonlinear network. The number of sampled predictions is a configured approximation choice.
  • supports Source: Swapout: Learning an ensemble of deep architectures · Locator: Section 3.1, PDF p. 4, inference alternatives and ReLU noncommutation; Section 4, PDF p. 6, stochastic inference and sampling paragraphs.
Scope #coupled-special-cases-and-skipforward
The comparison procedures use distinct selector constraints. Dropout on the residual sum uses one selector on X+F(X). Layer Dropout retains X and uses a single Bernoulli scalar shared across the transformed block. SkipForward instead uses complementary unitwise selectors, Theta on X and one-minus-Theta on F(X), choosing between those contributions rather than allowing their sum or jointly dropping them. These restricted comparisons do not make the full named procedures exact aliases of the general Swapout construction.
  • supports Source: Swapout: Learning an ensemble of deep architectures · Locator: Section 3.2, PDF p. 5, Dropout and Layer Dropout definitions and SkipForward Equation 4; Figure 1, PDF p. 3.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Compatible tensor shapes, valid Bernoulli probabilities and an acyclic computation are prerequisites. Selector sharing and coupling specify different procedures; the independent two-selector specialization is not silently replaced by complementary SkipForward, whole-branch stochastic depth, recurrent Zoneout, or the separately prescribed backward multipliers of Shake-Shake and ShakeDrop. The displayed Equation 2 outcome set omits the all-zero sum although the selectors permit it; Equation 3 explicitly includes zero. No implementation-level random-number, sharing, device or computation-skipping behavior is certified.
  • The generic Equation 2 stability sketch on PDF p. 4 does not justify its pointwise gradient comparison merely by selecting the sampled member with the largest global Lipschitz constant. As a hand example with parameter v, branches F1(v)=v and F2(v)=2 sin(v) and independent half-Bernoulli selectors yield four members with global Lipschitz constants 0,1,2,3. At v=2*pi/3 their derivatives are 0,1,-1,0: the mean gradient magnitude is 1/2, whereas the largest-Lipschitz member has derivative zero there. A common bound of 3 remains valid. This witness concerns the generic source argument, not an asserted instability result for ordinary ReLU residual networks, a full audit of the cited stability theorem, or an author-issued erratum.
  • Local expectations do not generally commute with downstream nonlinearities or losses. With fixed contributions X=1 and Y=-1 and independent half-Bernoulli selectors, expected ReLU of their selected sum is 1/4 while ReLU of its mean is zero. Likewise, local dropout/ReLU commutation does not establish exact inference for an entire nonlinear dropout network. Averaging sampled predictions is a finite approximation, not by itself a calibrated uncertainty estimate or a universal sample-count guarantee.
  • The source variance comparison requires explicit assumptions. If two Bernoulli(theta) masks are mutually independent and independent of finite-second-moment features X,Y, the variance excess over theta*X+theta*Y is theta*(1-theta)*(E[X squared]+E[Y squared]); X and Y need not be independent. For interior theta it also vanishes when X=Y=0 almost surely. The mean theta*X+theta*Y is conditional on the features; an unconditional mean uses their expectations. Different sharing or feature dependence changes the calculation. This does not prove that batch normalization always fails with deterministic inference or that stochastic inference always cures it.
  • Generalization, parameter-tying and co-adaptation explanations and the numerical comparisons are scoped empirical or conjectural observations. In Table 3, PDF p. 7, the first deterministic Swapout error is 8.58 versus ResNet 8.27, while the stochastic column improves every displayed row; nearby prose explicitly says most settings for deterministic inference. The caption does not specify an inference convention, so it is not treated as an unambiguous source contradiction. The preliminary ImageNet setting also performs worse in the source. No universal accuracy, stability, convergence, parameter-efficiency or runtime advantage is asserted.
  • Evidence is the exact nine-page official NIPS 2016 proceedings PDF. Its file-build timestamp is not an exact publication date, and separately inspected arXiv submission metadata does not establish byte identity with the preprint. The official supplementary ZIP returned material for Perspective Transformer Nets, a different paper; it is excluded from all Swapout evidence and retained as a separate pending source lead. Historical research read all main-paper text and selected visuals, without inspecting author code, executing training or reproducing benchmarks. Source-access failures and the pre-draft qualification of the Table 3 caption remain separately retained provenance.
