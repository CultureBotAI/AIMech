Cross-entropy optimization

Algorithms AIMech:CrossEntropyOptimization DRAFT
Aliases: Cross-entropy method for optimization
A stochastic optimization method that iteratively samples a parameterized search distribution, selects high-performing samples using an adaptive threshold, and refits the distribution by maximum likelihood on those selected samples.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the optimization branch of the cross-entropy method, represented by Algorithm2.3 in the inspected 2005 tutorial. It is distinct from the rare-event probability estimator, the cross-entropy loss used to train a classifier, NES natural-gradient steps, and CMA-ES evolution-path covariance adaptation. The sampling family and its fit define concrete instances; the generic algorithm is not limited to independent Bernoulli variables or a Gaussian implementation. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Specify a sampling family f(x;v), initial parameter, sample size N, elite parameter rho and stopping rule. For maximization, draw candidates from the current distribution, evaluate their scores and choose the empirical (1−rho) quantile as threshold gamma. Fit new parameters by maximizing the sum of log f(x;v) over sampled candidates with score at least gamma. The optimization formulation uses unit weights: it redefines the associated stochastic problem at each iteration, so the rare-event algorithm’s nominal-to-current likelihood ratio is absent. Optionally smooth the fitted parameter toward the old one via v_new=alpha*v_fit+(1−alpha)*v_old. For independent Bernoulli coordinates, the unsmoothed fit is the coordinatewise fraction of ones among selected samples. Iterate until the chosen stopping condition; the displayed Algorithm2.3 uses an unchanged threshold over several iterations. The source’s cross-entropy derivation minimizes KL from the ideal selected-event distribution to the parameterized sampling distribution, not the reverse direction.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A score>=threshold rule need not select exactly rho*N candidates: order-statistic indexing and tied scores can increase the elite count. Implementations must state their quantile, tie and optimization-direction conventions; minimization requires the corresponding lower-score criterion or an equivalent score transformation.
  • Unsmoothed finite-sample fitting can produce Bernoulli probabilities 0 or 1 and remove future exploration. Interior initialization and 0<alpha<1 prevent exact endpoints after a single convex smoothing step in exact arithmetic; smoothing alone does not guarantee exploration forever or a global optimum, and finite precision can still collapse support.
  • The choice of sampling family, initial support, sample size, elite fraction, fit constraints and stopping rule matters. A stabilized threshold is not an optimality certificate; the source explicitly permits getting trapped at a local minimum and limits its asymptotic discussion.
  • Maximum-likelihood fitting of selected candidates is a sampling-distribution update, not an unbiased gradient of raw expected fitness. It does not require derivatives of the black-box objective; solving the distribution fit may still require numerical optimization. No universal performance or convergence-rate claim is curated.
