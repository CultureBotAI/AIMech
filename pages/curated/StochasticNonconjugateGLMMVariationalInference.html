Stochastic nonconjugate GLMM variational inference

Algorithms AIMech:StochasticNonconjugateGLMMVariationalInference DRAFT
A finite-population Bayesian GLMM inference procedure that iterates selected cluster-local Gaussian factors and uses expanded cluster minibatch contributions to damp global Gaussian and inverse-Wishart natural parameters. This record follows the Tan–Nott natural-gradient derivation with its complete covariance target and explicit sampling and local-solution qualifications.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

7 Claims
Scope #model-and-family
For a fixed finite set of N clusters, use the partially noncentered Bernoulli-logit or Poisson-log mixed model: beta~N(0,Sigma0), u_i|D~N(0,D), D~IW(nu,S), with positive-definite Sigma0,S and nu>r-1. At fixed cluster maps B_i define a_i=u_i+B_i*beta and eta_i=V_i*beta+Z_i*a_i with V_i=X_i-Z_i*B_i; the source uses B_i=[(I-W_i)*C_i,0] for its shared/cluster-constant design. Approximate the posterior by q(beta)q(D)product_i q(a_i), using full Gaussian blocks q(beta)=N(m,Q), q(a_i)=N(m_i,Q_i) and q(D)=IW(nuq,Sq). Set nuq=nu+N. The ELBO is expected log joint plus entropy for this proper model; the algorithm infers global beta,D factors from cluster minibatches, not individual observation minibatches or an unspecified unbounded stream.
  • supports Source: A stochastic variational framework for fitting and diagnosing generalized linear mixed models · Locator: Sections 3–4.1, Equations 4 and 8, PDF pp. 4–8; Algorithm 2 initialization, PDF p. 14
Mechanism #local-iterations
At the current global Gaussian and inverse-Wishart factors, draw a nonempty cluster minibatch and hold the globals and W_i fixed while iterating each selected local Gaussian. Let A=nuq*Sq^-1 and let G_i and F_i be expected first and second observation-cumulant derivatives under the current predictor distribution. With P_i=A+Z_i^T*diag(F_i)*Z_i and g_i=-A*(m_i-B_i*m)+Z_i^T*(y_i-G_i), propose Q_i_new=P_i^-1 and m_i_new=m_i+Q_i_new*g_i, evaluating both derivatives at the same old local state and recomputing moments for another local iteration. The source uses a loose relative local-mean stopping criterion; this is not a certificate of an exact stationary local solution or maximizer. Proper globals and exact nonnegative Bernoulli/Poisson curvature yield positive local precision.
  • supports Source: A stochastic variational framework for fitting and diagnosing generalized linear mixed models · Locator: Sections 4.3–4.4, PDF pp. 10–14; Algorithm 2 step 2, PDF p. 14; Appendix B, PDF pp. 37–38
Mechanism #expected-observation-curvature
For a selected cluster the scalar predictor means and variances are t_i=V_i*m+Z_i*m_i and v_i=diag(V_i*Q*V_i^T+Z_i*Q_i*Z_i^T). With positive Poisson exposures E_i, G_i=F_i=E_i*exp(t_i+v_i/2), elementwise. For Bernoulli-logit, G_ij=E[sigmoid(t_ij+sqrt(v_ij)*Z0)] and F_ij=E[sigmoid(T)*(1-sigmoid(T))] for the same T and Z0~N(0,1). These are one-dimensional normal expectations, approximated by quadrature for logistic observations. The standard-normal density uses exp(-z^2/2)/sqrt(2*pi); the missing half occurs in both the 2014 Appendix B display (PDF p. 37) and the 2013 Section 4.3 display (PDF p. 8). The normalized construction in the 2013 Appendix B (PDF pp. 19–20) supports the expectation used here.
  • supports Source: A stochastic variational framework for fitting and diagnosing generalized linear mixed models · Locator: Appendix B definitions of g and F, PDF pp. 37–38; the printed logistic integral on PDF p. 37 has an exponent discrepancy
  • supports Source: Variational Inference for Generalized Linear Mixed Models Using Partially Noncentered Parametrizations · Locator: Appendix B normalized standard-Gaussian expectations, PDF pp. 19–20; contrast the malformed Section 4.3 display on PDF p. 8
Mechanism #frozen-global-targets
After selected local iterations, freeze the old global m,Q,Sq and the selected local factors for both global proposals. For minibatch B of size b, define P_hat=Sigma0^-1+(N/b)*sum_{i in B}[B_i^T*A*B_i+V_i^T*diag(F_i)*V_i], g_hat=-Sigma0^-1*m+(N/b)*sum_{i in B}[B_i^T*A*(m_i-B_i*m)+V_i^T*(y_i-G_i)], and h_hat=P_hat*m+g_hat. The covariance-factor target is S_hat=S+(N/b)*sum_{i in B}[(m_i-B_i*m)*(m_i-B_i*m)^T+Q_i+B_i*Q*B_i^T], with degrees nu+N. Global priors enter once; only cluster contributions are expanded. S_hat includes the local covariance Q_i, present in Appendix B Equation 21 but omitted in printed Algorithm 2. Frozen global targets implement the simultaneous natural-gradient construction rather than silently reusing newly changed beta moments inside the other target.
  • supports Source: A stochastic variational framework for fitting and diagnosing generalized linear mixed models · Locator: Section 4.3, Equations 10–13, PDF pp. 12–13; Algorithm 2, PDF p. 14; Appendix B, Equations 20–21, PDF pp. 38–39
Mechanism #natural-parameter-mixing
Choose 0<rho<=1 and write P_old=Q^-1 and h_old=P_old*m. Mix the Gaussian natural coordinates as P_new=(1-rho)*P_old+rho*P_hat and h_new=(1-rho)*h_old+rho*h_hat; then Q_new=P_new^-1 and m_new=Q_new*h_new=m+rho*Q_new*g_hat. Mix the inverse-Wishart scale as Sq_new=(1-rho)*Sq+rho*S_hat, keeping nuq=nu+N. This is not ordinary arithmetic averaging of Gaussian covariance matrices or means. Positive-definite old/target precisions and scales make this convex natural step proper. The source derives it from stochastic natural-gradient ascent; proper factors alone do not guarantee ELBO ascent or convergence.
  • supports Source: A stochastic variational framework for fitting and diagnosing generalized linear mixed models · Locator: Equations 11–13, PDF pp. 12–13; Appendix B Gaussian and inverse-Wishart natural vectors and Equations 20–21, PDF pp. 38–39
Limitation #sampling-and-profile-boundary
At a frozen global state, assume each cluster contribution is evaluated by the same specified local-solution rule. A fresh uniform subset of b clusters gives an unbiased N/b-scaled sum for the corresponding full sum; independent uniform draws with replacement also do so when repeated draws are counted with multiplicity. To identify that sum as the gradient of an optimized-local ELBO additionally requires the envelope argument’s differentiable stationary local solution and regularity. Approximate local solves, history-dependent warm starts or biased quadrature need separate error assessment. The source implementation description instead shuffles clusters into a sweep and samples from those remaining: its next-batch estimate is not generally conditionally unbiased at the changing current state under the fresh-subset argument. This does not by itself prove reshuffling fails; it limits the unbiased-gradient/convergence claim.
  • supports Source: A stochastic variational framework for fitting and diagnosing generalized linear mixed models · Locator: Sections 4.3–4.4 local-solution/envelope and minibatch derivations, PDF pp. 10–14; curator finite sampling comparison
Scope #schedule-and-hybrid-scope
The source discusses diminishing gains rho_t=a/(t+A)^alpha with 1/2<alpha<=1 and later uses a sweep-count clock, and optionally switches to the full-data procedure when an attained-bound progress criterion is small. The schedule must be defined at the initial index and respect the factor-domain requirement; Robbins–Monro summability is only part of the regularity conditions needed for convergence. A finite hybrid switching/stopping rule does not establish stationary or globally optimal inference, and its reported runtime belongs to that hybrid experiment. This record does not certify a streaming population-size rule, constant-memory implementation, author software or the separate prior-likelihood conflict diagnostic.
  • supports Source: A stochastic variational framework for fitting and diagnosing generalized linear mixed models · Locator: Sections 4.3–4.5, PDF pp. 12–16; experimental context and conclusions, PDF pp. 21–33

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The record follows the stochastic natural-parameter derivation with frozen global targets and the full covariance moment in Appendix B. It does not certify Algorithm 2 as printed: that display omits Q_i in the inverse-Wishart target, and the sequential-looking display can obscure the old-global evaluation required by the simultaneous construction.
  • Additional source discrepancies are explicit: the Gaussian motivating weight matrix on PDF p. 5 uses an identity with the wrong dimension for a general design; Poisson curvature on p. 7 omits an exponential; the logistic normal weight on p. 37 omits a half in its exponent, as does the 2013 Section 4.3 display on PDF p. 8. The normalized 2013 Appendix B construction on PDF pp. 19–20, likelihood differentiation and the full Appendix B target delimit the supported reconstruction. No source implementation is claimed corrected or executed.
  • The source sweep sampler and loose local-mean convergence check do not by themselves meet the fresh-batch, exact-local-envelope assumptions. Constant or diminishing gains cannot supply missing stability, smoothness, moment or local-solution conditions. Neither every iterate nor a sampled estimate must improve the full ELBO.
  • The factor family still omits dependence with D and across transformed Gaussian blocks. Partial noncentering captures restricted dependence in original coordinates, not an arbitrary joint posterior. Priors, initialization, numeric quadrature and conditioning affect results. A prior scale based on an estimated information inverse requires invertibility.
  • All 42 primary 2014 preprint pages and the supporting 21-page 2013 reprint were read. The reported clinical-data comparisons and simulated hybrid-fit timings are not curated as clinical conclusions or general accuracy/speed guarantees. Source minibatch percentages contain decimal errors; finite-population weights here use actual N and b. No fitting, author implementation, diagnostic calibration study or benchmark was run.
  • Independent arithmetic checks fixed supplied local Gaussian factors, all uniform subsets/iid draw multisets in a tiny example and one simultaneous global step. Those local factors were not optimized, so this is not a numerical test of the envelope theorem, a profiled-gradient reproduction or convergence. DRAFT remains.
