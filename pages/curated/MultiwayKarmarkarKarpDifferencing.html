Multiway Karmarkar–Karp differencing

A multiway number-partitioning heuristic that represents partial partitions by normalized bin-load vectors and repeatedly combines two selected subpartitions by pairing their largest and smallest bin loads, retaining one composition until a full partition remains.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #partial-bin-representation
For a fixed k>=3 and a finite nonempty multiset of nonnegative weights, initialize one k-bin subpartition per original item: its weight occupies one bin and the other bins are empty. Each subpartition represents disjoint original items already assigned to relative bins. Subtracting its minimum load from every coordinate preserves all within-subpartition load differences. Original memberships require bookkeeping beyond the normalized numbers.
  • supports Source: A Complete Anytime Algorithm for Number Partitioning · Locator: Section 7.3, pp. 20–21; original-index reconstruction interpretation
Mechanism #opposing-rank-composition
Select two subpartitions with the largest normalized maximum loads. Order the bins of one by decreasing load and the other by increasing load, merge corresponding bin memberships and add their loads, then subtract the new minimum and reinsert the result. This pairs large with small components within the two chosen subpartitions; it is not global placement of each next raw item in the lightest existing bin. Tie policy must be specified to reproduce a particular trace or membership output.
  • supports Source: A Complete Anytime Algorithm for Number Partitioning · Locator: Section 7.3, pp. 20–21
Scope #normalization-and-reconstruction
A normalized zero denotes a minimum relative load, which may belong to a nonempty subset. Merge the stored original-index groups according to each selected bin permutation; their final sums recover the actual loads, differing from the normalized vector by a common offset. In the source example 8,7,6,5,4 with three bins, successive combined normalized vectors are (8,7,0), (2,1,0), (5,4,0), then (3,3,0). The final actual loads are 11,11,8, so the normalized zero is not an empty bin.
  • supports Source: A Complete Anytime Algorithm for Number Partitioning · Locator: Section 7.3 worked example and reconstruction, pp. 20–21; constructed indexed arithmetic
Limitation #heuristic-not-complete
Only one opposing-rank composition is retained at each merge. This yields a feasible partition but is not guaranteed to minimize range. In a constructed run with declared deterministic tie handling on weights 2,2,2,3,3,5, the heuristic gives loads (7,5,5), range 2; exhaustive assignment admits (6,6,5), range 1. The source’s complete multiway CKK instead explores alternate relative bin permutations. This counterexample is a curator calculation, not a reported author experiment.
  • supports Source: A Complete Anytime Algorithm for Number Partitioning · Locator: Sections 7.3–7.4, pp. 20–24; constructed seven-fixture calculation and exhaustive assignment comparison
Limitation #range-objective-boundary
The normalized maximum equals the final load range, not the absolute largest bin load. Range minimization and makespan minimization can select different solutions for k>=3. On weights 4,4,4,6,6,9, the constructed heuristic returns loads (13,10,10) with globally minimum range 3, while a maximum load of 12 is possible with (12,12,9). The source’s opening claim that minimizing range also minimizes the largest load is therefore not adopted as a guarantee for the returned partition.
  • supports Source: A Complete Anytime Algorithm for Number Partitioning · Locator: Section 7 opening objective, p. 19; Section 7.3 normalization, pp. 20–21; constructed exhaustive load-set counterexample
  • contradicts Source: A Complete Anytime Algorithm for Number Partitioning · Locator: Section 7 opening, p. 19: claimed range/largest-load equivalence; contradicted by the constructed objective example
Scope #variant-and-evaluation-scope
This vector-subpartition record covers k>=3; the two-way scalar heuristic has its own maintained identity. The one-composition rule differs from complete multiway CKK and from randomized cyclic-differencing and compaction schemes in the original report. Korf’s three-way search experiments concern complete algorithms, so their node counts and runtime comparisons are not standalone validation of this heuristic across distributions, bin counts or precisions.
  • supports Source: A Complete Anytime Algorithm for Number Partitioning · Locator: Sections 2.6 and 7.3–7.5, pp. 8–11 and 20–25; original report Section 3 was separately inspected as variant context
  • supports Source: The Differencing Method of Set Partitioning · Locator: Section 3, PDF/printed pp. 16–30; binary and cyclic operations pp. 18–20 and randomized cyclic algorithm pp. 20–23

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The scope is unconstrained range partitioning into k>=3 interchangeable bins. Equal cardinality, nonempty-bin requirements and bin-dependent restrictions are not imposed.
  • Tie choices can affect the constructed trace or memberships. The curator calculation declares its policy and does not assert identity with an uninspected author implementation.
  • Normalization preserves differences but discards an absolute common offset unless separate membership or offset data are retained. A zero component need not denote an unused bin.
  • A heuristic result is not an optimality certificate, and even a range optimum need not be a minimum-makespan partition.
  • All 31 pages of the Korf manuscript and all 30 pages of the original report were read; constructed exact-integer examples do not establish typical performance or replace complete search.
