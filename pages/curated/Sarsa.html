Sarsa

Algorithms AIMech:Sarsa DRAFT
Aliases: State-action-reward-state-action
An on-policy temporal-difference control algorithm that updates the current state-action value toward the observed reward plus the discounted estimated value of the next action selected by the same behavior policy.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record specifies one-step Sarsa through the tabular control formulation. The historical Modified Connectionist Q-Learning report supplies the selected-next-action target in a broader neural and eligibility-trace method; its lambda=0 case shares the one-step error, but the entire trace-based neural method is not made an exact alias of this record. Sarsa(lambda), expected Sarsa, Q-learning and GAE have distinct scopes. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Maintain Q(s,a), choose the current action A using the current policy derived from Q, execute it, and observe reward R and next state S_next. For a nonterminal next state, choose A_next from that same policy before updating, then set Q(S,A)=Q(S,A)+alpha*(R+gamma*Q(S_next,A_next)−Q(S,A)), using pre-update values. Carry the selected A_next forward as the next executed action; it is not replaced with a fresh sample after the update. For a terminal next state, the continuation value is zero and no next environment action is executed. Control improves the policy from Q while retaining its declared exploration rule, such as epsilon-greedy selection. The sample target uses the actual selected next action, not a maximum over actions and not an expectation over all policy actions. Rummery and Niranjan’s Eq.9 uses this error multiplied by an eligibility-weighted sum of neural-output gradients; at lambda=0 only the current gradient remains. That historical extension does not make neural networks or nonzero traces necessary for one-step tabular Sarsa.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A persistently exploratory behavior policy changes the values being estimated; an exploratory next action can lower or raise the sampled target relative to another action. Greedy Sarsa and Q-learning can coincide on a transition, but such a special case does not equate the methods.
  • Convergence requires a specified setting, sampling policy and step-size assumptions. The inspected book draft attributes its tabular control convergence statement to personal communication, and the 1994 neural report discusses parameter sensitivity and missing general guarantees. Neither source is treated as an independently verified complete proof for arbitrary function approximation.
  • The September 2016 book draft is versioned as a draft. Its retrospective naming attribution differs from the SARSA footnote in the available 1994-covered report; no first-use date or explanation of that discrepancy is asserted.
  • Trace choice, replacing versus accumulating traces, n-step targets and expected backups define extensions requiring their own evidence. No robot-navigation or gridworld performance result is curated here.
