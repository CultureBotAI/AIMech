Penalized Focused Generalized Method of Moments (FGMM) for High-Dimensional Endogenous Regression

Algorithms AIMech:PenalizedFocusedGeneralizedMethodOfMomentsForHighDimensionalEndogenousRegression DRAFT
Aliases: Penalized Focused Generalized Method of Moments (FGMM), Fan-Liao High-Dimensional Endogenous FGMM Estimator, Focused GMM with Folded-Concave Penalty, Two-Step Semiparametrically Efficient Post-FGMM
A high-dimensional variable-selection and over-identified estimation methodology (introduced by Jianqing Fan and Yuan Liao in 2014) for linear and nonlinear single-index structural models satisfying $\mathbb{E}[g(Y, \mathbf{X}_S^T\boldsymbol{\beta}_{0S}) | \mathbf{W}] = 0$ with endogenous regressors ($p \gg n$, $|S| = s \ll n$) and auxiliary instruments $\mathbf{W}$. After proving that any penalized empirical risk $\min_{\boldsymbol{\beta}} L_n(\boldsymbol{\beta}) + \sum_{j=1}^p P_n(|\beta_j|)$ requires the necessary condition $|\partial L_n(\boldsymbol{\beta}_0) / \partial \beta_l| \xrightarrow{p} 0$ for all $l \le p$ to be selection consistent (Theorem 2.1, Equation 2.1) and hence that Penalized Least Squares is inconsistent whenever any unimportant regressor $X_l$ ($l \notin S$) is endogenous ($|\mathbb{E}(X_l\varepsilon)| > c > 0$, Theorem 2.2), FGMM constructs two instrument transformation vectors $\mathbf{F} = (f_1(\mathbf{W}), \dots, f_p(\mathbf{W}))^T$ and $\mathbf{H} = (h_1(\mathbf{W}), \dots, h_p(\mathbf{W}))^T$ and a $\boldsymbol{\beta}$-dependent working instrument vector $\mathbf{V}_i(\boldsymbol{\beta}) = (\mathbf{F}_i(\boldsymbol{\beta})^T, \mathbf{H}_i(\boldsymbol{\beta})^T)^T$ supported only on $\operatorname{supp}(\boldsymbol{\beta})$ (Section 3, Equations 3.1–3.2). Minimizing $Q_{\mathrm{FGMM}}(\boldsymbol{\beta}) = L_{\mathrm{FGMM}}(\boldsymbol{\beta}) + \sum_{j=1}^p P_n(|\beta_j|)$ where $L_{\mathrm{FGMM}}(\boldsymbol{\beta}) = \sum_{j=1}^p I_{(\beta_j \ne 0)}\left\{w_{j1}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n g(Y_i, \mathbf{X}_i^T\boldsymbol{\beta})f_j(\mathbf{W}_i)\right]^2 + w_{j2}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n g(Y_i, \mathbf{X}_i^T\boldsymbol{\beta})h_j(\mathbf{W}_i)\right]^2\right\}$ (Equations 3.3–3.4) with a folded-concave penalty $P_n$ (SCAD or MCP) focuses over-identifying moments on the active support, achieves the oracle property $P(\widehat{S} = S) \to 1$ and asymptotic normality when $s^3 \log p = o(n)$ without irrepresentable conditions (Theorems 4.1 and 5.1), attains Chamberlain's semiparametric efficiency bound via two-step Post-FGMM (Theorem 6.1, Equation 6.2), and admits a smoothed surrogate $Q_K(\boldsymbol{\beta})$ with $K(\beta_j^2 / h_n)$ for coordinate descent (Theorems 7.1 and 7.2).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Resolved against all 442 pinned AIO concepts and 667 maintained AIMech concepts. Distinct from `AIMech:SelfTuningPivotalHighDimensionalInstrumentalVariablesAndSensitivityConfidenceSets` (Gautier & Rose 2011/2021, which solves a convex conic program with $\ell_\infty$ instrument-residual constraints across all $d_Z$ instruments simultaneously, rather than restricting moment equations to $\operatorname{supp}(\boldsymbol{\beta})$ via indicator-weighted $I_{(\beta_j \ne 0)}$ over-identified quadratic forms and folded-concave penalties to achieve the nonconcave oracle property and Chamberlain's semiparametric efficiency bound), distinct from `AIMech:SelfNormalizedLassoOptimalInstrumentEstimationAndSupNormWeakIdentificationTesting` (Belloni, Chen, Chernozhukov, & Hansen 2012, which estimates first-stage optimal instruments for a low-dimensional endogenous regressor vector rather than variable selection among $p \gg n$ endogenous regressors), distinct from `AIMech:SomeInvalidSomeValidInstrumentalVariablesEstimatorAndMajorityRuleIdentifiability` (Kang, Zhang, Cai, & Small 2016, which estimates a scalar causal effect under invalid instruments via majority-rule $\ell_1$ penalization), and distinct from `AIMech:NonconcavePenalizedLikelihoodInUltrahighDimensionalVariableSelection` (Fan & Lv 2011, which studies exogenous generalized linear models where $\mathbb{E}[\partial L_n(\boldsymbol{\beta}_0) / \partial\boldsymbol{\beta}] = 0$ holds automatically).

Mechanism #necessary-gradient-condition-and-inconsistency-of-penalized-least-squares-under-endogeneity
For any loss function $L_n(\boldsymbol{\beta})$ that is twice differentiable in a neighborhood of $\boldsymbol{\beta}_0$ with bounded largest sparse eigenvalue $\lambda_{\max}(\partial^2 L_n(\boldsymbol{\beta}) / \partial\boldsymbol{\beta}_S^2) = O_p(1)$ and any folded-concave penalty $P_n$ satisfying $P_n'(0^+) = o(1)$, Theorem 2.1 (Section 2, p. 6, Equation 2.1) proves that a necessary condition for variable-selection consistency ($P(\operatorname{supp}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}) = S) \to 1$ and $\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_S - \boldsymbol{\beta}_{0S}\| \xrightarrow{p} 0$) is $|\partial L_n(\boldsymbol{\beta}_0) / \partial\beta_l| \xrightarrow{p} 0$ for all $l = 1, \dots, p$. Specializing to $L_n(\boldsymbol{\beta}) = \frac{1}{n}\|\mathbf{Y} - \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta}\|^2$, Theorem 2.2 (p. 7) proves that if there exists even one unimportant regressor $l \notin S$ that is endogenous ($|\mathbb{E}(X_l\varepsilon)| > c > 0$ for $\varepsilon = Y - \mathbf{X}_S^T\boldsymbol{\beta}_{0S}$), then for any sequences $\lambda_n > 0$ and $a_n > 0$, $\limsup_{n\to\infty} P(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\mathrm{PLS}} \text{ is Model Selection Consistent}) < 1$.

Scope #focused-gmm-oracle-property-and-near-global-overidentified-optimality
Under the conditional moment restriction $\mathbb{E}[g(Y, \mathbf{X}_S^T\boldsymbol{\beta}_{0S}) | \mathbf{W}] = 0$, the FGMM loss $L_{\mathrm{FGMM}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})$ (Equation 3.3) includes two instrument conditions $f_j(\mathbf{W})$ and $h_j(\mathbf{W})$ per active regressor $\beta_j \ne 0$, creating $2|\operatorname{supp}(\boldsymbol{\beta})|$ over-identifying equations that prevent the zero-loss overfitting of naive GMM when $|\operatorname{supp}(\boldsymbol{\beta})| \ge |\mathbf{V}|$ while avoiding accumulation of $O_p(\sqrt{p / n})$ estimation noise from unimportant regressors $j \notin S$. Under Assumptions 4.1–4.6 with $s^3 \log p = o(n)$ and signal strength $d_n \gg \lambda_n$, Theorem 4.1 (p. 16) establishes the oracle property $P(\widehat{S} = S) \to 1$ and $\sqrt{n}\boldsymbol{\alpha}^T\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_S - \boldsymbol{\beta}_{0S}) \xrightarrow{d} N(0, 1)$ for a local minimizer, and Theorem 5.1 (p. 18) proves under over-identification Assumptions 5.1–5.2 that the global minimizer on $\Theta_r = \{\boldsymbol{\beta} : \|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_0 \le r_n\}$ coincides with this oracle estimator with probability approaching 1.

Mechanism #post-fgmm-semiparametric-efficiency-and-smoothed-coordinate-descent-convergence
Let $\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{W}) = \mathbb{E}[\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\beta}_S} g(Y, \mathbf{X}_S^T\boldsymbol{\beta}_{0S}) | \mathbf{W}]$ and $\sigma^2(\mathbf{W}) = \mathbb{E}[g(Y, \mathbf{X}_S^T\boldsymbol{\beta}_{0S})^2 | \mathbf{W}]$ with optimal instrument vector $\mathbf{A}^*(\mathbf{W}) = \sigma^{-2}(\mathbf{W})\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{W})$ (Section 6, p. 19). Using the first-stage FGMM estimator $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}$ to recover $\widehat{S} = S$ and form nonparametrically estimated optimal instruments $\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_i = \widehat{\sigma}(\mathbf{W}_i)^{-2}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}(\mathbf{W}_i)$, the two-step Post-FGMM estimator $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_S^* = \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\beta}_S} \left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n g(Y_i, \mathbf{X}_{i,\widehat{S}}^T\boldsymbol{\beta}_S)\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_i\right]^T\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n g(Y_i, \mathbf{X}_{i,\widehat{S}}^T\boldsymbol{\beta}_S)\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_i\right]$ (Equation 6.2) achieves Chamberlain's (1987) semiparametric efficiency bound $\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_S^* - \boldsymbol{\beta}_{0S}) \xrightarrow{d} N\left(0, \left[\mathbb{E}(\sigma(\mathbf{W})^{-2}\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{W})\mathbf{D}(\mathbf{W})^T)\right]^{-1}\right)$ when $s$ is fixed (Theorem 6.1, p. 20). Moreover, replacing $I_{(\beta_j \ne 0)}$ in Equation (3.3) with a smooth approximation $K(\beta_j^2 / h_n)$ satisfying $K(0) = 0$ and $K(t) = 1$ for $t > 1$ yields $Q_K(\boldsymbol{\beta})$ (Equation 7.1), which preserves exact selection consistency (Theorem 7.1, p. 21) and Cauchy coordinate-descent convergence $\|\boldsymbol{\beta}^{(m+1)} - \boldsymbol{\beta}^{(m)}\| \to 0$ (Theorem 7.2, p. 23).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Theorem 5.1's global over-identification guarantee requires the secondary instruments $\mathbf{H} = (h_1(\mathbf{W}), \dots, h_p(\mathbf{W}))^T$ to satisfy Assumption 5.1 ($\min_{\boldsymbol{\beta} \in \Theta_r : \operatorname{supp}(\boldsymbol{\beta}) \ne S}\|\mathbb{E}[g(Y, \mathbf{X}^T\boldsymbol{\beta})\mathbf{H}(\boldsymbol{\beta})]\| \ge \underline{\kappa}_s d_n$), so that no false support $\widetilde{S} \ne S$ of size $\le r_n$ can simultaneously satisfy both sets of moment equations.
  • Because both the indicator-weighted FGMM objective $Q_{\mathrm{FGMM}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})$ (Equation 3.4) and its smoothed surrogate $Q_K(\boldsymbol{\beta})$ (Equation 7.1) with folded-concave penalties are nonconvex, coordinate descent is guaranteed to converge to a stationary point (Theorem 7.2) rather than certifying global optimality from an arbitrary initialization.
  • Primary-source typographical audit of `arXiv:1204.5536v2`: (1) Section 2 paragraph 1 line 8 (p. 6) writes 'a exogenous relationship' instead of 'an exogenous relationship'; (2) Section 4.1 Assumption 4.6(iv) (p. 15) writes '$\max_{j \le p}\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n [g(y_i, \mathbf{X}_{iS}^T\boldsymbol{\beta}_{0S})^2 - \mathbb{E}g(y_i, \mathbf{X}_{iS}^T\boldsymbol{\beta}_{0S})^2]f_j(\mathbf{W}_i)^2\| = o(P_n(0^+))$' omitting the derivative prime on '$P_n\'(0^+)$' (and using lowercase '$y_i$' instead of '$Y_i$'); (3) Section 4.2 Remark 4.4 line 1 (p. 17) spells 'irrpresentable condition' instead of 'irrepresentable condition'; (4) Section 6 Theorem 6.1 display (p. 20) writes '$\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_S * - \boldsymbol{\beta}_{0S})$' with a baseline asterisk '$*$' instead of superscript '$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_S^*$'; (5) Appendix B.2 Equation (B.7) (p. 33) writes '$\sum_{j \in S} P_n(\beta_j)$' omitting the absolute value bars in '$P_n(|\beta_j|)$'; (6) Appendix B.2 display for $\mathbf{Z}(\boldsymbol{\beta}_{0S})$ (p. 34) writes '$q_i(Y_i, \mathbf{X}_{iS}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{0S})$' omitting the transpose '$^T$' on '$\mathbf{X}_{iS}^T$' and adding a spurious subscript '$i$' on '$q$'; and (7) Appendix C.2 Proof of Lemma C.2 line 8 (p. 39) writes '$\sum_{i=1}^s v_j^2$' with mismatched summation index '$i$'/'$j$', and Reference [10] (p. 46) spells 'Endogeity'.
