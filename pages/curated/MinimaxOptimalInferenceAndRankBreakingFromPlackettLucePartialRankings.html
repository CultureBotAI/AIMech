Minimax-Optimal Inference and Rank-Breaking from Plackett-Luce Partial Rankings

Mathematical Methods AIMech:MinimaxOptimalInferenceAndRankBreakingFromPlackettLucePartialRankings DRAFT
Aliases: Plackett-Luce Partial Ranking MLE and Full Rank-Breaking, Hajek-Oh-Xu Minimax-Optimal Inference from Partial Rankings, Weighted Composite Pairwise Likelihood Rank-Breaking for Plackett-Luce Models
A statistical estimation and rank-aggregation methodology (introduced by Bruce Hajek, Sewoong Oh, and Jiaming Xu in 2014) for inferring latent item parameters $\theta^* \in \Theta_b = \{\theta \in [-b,b]^n : \langle \theta, \mathbf{1}\rangle = 0\}$ under the Plackett–Luce (PL) random utility model ($U_i = \theta_i^* + Z_i$ with i.i.d. standard Gumbel noise $f(z) = e^{-(z+e^{-z})}$, equivalently sequential Luce choice $\mathbb{P}[\sigma_j \mid S_j] = \prod_{\ell=1}^{k_j-1} e^{\theta^*_{\sigma_j(\ell)}} / \sum_{r=\ell}^{k_j} e^{\theta^*_{\sigma_j(r)}}$) from $m$ partial rankings of subsets $S_j \subseteq [n]$ of size $k_j$. By coupling observed rankings to an oracle revealing exact Gumbel utilities $U_i$ and applying the multivariate van Trees Bayesian Cramér–Rao inequality with product prior $\mu_0(t) = (1+\cos(\pi t))/2$ ($I(\mu_0)=\pi^2$) and Gumbel location Fisher information $I(f)=1$, Theorem 1 establishes the non-asymptotic minimax lower bound $\inf_{\hat{\theta}}\sup_{\theta^*\in\Theta_b}\mathbb{E}[\|\hat{\theta}-\theta^*\|_2^2] \ge \frac{(n-1)^2}{2\sum_{j=1}^m k_j + 2n\pi^2/b^2}$. Theorem 2 proves that the constrained Plackett–Luce Maximum Likelihood Estimator $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{ML}} = \arg\max_{\theta\in\Theta_b}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PL}}(\theta)$ achieves $\|\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{ML}} - \theta^*\|_2 \le \frac{C e^{4b}\sqrt{n\log n + \frac{k_{\max}}{k_{\min}}\log^2 n}}{\sqrt{m k_{\mathrm{ave}}}}$ via strongly convex Hessian lower bounds $\lambda_2(H(\theta)) \ge \frac{1}{2e^{4b}} \lambda_2(L)$ on the hypergraph Laplacian $L = \sum_{j=1}^m \frac{1}{k_j}\sum_{i<i'\in S_j}(e_i-e_{i'})(e_i-e_{i'})^\top$ (Lemmas 1–2, Claim 1). Furthermore, comparing *Random Independence-Preserving Breaking* ($\lfloor k_j/2\rfloor$ disjoint pairs per ranking, Theorem 3) against *Full Breaking* (all $\binom{k_j}{2}$ pairs weighted by $1/(k_j-1)$ in the composite pairwise log-likelihood $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FB}}(\theta)$, Definition 5, Equation 2, Theorem 4) proves that Full Breaking achieves the minimax-optimal $1/\sqrt{m k_{\mathrm{ave}}}$ rate despite dependent pairwise comparisons.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Resolved against all 442 pinned AIO concepts and 683 maintained AIMech concepts. Distinct from `AIMech:RankCentralityIterativeRandomWalkSpectralRankingFromPairwiseComparisons` (Negahban, Oh, & Shah 2017, which analyzes spectral random-walk aggregation of pairwise comparisons $k=2$) and from `AIMech:TopKRankingSpectralAndRegularizedMLEByLeaveOneOutAnalysis` (Chen, Fan, Ma, & Wang 2019, which analyzes entrywise $\ell_\infty$ top-$K$ recovery from pairwise comparisons rather than $k_j$-way Plackett–Luce partial rankings, Gumbel-utility van Trees oracle lower bounds, and dependent Full Rank-Breaking composite likelihoods).

Mechanism #claim-van-trees-gumbel-utility-oracle-minimax-lower-bound
Under the Plackett–Luce random utility representation $U_i = \theta_i^* + Z_i$ with standard Gumbel density $f(z) = e^{-(z+e^{-z})}$ having Fisher information $I(f) = \int (f'(z))^2/f(z)\,dz = 1$, revealing the continuous utilities $\{U_i\}_{i\in S_j}$ strictly dominates observing only their induced ranking $\sigma_j$, and applying the van Trees inequality with prior density $\mu_0(t) = (1+\cos(\pi t))/2$ ($I(\mu_0) = \pi^2$) on $\Theta_b$ proves $\inf_{\hat{\theta}}\sup_{\theta^*\in\Theta_b}\mathbb{E}[\|\hat{\theta}-\theta^*\|_2^2] \ge \frac{(n-1)^2}{2\sum_{j=1}^m k_j + 2n\pi^2/b^2}$, while the exact Plackett–Luce Fisher information matrix at $\theta^* = 0$ satisfies $I(0) = \sum_{j=1}^m (1 - \frac{1}{k_j}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k_j}\frac{1}{\ell}) L_j$ (Section 2, Theorem 1, Corollary 1, Remark 1, Proof of Theorem 1 & Claim 3).

Mechanism #claim-plackett-luce-mle-hypergraph-laplacian-curvature-and-upper-bound
For any $\theta \in \Theta_b$, Claim 1 and Lemma 2 prove that each $k_j$-way Plackett–Luce log-likelihood Hessian block dominates the scaled clique Laplacian with $\lambda_2(H(\theta)) \ge \frac{1}{2e^{4b}}\lambda_2(L)$ where $L = \sum_{j=1}^m \frac{1}{k_j}\sum_{i<i'\in S_j}(e_i-e_{i'})(e_i-e_{i'})^\top$, while martingale score differences satisfy $\|\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PL}}(\theta^*)\|_2 \le \sqrt{6 m k_{\mathrm{ave}}\log n} + \frac{2\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{k_{\min}}}\log n$ (Lemma 1), yielding $\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\|\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{ML}} - \theta^*\|_2 \le C e^{4b}\sqrt{\frac{\log n + (k_{\max}/k_{\min})\log^2 n}{m k_{\mathrm{ave}}}}$ when $m k_{\mathrm{ave}} \ge c(n\log n + k_{\max}^2\log n)$ (Section 2, Theorem 2, Corollary 1, Section 4.2, Lemmas 1–3, Claim 1).

Application #claim-full-rank-breaking-weighted-composite-likelihood-minimax-optimality
Whereas Independence-Preserving Breaking (Definition 4, Theorem 3) can extract at most $\lfloor k_j/2\rfloor$ independent pairs per $k_j$-ranking, Full Breaking (Definition 5, Equation 2) includes all $\binom{k_j}{2}$ pairs weighted by $1/(k_j-1)$ in $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FB}}(\theta) = \sum_{j=1}^m \frac{1}{k_j-1}\sum_{(i,i')\in\sigma_j}\log\frac{e^{\theta_i}}{e^{\theta_i}+e^{\theta_{i'}}}$; using the Efron–Stein inequality to bound the dependent gradient norm $\|\nabla\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FB}}(\theta^*)\|_2 \le 4\sqrt{m k_{\mathrm{ave}}\log n}$ (Lemma 4) and Hessian curvature $\lambda_2(H_{\mathrm{FB}}(\theta)) \ge \frac{e^{2b}}{(1+e^{2b})^2}\lambda_2(L)$ (Lemma 5), Theorem 4 and Corollary 2 prove that $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{FB}}$ achieves the minimax-optimal $O(\sqrt{n\log n / (m k_{\mathrm{ave}})})$ error rate (Section 3, Theorems 3–4, Corollary 2, Section 4.4, Lemmas 4–5).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Both Theorem 2 (Plackett-Luce MLE) and Theorem 4 (Full Breaking) require the dynamic range parameter b = max_i |theta*_i| to be bounded, with upper bounds scaling exponentially in b as e^{4b} and (1+e^{2b})^2 / e^{2b} because extreme score gaps make informative upsets exponentially rare.
  • Full Breaking weights all (k_j choose 2) pairs from a k_j-ranking uniformly by 1/(k_j - 1) (Equation 2); when subset sizes k_j vary widely across rankings, unweighted pairwise pooling without the 1/(k_j - 1) normalization overweights large subsets and degrades estimation accuracy.
