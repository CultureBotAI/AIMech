Gaussian-process moment matching for Gaussian inputs

Algorithms AIMech:GaussianProcessGaussianInputMomentMatching DRAFT
Aliases: Gaussian input Gaussian process moment matching
A Gaussian-process prediction procedure that analytically integrates a fixed squared-exponential posterior over an independently prescribed Gaussian test-input distribution and represents the resulting latent prediction by a Gaussian matching its exact first two moments.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #mixture-moments
Let X have a prescribed Gaussian distribution independent of the latent GP posterior, conditional on the training data and fixed hyperparameters. Integrating p(f(x)|x,data) over this input law gives a generally non-Gaussian mixture. Its mean is E_X[m(X)] and its variance is E_X[v(X)]+Var_X[m(X)], where m and v are the fixed-input latent posterior mean and variance. Replacing this mixture by a Gaussian with those moments is a distributional approximation, even when both moments are evaluated exactly.
  • supports Source: Gaussian Process Priors with Uncertain Inputs Application to Multiple-Step Ahead Time Series Forecasting · Locator: Sections 3–3.1, Equations 5–7, PDF p. 3; exact moments, Equations 10–13, PDF p. 4
  • supports Source: On Simulation and Trajectory Prediction with Gaussian Process Dynamics · Locator: Sections 1–3, especially the dependence distinction on PDF pp. 1–4
Mechanism #squared-exponential-integrals
For the paper’s zero-mean, unit-amplitude squared-exponential GP with Gaussian observation noise, write beta=K_y^{-1}y, k_X for covariances between the training inputs and X, q=E[k_X], and Q=E[k_X k_X^T]. Then the latent predictive mean is q^T beta and variance is 1-tr(K_y^{-1}Q)+beta^T Q beta-(q^T beta)^2. Gaussian integration supplies closed forms for q and Q. For the one-dimensional unit-lengthscale kernel and X~N(mu,s), q_i=exp(-(mu-x_i)^2/(2(1+s)))/sqrt(1+s) and Q_ij=exp(-(x_i-x_j)^2/4-(mu-(x_i+x_j)/2)^2/(1+2s))/sqrt(1+2s), with s denoting input variance. The zero-input-variance limit recovers ordinary fixed-input prediction. These exact moment statements condition on the specified kernel, data and hyperparameters.
  • supports Source: Gaussian Process Priors with Uncertain Inputs Application to Multiple-Step Ahead Time Series Forecasting · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.2, Equations 1–4, PDF pp. 2–3; Section 3.1, Equations 10–13, PDF p. 4
Application #forecasting-extension
For iterative autoregressive forecasting, the source carries a joint Gaussian approximation to the lagged outputs, computes the next scalar output moments and its cross-covariances with the existing lags, and shifts the resulting covariance block. Full lag covariance is part of that construction; independently propagating only marginal variances is a different approximation. When the predicted quantity is a future noisy observation, add its independent observation-noise variance once to the new output diagonal. Gaussian moment closure and treating the current input distribution as independent of the uncertain GP function are additional assumptions; retained lag covariance alone does not make the complete shared-function trajectory posterior exact.
  • supports Source: Gaussian Process Priors with Uncertain Inputs Application to Multiple-Step Ahead Time Series Forecasting · Locator: Section 4, iterative forecasting and the unnumbered lagged covariance construction, PDF pp. 4–5
  • supports Source: On Simulation and Trajectory Prediction with Gaussian Process Dynamics · Locator: Introduction and Sections 2–3, PDF pp. 1–4
Scope #taylor-boundary
The earlier first-order posterior-mean and second-order posterior-variance Taylor construction approximates the same total-moment integrals locally; it is not the closed-form squared-exponential Gaussian integral. The technical report explicitly leaves exact evaluation of those moments to future work. Curator boundary calculations show why the distinction matters: with the unit kernel, an exact observation f(0)=0, input mean 1 and input variance 2, the local Taylor variance is 1-3/e<0, whereas the exact mixture variance is 1-exp(-1/5)/sqrt(5)>0. This deliberately nonlocal example is not a reproduction of the paper’s benchmark or a rejection of the approximation within its small-uncertainty regime.
  • supports Source: Gaussian Process Priors with Uncertain Inputs Application to Multiple-Step Ahead Time Series Forecasting · Locator: Section 3.1, Equations 8–9, PDF p. 3
  • supports Source: Gaussian Process priors with Uncertain Inputs: Multiple-Step-Ahead Prediction · Locator: Sections 3.3–3.4, Equations 16–22, PDF pp. 8–9; conclusions, PDF p. 17
Limitation #shared-function-limit
In a trajectory generated by a single uncertain GP dynamics function, later inputs generally depend on that same function. Repeated independent-input moment calculations do not preserve this dependence. Hewing and colleagues explicitly identify this issue in established GP trajectory prediction, including the Girard construction. A curator linear-kernel illustration uses f(x)=A*x with A~N(0,1) and x_0=1: reusing A gives x_2=A^2 with mean 1 and variance 2, while independently redrawing A at each step gives a product with mean 0 and variance 1. This illustrates a change in the modeled law, not a squared-exponential benchmark replication or a universal direction of variance error.
  • supports Source: On Simulation and Trajectory Prediction with Gaussian Process Dynamics · Locator: Introduction, persistent-versus-independent parameter examples and Gaussian trajectory discussion, PDF pp. 1–4

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Closed-form moments here concern the zero-mean squared-exponential GP and a prescribed Gaussian test-input distribution with fixed hyperparameters. Other kernels, non-Gaussian inputs, uncertain training locations or hyperparameter integration require further treatment. The method does not automatically estimate the input distribution or solve a general errors-in-variables problem.
  • Exact first and second moments do not make the mixture Gaussian or its Gaussian credible intervals calibrated. A unit-kernel f(0)=0 example in the curator calculator has a fourth moment different from that of the matched Gaussian. Input integration can also lower variance relative to evaluating the GP only at the input mean; uncertainty is not guaranteed to increase at every step.
  • Iterative lag covariance and observation noise must be handled consistently. A singular Gaussian input covariance can occur when initial lags are exact; Gaussian expectations have well-defined degenerate limits, but an implementation must not blindly invert that input covariance. Noise in training K_y and future observation noise have different roles.
  • The earlier Taylor report is contextual evidence for a distinct local approximation. Its notation changes the meaning of kernel width parameters between sections. The proceedings text also contains a lengthscale irrelevance statement inconsistent with its displayed kernel and ambiguous trace/parenthesis typography; the record uses the total-variance identity and directly checked scalar integrals instead of reproducing those ambiguities.
  • The source’s Mackey–Glass and pH illustrations do not establish universal forecasting superiority or calibration. The proceedings pH test count is printed as an unresolved placeholder; the earlier technical report has different preprocessing and results, so its count is not silently transferred. No comparative numerical performance claim is curated.
  • No author implementation, fitted GP, stochastic trajectory simulation or benchmark was executed. Curator analytic and numerical-quadrature checks establish only the displayed mathematical examples. The record remains DRAFT after author self-review; correlation-aware trajectory methods require their own identity and implementation-scope assessments.
