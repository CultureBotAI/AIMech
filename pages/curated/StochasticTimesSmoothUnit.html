Stochastic-times-smooth unit

Mathematical Methods AIMech:StochasticTimesSmoothUnit DRAFT
Aliases: STS unit
A stochastic activation that sets p=sigmoid(a), samples a Bernoulli gate with success probability sqrt(p), and outputs the gate multiplied by sqrt(p).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record covers Bengio et al.’s Section 2.2 STS construction and its smooth-amplitude training path. The Bernoulli gate and nonzero amplitude are both sqrt(p); it is neither an ordinary Bernoulli(p) output nor fixed-rate dropout. The source’s Figure 2 uses the reversed wording Smooth Times Stoch. for this experimental unit family. The identity is not the generic straight-through estimator or stochastic depth. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Compute p=sigmoid(a), draw b in {0,1} with P(b=1)=sqrt(p), and return h=b*sqrt(p). Conditional on a, E[h]=p, P(h>0)=sqrt(p), and the nonzero output is sqrt(p). Backpropagation through the smooth multiplier can hold the discrete gate fixed. This path does not include the derivative of the gate’s probability law: as a curator consequence of the displayed rule, its expected derivative for the loss h is p-prime/2, while d E[h]/d a=p-prime. Proposition 2 gives an asymptotic relationship E[f(h)]=f(p)+o(sqrt(p)) as p tends to zero for the stated differentiable-function setting; it does not state equality at a finite activation probability.
  • supports Source: Estimating or Propagating Gradients Through Stochastic Neurons for Conditional Computation · Locator: Section 2.2, Equation (2), Proposition 2 and proof, PDF/printed p.5; Section 5 and Figure 2 naming context, p.8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The smooth-amplitude backward path is not generally an unbiased gradient of the expected stochastic loss. An estimator for changes in the gate probability would require an additional treatment.
  • Matching the mean to p does not preserve a nonlinear downstream function. For example, at p=1/4 the second moment is 1/8 while the squared mean is 1/16; this is a curator calculation from the source’s distribution.
  • The asymptotic small-p statement is not a uniform finite-p error bound, a guarantee about optimization, or a theorem that the stochastic network equals a deterministic sigmoid network.
  • The conditional-computation motivation does not establish a universal speed or accuracy improvement. No author implementation or trained model was run.
