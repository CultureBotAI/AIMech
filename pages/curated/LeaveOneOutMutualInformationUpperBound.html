Leave-one-out mutual information upper bound

Mathematical Methods AIMech:LeaveOneOutMutualInformationUpperBound DRAFT
Aliases: Leave-one-out variational information upper bound
An expectation-level upper bound on mutual information that compares a known conditional density for each paired sample with a mixture of conditional densities from the other independent inputs in its batch.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the leave-one-out upper bound in Poole et al., distinct from InfoNCE’s lower bound and from generic leave-one-out cross-validation. It requires evaluable normalized p(y|x), which may be the encoder defining the modeled joint distribution; an arbitrary learned critic does not supply that requirement. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Draw K>=2 independent pairs (X_i,Y_i) from p(x)p(y|x), with evaluable normalized conditional density p(y|x). For each i form q_-i(y)=(K−1)^−1 sum_(j!=i) p(y|X_j). Then I(X;Y)<=E[K^−1 sum_i log(p(Y_i|X_i)/q_-i(Y_i))], assuming the expectations are well-defined. The mixture q_-i is independent of the held-out pair because it uses only the other inputs. Conditional on those inputs, the gap of the corresponding variational upper bound is KL(p(Y)||q_-i(Y)), a nonnegative quantity. Including the paired input in this denominator instead gives the known-conditional InfoNCE lower-bound construction, so the exclusion is essential. A finite batch computes an estimator of the upper-bound expectation rather than a guaranteed upper bound on its own.
  • supports Source: On Variational Bounds of Mutual Information · Locator: Section 2.5, PDF p. 5, Equations (12)–(13), Leave one out upper bound; Section 2.1 p. 2, Equation (1), variational upper-bound identity

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The conditional must be normalized and evaluable for cross-sample inputs. Treating an uncalibrated score as p(y|x) invalidates the stated derivation.
  • For small batches or sharply separated conditional supports, the leave-one-out mixture can be tiny or zero where the held-out conditional is positive, producing a loose or infinite upper bound.
  • Independence of batch examples is part of the proof. Correlated samples or using the held-out input to construct its own denominator require a different argument.
  • The expected bound does not certify a realized finite-sample number. Optimization of a noisy estimate does not guarantee monotonic true information reduction or convergence. No encoder implementation or benchmark was reproduced.
