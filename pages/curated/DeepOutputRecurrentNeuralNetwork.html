Deep Output Recurrent Neural Network

Architectures AIMech:DeepOutputRecurrentNeuralNetwork DRAFT
Aliases: DO-RNN
A recurrent neural architecture whose hidden-to-output function uses a multilayer perceptron with intermediate nonlinear feedforward layers to compute an output from the current recurrent hidden state.

DO-RNN is defined by a feedforward MLP hidden-to-output map in the inspected paper. DOT further requires a deep transition, while DT alone does not require deep output. DO is not an exclusive DO-only benchmark configuration, a conditional generative output model or a separate recurrent hidden stack.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #multilayer-output-map
The current hidden state enters a sequence of learned feedforward transformations and nonlinearities before the final output transformation. The paper’s formal DO-RNN description deepens the output function rather than requiring additional recurrent state variables. The generic output notation does not by itself guarantee normalized probabilities; the final output function and task determine that interpretation.
  • supports Source: How to Construct Deep Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 2, PDF p. 2, Equation 2; Sections 3.2.2 and 3.3.2, pp. 3–5, unnumbered MLP output formula
Scope #combinable-depth-scope
The deep-output criterion does not require a shallow state transition. Combining it with a deep transition yields DOT-RNN, whose illustrated version has one intermediate layer in each map. Recurrent stacking and transition depth remain separate compatible choices. The paper discusses conditional generative output models as related alternatives but restricts its own construction to feedforward intermediate layers.
  • supports Source: How to Construct Deep Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Sections 3.2.2–3.3.3, PDF pp. 3–6, Figure 2(c) and formal descriptions
Scope #state-information-and-gradient-boundary
A deeper deterministic output map can change predictions and the gradients propagated from the output loss while leaving a fixed hidden-state transition unchanged. It adds no recurrent state on its own and cannot distinguish two histories already mapped to the same hidden state. The suggested benefit of a more compact or disentangled state is a motivation, not a guarantee that arbitrary lost historical information is recovered.
  • supports Source: How to Construct Deep Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Section 3.2.2, PDF p. 3; Section 3.3.2, p. 5; constructed fixed-transition, output-derivative and state-collision examples
Limitation #combined-evaluation-limits
The experimental tables compare shallow RNN, DT(S), combined DOT(S) and stacked RNN; they do not contain a separate DO-only model. DOT(S) results consequently do not establish an isolated benefit from output depth. The main combined model is worse than the shallow RNN on JSB Chorales, while a separate starred music setting changes units, dropout and pretraining together. Reported language-model gains remain conditional on the combined architecture and its unequal training and capacity choices.
  • supports Source: How to Construct Deep Recurrent Neural Networks · Locator: Sections 5.1–5.3, PDF pp. 7–9, Tables 1–3; Discussion, p. 10

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The definition is scoped to the paper’s feedforward deep-output construction, not every conditional generative output model or every later use of deep output terminology.
  • Deep output, deep transition and stacking are compatible properties; DOT is a more specifically constrained combination, not an exact alias for DO.
  • No DO-only empirical comparison is reported in the inspected tables. Output depth alone provides no new recurrent state or universal performance and information-recovery guarantee.
  • The complete 13-page primary PDF was visually read and reused with its original access time and hash. Constructed examples are not author-code or benchmark replication; DRAFT remains.
