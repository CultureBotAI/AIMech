Recursive best-first search with controlled re-expansion

Algorithms AIMech:RBFSControlledReexpansion DRAFT
Aliases: RBFS_CR, RBFS with bounded overhead
An RBFS variant that estimates subtree evaluation-value distributions and inflates backed-up values to make later visits expand geometrically more nodes, while retaining original lower-bound information for solution-cost pruning.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope RBFS_CR is the histogram-based controlled re-expansion method of Hatem, Kiesel and Ruml. It is distinct from their fixed-tolerance RBFS-epsilon and weighted-root RBFS-kth-root heuristics. Its inflated ordering can depart from strict best-first order in the original evaluation values. No normalized identity match was found across the 442 AIO and 124 prior native records; ignored-inclusive text hits in the main checkout were prior discovery leads, source citations and search-order contrasts, plus incidental dependency substrings, not existing concept records.

Mechanism #search-procedure
Maintain original backed-up values F and additional inflated values F_CR. Histograms of generated but pruned evaluation values guide the inflation so the next visit is estimated to perform geometrically more work. Use inflated values to control traversal and backtracking, and original bounds for branch-and-bound after an incumbent solution is available.

Limitation #conditional-bound
The overhead theorem relies on explicit distribution and extra-node assumptions. The paper also explains that relaxing original best-first order is intentional and that its Dockyard experiment uses an unlimited transposition table; those experiments do not demonstrate the memory bound of a path-only implementation.
  • supports Source: Recursive Best-First Search with Bounded Overhead · Locator: Assumptions 1–3, PDF p. 4; Theorem 2, PDF p. 5; Dockyard Robot Planning and Conclusion, PDF p. 6

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The published O(N) expansion analysis is conditional on accurate subtree histograms, enough counts to choose appropriate backups, and a bound of O(N) on extra unique nodes beyond optimal solution cost. N is the specified A-star search-tree expansion count without duplicate pruning and with worst-case ties.
  • The implementation’s finite histogram buckets estimate distributions; they do not automatically satisfy the analysis’s exact-distribution assumption on every problem.
  • The paper retains one histogram per current-path node. Its depth-linear storage argument assumes bounded per-node histogram and successor storage; an added unbounded transposition table changes the memory scope.
  • Liberal thresholds require continuing branch-and-bound after finding an incumbent to establish optimality under admissible bounds. The first encountered solution is not automatically certified optimal.
  • The record does not certify the pseudocode as a complete executable implementation or claim universal speed improvement.
