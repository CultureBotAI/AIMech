Orthogonal Score and Weighted Double-Selection Post-Selection Inference in High-Dimensional Quantile Regression

Algorithms AIMech:OrthogonalScoreAndWeightedDoubleSelectionPostSelectionInferenceInHighDimensionalQuantileRegression DRAFT
Aliases: Orthogonal Score Quantile Regression Estimator, Weighted Double-Selection Quantile Regression, Belloni-Chernozhukov-Kato Post-Selection Quantile Inference, Debiased Post-Selection Quantile Regression
A post-selection inference methodology (introduced by Alexandre Belloni, Victor Chernozhukov, and Kengo Kato in 2019) for a target treatment or regression coefficient $\alpha_\tau$ in high-dimensional approximately sparse conditional quantile regression models $y_i = d_i\alpha_\tau + x_i'\beta_\tau + \epsilon_i$ with $\mathrm{P}(\epsilon_i \le 0 \mid d_i, z_i) = \tau$ and $p \gg n$ control basis functions $x_i = X(z_i)$ without beta-min separation assumptions. To immunize inference against model-selection mistakes in the nuisance vector $\beta_\tau$, the method constructs the Neyman-orthogonal quantile score $\psi_i(\alpha, \beta, v_i) = (\tau - \mathbb{I}\{y_i \le d_i\alpha + x_i'\beta\})v_i$ using the conditional-density-weighted residual instrument $v_i = f_\tau(0 \mid z_i, d_i)(d_i - x_i'\theta_\tau)$, where $f_\tau(0 \mid z_i, d_i)$ is the conditional error density at zero and $\theta_\tau = \arg\min_\theta \mathrm{E}[f_\tau^2(0 \mid z_i, d_i)(d_i - x_i'\theta)^2]$ solves the density-weighted projection of $d_i$ onto $x_i$ so that $\partial_\beta \mathrm{E}[\psi_i(\alpha_\tau, \beta_\tau, v_i)] = 0$ and $\partial_{\theta}\mathrm{E}[\psi_i(\alpha_\tau, \beta_\tau, v_i)] = 0$ (Section 2.1, Equations 2.6–2.9). Given the outcome $\ell_1$-penalized quantile regression support $\widehat{S}_y$ (Equation 2.11), conditional density estimates $\widehat{f}_i$ via difference quotients of local quantile regressions (Equations 2.15–2.16), and the heteroscedastic Lasso auxiliary support $\widehat{S}_d = \operatorname{supp}(\widehat{\theta}_\tau)$ of $\widehat{f}_i d_i$ on $\widehat{f}_i x_i$ (Equation 2.12), the *Orthogonal Score Estimator* $\widehat{\alpha}_\tau$ (Algorithm 2.1, p. 8) refits $\widetilde{\beta}_\tau$ on $\widecheck{T} \supseteq \widehat{S}_y \cup \widehat{S}_d$ and minimizes the self-normalized criterion $L_n(\alpha) = |\mathrm{E}_n[\widehat{\psi}_i(\alpha)]|^2 / \mathrm{E}_n[\widehat{\psi}_i^2(\alpha)]$, while the *Weighted Double-Selection Quantile Estimator* $\widecheck{\alpha}_\tau$ (Algorithm 2.2, p. 9) solves the $\widehat{f}_i$-weighted quantile regression $(\widecheck{\alpha}_\tau, \widecheck{\beta}_\tau) \in \arg\min_{(\alpha, \beta): \operatorname{supp}(\beta) \subseteq \widecheck{T}} \mathrm{E}_n[\widehat{f}_i\rho_\tau(y_i - d_i\alpha - x_i'\beta)]$, with both estimators sharing the first-order Bahadur representation $\sigma_n^{-1}\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\alpha}_\tau - \alpha_\tau) = \sigma_n^{-1}\sqrt{n}(\widecheck{\alpha}_\tau - \alpha_\tau) + o_P(1) \xrightarrow{d} N(0, 1)$ uniformly over approximately sparse models (Theorem 1, p. 14).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Resolved against all 442 pinned AIO concepts and 669 maintained AIMech concepts. Distinct from `AIMech:L1PenalizedAndPostPenalizedQuantileRegressionProcessInHighDimensionalSparseModels` (Belloni & Chernozhukov 2011, which establishes estimation rates for single-equation $\ell_1$-QR and Post-$\ell_1$-QR processes under restricted nonlinearity but does not construct Neyman-orthogonal scores, density-weighted auxiliary Lasso projections $\widehat{f}_i d_i$ on $\widehat{f}_i x_i$, or valid post-selection confidence intervals without beta-min conditions), distinct from `AIMech:PostDoubleSelectionInferenceForHighDimensionalTreatmentEffects` (Belloni, Chernozhukov, & Hansen 2014, which performs unweighted least-squares double selection for conditional mean models whereas unweighted double selection in heteroscedastic quantile regression violates Neyman orthogonality $\mathrm{E}[f_\tau(0 \mid z_i, d_i)(d_i - x_i'\vartheta)x_i] \ne 0$ when $f_\tau$ varies across observations), and distinct from `AIMech:SmoothlyClippedAbsoluteDeviationAndOracleHighDimensionalQuantileRegression` (Wu & Liu 2009 / Wang, Wu, & Li 2012, which rely on folded-concave penalties and beta-min signal separation).

Mechanism #density-weighted-instrument-neyman-orthogonality-and-semiparametric-efficiency
For the quantile score $\psi_i(\alpha, \beta, v_i) = (\tau - \mathbb{I}\{y_i \le d_i\alpha + x_i'\beta + r_{i\tau}\})v_i$, the Gateaux derivative with respect to the nuisance vector $\beta$ at $(\alpha_\tau, \beta_\tau)$ is $-\mathrm{E}[f_\tau(0 \mid z_i, d_i)v_i x_i]$. Setting $v_i = w_{i\tau}(d_i - x_i'\theta_\tau)$ with minimax weight $w_{i\tau} = f_\tau(0 \mid z_i, d_i)$ and $\theta_\tau = \arg\min_\theta \mathrm{E}[f_\tau^2(0 \mid z_i, d_i)(d_i - x_i'\theta)^2]$ (Section 2.1, pp. 6–7, Equations 2.7–2.9) satisfies the exact orthogonality condition $\mathrm{E}[f_\tau(0 \mid z_i, d_i)v_i x_i] = \mathrm{E}[f_\tau^2(0 \mid z_i, d_i)(d_i - x_i'\theta_\tau)x_i] = 0$ and minimizes the asymptotic variance $\sigma_\tau^2 = \tau(1-\tau)\mathrm{E}[v_i^2] / (\mathrm{E}[f_\tau(0 \mid z_i, d_i)d_i v_i])^2 = \tau(1-\tau) / \mathrm{E}[v_i^2]$ (Equation 2.6, Comment 2.2), whereas an unweighted residual $d_i - x_i'\vartheta$ fails orthogonality under heteroscedasticity unless multiplied by $f_\tau^{-1}(0 \mid z_i, d_i)$ at the cost of strictly larger asymptotic variance.

Mechanism #first-order-equivalence-of-orthogonal-score-and-weighted-double-selection-under-approximate-sparsity
Under Conditions R.1–R.6 (allowing $s^2\log^3(p \vee n) = o(n)$ and density estimation error $\bar{\mathcal{E}}_f^2\sqrt{s\log(p \vee n)} + s\bar{\mathcal{E}}_f\sqrt{\log(p \vee n)} = o(1)$, Section 3.1, pp. 13–14), Theorem 1 (Section 3.2, p. 14, Equation 3.22) proves that the Orthogonal Score Estimator $\widehat{\alpha}_\tau$ (Algorithm 2.1) and the Weighted Double-Selection Quantile Estimator $\widecheck{\alpha}_\tau$ (Algorithm 2.2, which refits $\widehat{f}_i$-weighted quantile regression on $\widecheck{T} = \widehat{S}_y \cup \widehat{S}_d$ without forming the score criterion $L_n(\alpha)$) satisfy the identical first-order Bahadur expansion $\sigma_n^{-1}\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\alpha}_\tau - \alpha_\tau) = \frac{\sigma_n^{-1}}{\mathrm{E}[v_i^2]}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^n \psi_i(\alpha_\tau, \beta_\tau, v_i) + o_P(1) \xrightarrow{d} N(0, 1)$ and $\sigma_n^{-1}\sqrt{n}(\widecheck{\alpha}_\tau - \alpha_\tau) = \frac{\sigma_n^{-1}}{\mathrm{E}[v_i^2]}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^n \psi_i(\alpha_\tau, \beta_\tau, v_i) + o_P(1) \xrightarrow{d} N(0, 1)$, so $\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\alpha}_\tau - \widecheck{\alpha}_\tau) = o_P(1)$.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Uniform asymptotic normality requires the sparsity and density-estimation rate conditions s^2 log^3(p v n) = o(n) and E_bar_f^2 sqrt(s log(p v n)) + s E_bar_f sqrt(log(p v n)) = o(1) (Condition R.6, p. 14), which are stronger than the s log p = o(n) rate needed for point estimation alone.
  • Unlike conditional mean regression, the orthogonal score psi_i is non-smooth in alpha, so the Orthogonal Score Estimator requires grid search or smooth approximation to minimize L_n(alpha), while the Weighted Double-Selection Estimator requires positive estimated conditional densities f_hat_i > 0.
  • Primary-source typographical audit of Belloni, Chernozhukov, & Kato (2019, arXiv:1312.7186v4) uncovered four mathematical and typographical issues that are corrected in this record: (1) Section 2.2 Eq. (2.16) (p. 11) writes the fourth-order conditional density estimator denominator as '(3/4){Q_hat(tau + h | z_i, d_i) - Q_hat(tau - h | z_i, d_i)} - (1/12){Q_hat(tau + 2h | z_i, d_i) - Q_hat(tau - 2h | z_i, d_i)}'; by Taylor expansion, (3/4)*(2h Q'(tau)) - (1/12)*(4h Q'(tau)) = (7/6) h Q'(tau) != h Q'(tau) (yielding an inconsistent limit (6/7) f_tau) and (3/4)*(1/3 h^3 Q'''(tau)) - (1/12)*(8/3 h^3 Q'''(tau)) = (1/36) h^3 Q'''(tau) != 0, whereas the exact 4th-order Richardson coefficient on {Q_hat(tau + h) - Q_hat(tau - h)} is 2/3 since (2/3)*2h - (1/12)*4h = h and (2/3)*(1/3)h^3 - (1/12)*(8/3)h^3 = 0; (2) Section 1 p. 5 line 11 spells 'heteroscedsaticity'; (3) Supplementary Appendix Section 1.2 p. 4 (PDF p. 31) spells 'large sample sieve estimatin'; and (4) Supplementary Appendix Section 2.2 p. 11 (PDF p. 38) writes 'To estimate theta_{theta tau} and v_i' with a duplicated subscript 'theta' instead of 'theta_tau'.
