Variational Expectation Maximization

Algorithms AIMech:VariationalExpectationMaximization DRAFT
Aliases: Variational EM, VEM (variational expectation maximization)
A latent-variable model-fitting procedure that alternates optimization of a posterior approximation within a specified restricted family and optimization of model parameters using that approximation, targeting an evidence lower bound.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes variational EM with point-estimated model parameters and an explicitly restricted latent-posterior family, as analyzed by Gunawardana-Byrne. It is not all variational inference, an inference-only fit with fixed model parameters, fully Bayesian parameter inference, an amortized architecture, or a particular score/pathwise gradient estimator. Mean-field factorization is one family choice rather than the definition. Exact global coordinate solves are the clean projection realization; partial numerical solves need their own progress conditions. Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #restricted-e-and-parameter-m
Choose a normalized tractable family q_lambda(z) on latent states compatible with observed x and a model p_theta(x,z). At fixed theta optimize F(lambda,theta)=E_q[log p_theta(x,z)-log q_lambda(z)], equivalently minimize KL(q_lambda||p_theta(z|x)). Then, holding q fixed, maximize its expected complete-data log likelihood over theta and repeat. Exact coordinate optima give the source’s restricted projection formulation; checked partial ascent steps give a generalized realization. The latent family, model support and finite expectations must be specified.
  • supports Source: Convergence Theorems for Generalized Alternating Minimization Procedures · Locator: Sections2.1-2.3, pp2052-2056 / PDF4-8; Section5, pp2065-2067 / PDF17-19
Scope #approximation-gap
The lower bound equals log p_theta(x)-KL(q_lambda||p_theta(z|x)). Bound ascent therefore does not generally imply observed-likelihood ascent or exact posterior recovery. At a differentiable interior fixed pair with a stationary parameter M-step, the observed log-likelihood gradient equals the theta gradient of that KL gap with q fixed. A zero gap is sufficient for the usual touching-bound argument, but zero gradient of the gap can also occur when the gap is positive; stationary error is not necessarily a local error minimum.
  • supports Source: Convergence Theorems for Generalized Alternating Minimization Procedures · Locator: Section3 decomposition, p2060 / PDF12; Section5 stationary-error discussion, pp2065-2067 / PDF17-19
Limitation #inexact-posterior-can-be-stationary
The source’s broad claim that an independence approximation permits likelihood stationarity only in an exact-family degenerate case is not adopted as a general impossibility theorem. In the saved normalized counterexample, p_theta(y,z)=Bernoulli_theta(y)*r(z), fixed correlated r has probabilities(0.3,0.2,0.2,0.3), and the unique best product approximation is uniform. Its positive KL gap is independent of theta, so variational EM still attains the observed-data likelihood optimum. This qualifies the general prose without refuting the conditional GAM theorem.
  • supports Source: Convergence Theorems for Generalized Alternating Minimization Procedures · Locator: Section5 broad independence-approximation argument, pp2066-2067 / PDF18-19; curator finite counterexample
Limitation #solver-and-theorem-boundaries
A mean-field fixed-point equation alone does not establish the global restricted E minimizer. The GAM convergence assumptions, including compactness, continuity, closed maps and sufficient strict progress, remain necessary for the cited conditional result. The printed fixed-q Boltzmann KL derivative has the opposite sign: the correct derivative is model moment minus q moment, although setting either sign to zero gives the same moment equation. Marginal independence of one pair does not alone imply a zero direct interaction when other latent paths can cancel its marginal association.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A restricted variational optimum is not automatically a stationary point of observed likelihood, a local maximum, a global optimum or an accurate posterior. Conversely, approximation error alone does not preclude likelihood stationarity.
  • The broad family accommodates structured approximations; it does not equate the existing factorial-HMM procedure, BBVI, NVIL or topic models. No original Jordan1999 chapter or uninspected implementation is claimed read.
  • No historical software, benchmark or source experiment was reproduced. Saved normalized finite calculations challenge bounded formula/prose claims, not the complete conditional convergence theorems.
