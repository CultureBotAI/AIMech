Structured variational inference for factorial HMMs

Algorithms AIMech:StructuredVariationalInferenceForFactorialHMMs DRAFT
Aliases: Structured mean-field inference for factorial HMMs
A variational inference procedure for factorial hidden Markov models that approximates the posterior by independent chain-path distributions while retaining temporal dependence within each chain.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the chain-structured approximation and additive-Gaussian updates in Ghahramani–Jordan Section 3.5 and Appendix D. It is narrower than all structured variational inference and distinct from the factorial HMM model, a fully factorized state-by-time approximation, exact joint-state forward–backward, Gibbs sampling, or BBVI score-function optimization. It requires neither an amortized recognition network nor a variational autoencoder architecture. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For fixed model parameters and observations, choose q(S)=product_m q_m(s_1:T^m) and optimize the evidence lower bound E_q[log p(Y,S)-log q(S)], equivalently minimizing KL(q||p(S|Y)). Each chain factor is globally normalized over its path and retains the model’s initial and transition factors multiplied by positive unary potentials h_t^m. In the additive Gaussian model, h_t^m is proportional to exp((W_m)^T C^-1 [y_t-sum_(l!=m) W_l E_q[s_t^l]] - 0.5 diag((W_m)^T C^-1 W_m)), componentwise over the chain’s states. With other chain factors fixed, run forward–backward using these unary factors to update that chain’s state and temporal-pair moments, then repeat across chains. Same-time moments between different chains factorize under q; within-chain temporal moments do not. Such exact single-chain coordinate updates increase or preserve the bound for fixed model parameters. The moments may also supply a variational E-step for model fitting, but this approximate posterior does not become the exact posterior merely by convergence.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The approximation removes posterior dependence between chains even when the shared observation creates explaining-away effects. It preserves each chain’s time dependence, unlike a fully factorized state-by-time distribution.
  • The h factors are positive unnormalized potentials, not row-normalized transition probabilities or already-normalized state posteriors. Normalization and initial/boundary conditions belong to the complete chain computation.
  • The residual/diagonal formula depends on additive Gaussian emissions with common covariance. General emissions require their own expected-log-factor calculations; the formula is not an arbitrary-model black-box update.
  • Coordinate-ascent guarantees require an exact update of one factor while holding the others fixed. Simultaneous arbitrary updates, local stopping criteria or approximate model steps do not establish a global optimum, exact evidence, or universal likelihood improvement.
  • Appendix C Equation C.9 displays prior/transition signs inconsistent with its energy definition and derivative; that display is not adopted. Appendix D cancels explicit prior/transition log terms when the same factors are retained, but its moments and normalizer still depend on those factors; the accompanying independence wording is not literal total independence.
  • The general product-of-path-distributions variational family contains independent-time factors, but the displayed fixed-transition/unary parameterization and local optimization do not justify claiming every run outperforms a fully factorized approximation. No benchmark or author implementation is certified.
