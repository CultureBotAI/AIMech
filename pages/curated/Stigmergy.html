Stigmergy

Coordination Mechanisms AIMech:Stigmergy DRAFT
A computational coordination mechanism in which actions modify an accessible environment or medium and those changes influence subsequent actions, allowing information to pass through the evolving state rather than requiring direct communication between the participating agents.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Selects environment-mediated coordination through action-produced state changes, as qualified by Heylighen 2016 and the computational treatment in Dorigo et al. 1999. It is a mechanism, distinct from the broader swarm design paradigm and from individual ant algorithms. The general mechanism does not require learning or beneficial coordination. Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #action-trace-response
An action changes the medium, leaving a perceivable result that can trigger or modify a subsequent action. The defining relationship is the mediated action–trace–response loop; observing similar behavior without that link does not establish the mechanism.
  • supports Source: Stigmergy as a universal coordination mechanism I: Definition and components · Locator: Sections 2–3, PDF pp. 3–4 / printed pp. 6–7, Figure 1 and the medium definition.
Mechanism #computational-local-state
The 1999 artificial-stigmergy construction associates state variables with problem states and gives artificial agents local access to their values. Agents modify these variables during activity, allowing later behavior to respond to changes left by other activity.
  • supports Source: Ant Algorithms for Discrete Optimization · Locator: Introduction, PDF p. 4 / printed p. 140, artificial-agent extension through problem-state variables and local access.
Scope #action-coordination-scope
The 2016 formulation allows successive actions of one agent as well as actions of different agents, and allows an internal medium. Its medium must be affected by the relevant actions and perceptible as conditions for further action; this is the later author’s stated scope.
  • supports Source: Stigmergy as a universal coordination mechanism I: Definition and components · Locator: Section 3, PDF pp. 3–4 / printed pp. 6–7, single-agent scope and controllable/perceivable internal or external medium.
Mechanism #feedback-forms
The 2016 account distinguishes responses that reduce perceived deviations from responses that amplify an emerging activity. These negative- and positive-feedback descriptions identify possible coordination dynamics; they do not specify one universal response law or guarantee convergence.
  • supports Source: Stigmergy as a universal coordination mechanism I: Definition and components · Locator: Sections 6–7, PDF pp. 7–8 / printed pp. 10–11, negative and positive feedback discussions.
Application #aco-instantiation-boundary
In the inspected ACO formulation, ants read and write local numerical pheromone information that influences later decisions. Pheromone deposition, usually evaporation, private ant memory and problem-dependent transition rules belong to that implementation framework; they are not universal requirements of the broader coordination mechanism.
  • supports Source: Ant Algorithms for Discrete Optimization · Locator: Section 2.1, PDF pp. 5–6 / printed pp. 141–142, especially pheromone trail and stigmergy, state-transition policy, and additional artificial-ant characteristics.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The original Grassé 1959 full text was not inspected. Its reported definition and historical role are mediated by the later sources and publisher metadata. The 2016 author’s broader formulation is not presented as independently verified original wording or a priority finding.
  • The 2016 display P(action|condition)>P(action) is not by itself a causal-identification criterion. For example, a common cause can determine both indicators and produce this inequality without the condition causing the action. The selected identity requires the actual mediating action–trace–response relationship.
  • Shared access and aligned goals alone do not prove convergence. In a hand example, two agents observing the same real state x each propose the individually corrective increment −x; simultaneous application gives x_next=−x and a two-cycle for x≠0. Scheduling and response magnitudes require additional assumptions.
  • The general mechanism does not fix a complete algorithm: trace representation, visibility, persistence, write conflicts, response timing, agent capabilities and stopping conditions depend on an implementation. A model must specify these before claims about its behavior or executable reproduction can be assessed.
  • Broad source statements about perfect robustness, no wasted effort or unlimited scale are not adopted as universal guarantees. Access to a medium can have communication, storage, contention or processing costs. Indirect information exchange is not absence of information exchange or a guarantee of zero overhead.
  • Pheromone reinforcement, evaporation, rewards, optimization objectives and learning are not mandatory for every instance. Positive feedback can also concentrate behavior prematurely. ACO, AntNet, telephone routing and other particular applications need their own evidence and do not make the general mechanism a reinforcement-learning task.
  • Evidence scope is the complete ten-page 2016 article and the defining first six pages of the 36-page 1999 article, with selected method visuals. No complete audit of all cited biological experiments, all ACO variants, universal theorem, author implementation or benchmark reproduction is asserted.
