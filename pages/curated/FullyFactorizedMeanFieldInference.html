Fully factorized mean-field inference

Mathematical Methods AIMech:FullyFactorizedMeanFieldInference DRAFT
Aliases: Naive mean-field inference, Fully factorized variational inference
A variational inference method that restricts its approximating distribution to a product of single-variable factors and adjusts those factors to reduce the approximation-to-target Kullback–Leibler divergence.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the complete-factorizability mean-field construction, not a claim that the target variables are actually independent. It is the singleton-factor limit of structured mean field and differs from block or chain factors that retain dependencies. The approximation family and variational objective do not prescribe BBVI’s score-function estimator, amortized recognition, a training algorithm or any particular probabilistic model. Factorization depends on the declared variable units; treating a whole chain as one variable would be a different, structured partition at the state-variable level. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a fixed target p(s)=exp(F(s))/Z, choose q(s)=product_i q_i(s_i) and maximize E_q[F]+H(q), equivalently minimizing KL(q||p). For the source’s binary s_i in {-1,+1} and F(s)=sum_(i<j) J_ij*s_i*s_j+sum_i h_i*s_i, write q_i(s_i)=(1+m_i*s_i)/2. Distinct-variable moments factor as E_q[s_i*s_j]=m_i*m_j, and entropy is the sum of the binary factor entropies. Interior stationarity gives atanh(m_i)=h_i+sum_(j!=i) J_ij*m_j, with symmetric interaction indexing. Updating one mean to tanh of that effective field while holding the others fixed solves its coordinate subproblem. These equations characterize the chosen approximate inference problem; they do not compute the exact target marginals in general.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The product family discards all dependence among the declared individual variables. Matching or optimizing its means does not certify correct covariance, calibrated uncertainty or exact posterior recovery.
  • KL(q||p) is directional and differs from KL(p||q). A lower bound equals log Z only when the approximating distribution equals the target almost everywhere under appropriate support conditions.
  • Stationarity equations do not ensure global optimality. Exact sequential singleton updates can increase the bound, while synchronous fixed-point iteration can decrease it or fail to converge.
  • The tanh equations apply to the stated binary pairwise energy and parameter convention. Other state spaces, higher-order factors or restricted factor families need their own expectations and updates.
  • Using approximate hidden statistics in learning is a further choice; the approximation alone does not establish monotonic observed likelihood. No model fitting, author implementation or empirical accuracy claim is included.
