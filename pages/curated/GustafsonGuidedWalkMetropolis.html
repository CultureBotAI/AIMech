Gustafson guided-walk Metropolis sampler

Algorithms AIMech:GustafsonGuidedWalkMetropolis DRAFT
A continuous-state Metropolis sampler that augments a univariate state with a binary direction, proposes a half-normal displacement along that direction, retains direction after acceptance and reverses it after rejection.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #direction-augmented-half-normal-proposal
For a proper univariate target with density pi, retain state (x,p), where p is either −1 or +1, with augmented target pi(x)/2. At a current point with positive target density, draw z from N(0,sigma²) for a fixed sigma>0 and propose y=x+p*abs(z). The sign selects the proposal direction while the absolute Gaussian draw supplies its random magnitude; target derivatives are not used.
  • supports Source: A guided walk Metropolis algorithm · Locator: Section 2 and Section 3, printed p. 358 / PDF p. 2, continuous univariate setting and displayed guided-walk proposal
Mechanism #acceptance-and-rejection-direction-rules
Accept with probability min(1,pi(y)/pi(x)). On acceptance the new state is (y,p); on rejection it is (x,−p). Thus accepted moves retain direction, while rejection retains the position and reverses the direction for the next proposal. There is no additional random sign refresh in this displayed univariate kernel. An unknown common normalizing constant in the target density cancels from the ratio.
  • supports Source: A guided walk Metropolis algorithm · Locator: Section 3, printed p. 358 / PDF p. 2, displayed guided-walk transition and immediately following explanation
Mechanism #two-stage-invariance-construction
The transition can be decomposed into a Metropolis–Hastings stage proposing (x+p*abs(z),−p), followed by unconditional negation of the resulting sign. The first proposal is symmetric on the augmented space and preserves the target under the Metropolis correction; the second operation preserves the uniform sign factor. Their composition therefore preserves pi(x)/2. This argument establishes invariance, not a general convergence rate or ergodicity theorem for every target and tuning choice.
  • supports Source: A guided walk Metropolis algorithm · Locator: Section 3, printed p. 359 / PDF p. 3, first paragraph; introduction continuation, printed p. 358 / PDF p. 2
Scope #univariate-and-componentwise-scope
The paper applies univariate invariant updates in turn to full conditional distributions and studies componentwise guided walks for multivariate targets. This record’s operational kernel is the displayed univariate update. The separate simultaneous-vector variant mentioned in the discussion includes direction perturbation and is not specified by the same univariate formula alone; it is not asserted as an exact alias or complete implementation here.
  • supports Source: A guided walk Metropolis algorithm · Locator: Introduction continuation, printed p. 358 / PDF p. 2; Examples 2–4, printed pp. 359–363 / PDF pp. 3–7; Section 5, printed p. 363 / PDF p. 7, simultaneous-vector discussion
Limitation #evidence-and-performance-boundaries
The paper evaluates synthetic targets and distinguishes its continuous guided walk from related finite-state theory. Its reported convergence experiment uses entry into a selected target ellipsoid as a criterion, rather than a general proof of distributional convergence. The paper also assigns the best acceptance-rate labels inconsistently between Example 2 and Example 4. These observations support keeping the mechanism separate from claims of universal efficiency, convergence or an optimal acceptance rate.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The definition is restricted to the displayed continuous univariate half-normal proposal and fixed positive scale. Other displacement laws, adaptive scales and simultaneous vector proposals require their own evidence and validity conditions.
  • The current point must have positive target density and the acceptance calculation must be defined. If an outside-support proposal has zero target density, the displayed formula rejects it and flips the sign; it does not specify clipping, resampling or a reflected proposal. This is the formula’s support consequence, not a separate boundary theorem.
  • The paper gives no general target-regularity and ergodicity theorem for this continuous kernel. The finite positive-target, nonzero-sign-randomization conditions in the related DHN construction cannot be transferred automatically.
  • The method shares persistent-direction lifting with the DHN finite fiber sampler, but uses random continuous displacement and deterministic rejection reversal without its additional theta sign randomization. It is not an exact alias of the finite fiber construction, ordinary random-walk Metropolis or Hamiltonian Monte Carlo.
  • The inspected source is the 1998 journal-layout paper obtained through a public mirror. Exact byte equivalence to a publisher download or to the 1997 preprint cited by DHN was not established. No numerical performance or runtime claim is made.
