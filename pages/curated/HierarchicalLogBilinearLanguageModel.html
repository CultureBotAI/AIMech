Hierarchical log-bilinear language model

Architectures AIMech:HierarchicalLogBilinearLanguageModel DRAFT
Aliases: HLBL
A next-word language architecture that computes one linear context feature vector and uses its dot products with internal-node vectors to assign normalized vocabulary-tree probabilities, summing path probabilities when a word has multiple codes.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is Mnih and Hinton’s 2008 HLBL architecture, not a synonym for every hierarchical language model or the hierarchical-softmax component. It combines a position-weighted linear context representation with learned node vectors. Flat LBL, Morin–Bengio’s node-conditioned nonlinear network, and the tree-building algorithm each have distinct scopes. One-code and multiple-code forms are explicitly part of the source’s model family. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Using the 2008 column-vector convention, form r_hat=sum_i C_i r_context_i once for the preceding words. At internal node j, assign left-branch probability sigmoid(r_hat^T q_j+b_j) and use its complement for the right branch. Multiply the decisions along a leaf code. With one leaf per word this is its probability; with multiple codes, sum all disjoint leaf probabilities assigned to that word. Optimize the penalized observed-word log likelihood. For multiple codes, differentiating the log of that sum weights each path gradient by its posterior share of the word’s probability; choosing one path or averaging their log losses is a different objective. The paper uses diagonal C_i to reduce context-transformation cost. Its data-driven trees bootstrap features from a random-tree model and represent each word by the mean predicted context feature at its occurrences before recursive clustering; balanced, adaptive and multiple-code tree policies are distinct choices.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The linear context feature is reusable across nodes; it does not include Morin–Bengio’s node-dependent tanh hidden computation. A diagonal transform is a restriction on the model parameters, not a requirement for the general probability construction.
  • The tree defines its own normalized distribution and need not reproduce flat-LBL probabilities for matching-looking parameters. Tree choice and finite context constrain the representation.
  • Target cost includes context computation plus the visited path nodes. Multiple codes require their summed probability and increase work; a general adaptive or full binary tree need not have logarithmic worst-case depth. Reporting every word probability requires at least linear output work.
  • Multiple codes need not represent distinct senses. The source’s discussion reports that replicated words were often rare or difficult to cluster and notes that averaging their contexts can lose multimodal usage structure.
  • The paper’s mean-code-length statistic sums lengths over all codes of a word before frequency weighting; treating it as the mean of that word’s code lengths would understate its reported evaluation work.
  • No universal semantic clustering quality, speedup, predictive advantage, convergence guarantee or author implementation behavior is asserted. The tree-building procedure is described as architectural context, not separately curated as an equivalent model.
