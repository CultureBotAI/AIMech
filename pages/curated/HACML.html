HAC-ML network clustering

Algorithms AIMech:HACML DRAFT
Aliases: HAC-ML
A greedy network-clustering algorithm that builds a hierarchical stochastic block structure by merging groups with the least loss of profiled network likelihood, using separate Bayesian criteria to collapse terminal groups and stop top-level merging.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #profile-likelihood-guide
For undirected, unweighted graphs without self edges, the method models edges and holes between vertex groups with Bernoulli probabilities. It begins with singleton groups and a full block model at the top level. At each step it selects a pair with greatest profiled-likelihood merge ratio, which depends on their connectivity to other groups. This greedy guide is neither a globally optimal tree search nor posterior averaging over trees.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Methods, PDF pp. 2–3, Equations 1–5 and greedy pseudocode; Conclusions, p. 9
Mechanism #bayesian-boundaries
Uniform-prior integration of a block probability gives a Beta factor. A separate integrated-probability merge ratio supplies the proposed top-level stopping rule. When both children are already collapsed, another integrated ratio compares a single terminal group with their separate within- and between-group structure. The paper explicitly omits multiplicity factors for alternative merges and splits; these are stated model-selection conventions rather than a posterior calculation over all partitions.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Methods, PDF pp. 2–4, Equations 2, 7–8 and model-selection qualifications
Mechanism #multiple-edge-types
For multiple interaction types, each type retains its own likelihood factors under a shared group structure. The type-specific likelihoods multiply, so their log scores add. This conditional independence and shared-structure construction differs from taking the union of all edges and does not guarantee that adding another network improves predictions.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Extension to multiple edge types, PDF p. 4; Synergy in mixed networks, pp. 8–9 and Table 3
Limitation #printed-score-boundary
The singleton mismatch expression printed in the prose is not numerically equal to the ratio obtained from Equations 2 and 5: one differing external adjacency gives a profiled ratio of one quarter, whereas the prose expression gives one half. The record uses the general profiled-likelihood construction and preserves this printed discrepancy. Its effect on author code or empirical results was not checked.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Methods, PDF pp. 2–3, Equations 2 and 5 and singleton-mismatch paragraph; curator calculation in research/discovery/hacml-boundaries.py
Scope #complexity-and-search
The paper contrasts a naive quartic implementation with a faster implementation using a priority queue, lazy evaluation and a restriction to candidate pairs sharing a neighbor. The stated faster complexity depends on those choices and network sparsity. Merge ties, excluded candidates and irreversible greedy decisions require implementation conventions; no universal global-optimum or posterior-coverage guarantee is claimed.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Greedy agglomerative algorithm, PDF pp. 3–4; Conclusions, p. 9
Scope #evaluation-scope
The link-prediction study hides observed edges and evaluates them against an equal-sized random set of holes, reporting rankings and maximum F scores. The authors acknowledge that this balanced test overstates practical performance on sparse networks. They prune isolated and degree-one vertices and restrict joint-network comparisons to shared vertices. Reported advantages are empirical across the inspected networks; Table 3 does not show a consistent benefit from combining interaction types.
  • supports Source: Resolving the structure of interactomes with hierarchical agglomerative clustering · Locator: Performance Evaluation and Results, PDF pp. 4–8, Tables 1–3 and Figures 1–4

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This is Park and Bader’s 2011 HAC-ML network procedure, not the generic family of hierarchical agglomerative clustering methods or a fitted interactome. The separate HAC-E, HAC-ES and HAC-Q scores are not aliases.
  • The input-network graph records observed interactions; it is not by itself a conditional-dependence graph, a causal graph or a Bayesian network. The grouping model assumes Bernoulli edge trials conditional on its structure.
  • The implementation complexity claim uses restricted candidate search. The printed top-level product notation and singleton-score shorthand do not provide a completely unambiguous executable specification; no author-code conformance is asserted.
  • Missing edges are used as holes for fitting and sampled negatives for evaluation, although biological data can contain unobserved true links. The balanced held-out test is not a calibrated estimate of deployment precision.
  • No author implementation, experimental dataset or supplementary archive was audited or executed. Finite curator calculations assess printed formulas and bounded examples, not benchmark replication or global optimality. The record remains DRAFT.
