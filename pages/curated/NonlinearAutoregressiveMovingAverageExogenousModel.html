Nonlinear autoregressive moving average model with exogenous inputs

Model Families AIMech:NonlinearAutoregressiveMovingAverageExogenousModel DRAFT
Aliases: NARMAX model
A discrete-time nonlinear input-output model family whose current output is a function of finite lagged outputs, exogenous inputs and errors, plus an additive current error.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #lagged-error-recurrence
In the strictly causal scalar form, y_t=F(y_{t−1},…,y_{t−ny},u_{t−1},…,u_{t−nu},e_{t−1},…,e_{t−ne})+e_t. Removing explicit lagged-error dependence gives NARX; removing exogenous inputs gives a NARMA form. The moving-average terminology here concerns lagged errors inside a possibly nonlinear function, not a running arithmetic average of observations. Finite-lag recurrence structure does not require a neural network.
  • supports Source: Orthogonal least squares methods and their application to non-linear system identification · Locator: Section 2, Equations 1–6, PDF p. 3 / printed p. 1874
  • supports Source: Representations of non-linear systems: the NARMAX model · Locator: Section 6, Equations 69–72, PDF p. 16 / printed p. 1028
Scope #innovation-versus-fitted-residual
In the conditional-mean innovation construction, the prediction is the conditional expectation of the current output given available past information, and the innovation is output minus that expectation. This interpretation requires the relevant moments and information set; it does not imply independent Gaussian errors. The companion OLS formulation separately assumes zero-mean independent noise. For an estimated model with chosen parameters, recursively computed prediction residuals are parameter-dependent and are not automatically the true innovations of the data-generating system.
  • supports Source: Representations of non-linear systems: the NARMAX model · Locator: Section 6, Equations 58–69 and 77–81, PDF pp. 15–16,18 / printed pp. 1027–1028,1030
  • supports Source: Orthogonal least squares methods and their application to non-linear system identification · Locator: Section 2, Equations 1–5 and noise definition, PDF p. 3 / printed p. 1874
Mechanism #latent-error-estimation
Polynomial expansion is formally linear in coefficients, but lagged noise variables are not directly observed. Consequently the regressor matrix is not fixed directly from input-output measurements. The OLS paper describes an iterative extension that substitutes prediction residuals, rebuilds noise-containing regressors and refits. This is one estimator for the family; its reported few-iteration behavior is not a universal convergence guarantee or a definition of NARMAX itself.
  • supports Source: Orthogonal least squares methods and their application to non-linear system identification · Locator: Sections 2 and 5, Equations 83–91, PDF pp. 5,18–20 / printed pp. 1876,1889–1891
Limitation #residual-initialization-and-stability
Prediction with lagged errors requires residual initialization. Model invertibility asks whether residual sequences from different initializations forget that difference for the specified model and input process. Linearity in lagged errors alone is insufficient: in y_t=u_{t−1}²+a*e_{t−1}+e_t, two residual recursions driven by identical observations differ by (−a)^t times their initial difference. With initial difference 1, the difference after eight steps is 1/256 for a=1/2 and 256 for a=2. The source’s particular stochastic sufficient condition and example must not be generalized to arbitrary coefficients or input distributions.
  • supports Source: Representations of non-linear systems: the NARMAX model · Locator: Section 6, Equations 74–83 and Example 6, PDF pp. 17–19 / printed pp. 1029–1031; curator exact examples in research/discovery/narx-narmax-boundaries.py
Scope #polynomial-scope
A polynomial parameterization expands finite lag variables into monomials and is linear in the monomial coefficients, while it can be nonlinear in the input and output values. The continuous scalar recurrence function on a specified compact finite-dimensional lag domain can be uniformly approximated by some polynomial; this does not prescribe a fixed degree, identify coefficients from finite observations, justify extrapolation, or guarantee stable recursive trajectories. The general model family is not restricted to polynomial parameterizations.

Limitation #published-approximation-bound
The source supplies the displayed formulas and stated error bound below; the refutation and trajectory calculation are curator derivations. The representation paper’s particular degree-four approximation example claims error below 0.04 on |y|<=1/4, |u|<=1. Exact substitution in its displayed formulas gives error 5/16 at y=−1/4,u=1, and 2336/15625 at y=−6/25,u=1 (the y coordinate is interior; u is on the boundary). These curator counterexamples reject that numerical bound, not the compact-domain polynomial approximation existence statement. A small one-step function error also need not remain small under repeated simulation: comparing f(y)=2y and g(y)=2y+1/100 from zero gives difference (2^t−1)/100 after t steps.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • This record covers finite-lag, real-valued, discrete-time, strictly causal input-output formulations; current-input/feedthrough conventions and broader vector/noise assumptions need explicit specification. The family is not a trained model, dataset or unique estimator.
  • Local realization, uniqueness, stability, identifiability and predictive accuracy are separate questions. No claim is made that every nonlinear or stochastic system has an exact global representation of this form. Original 1984 reports and 1985 journal proofs are follow-up sources, not audited theorem evidence here.
  • The 1989 representation paper contains problematic unrestricted inverse formulas and a numerical approximation bound contradicted by exact substitution. Those statements are not adopted. Its explicit recurrence definitions and scoped polynomial construction are distinguishable from these source defects.
  • No model was trained and no empirical result was replicated. Exact finite curator examples check stated boundaries. The record remains DRAFT; structural validation and author self-review are not independent scientific approval.
