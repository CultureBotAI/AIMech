Fast Dropout Training

Algorithms AIMech:FastDropoutTraining DRAFT
Aliases: Fast dropout
A dropout approximation procedure that replaces masked weighted sums by Gaussian distributions with matched means and variances, using Gaussian sampling or moment integration to approximate training and prediction computations.

Fast dropout denotes Wang and Manning’s Gaussian approximation algorithm, including sampling and moment-integration variants and the inspected recurrent application. It is not the Bernoulli masking layer itself, a fixed Gaussian-noise layer, a recurrent architecture or the original DropConnect layer’s connection-masked training. DropConnect shares a Gaussian moment approximation at inference, which does not identify its full procedure with fast-dropout training. Recurrent use is kept within this method; no duplicate recurrent-method ID is created.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

5 Claims
Mechanism #masked-sum-moments
For fixed input coordinates x_i, weights w_i and independent Bernoulli retention masks z_i with probabilities p_i, the masked preactivation has mean sum(p_i*w_i*x_i) and variance sum(p_i*(1-p_i)*(w_i*x_i)^2). These moments are exact under those assumptions, but the Gaussian distribution replacing the masked sum is generally approximate. Central-limit reasoning additionally needs conditions such as no dominant contribution; arbitrary finite width or independence alone does not ensure a good Gaussian approximation.
  • supports Source: Fast dropout training · Locator: Section 2.2, PDF p. 2; limitations and sparse features, Section 6.2, p. 6; direct moment algebra
Scope #sampling-integration-and-loss
The procedure includes a Gaussian-sampling route and approximations that integrate nonlinear responses. Sigmoid-Gaussian and expected log-sigmoid expressions in the paper are approximations; evaluating a nonlinear loss at a mean is generally different from its expectation. Squared-error expectation is a special exact moment identity: variance plus squared error of the mean. Replacing Bernoulli masks by a matched Gaussian does not preserve the complete distribution or every expected loss, and the sampled and closed-form gradient approximations are not interchangeable exact gradients.
  • supports Source: Fast dropout training · Locator: Sections 2.3–2.4, PDF pp. 3–4, Equations 5–8; Section 3.1, Equation 9, p. 4
Mechanism #propagated-variance-and-recurrence
For independent random input coordinates with means mu_i and variances sigma_i^2, independent of a common-probability retention mask p, the preactivation variance is sum(w_i^2*(p*(1-p)*mu_i^2+p*sigma_i^2)). The original neural-network formulation displays incoming variance before simplifying it away in a practical approximation. The recurrent followup retains it and concatenates the previous hidden state with the current input for the recurrent transform. This does not make recurrent hidden components independent or their nonlinear joint distribution exactly Gaussian. Omitting final-layer variance propagation differs from merely setting its dropout probability to zero.
  • supports Source: Fast dropout training · Locator: Sections 4.1–4.2, PDF p. 5, displayed hidden mean/variance and practical simplification
  • supports Source: On Fast Dropout and its Applicability to Recurrent Networks · Locator: Section 2.2.1, PDF pp. 3–4, Equations 1–2 and Fast Dropout for RNNs; Section 2.3, pp. 7–8
Limitation #local-regularization-boundary
The recurrent paper decomposes the derivative through a unit into mean and variance paths. Holding incoming moments fixed, the variance contribution for weight w_i is 2*w_i*(p*(1-p)*mu_i^2+p*sigma_i^2) times the loss derivative with respect to preactivation variance. Its sign can encourage shrinkage, growth or neither. This is a local derivative interpretation, not an established global positive quadratic penalty, a guarantee that each contribution vanishes at every parameter-space stationary point, or a proof of stable recurrent dynamics.
  • supports Source: On Fast Dropout and its Applicability to Recurrent Networks · Locator: Section 2.2.2, PDF pp. 4–7, Gaussian approximation and explicit local-at-a qualification on p. 5; Section 2.3, p. 7; direct variance differentiation
Limitation #evaluation-and-version-scope
The original paper evaluates selected document classifiers, digit networks and smaller classification/regression datasets, with different optimizers and additional-noise settings. It reports no gain with its original minibatch schedule and unfavorable linear-regression comparisons, so speed and accuracy gains are not universal. The recurrent followup evaluates four polyphonic-music datasets using validation-selected random-search configurations with several optimization aids; its comparison is not an isolated test of one variance term. The inspected v7 version removes the earlier erroneous Penn Treebank experiments, which supply no retained language-modeling result here.
  • supports Source: Fast dropout training · Locator: Sections 6.1–6.5, PDF pp. 6–8, Figures 5–6 and Tables 1–5
  • supports Source: On Fast Dropout and its Applicability to Recurrent Networks · Locator: Sections 2.3 and 3.1, PDF pp. 7–9; Appendix 5.1, pp. 11–12; primary arXiv v7 version comments

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Retention probability p in the moment formulas is one minus the dropout probability. Scaling conventions must be reconciled before comparing with an inverted-dropout implementation.
  • Means and variances do not identify every non-Gaussian distribution. Cross-coordinate or temporal correlations and nonlinear moment closure can invalidate an independent Gaussian approximation.
  • Fast dropout denotes an approximation algorithm, not every Gaussian-noise layer, a new recurrent architecture, or a fixed optimizer. Its recurrent application is retained within this method record.
  • Source notation and broad regularization/stability claims are qualified by the actual moment formulas and local derivative scope. No exact Bayesian posterior, universal generalization improvement or hardware speedup is asserted.
  • The complete 9-page original and 12-page v7 recurrent followup were visually inspected. Constructed finite calculations check mathematical boundaries only; author code, training and empirical replication were not performed.
