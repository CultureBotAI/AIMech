Context-sensitive emission hidden Markov model

Model Families AIMech:ContextSensitiveEmissionHiddenMarkovModel DRAFT
A hidden-state sequence-model family with a first-order Markov prior whose observation distributions depend jointly on hidden states at multiple temporal positions, introducing context beyond the contemporaneous hidden state.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the Saul–Jordan context-sensitive emission construction. Its displayed one-sided Gaussian example uses states at t and t+Delta, while the reported illustration extends to left and right context. The defining distinction is the emission dependency, not a higher-order hidden transition, an external stack/queue, mixed-memory predecessor selection, independent factorial chains or the structured mean-field algorithm used to approximate its posterior. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a finite latent window s_1,...,s_T, specify a normalized initial law pi and row-normalized transition table A. In the source’s one-sided Gaussian construction with integer lag Delta between1 andT-1, observations x_1,...,x_(T-Delta) have conditional means u_(s_t)+v_(s_(t+Delta)) and identity covariance. The joint density is pi(s_1) times product_(t=1)^(T-1) A_(s_t,s_(t+1)) times product_(t=1)^(T-Delta) Normal(x_t;u_(s_t)+v_(s_(t+Delta)),I). Given the complete hidden path, observations are independent, but each observation couples two hidden positions in posterior inference. For binary states, expanding the Gaussian terms produces a long-range pair interaction K=-(u_+-u_-) dot (v_+-v_-)/4. At hidden index j, the contribution through the delayed v term uses observation x_(j-Delta), when that observation lies within the valid window. Summing the normalized joint density over paths gives the observed likelihood. The paper approximates the hidden posterior by retaining a tractable chain and treating added interactions through structured mean field; that inference choice is separate from model identity.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The source’s Equation(23) omits the initial-state law and Equation(26) suppresses boundary corrections. The normalized finite-window convention is stated explicitly here; other endpoint or padding choices define different models.
  • Equation(26) uses the contemporaneous observation in a combined unary expression. In a general sequence, a delayed-state contribution must instead use the corresponding earlier observation; this cannot be repaired only by ignoring endpoints.
  • The prior remains first-order, but the conditioned hidden distribution generally has long-range interactions. A standard HMM forward–backward pass over the original single state is not automatically exact; exact inference can require an expanded state with cost growing with context width.
  • The Gaussian additive-mean, identity-covariance form is the inspected example, not a claim that every contextual emission distribution must have that form. The synthetic Figure1 uses five states and two-sided context rather than only the displayed binary one-sided specialization.
  • A usual HMM can retain marginal correlations over many time steps. The distinction is added direct conditional dependency through emissions, not the existence of any long-lag correlation or a causal interpretation of future context. No speech benchmark or accuracy guarantee is asserted.
