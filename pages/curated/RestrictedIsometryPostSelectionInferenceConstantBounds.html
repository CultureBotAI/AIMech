Restricted isometry post-selection inference (PoSI) constant upper and lower bounds

Aliases: RIP-based post-selection inference (PoSI) constant bounds (Bachoc-Blanchard-Neuvial 2018), Correlation-matrix restricted isometry interpolation for universal post-selection confidence intervals, Gaussian-width and Beta-Fisher quantile bounds for the sparse PoSI constant, Block-equicorrelated minimax lower bound for the sparse PoSI constant
A post-selection inference methodology for linear Gaussian regression Y = mu + epsilon (epsilon ~ N(0, sigma^2 I_n)) that bounds the Berk-Brown-Buja-Zhang-Zhao (2013) Post-Selection Inference (PoSI) constant K(X, M_s, alpha, r)—the (1 - alpha) quantile of the studentized maximum projection statistic gamma_{M_s, r} = (1 / N) max_{M in M_s, i in M} |w_{M, i}^t xi| over all s-sparse submodels M_s = {M subset {1, ..., p} : rank(X_M) = |M| <= s} where w_{M, i} = v_{M, i} / ||v_{M, i}|| and v_{M, i} is the i-th column of X_M (X_M^t X_M)^{-1}—as an explicit function of the correlation-matrix Restricted Isometry Property (RIP) constant delta(X, s) = sup_{M in M_s} ||corr(X_M^t X_M) - I_{|M|}||_op. Because K(X, M, alpha, r) depends on X only through corr(X^t X) (Lemma 2.2) and satisfies the Cirel'son-Ibragimov-Sudakov Gaussian-concentration quantile bound K(X, M, alpha, r) <= T(E[gamma_{M, infty}], r, 1 - alpha/2) (Proposition 2.3), decomposing each dual projection vector w_{M, i} into its normalized column z_{M, i} = X_{M, i} / ||X_{M, i}|| plus a residual of norm at most 2 * delta * c(delta) with c(delta) = sqrt(1 + delta) / (1 - delta) yields the RIP-interpolating Gaussian-width upper bound E[gamma_{M_s, infty}] <= U_RIP(p, s, delta) = sqrt(2 log(2p)) + 2 * delta * c(delta) * sqrt(2 s log(6p / s)) (Theorem 3.3) and the sharper Beta-Fisher quantile union-split bound U_tilde_RIP(p, s, delta) = inf_{t in (0, 1)} {B_{t*alpha}(n wedge p, r, p) + 2 * delta * c(delta) * B_{(1-t)*alpha}(n wedge p, r, |M_s|)} (Section 3.3), smoothly interpolating between the orthogonal-design rate sqrt(2 log(2p)) (delta = 0) and the generic sparse rate sqrt(2 s log(6p / s)) (Lemma 2.4). A matching lower bound on block-equicorrelated designs Z^{(c, k)} with exact RIP constant delta(X, s) = c * sqrt(s - 1) (Lemma 4.1) proves K(X, M_s, alpha, infty) >= A * (c * (s - 1) / sqrt(1 - (s - 1) * c^2)) * sqrt(log floor(k / s)) - sqrt(2 log 2) (Proposition 4.2) and establishes minimax rate optimality whenever delta_p = O(p^{-lambda}) (Corollary 4.3).

Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from AIMech:ValidPostSelectionInferencePoSIConstant (Berk, Brown, Buja, Zhang, & Zhao 2013, The Annals of Statistics 41(2):802-837, arXiv:1206.6451) and AIMech:SimultaneousLinearContrastPostSelectionInference (Scheffé / simultaneous sphere-intersection bounds), which introduce the all-subsets PoSI constant K(X, M_all, alpha, r), orthogonal vs. worst-case full-model scaling O(sqrt(p)), and canonical-angle sphere-packing bounds without Restricted Isometry Property (RIP) perturbation bounds over s-sparse model families M_s. Bachoc, Blanchard, & Neuvial (2018, Electronic Journal of Statistics 12(2):3736-3757, arXiv:1804.07566v2, DOI:10.1214/18-EJS1490) establish correlation-matrix scale invariance of K(X, M, alpha, r) (Lemma 2.2), Gaussian-concentration control of studentized maxima via T(E[gamma_{M, infty}], r, 1 - alpha/2) (Proposition 2.3, Proposition A.2, Lemma A.3), the correlation-matrix RIP constant delta(X, s) <= 2*kappa(X, s)/(1 - kappa(X, s)) (Lemma 3.2), the explicit RIP perturbation upper bound U_RIP(p, s, delta) = sqrt(2 log(2p)) + 2*delta*(sqrt(1+delta)/(1-delta))*sqrt(2s log(6p/s)) (Theorem 3.3) and Beta-Fisher quantile union-split bound U_tilde_RIP(p, s, delta) (Section 3.3), and the matching block-equicorrelated lower bound (Lemma 4.1, Proposition 4.2, Corollary 4.3). Also distinct from AIMech:ReferenceFamilyJERPostHocConfidenceBounds (Blanchard, Neuvial, & Roquain 2020) and AIMech:ForestStructuredDKWPostHocConfidenceBounds (Durand, Blanchard, Neuvial, & Roquain 2020), which bound false positive counts in multiple hypothesis testing rather than linear regression submodel projection coefficients.

4 Claims
Mechanism #correlation-matrix-scale-invariance-and-gaussian-concentration-envelope
For linear Gaussian regression Y = mu + epsilon (epsilon ~ N(0, sigma^2 I_n)), submodel family M subset M_all = {M subset {1, ..., p} : rank(X_M) = |M|}, and dual projection vectors w_{M, i} = v_{M, i} / ||v_{M, i}|| where v_{M, i} is the i-th column of X_M (X_M^t X_M)^{-1} (Equations 1-5), the PoSI constant K(X, M, alpha, r) = q_{1 - alpha}(gamma_{M, r}) with gamma_{M, r} = (1 / N) max_{M in M, i in M} |w_{M, i}^t xi| (Definition 2.1, Equation 6) is invariant to column rescaling and depends on X only through the correlation matrix corr(X^t X) = D X^t X D: if corr(X^t X) = corr(Z^t Z), then K(X, M, alpha, r) = K(Z, M, alpha, r) (Lemma 2.2, page 5, Appendix B). Moreover, by the Cirel'son-Ibragimov-Sudakov Gaussian concentration inequality (Theorem A.1) and studentized conditioning on N ~ sqrt(chi^2_r / r) (Proposition A.2, Equations 19-20), K(X, M, alpha, r) <= T(E[gamma_{M, infty}], r, 1 - alpha/2) where T(mu, infty, 1 - alpha/2) = mu + sqrt(2 log(2 / alpha)) and for finite r > 0, T(mu, r, 1 - alpha/2) <= (mu + sqrt(2 log(2 / alpha))) / (1 - 2 * sqrt(2 log(2 / alpha)) / r)_+ (Proposition 2.3, Equation 8, and Lemma A.3, Equation 22), while for the s-sparse model collection M_s = {M in M_all : |M| <= s}, E[gamma_{M_s, infty}] <= U_sparse(p, s) = sqrt(2 s log(6p / s)) (Lemma 2.4, page 5).

Mechanism #restricted-isometry-perturbation-upper-bound-on-gaussian-width
Defining the raw Restricted Isometry Property (RIP) constant kappa(X, s) = sup_{M in M_s} ||X_M^t X_M - I_{|M|}||_op and the scale-invariant correlation-matrix RIP constant delta(X, s) = sup_{M in M_s} ||corr(X_M^t X_M) - I_{|M|}||_op (Definition 3.1, Equations 9-10), Lemma 3.2 (page 6, Appendix B) proves that delta(X, s) <= 2 * kappa(X, s) / (1 - kappa(X, s)) whenever kappa(X, s) < 1 (and delta(X, s) = kappa(X, s) when all columns of X have unit norm). Whenever delta = delta(X, s) < 1, writing C_M = corr(X_M^t X_M) = Z_M^t Z_M for unit-norm columns z_{M, i} = X_{M, i} / ||X_{M, i}||, the spectrum of C_M lies in [1 - delta, 1 + delta], so ||C_M^{-1} e_i|| in [(1 + delta)^{-1}, (1 - delta)^{-1}] and ||C_M^{-1/2} e_i|| in [(1 + delta)^{-1/2}, (1 - delta)^{-1/2}] (Equations 14-15), which bounds the Euclidean distance between the dual projection vector w_{M, i} and z_{M, i} by ||w_{M, i} - z_{M, i}|| <= (1 + delta)^{1/2} * (||C_M^{-1} e_i - e_i|| + |1 - ||C_M^{-1/2} e_i|||) <= 2 * delta * c(delta) with c(delta) = sqrt(1 + delta) / (1 - delta) (Equation 16). Taking expectations of sup_{M in M_s, i in M} |z_{M, i}^t xi| + sup_{M in M_s, i in M} |(w_{M, i} - z_{M, i})^t xi| proves Theorem 3.3 (Equation 11, page 7): E[gamma_{M_s, infty}] <= U_RIP(p, s, delta) = sqrt(2 log(2p)) + 2 * delta * (sqrt(1 + delta) / (1 - delta)) * sqrt(2 s log(6p / s)).

Mechanism #computable-concentration-and-beta-fisher-union-split-posi-bounds
Section 3.3 and Appendix C (Pages 9, 18-20, Figure 2, Equation 25) derive two computable upper bounds on the PoSI constant K(X, M_s, alpha, r) when delta = delta(X, s) < 1: (1) the Gaussian-concentration bound U_bar_RIP(p, s, delta) = T(U_RIP(p, s, delta), r, 1 - alpha/2) via Proposition 2.3 and Theorem 3.3, and (2) the direct Beta-Fisher quantile union-split bound U_tilde_RIP(p, s, delta) = inf_{t in (0, 1)} {B_{t*alpha}(n wedge p, r, p) + 2 * delta * c(delta) * B_{(1-t)*alpha}(n wedge p, r, |M_s|)} where |M_s| = sum_{i=1}^s i * comb(p, i) and B_beta(d, r, k) is the union-bound envelope for the maximum of k projections of a spherical / Beta-Fisher variate in dimension d = n wedge p (equal to sqrt(d) * sqrt(q_{1 - beta/(2k)}(Beta(1/2, (d - 1)/2))) when r = infty and sqrt(d) * sqrt(q_{1 - beta/(2k)}(Fisher(1,r)/d)) when r < infty, Equation 25), which avoids the additive concentration slack sqrt(2 log(2 / alpha)) and yields strictly tighter PoSI constants than U_bar_RIP for small delta (Figure 2, page 9).

Mechanism #block-equicorrelated-rip-lower-bound-and-minimax-rate-optimality
For integers p >= 2 and 1 <= k <= p - 1 and correlation parameter c >= 0 with k * c^2 < 1, Section 4 (Equation 17, page 10) constructs the unit-column design Z^{(c, k)} whose Gram matrix has unit diagonal, pairwise correlation c between column p and each of the first k columns, and 0 elsewhere. Lemma 4.1 (page 10) proves that for any s <= k <= p - 1, kappa(Z^{(c, k)}, s) = delta(Z^{(c, k)}, s) = c * sqrt(s - 1), attained by every submodel M of size s containing p and s - 1 indices from {1, ..., k}. Partitioning {1, ..., k} into floor(k / s) >= 4 disjoint blocks I_j of size s - 1 and setting M_j = I_j cup {p}, Equation (18) (page 11) shows w_{M_j, p}^t xi = (1 / sqrt(1 - (s - 1) * c^2)) * (xi_p - c * sum_{i in I_j} xi_i), whence Sudakov's lower bound on the independent block sums proves Proposition 4.2 (page 10): K(Z^{(c, k)}, M_s, alpha, infty) >= A * (c * (s - 1) / sqrt(1 - (s - 1) * c^2)) * sqrt(log floor(k / s)) - sqrt(2 log 2) for a universal constant A > 0 (and E[gamma_{M_s, infty}] >= A * (c * (s - 1) / sqrt(1 - (s - 1) * c^2)) * sqrt(log floor(k / s))). Corollary 4.3 (pages 11-12) concludes that for any regime s_p = O(p^gamma) (0 <= gamma < 1) and delta_p = O(p^{-lambda}) (lambda >= 0), the worst-case PoSI constant over {X : delta(X, s_p) <= delta_p} scales as Theta(sqrt(log p) + delta_p * sqrt(s_p * log p)), proving that Theorem 3.3 is minimax rate-optimal whenever delta_p = O(1).

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Theorem 3.3 and the computable bounds U_bar_RIP(p, s, delta) and U_tilde_RIP(p, s, delta) require the correlation-matrix Restricted Isometry Property constant delta(X, s) < 1 and become non-informative compared to the generic sparse bound U_sparse(p, s) = sqrt(2 s log(6p / s)) (Lemma 2.4) once 2 * delta * sqrt(1 + delta) / (1 - delta) >= 1 (i.e., delta >= 0.36, Section 3.2, page 8, Figure 1).
  • Evaluating delta(X, s) = sup_{|M| <= s} ||corr(X_M^t X_M) - I_{|M|}||_op exactly over all comb(p, s) sparse submodels of a deterministic design matrix X is NP-hard in general (though delta(X, s) can be upper-bounded via the mutual coherence (s - 1) * max_{i != j} |corr(X^t X)_{i, j}| or high-probability subgaussian random-matrix bounds, Section 5, pages 12-13).
  • Three minor typographical slips in arXiv:1804.07566v2 were identified and verified during full-text inspection: (1) in Section 2.1 (page 3, line 4), 'irrespective of the model selection mehod used' prints 'mehod' for 'method'; (2) in Section 5 (page 12, line 13), the coverage display writes 'P(forall i in M_hat, (M_hat)_{i.M_hat} in CI_{i, M_hat}) >= 1 - alpha', printing '(M_hat)_{i.M_hat}' instead of the projected regression coefficient '(beta_{M_hat})_{i.M_hat}' (cf. Equation 5 on page 3); and (3) in Appendix A Lemma A.3 (page 14, fourth line from bottom), the inline bound '(mu + sqrt(2 log(2/alpha))/(1 - 2*sqrt(2 log(2/alpha))/r)_+' omits the closing parenthesis after 'sqrt(2 log(2/alpha))' in the numerator (whereas Equation 22 in the proof on page 15 correctly writes '(mu + sqrt(2 log(2/alpha))) / (1 - 2*sqrt(2 log(2/alpha))/r)_+').
  • All 22 pages of the full-text PDF of Bachoc, Blanchard, & Neuvial (2018, Electronic Journal of Statistics 12(2):3736-3757, arXiv:1804.07566v2, DOI:10.1214/18-EJS1490) were read. Constructed numerical checks in research/discovery/restricted-isometry-posi-constant-bounds-boundaries.py verify (1) Lemma 2.2 column-scale invariance, Lemma 2.4 sparse cardinality inequality, and Lemma A.3 finite-r quantile bound, (2) Theorem 3.3 RIP Gaussian-width bound and Section 3.3 Figure 2 comparison (U_orth < U_RIP < U_tilde_RIP < U_bar_RIP << U_sparse), and (3) Lemma 4.1 exact RIP constant delta(Z^{(c, k)}, s) = c * sqrt(s - 1), Equation (18) block inverse identity, and Theorem 3.3 vector perturbation inequality ||w_{M, i} - z_{M, i}|| <= 2 * delta * c(delta).
