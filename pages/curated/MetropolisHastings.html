Metropolis–Hastings sampling

Algorithms AIMech:MetropolisHastings DRAFT
Aliases: MH
A Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm that proposes a new state from a specified transition distribution and accepts it with the smaller of one and the target-density ratio multiplied by the reverse-to-forward proposal ratio, otherwise retaining the current state.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the usual Metropolis acceptance choice within Hastings’s more general family of reversible updates. It is not the whole MCMC family, a Markov-chain model, the Barker acceptance rule or an independent-draw generator. Asymmetric proposals require the proposal-ratio correction. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method
At a current state x with positive target mass or density pi(x), draw y from q(y|x). For a supported proposal define R=pi(y)q(x|y)/(pi(x)q(y|x)), and accept y with probability min(1,R); on rejection record x again. A common unknown normalizing constant in pi cancels. The accepted-move probabilities satisfy detailed balance with pi. For symmetric q the proposal factors cancel, recovering the Metropolis choice. A valid target-invariant kernel is not by itself a finite-run convergence certificate.

Limitation #error-and-mixing
Hastings distinguishes invariance from practical error assessment: initial-state effects, correlated samples, incomplete exploration and inaccurate ratio calculations can undermine finite-run estimates.
  • supports Source: Monte Carlo sampling methods using Markov chains and their applications · Locator: Sections 2.1 and 2.6, printed pp. 98–99 and 104–105 / PDF pp. 2–3 and 8–9

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The target must be proper, and the proposal and acceptance calculation must be defined on the relevant support. A proposed move with no reverse support has zero acceptance; zero-over-zero expressions are not a general operational rule.
  • Target invariance does not ensure irreducibility, aperiodicity, rapid mixing, independent samples or convergence from every start. Those properties depend on the target and proposal.
  • Rejections repeat the current state. Keeping only accepted moves without correcting their weighting changes the sampling process.
  • Poor proposals, isolated modes, finite precision and inadequate run length can yield misleading estimates; acceptance rate alone does not establish adequate exploration.
  • The record does not reproduce an implementation or assert a universal acceptance target, speed improvement or numerical posterior result.
