Hierarchical mixture of experts

Model Families AIMech:HierarchicalMixtureOfExperts DRAFT
Aliases: HME
A conditional mixture-model family that organizes output experts as leaves of a tree and forms each leaf’s mixture weight by multiplying input-dependent probabilistic gates along its root-to-leaf path.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The inspected Jordan–Jacobs construction uses generalized-linear experts and softmax gates with shared external input, with a finite tree defining nested soft partitions. It is static conditional modeling, distinct from temporal hidden Markov decision trees, hard classification trees, hierarchical clustering and language-specific sparse MoE routing. A mixture density network shares conditional-mixture semantics but need not impose this tree factorization or generalized-linear leaf structure. Flattening a fixed tree gives leaf weights without making every flat gating parameterization the same hierarchical model. EM, IRLS and the online surrogate are fitting procedures, not the model identity. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For input x and leaf l, define w_l(x) as the product of normalized child-gate probabilities along the path to l. Then p(y|x)=sum_l w_l(x) p_l(y|x). In the two-level presentation, p(y|x)=sum_i g_i(x) sum_j g_(j|i)(x) p_ij(y|x), with softmax gates and generalized-linear conditional expert densities. The conditional output mean is the corresponding weighted sum of expert means. Given an observed y, leaf responsibilities are proportional to w_l(x) p_l(y|x); they condition on both x and y, unlike input-only gates. Conditional EM alternates these responsibilities with weighted expert fits and gate fits to soft multinomial targets; a lower gate’s objective is weighted by the posterior probability of reaching its parent. The paper uses IRLS inside those maximum-likelihood subproblems. Its separate Algorithm 2 instead regresses log responsibilities by least squares, and its online variant uses discounted recursive weighted least squares. Those surrogate procedures are approximations to the specified fitting approach.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The source uses soft gates that mix expert densities; it does not require hard routing, top-k sparse activation, a language model or a causal interpretation of the decision hierarchy.
  • Exact EM likelihood monotonicity requires a valid E-step and an M-step that increases the expected complete log likelihood under suitable regularity. It does not automatically apply to arbitrary gradient steps, the log-target least-squares surrogate or discounted online updates, and does not guarantee a global optimum.
  • The logistic expert output is a Bernoulli probability, not log odds; printed p.184 is qualified by the explicit Bernoulli density on p.188. In Appendix B, the multinomial total is m=sum_i y_i; displayed n factors in Equation (6.2) and the following mean/log-partition expressions cannot be copied when n denotes the number of categories.
  • Near-uniform gates average expert predictions, but a mixture of distinct expert densities need not become one generalized-linear density. Finite log-responsibility targets also require nonzero responsibilities.
  • The robot simulation uses test-error stopping and selected successful comparison runs. No unbiased generalization comparison, universal performance advantage, author-code execution or benchmark reproduction is asserted.
