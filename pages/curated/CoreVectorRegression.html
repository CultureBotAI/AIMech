Core vector regression

Aliases: CVR
A support-vector regression training algorithm that approximates a penalized-bias, paired squared-slack formulation with a learned unconstrained margin using a center-constrained minimum enclosing ball over two transformed points per observation.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #paired-squared-slack-regression
For m observations and C,mu>0, the displayed formulation minimizes ||w||²+b²+C/(mu*m)*sum_i(xi_i²+xi_i_star²)+2*C*epsilon_bar, subject to y_i-f_i<=epsilon_bar+xi_i and f_i-y_i<=epsilon_bar+xi_i_star, where f_i=w^T*phi(x_i)+b. The bias is penalized and the paired slack errors are squared. The source displays no nonnegativity constraint on the learned epsilon_bar. This formulation is distinct from L1-slack SVR and from a learned-width formulation explicitly requiring epsilon_bar>=0.
  • supports Source: Generalized Core Vector Machines · Locator: Section III-A, printed p1129 / PDF p4, Equations9-15
  • supports Source: Core Vector Regression for Very Large Regression Problems · Locator: Sections3.1-3.3, PDF pp3-5, Equations6-15 and Property3
Mechanism #two-copy-simplex-dual-and-recovery
Let alpha=(lambda,lambda_star)>=0 sum to1 and v=(y,-y). The scaled dual maximizes (2/C)*v^T*alpha-alpha^T*Ktilde*alpha, where Ktilde has diagonal blocks K+11^T+(mu*m/C)*I and off-diagonal blocks -(K+11^T). Thus m original observations yield2m transformed points. Recover w=C*sum_i(lambda_i-lambda_i_star)*phi(x_i), b=C*sum_i(lambda_i-lambda_i_star), xi_i=mu*m*lambda_i, xi_i_star=mu*m*lambda_i_star and epsilon_bar=v^T*alpha-C*alpha^T*Ktilde*alpha. The original primal optimum equals C² times this unshifted scaled-dual optimum.

  • supports Source: Generalized Core Vector Machines · Locator: Section III-B, printed p1130 / PDF p5; Appendix Property3 proof, printed p1139 / PDF p14
  • supports Source: Core Vector Regression for Very Large Regression Problems · Locator: Sections3.1-3.3, PDF pp3-5, Equations6-15 and Property3
Mechanism #core-vector-regression-training
Choose Delta=(2/C)*v-diag(Ktilde)+eta*1>=0 and apply center-constrained MEB core growth to the2m transformed points. Add an outside point, solve the enlarged core problem and recover the regression coefficients from its simplex weights. This is the regression-specific application of the generalized CVM solver. A selected transformed point, an original observation and a nonzero final regression coefficient are different notions; the two copies of an observation can contribute with opposite signs.

  • supports Source: Core Vector Regression for Very Large Regression Problems · Locator: Sections3.1-3.3, PDF pp3-5, Equations6-15 and Property3
Limitation #learned-width-and-loss-interpretation
The missing nonnegative-width constraint is material to interpreting the displayed formulation. An exact curator calculation with one zero-feature observation y=0, C=mu=1 gives optimal lambda=lambda_star=1/2, w=b=0, xi=xi_star=1/2 and epsilon_bar=-1/2, with primal and scaled-dual value-1/2. The mean paired-slack sum equals mu=1, whereas max(|y-f|-epsilon_bar,0)=1/2. Thus the paper’s interpretation of mu as the ordinary epsilon-insensitive error needs a nonnegative-width qualification; it is not asserted unconditionally here. This result follows from the displayed primal/dual, not from executing historical CVR code.

  • supports Source: Core Vector Regression for Very Large Regression Problems · Locator: Section3.1, PDF p3; exact curator derivation in research/discovery/generalized-core-vector-boundaries.py
Limitation #shifted-scaled-objective-guarantee
With exact core optimization and exhaustive all-point termination, a core radius R gives R²<=p_star/C²+eta<=(1+epsilon)²*R² for the original primal optimum p_star. This is a bound on the shifted and scaled objective, not a multiplicative bound on p_star alone; p_star can be negative, as the exact zero-output example shows. Tolerance is not a test-error bound. Finite inner numerical tolerance, sampled searches, kernel costs and total input storage require separate treatment.

Scope #version-and-application-boundaries
The 2005 ICML CVR paper is an abridged precursor to the 2006 generalized-CVM paper, not a separate CVR identity. The earlier paper applies regression fits to implicit surface reconstruction using nonzero level sets and multiscale residual fitting. Historical regression comparisons use specified datasets, hyperparameter choices, hardware, LIBSVM/SVMlight versions and different loss formulations; the 2006 paper notes a change in LIBSVM version. These experiments do not establish universal speed, sparsity or prediction superiority. No historical software or benchmark was reproduced.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The source assumes valid positive-semidefinite kernels and particular simplex-compatible formulations. Approximation guarantees depend on the additive objective shift, exact inner optimization and exhaustive violation checking.
  • All23 pages of the two primary PDFs were visually read. Source-specific claims are retained with their versions; historical software access failed, and no optimizer, training or benchmark was executed.
  • Exact rational calculations test negative learned width, primal-dual scaling and shift-sensitive termination. They are curator derivations from the displayed mathematics, not reproduced implementation behavior.
  • The record remains a native DRAFT with a related AIO model mapping. Later core-vector, soft-margin, multiview, ranking and data-description extensions remain unresolved.
