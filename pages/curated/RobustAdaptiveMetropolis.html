Robust adaptive Metropolis

Algorithms AIMech:RobustAdaptiveMetropolis DRAFT
Aliases: RAM
An adaptive random-walk Metropolis algorithm that updates the proposal shape factor by a rank-one transformation driven by the proposal direction and the difference between its acceptance probability and a chosen target rate.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is Vihola’s RAM update, which avoids estimating the target covariance. It differs from covariance-based adaptive Metropolis, scale-only adaptation and a fixed random-walk kernel. RAM here denotes this sampler, not computer memory. The history-dependent state sequence is not assumed to be a homogeneous Markov chain. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Let q be a spherical density, S a lower-triangular factor with positive diagonal, x satisfy pi(x)>0, eta_n lie in (0,1] and decay to zero, and alpha_star lie in (0,1). Draw U from q and propose y=x+S U. Set alpha=min(1,pi(y)/pi(x)), accept y with this probability or retain x. Whether accepted or rejected, choose the next positive-diagonal Cholesky factor S_new satisfying S_new S_new^T=S[I+eta_n(alpha−alpha_star)UU^T/||U||²]S^T. The update uses the acceptance probability, not the binary acceptance outcome; the normalized direction U determines the rank-one adjustment. It expands that component when alpha exceeds the target and contracts it when alpha is smaller.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Spherical continuous proposals make U=0 a null event; numerical implementations still need a zero-norm guard and stable factor updates.
  • Positive definiteness of each update does not prove long-run stability. The source’s strong-law result assumes target/proposal regularity and step-size conditions and holds on an event where shape eigenvalues stay bounded away from zero and infinity.
  • The paper explicitly leaves unrestricted stability unproved. Enforcing fixed eigenvalue bounds by rejecting a proposed shape update is a separate safeguard that can alter the limiting shape.
  • A chosen acceptance rate is a tuning target, not a universal optimum or convergence diagnostic. Strong multimodality can remain difficult; no benchmark or arbitrary-heavy-tail convergence guarantee is asserted.
