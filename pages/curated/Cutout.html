Cutout

Preprocessing Methods AIMech:Cutout DRAFT
A training-image augmentation method that removes information from a randomly located square region by replacing that region with zeros in the normalized input while keeping the original target.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the original random square masking procedure. It is distinct from unit-level Dropout, input-and-label MixUp, replacement with content from another image, and an augmentation policy that merely includes a cutout operation. A policy implementation can use a different fill value or mask-size convention without redefining the original paper’s exact rule. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For each training image, randomly choose a pixel within the image as the center of a fixed-size square mask. Set pixels in the overlapping image region to zero; the square may extend beyond the image boundaries, so its visible area can be smaller than the specified square. Apply the mask during training with the original label. The paper applies cutout to zero-centered data: zero denotes the normalization origin rather than necessarily a black pixel in the unnormalized image. Mask size is a tunable parameter. At test time, use the complete image without cutout or the activation rescaling associated with dropout. The earlier feature-activation-guided masking investigation and the fully-contained mask used with 50% probability are explored alternatives, not the final random-center rule.
  • supports Source: Improved Regularization of Convolutional Neural Networks with Cutout · Locator: Sections 3–3.2, PDF pp. 3–4; original masking rule and border treatment on p. 3

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A removed region can contain the class-defining information; suitable mask size depends on image scale, data and task.
  • Masking with unchanged labels assumes that the remaining image supports the original target. This is an inductive bias, not a semantic guarantee.
  • The record does not assert a universal mask length, implementation-specific integer rounding, channel convention, benchmark gain or robustness guarantee.
