Randomly Initialized Gradient Descent and Leave-One-Out Sign-Flip Phase Retrieval Analysis

Algorithms AIMech:RandomlyInitializedGradientDescentAndLeaveOneOutSignFlipPhaseRetrievalAnalysis DRAFT
Aliases: Randomly Initialized Wirtinger Flow Global Convergence Analysis, Chen-Chi-Fan-Ma Two-Stage Phase Retrieval Trajectory Analysis, Leave-One-Out and Random-Sign Auxiliary Sequences for Phase Retrieval
The global convergence theory and two-stage trajectory analysis of vanilla Wirtinger Flow gradient descent $x^{t+1} = x^t - \eta_t \nabla f(x^t)$ on the nonconvex fourth-order intensity loss $f(x) = \frac{1}{4m}\sum_{i=1}^m ((a_i^\top x)^2 - y_i)^2$ from a *random initialization* $x^0$ without spectral initialization, truncation, or sample splitting, established by Yuxin Chen, Yuejie Chi, Jianqing Fan, and Cong Ma (2019). Decomposing $x^t$ into signal strength $\alpha_t = x_1^t$ (aligned with $x^\natural = e_1$) and orthogonal component $\beta_t = \|x_{2:n}^t\|_2$, the approximate state evolution $\alpha_{t+1} \approx [1 + 3\eta(1 - (\alpha_t^2+\beta_t^2))]\alpha_t$ and $\beta_{t+1} \approx [1 + \eta(1 - 3(\alpha_t^2+\beta_t^2))]\beta_t$ (Equations 9a–9b) satisfies $\frac{\alpha_{t+1}/\alpha_t}{\beta_{t+1}/\beta_t} = 1 + \frac{2\eta}{1 + \eta(1 - 3(\|x^t\|_2^2))} > 1$, driving exponential growth of the signal-to-noise ratio $\alpha_t/\beta_t$ from $\Theta(1/\sqrt{n\log n})$ to $\Omega(1)$ in $T_0 = O(\log n)$ steps (Stage 1 saddle-escaping phase, Lemma 1) followed by geometric contraction $\mathrm{dist}(x^t, x^\natural) \le O((1-c\eta)^{t-T_0})$ in $O(\log(1/\varepsilon))$ additional steps (Stage 2, Lemma 2) whenever $m \gtrsim n\,\mathrm{poly}\log n$ (Theorems 1 and 3). To control statistical dependence between $x^t$ and $a_i$ during Stage 1 where $|x_1^t| \asymp 1/\sqrt{n}$ is as small as the leave-one-out perturbation, the proof introduces coupled *leave-one-out* ($x^{t,(l)}$), *random-sign* ($x^{t,\mathrm{sgn}}$ with $a_{i,1}^{\mathrm{sgn}} = \xi_i^{\mathrm{sgn}}|a_{i,1}|$), and *leave-one-out random-sign* ($x^{t,\mathrm{sgn},(l)}$) auxiliary trajectories (Algorithms 1–3, Lemmas 4–7).

Mechanism #claim-two-stage-signal-to-noise-ratio-growth-and-logarithmic-saddle-escape
Under Gaussian design $a_i \stackrel{\text{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,I_n)$ with sample size $m \ge C n\log^{13} n$ and random initialization $x^0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, (n\|x^\natural\|_2^2)^{-1} I_n)$ (Theorem 1) or data-scaled spherical initialization $x^0 = \sqrt{\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m y_i}\,u$ with $u\sim\mathcal{S}^{n-1}$ (Theorem 3), Lemma 1 proves that during Stage 1 ($0 \le t < T_0 = O(\log n)$) the signal-to-noise ratio $\alpha_t/\beta_t$ grows by at least a multiplicative factor $1 + c\eta > 1$ at every iteration via the coupled state evolution Equations (9a)–(9b), entering the local contraction region ($|\alpha_{T_0}-1| \le \delta, \beta_{T_0} \le \delta$) in $T_0 \le C\log n$ iterations and thereafter contracting geometrically $\mathrm{dist}(x^t, x^\natural) \le \delta (1 - \eta/2)^{t - T_0}\|x^\natural\|_2$ (Lemma 2).

Mechanism #claim-coupled-leave-one-out-and-random-sign-double-difference-decoupling
Because standard leave-one-out error $\|x^t - x^{t,(l)}\|_2 \lesssim O(\sqrt{\log n/n})\alpha_t$ is no smaller than the initial signal coordinate $|x_1^0| \asymp 1/\sqrt{n\log n}$ during Stage 1, Section 3.2 constructs three auxiliary gradient trajectories — leave-one-out $x^{t,(l)}$ (Algorithm 1), random-sign $x^{t,\mathrm{sgn}}$ using $a_i^{\mathrm{sgn}} = (\xi_i^{\mathrm{sgn}}|a_{i,1}|, a_{i,-1})^\top$ which preserves $(a_i^{\mathrm{sgn}\top} x^\natural)^2 = (a_i^\top x^\natural)^2$ (Algorithm 2, Equation 37), and leave-one-out random-sign $x^{t,\mathrm{sgn},(l)}$ (Algorithm 3) — and proves via Lemmas 4–7 (Equations 40a–40d) that the second-order difference $\|x^t - x^{t,\mathrm{sgn}} - x^{t,(l)} + x^{t,\mathrm{sgn},(l)}\|_2$ is bounded at a strictly higher order $O(\frac{\mathrm{poly}\log n}{n})\alpha_t$, decoupling $x_1^t$ from $a_{i,1}$ across all $O(\log n)$ steps.

Scope #claim-scope-computational-efficiency-over-generic-cubic-regularization-and-noisy-gd
Table 1 (Section 2.2) contrasts the $O(\log n + \log(1/\varepsilon))$ iteration complexity of randomly initialized vanilla gradient descent against generic strict-saddle solvers, which require $O(\mathrm{poly}(n)/\varepsilon^2)$ iterations for perturbed gradient descent or $O(\mathrm{poly}(n)\log\log(1/\varepsilon))$ iterations for trust-region / cubic regularization methods because generic worst-case analyses allow the iterate to visit many saddle points sequentially, whereas randomly initialized phase retrieval GD never approaches the unstable saddle manifold after $t=0$.
  • supports Source: Gradient Descent with Random Initialization: Fast Global Convergence for Nonconvex Phase Retrieval · Locator: Section 1.2 & Section 2.2, Table 1 & Remark 2, pp. 3-8
Limitation #claim-limitation-measure-zero-orthogonal-hyperplane-saddle-trapping-and-gaussian-design
If initialized deterministically on the measure-zero hyperplane orthogonal to the true signal ($\langle x^0, x^\natural\rangle = 0$, i.e. $\alpha_0 = 0$), population gradient descent remains trapped on the orthogonal subspace ($\alpha_t = 0$ for all $t \ge 0$) and reaches an unstable saddle circle $\|x\|_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\|x^\natural\|_2$; furthermore, the random-sign decoupling symmetry relies on i.i.d. Gaussian design distributions.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Fails if initialized deterministically on the measure-zero orthogonal hyperplane $\langle x^0, x^\natural\rangle = 0$ ($\alpha_0 = 0$), and the leave-one-out random-sign proof requires i.i.d. Gaussian sensing vectors with $m \gtrsim n\log^{13} n$.
