Stochastic factorized variational sparse-spectrum Gaussian-process inference

Algorithms AIMech:StochasticFactorizedVariationalSparseSpectrumGaussianProcessInference DRAFT
Aliases: sfVSSGP
The Gal–Turner stochastic extension of factorized variational sparse-spectrum regression that estimates its full-data evidence lower bound with uniformly sampled observation minibatches, scaling likelihood contributions by the population-to-batch ratio while retaining global KL penalties once.

Mechanism #finite-feature-model
For one squared-exponential component, use K features phi_k(x)=sqrt(2*sigma^2/K)*cos(2*pi*w_k^T*L^{-1}*(x-z_k)+b_k), where L=diag(2*pi*l_j), l_j>0, and z_k are feature locations. Independent standardized frequencies have prior N(0,I); phases have uniform priors on [0,2*pi]. For N inputs, Phi has shape N by K. Each output column y_d has likelihood N(Phi*a_d,tau^{-1}*I), tau>0, with independent unit-Gaussian coefficient priors a_d. The variational family separates q(A) from q(omega), omega collecting frequencies and phases, and factors q(omega) across k with diagonal-Gaussian frequency factors and uniform phase intervals [alpha_k,beta_k] with 0<=alpha_k<beta_k<=2*pi. This is a finite-feature hierarchical regression model; integrating its random features does not generally yield the original full-kernel GP.

  • supports Source: Variational Inference for Sparse Spectrum Approximation in Gaussian Process Regression – Appendix · Locator: Propositions 1–2, PDF pp. 1–2
Mechanism #variational-objective
The evidence lower bound is the expected Gaussian log likelihood minus KL(q(A)||p(A)) and KL(q(omega)||p(omega)). For F=E_q[Phi], H=E_q[Phi^T*Phi], q(a_d)=N(m_d,S_d) and response column y_d, its output-d likelihood contribution is -N/2*log(2*pi/tau)-tau/2*[y_d^T*y_d-2*y_d^T*F*m_d+tr(H*(S_d+m_d*m_d^T))]. Gaussian trigonometric moments and uniform-phase integrals give F and H analytically. Distinct feature moments factor under the declared independent spectral factors; a feature’s second moment must be computed as E[phi_k^2], not (E[phi_k])^2.
  • supports Source: Improving the Gaussian Process Sparse Spectrum Approximation by Representing Uncertainty in Frequency Inputs · Locator: Section 4.1, Equation 6 and diagonal/off-diagonal moments, PDF p. 4
  • supports Source: Variational Inference for Sparse Spectrum Approximation in Gaussian Process Regression – Appendix · Locator: Identities 1–3, PDF p. 1
Mechanism #minibatch-estimator
Let y_nd be output d observed at training input x_n and phi_n the K-dimensional column of features at that input. Define L_nd=E_q[log N(y_nd;phi_n^T*a_d,tau^{-1})], the per-observation, per-output expected Gaussian log likelihood under q(a_d)q(omega). For a nonempty batch S of uniformly sampled observations, Equation 9 uses (N/|S|)*sum_{n in S,d} L_nd-KL(q(A)||p(A))-KL(q(omega)||p(omega)), retaining the explicit diagonal coefficient family of fVSSGP. With fixed current variational parameters and the stated uniform sampling, its expectation is the full fVSSGP objective; gradient unbiasedness additionally requires valid differentiation under expectation. Stochastic optimization updates spectral and coefficient variational parameters using this noisy objective or its gradients. A sampled value need not be a lower bound pointwise. Subsampling the already collapsed VSSGP log determinant and matrix inverse would be a different estimator.
  • supports Source: Improving the Gaussian Process Sparse Spectrum Approximation by Representing Uncertainty in Frequency Inputs · Locator: Section 5, Equations 8–9 and Section 7 sfVSSGP label, PDF p. 5
Mechanism #predictive-moments
For a fixed query, let phi be its K-dimensional column feature vector, f=E_q[phi], R=E_q[phi*phi^T], and M=[m_1,...,m_D]. Under q(A)q(omega), the response mean is M^T*f and its covariance is tau^{-1}*I+diag_d[tr(R*S_d)]+M^T*(R-f*f^T)*M. Shared spectral uncertainty can therefore correlate different outputs even though their coefficient factors are independent. These are moments of the variational predictive mixture, which is not generally Gaussian; the latent-output covariance omits only the future-observation-noise term.

  • supports Source: Variational Inference for Sparse Spectrum Approximation in Gaussian Process Regression – Appendix · Locator: Propositions 4–5, PDF pp. 2–3
Scope #implementation-boundary
The inspected author model source computes Gaussian-frequency moments with the phase alpha fixed inside each cosine; its beta argument does not enter those moments and its objective contains frequency KL without a phase KL. Thus that revision implements a fixed-phase conditional variant, not integration over the paper’s positive-width uniform phase factors. Its second-moment diagonal uses the cosine-square expectation. The optimal-coefficient routine retains a full covariance and adds numerical diagonal regularization; source-code reading does not establish an executed result or exact correspondence to every published experiment.

  • supports Source: Improving the Gaussian Process Sparse Spectrum Approximation by Representing Uncertainty in Frequency Inputs · Locator: Experimental setup, fixed randomized phases, Section 8.1, PDF p. 6

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The ELBO and predictive moments concern the specified finite-feature model and variational family. Spectral posterior factorization discards dependencies, and optimization can have local optima. Neither exact full-GP inference nor prevention of overfitting is guaranteed.
  • A single random-phase cosine per frequency differs from the earlier paired cosine/sine SSGP prior. Conditional finite-feature covariances need not be stationary. Frequency uncertainty here is not uncertainty in a supplied test-input distribution.
  • Paper phase integration and fixed phases in the inspected implementation are separate cases. A point mass against a continuous phase or frequency prior is not a finite-KL endpoint of the stated density family; no zero-width or zero-variance ELBO equivalence is asserted.
  • The supplement’s displayed covariance diagonal on its final page appears to substitute squared first moments for second moments. This record follows the main derivation, supplement Identity 3 and inspected implementation instead. Decay of expected features does not mean each random feature vanishes, and far-field variance need not equal the original GP prior variance.
  • All ten main and four supplementary pages were read. Author code was inspected statically only; no model fitting, author-code execution, paid provider or benchmark was run. Fixed finite arithmetic checks selected moment and objective identities, not convergence or empirical calibration. The record remains DRAFT.
  • Uniform-sampling and weight assumptions matter. KL penalties are global and must not receive the likelihood expansion factor. Fixed-batch per-iteration cost excludes total iterations and data storage. The inspected optimizer’s batching flags and integer-division conventions were not validated as an executable reproduction of every sampling regime.
