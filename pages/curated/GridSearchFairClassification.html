Grid-search reduction for fair classification

Algorithms AIMech:GridSearchFairClassification DRAFT
Aliases: Agarwal grid-search fairness reduction
A fairness-aware classification reduction that evaluates cost-sensitive classifier best responses over a grid of Lagrange-multiplier-derived costs and selects a deterministic candidate with a desired empirical accuracy–fairness tradeoff.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The paper’s Section 3.4 grid search is a separate reduction producing deterministic candidates. It shares a cost-sensitive oracle with the randomized exponentiated-gradient reduction but does not inherit that procedure’s mixture or saddle-point stopping rule. It is not generic hyperparameter grid search or the bivariate causal-sensitivity grid procedure. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method-scope
Use the same linear conditional-moment constraint formulation and per-example cost-sensitive oracle as the exponentiated-gradient reduction, but enumerate candidate multiplier values or equivalent cost adjustments. For binary protected groups the paper reduces demographic-parity search to one adjustment dimension and equalized-odds search to two, then compares candidate classifiers to select the desired tradeoff.
  • supports Source: A Reductions Approach to Fair Classification · Locator: Section 3.2 BESTh construction, PDF pp. 4–5; Section 3.4, Examples 7–8, PDF pp. 6–7

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A finite grid and heuristic cost-sensitive learner do not guarantee a globally optimal fair deterministic classifier.
  • The paper explicitly allows lower accuracy or constraint violation when choosing a deterministic candidate; the randomized-mixture guarantees should not be transferred automatically.
  • Grid search is intended for small numbers of effective constraint dimensions and scales poorly as that dimension grows.
  • Fairness is assessed under the specified empirical constraints and sampling context; evaluation on unseen or shifted data is separate.
