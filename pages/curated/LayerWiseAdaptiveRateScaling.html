Layer-wise adaptive rate scaling

Algorithms AIMech:LayerWiseAdaptiveRateScaling DRAFT
A gradient optimization method that multiplies each selected layer or parameter block direction by a scalar based on its current weight L2 norm relative to its gradient L2 norm, together with a trust coefficient and global learning rate. Its stated weight-decay extension scales a combined gradient-and-decay direction using a sum-of-norms denominator and can be composed with momentum.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #block-scalar-adaptation
For a selected layer or parameter block with nonzero weight and gradient L2 norms, the basic local multiplier is lambda_t=eta*norm(w_t)/norm(g_t), where g_t is the current minibatch objective gradient and eta is a positive trust coefficient. A global learning-rate schedule gamma_t supplies the fresh descent increment gamma_t*lambda_t*g_t. This is one scalar per chosen block, rather than a coordinate-wise accumulated gradient-moment denominator or a new network layer.
  • supports Source: Large Batch Training of Convolutional Networks · Locator: Section 4, Equations 4–5, PDF/printed p. 4; comparison with Adam and RMSprop, p. 2
Mechanism #coupled-decay-extension
For nonnegative coupled weight decay beta, Equation 6 uses lambda_t=eta*norm(w_t)/(norm(g_t)+beta*norm(w_t)) and scales the combined direction g_t+beta*w_t. The denominator is the sum of two norms with the decay coefficient, not norm(g_t+beta*w_t). Decay therefore participates in the adaptive multiplier and momentum direction; this is not an independent decoupled shrinkage step.
  • supports Source: Large Batch Training of Convolutional Networks · Locator: Section 4, Equation 6 and Algorithm 1, PDF/printed p. 4
Mechanism #momentum-and-schedule
A consistently indexed momentum composition is v_(t+1)=m*v_t+gamma_t*lambda_t*(g_t+beta*w_t), followed by w_(t+1)=w_t-v_(t+1), starting with zero velocity. Here gamma_t denotes the schedule value applied to the current update. This record retains eta from Equations 5–6: Algorithm 1 lists eta but omits it from its local-multiplier line, computes gamma_t but uses gamma_(t+1), and omits an explicit loop-counter increment. Polynomial decay and warm-up are schedule choices in the examples, not the identity of the norm-ratio adaptation.
  • supports Source: Large Batch Training of Convolutional Networks · Locator: Section 4, Equations 4–6 and Algorithm 1, p. 4; Section 5 and footnote 6, p. 5; ResNet training setup, p. 7
Limitation #relative-increment-boundary
With no decay, nonzero norms and nonnegative gamma_t, the fresh increment has norm gamma_t*eta*norm(w_t) and is unchanged by multiplying g_t by a positive scalar. With beta>=0 and a positive denominator, the triangle inequality bounds the fresh combined-direction increment by that same quantity. These are algebraic properties of the stated formula, not a bound on the whole accumulated momentum displacement, a general stability theorem, or a solution of a trust-region subproblem.
  • supports Source: Large Batch Training of Convolutional Networks · Locator: Section 4, Equations 4–6 and momentum composition, PDF/printed p. 4; mathematical consequences qualified by the stated conditions
Limitation #zero-norm-and-implementation-boundaries
The primary formula leaves a zero denominator undefined and supplies no epsilon or fallback policy. At zero weight norm with a positive denominator it gives zero fresh increment; existing momentum can still move the block. Bias/normalization exclusions, parameter grouping, clipping and zero-norm handling must be specified by a concrete implementation and cannot be inferred from the method name. The source discusses weights and biases separately, but does not establish a universal exclusion rule.
  • supports Source: Large Batch Training of Convolutional Networks · Locator: Section 4, Table 2, Equations 5–6 and Algorithm 1, p. 4; separate weight/bias local-rate plots, Figure 2, p. 5
Limitation #scope-and-evidence-limits
The primary study targets large-batch convolutional-network training with synchronous minibatch gradients, global schedules, momentum and model-specific experimental settings. Its reported accuracy depends on architecture, batch size, schedule and training duration. The norm-ratio rule does not by itself guarantee convergence, eliminate every vanishing/exploding-gradient problem, preserve accuracy at arbitrary batch size, or establish distributed speedup. This record curates the mechanism without importing a numerical benchmark or claiming a source training reproduction.
  • supports Source: Large Batch Training of Convolutional Networks · Locator: Abstract and Sections 1–2, pp. 1–2; Sections 3–7, Tables 1–5 and Figures 1–5, pp. 3–7

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Bare LARS is not asserted as an alias because AIMech already uses it for Least-angle regression, an unrelated equiangular regression algorithm. This record uses the expanded optimizer name.
  • The mechanism follows Equations 5–6 of arXiv1708.03888v3 and an explicitly consistent current-step indexing. The displayed Algorithm 1 omits eta in one line and has schedule/counter inconsistencies; no literal pseudocode reproduction or author implementation audit is claimed.
  • A zero-norm fallback, epsilon, clipping rule, bias/normalization exclusion and precise parameter grouping are implementation decisions not specified by the inspected formula. Later similarly named optimizer variants require separate source and identity assessments.
  • The fresh-increment norm statement does not bound momentum accumulation or establish universal stability, scale invariance of an entire network, convergence or generalization.
  • Source prose, printed ratios and experimental tables contain inconsistencies retained in the discovery notes. This record makes no universal no-accuracy-loss assertion or numerical performance claim.
  • The complete eight-page September 13, 2017 technical-report preprint was inspected. Later Berkeley/ICLR versions and the linked author implementation were not treated as identical evidence. No training, source software execution or empirical evaluation occurred.
