Aliases: FAA
An augmentation-policy search procedure that evaluates candidate transformations on held-out training examples using a fixed classifier trained on another split, combines high-scoring policies across splits and uses them for final classifier training.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method
Form repeated stratified train/exploration splits. Train a classifier on each model-training split, then hold its weights fixed while exploring augmentation policies on that split’s held-out labeled examples. A subpolicy composes image operations with individual application probabilities and magnitudes; the published search uses continuous probability and normalized magnitude values in [0,1]. Use Bayesian hyperparameter optimization, implemented with a tree-structured Parzen estimator, to propose policies according to their predictive scores. The paper frames the goal as maximizing augmented-split accuracy and states that its practical objective minimizes categorical cross-entropy. Select top policies from each search stage and split, merge their subpolicies, and train the final classifier on the full training data with the selected augmentation. The published configuration uses two operations per subpolicy and five subpolicies per candidate policy, but these are protocol choices. The method uses classifier predictions as a proxy for matching distributions; it does not directly compute a distributional divergence. Its essential efficiency distinction is reuse of fixed model weights during candidate scoring, while initial and final classifier training are still required.

Mechanism #implementation-profile
The pinned search.py evaluates a saved model in evaluation mode on multiple stochastic augmented views of held-out examples. For non-singleton validation batches, its default top1_valid reward is the mean indicator that any view of each example is classified correctly. Its logged minus_loss has a different reduction: each unreduced cross-entropy array has shape(batch,), concatenation flattens views and examples into one vector, and min(axis=0) yields one minimum for the whole batch. The Accumulator sums the negatives of those batch-wide scalar minima and divides by the total example count. This is neither ordinary mean cross-entropy nor a mean of per-example best-view losses. Search and selection use top1_valid by default, not this logged loss. data.py samples a subpolicy for each image and keeps baseline training transforms for these held-out views. The active augment_list(False) contains 15 operations, with SamplePairing commented out, while the paper lists 16. The inspected search loop performs one repetition per split rather than the paper’s reported two. These are version-specific conventions, not silently combined definitions.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • High predictive scores on augmented held-out examples do not prove label preservation, equality of data distributions, global optimality or improved final generalization.
  • Section3.2.1 describes split-model training without augmentation, whereas Section3.3 specifies default augmentation rules. The pinned data loader retains baseline crop/flip transformations; the record does not assert an absence of all augmentation.
  • The paper’s Equation4 prints expected min(loss−threshold,0), a nonpositive quantity. It should not be silently identified with the usual nonnegative improvement or assigned an unstated maximization direction. The method-level record identifies TPE search without asserting an author-issued correction.
  • Paper and code differ in scoring, active operation count and search repetitions. No claim that the inspected code exactly reproduces the paper experiment or that any-view correctness is deployable test accuracy is made.
  • Search settings, baseline transforms, proxy/full-data choices and policy transfer depend on the task. No speedup, benchmark outcome or author-code execution is claimed.
  • The inspected evaluator squeezes correctness arrays before taking their length; this code-profile description is scoped to non-singleton validation batches and does not assert successful execution for every batch size. The logged loss also depends on batch grouping, unlike an ordinary mean of per-example losses.
