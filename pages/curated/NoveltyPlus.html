Novelty+ SAT heuristic

Algorithms AIMech:NoveltyPlus DRAFT
Aliases: Novelty+
A Novelty extension that, at each SAT search step, chooses a random variable from a randomly selected unsatisfied clause with a fixed walk probability and otherwise uses Novelty’s recency-conditioned best/second-best selector.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is Hoos’s 1999 Novelty+ random-walk extension. The plus sign identifies an additional per-step random-walk branch, distinct from Novelty’s conditional noise parameter and from later adaptive, diversification or look-ahead variants. It does not require a periodic schedule or an SKC zero-break priority. The theorem concerns asymptotic model finding on satisfiable formulas, not probably-approximately-correct learning or a finite UNSAT decision. Mechanism #method
Maintain a complete truth assignment and variable flip timestamps. If the formula is not satisfied, randomly choose an unsatisfied clause. With fixed probability w in [0,1], choose a variable uniformly from that clause and flip it. Otherwise rank clause variables by the global post-flip violation count, breaking score ties in favor of the least recently flipped variable. If the best is not the most recently flipped variable in the clause, select it; otherwise select second-best with probability p and best with probability 1−p, where p is in [0,1]. Update the flipped variable’s timestamp and continue under the declared stopping policy. The walk branch is available regardless of scores, recency or zero-break candidates. With w=1 this is the pure clause-first random walk; with w=0 it is the underlying Novelty selector. Any fixed w>0 satisfies the source’s random-walk premise once all remaining move cases are well-defined.

Scope #asymptotic-model-finding
For a finite satisfiable CNF and a well-defined remaining move policy, a fixed positive lower bound on the probability of a uniform random-clause/random-variable step at every search step gives success probability approaching one as the number of steps tends to infinity. Hoos1999 Theorem 2 establishes this via a positive probability of a sequence reducing Hamming distance to a model. The conclusion does not give a polynomial or practically small runtime, a finite-run guarantee, or an UNSAT certificate.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The asymptotic guarantee requires a strictly positive walk probability at every step, bounded below independently of elapsed search history. A scheduled-only override or a probability that tends to zero does not automatically satisfy that premise. At w=0 the random-walk argument no longer applies.
  • The guarantee concerns finding a model on a finite satisfiable formula in the limit of continued search. A finite budget can miss an existing model and cannot certify UNSAT. The source’s lower bound can be extremely small; no polynomial or practical runtime follows.
  • The walk probability w is separate from the inner selector’s noise parameter p. The reported experimental w=0.01 is not a defining or universally optimal constant. Optional outer restarts and cutoffs must be specified separately.
  • Empty clauses, unit clauses lacking a second candidate, initial age ties, duplicate literals and timestamp/reset conventions require a well-defined implementation. The general random-walk proof does not certify undocumented choices in the short selector description. No author solver or benchmark was executed.
