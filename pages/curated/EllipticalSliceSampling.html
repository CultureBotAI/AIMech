Elliptical slice sampling

Algorithms AIMech:EllipticalSliceSampling DRAFT
Aliases: ESS
A Markov-chain update for a target factored into a Gaussian prior and a likelihood, which performs an angular slice search along the ellipse defined by the current state and an independent prior draw.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the full-circle default algorithm in Murray et al. Figure 2 for a fixed Gaussian prior covariance. It differs from a fixed-angle Gaussian autoregressive Metropolis proposal and from scalar interval stepping-out or doubling. ESS here denotes this sampler, not the effective-sample-size diagnostic. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For pi(f) proportional to Normal(f;0,Sigma)L(f), draw nu from Normal(0,Sigma), then log y=log L(f)+log u for independent u uniform on (0,1). Draw theta uniformly on (0,2pi) and initialize [theta_min,theta_max]=[theta−2pi,theta]. Test f_prime=f cos(theta)+nu sin(theta); accept when log L(f_prime)>log y. Otherwise shrink the bracket at theta on the side determined by its sign relative to zero, redraw theta uniformly from that bracket, and repeat with the same nu, f and threshold. The randomly positioned bracket retains the angle zero corresponding to the current state. Gaussian rotational invariance and the reversible bracket construction give the target-invariant transition.
  • supports Source: Elliptical slice sampling · Locator: Sections 2.1–2.3, Figure 2 and correctness proof, printed pp. 542–545 / PDF pp. 2–5

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A Gaussian prior draw and likelihood evaluation are required. A nonzero prior mean must be handled by centering the ellipse; an arbitrary non-Gaussian prior is outside this formula.
  • Fixing the initial bracket deterministically to [−pi,pi] is not the proved algorithm; the paper explicitly explains why its random placement matters.
  • The algorithm may reject multiple internal angle trials before returning an update. Its lack of an outer Metropolis rejection is not a claim of one likelihood evaluation or independent retained states.
  • The default full-circle algorithm avoids a user-specified proposal scale, but covariance, model hyperparameters and optional narrower angular variants still involve choices.
  • Strong offsets, covariance structure and likelihood geometry can impede exploration. No universal mixing rate, finite-run accuracy or timing advantage is asserted.
