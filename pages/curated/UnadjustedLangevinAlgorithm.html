Unadjusted Langevin algorithm

Algorithms AIMech:UnadjustedLangevinAlgorithm DRAFT
Aliases: ULA
An approximate sampling algorithm that discretizes overdamped Langevin dynamics by an explicit gradient drift and Gaussian noise without a Metropolis acceptance correction.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the full-gradient Euler discretization for a differentiable continuous target. It is not deterministic gradient descent, stochastic-gradient Langevin dynamics, the exact continuous-time diffusion or Metropolis-adjusted Langevin sampling. A fixed-step chain generally has a different invariant law from its intended target. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method
For a proper target pi(x) proportional to exp(−U(x)), with differentiable U, update X_(k+1)=X_k−gamma_(k+1) grad U(X_k)+sqrt(2 gamma_(k+1)) Z_(k+1), where the Z values are independent standard Gaussian vectors and gamma is positive. There is no accept/reject step. Constant and predetermined decreasing step-size schedules are both covered by the source. Equivalently, writing h=2 gamma and grad log pi=−grad U gives mean x+(h/2)grad log pi(x) and noise covariance hI. This equivalence is an explicit change of notation, not a separate sampling method.
  • supports Source: Non-asymptotic convergence analysis for the Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm · Locator: Introduction, Equations (1)–(2) and constant/decreasing step-size discussion, PDF/printed pp. 1–2; Section 2, assumptions and kernel, pp. 3–4
Limitation #discrete-bias-and-instability
Roberts and Tweedie distinguish ULA’s generally biased stationary law from the intended continuous-diffusion target and show that discretization can qualitatively change recurrence and convergence behavior. These are conditional results, not a blanket claim that every ULA run is unstable.
  • supports Source: Exponential convergence of Langevin distributions and their discrete approximations · Locator: Section 3.1, printed pp. 351–354 / PDF pp. 12–15

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • At a fixed positive step size, a stationary distribution, when it exists, generally differs from pi. Running longer does not by itself remove that discretization bias.
  • The paper’s convergence results have regularity, drift and step-size assumptions. Merely taking gamma to zero does not establish convergence for every target or schedule.
  • Discrete Euler dynamics can be unstable even when the continuous diffusion is well behaved. Gradients and noise scaling both depend on the step-size convention.
  • Roberts and Tweedie’s introductory ULA display on printed p. 345 / PDF p. 6 appears to omit h in the drift. This record uses the unambiguous Durmus–Moulines Equation (2), consistent with the h-scaled drift in the older paper’s later analysis; it does not claim a published erratum was found.
  • No minibatch gradient approximation, preconditioning, projection, taming or empirical accuracy bound is included in this record.
