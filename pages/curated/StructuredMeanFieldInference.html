Structured mean-field inference

Mathematical Methods AIMech:StructuredMeanFieldInference DRAFT
Aliases: Structured mean-field approximation
A variational inference method that approximates a target distribution with a tractable family retaining selected dependencies, choosing its parameters to reduce the approximation-to-target Kullback–Leibler divergence.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record covers the tractable-substructure mean-field construction exemplified by Saul and Jordan, including partially factorized modules and the qualifications for a tractable correlated skeleton. It is broader than the maintained factorial-HMM-specific algorithm and distinct from a fully factorized approximation that removes every intervariable dependence. It is an inference method, not a graphical-model architecture, all variational inference, a particular Monte Carlo gradient estimator or a variational autoencoder. Fully factorized mean field is a limiting restricted family when retained substructures reduce to individual variables; that inclusion does not make the two concepts exact aliases. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For a fixed normalized target p(s)=exp(F(s))/Z and tractable q, maximize L(q)=E_q[F(s)]+H(q), so log Z-L(q)=KL(q||p). The source’s binary pairwise modular construction retains all intramodule interactions J and factorizes q across modules, replacing omitted intermodule interactions K by adjustable unary fields H. Expectations within a module are computed exactly using its tractable algorithm; expectations between modules factorize. A module’s field update is H_i^mu=h_i^mu+sum_(nu!=mu),j K_ij^(mu,nu) E_q[s_j^nu]. Holding all other factors fixed, recomputing that module with these fields gives a coordinate variational update. A chain module can use forward–backward while retaining within-chain correlations. If omitted links connect variables within the same correlated skeleton, the simple intermodule rule no longer follows: the source’s stationarity equations contain covariance and triple-moment terms. Auxiliary binary link variables provide another construction that induces a tractable core-versus-link factorization.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Tractability of retained substructures and evaluation of the required expectations must be established for the chosen family. Compact notation or a sparse-looking graph does not by itself ensure efficient inference.
  • The optimized bound concerns log Z. It is an observed-data log-likelihood bound only when Z is that likelihood under the specified unnormalized posterior construction. A generic partition function is not automatically a probability of observations.
  • Coordinate ascent with exact factor optimization can improve the bound; arbitrary simultaneous field updates need not. The objective can have multiple stationary points, and neither global optimality nor accurate posterior marginals is guaranteed.
  • Omitted links inside one correlated substructure require the covariance/triple-moment terms of Equation (19); simply substituting independent means would change the derivation.
  • The auxiliary-variable equality in Equations (20)–(21) omits a coupling-dependent constant. For one binary edge K, a=acosh(exp(2*abs(K)))/2, b=sign(K)*a and C=exp(-abs(K))/2 give exp(K*s_i*s_j)=C*sum_w exp((a*s_i+b*s_j)*w). Dropping C preserves a normalized marginal but shifts the raw log partition; evidence bounds must retain the offset.
  • The context-sensitive HMM example has boundary conventions and a delayed observation term that need care; the displayed unary-field formula is not adopted as an implementation-ready recipe. Its synthetic illustration does not establish general weak-coupling accuracy or benchmark superiority.
