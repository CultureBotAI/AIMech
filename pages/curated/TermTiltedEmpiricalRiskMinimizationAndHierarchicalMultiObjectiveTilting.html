TERM (Tilted Empirical Risk Minimization and Hierarchical Multi-Objective Tilting)

Aliases: TERM, Tilted Empirical Risk Minimization, Hierarchical Multi-Objective Tilted Empirical Risk Minimization, t-Tilted Empirical Risk Minimization
A parametric family of exponentially tilted risk minimization objectives and batch/stochastic first-order solvers (Li, Beirami, Sanjabi, & Smith 2021, Sections 1-4, Equations 2-4, Lemmas 1-7, Theorems 1-10, Algorithms 1-4) that generalizes standard Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) via a real-valued tilt hyperparameter t in R \ {0}: R_tilde(t; theta) := (1 / t) log((1 / N) sum_{i in [N]} exp(t f(x_i; theta))) (Equation 2), with continuous extension R_tilde(0; theta) = R_bar(theta) = (1 / N) sum_{i in [N]} f(x_i; theta), R_tilde(-inf; theta) = min_{i in [N]} f(x_i; theta), and R_tilde(+inf; theta) = max_{i in [N]} f(x_i; theta) (Lemma 2). Its tilted gradient dynamically reweights individual sample gradients exponentially by their relative loss: grad_theta R_tilde(t; theta) = sum_{i in [N]} w_i(t; theta) grad_theta f(x_i; theta) with w_i(t; theta) = exp(t f(x_i; theta)) / sum_{j in [N]} exp(t f(x_j; theta)) = (1 / N) exp(t (f(x_i; theta) - R_tilde(t; theta))) (Lemma 1, Equation 3), magnifying high-loss samples to reduce loss variance and promote fairness when t > 0 or suppressing high-loss outliers when t < 0. For compound problems involving both group fairness/imbalance and sample-level noise across groups g in [G], hierarchical multi-objective TERM optimizes J_tilde(t, tau; theta) := (1 / t) log((1 / N) sum_{g in [G]} |g| exp(t R_tilde_g(tau; theta))) where R_tilde_g(tau; theta) := (1 / tau) log((1 / |g|) sum_{x in g} exp(tau f(x; theta))) (Equation 4).

Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from q-FFL (AIMech:QFairFederatedLearningAlphaFairResourceAllocation, Li et al. 2020), which raises positive losses to a polynomial power (1/(q+1)) F_k^{q+1}(w) for q >= 0 only and cannot take negative exponents to suppress outliers or nest hierarchical group/sample tilts; and distinct from Agnostic Federated Learning (AIMech:AgnosticFederatedLearningMinimaxDistributionallyRobustOptimization, Mohri et al. 2019), which solves a hard minimax saddle-point problem min_w max_{lambda in Lambda} L_lambda(w) rather than a smooth, tunable cumulant-generating log-sum-exp objective spanning min-loss (t -> -inf), average-loss (t -> 0), and max-loss (t -> +inf).

Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #r496-claim-1
Under generalized linear model and strict-saddle assumptions (Assumptions 1-4), as the tilt parameter t in R increases, the t-tilted solution theta_breve(t) in argmin_theta R_tilde(t; theta) monotonically reduces empirical loss variance (d/dt var(f(theta_breve(t))) < 0, Theorem 4), monotonically increases the cosine similarity between the loss vector f(theta_breve(t)) and the all-ones vector 1_N (d/dt s(f(theta_breve(t)), 1_N) > 0, Theorem 5), and increases the Shannon entropy H(w(tau; theta_breve(t))) of the tilted gradient weights (Theorem 6), while trading off average loss against max-loss for t > 0 (Theorem 2) and against min-loss for t < 0 (Theorem 3).
  • supports Source: Tilted Empirical Risk Minimization · Locator: Section 2, pp. 3-4; Appendix E, pp. 23-29, Theorems 1-8
Mechanism #r496-claim-2
Hierarchical multi-objective TERM J_tilde(t, tau; theta) (Equation 4) recovers sample-level TERM R_tilde(t; theta) when tau = t (Lemma 7), has closed-form factored gradient weights w_{g,x}(t, tau; theta) = w_g(t, tau; theta) w_{g,x}(tau; theta) (Lemma 6, Equations 44-52), and is solved in the stochastic setting (Algorithm 2) by maintaining a tilted moving-average estimator R_tilde_{g,tau} <- (1/tau) log((1 - lambda) exp(tau R_tilde_{g,tau}) + lambda exp(tau R_tilde_{B,tau})) and sampling groups from a Gumbel-Softmax distribution with logits R_tilde_{g,tau} + (1/t) log |g| and temperature 1/t.
  • supports Source: Tilted Empirical Risk Minimization · Locator: Sections 3-4, pp. 4-5, Equation 4, Algorithm 1; Appendix C, pp. 19-20, Lemmas 6-7; Appendix H, pp. 34-35, Algorithms 2-4
Limitation #r496-claim-3
For t < 0, R_tilde(t; theta) can become non-convex with spurious local minima even when individual losses f(x_i; theta) are strongly convex (requiring continuation from t = 0) and can overfit to structured or adversarial outliers (Section 5.1 & Appendix H.1, Figure 14); for t -> +inf, the local smoothness constant beta(t) scales linearly as O(t) (Lemma 4, Equation 31), slowing gradient-descent convergence rate (1 - beta_min / (C_1 + C_2 t))^m (Theorem 13).
  • supports Source: Tilted Empirical Risk Minimization · Locator: Appendix B, pp. 17-18, Lemma 4; Appendix H.1, pp. 35-36, Theorem 13, Figure 8; Appendix I.2, p. 39, Figure 14
Application #r496-claim-4
Applied to robust regression on Drug Discovery under 20%-80% target corruption, robust DNN classification on CIFAR-10 with noisy labels and crowd-sourced spammers, fair PCA on Default Credit, fair federated learning on Vehicle sensor data, and hierarchical class-imbalance plus 30% label-noise classification on HIV-1 (achieving 0.778 rare-class and 0.900 overall accuracy with TERM_sc vs. 0.240 and 0.831 for ERM at 1:20 imbalance).
  • supports Source: Tilted Empirical Risk Minimization · Locator: Section 5, pp. 5-8, Tables 1-3, Figures 3-5; Appendix I.2, pp. 38-41, Tables 5-8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Negative tilt values (t < 0) induce non-convexity even when the base loss is strongly convex and fail under structured or adversarial outlier corruption (Appendix H.1 & Appendix I.2, Figure 14).
  • Large positive tilt values (t >> 0) increase the objective's smoothness constant linearly with t (Lemma 4), requiring smaller step sizes O(1/t) (Theorem 13).
