Full-echo Q-routing

Algorithms AIMech:FullEchoQRouting DRAFT
An information-exploration modification of tabular Q-routing that queries immediate neighbors whenever a node needs a routing decision and uses their destination-time estimates to adjust every corresponding neighbor entry, with feedback carried on a separate channel assumed not to contribute to congestion in the source model.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record selects the NIPS source-defined Full-echo information policy: decision-triggered immediate-neighbor querying and adjustment of all corresponding local delivery-time estimates. It is distinct from selected-neighbor baseline QRouting, measured-trip AntNet and ant-based telephone routing. The source does not fully specify alternative-link delay measurements or a complete backup/message protocol. Existing owner 267 is reused. Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Scope #source-defined-extension
The named Full-echo modification retains Q-routing’s local table of estimated delivery times indexed by destination and neighboring node. In the source notation, Q_x(d,y) estimates the delivery time from node x to destination d through neighbor y, including the local queue time. Full-echo changes the information-exploration and entry-adjustment policy; it is not a synonym for the original selected-neighbor-only baseline.
  • supports Source: Packet Routing in Dynamically Changing Networks: A Reinforcement Learning Approach · Locator: Section 2, PDF p. 2 / proceedings p. 672, definition of Q_x(d,y); Section 3.2, PDF p. 6 / proceedings p. 676, greedy-update limitation and named modification.
Mechanism #decision-triggered-neighbor-query
Whenever a node using Full-echo needs to make a routing decision, it sends information requests to its immediate neighbors. Each neighbor returns a single scalar giving that neighbor’s current estimate of the total time to the destination. These responses are estimates supplied by neighboring learners, not observations of complete trips along every alternative route.
  • supports Source: Packet Routing in Dynamically Changing Networks: A Reinforcement Learning Approach · Locator: Section 3.2, PDF p. 6 / proceedings p. 676, paragraph beginning “Instead of sending actual packets” and the description of each scalar response.
Mechanism #all-neighbor-estimate-adjustment
The returned estimates are used to adjust the local Q_x(d,y) value for each neighboring node y. This addresses the greedy baseline’s failure to refresh unused routes whose delivery times were overestimated. The source specifies this all-neighbor information policy without supplying a distinct algebraic Full-echo backup or a measurement rule for an untraversed link’s delay.
  • supports Source: Packet Routing in Dynamically Changing Networks: A Reinforcement Learning Approach · Locator: Section 3.2, PDF p. 6 / proceedings p. 676, the unused-route overestimate example and the sentence specifying adjustment for each neighbor.
Mechanism #information-exploration-channel
Full-echo obtains information about alternatives by querying neighbors instead of deliberately forwarding actual data packets in random directions for exploration. The returned scalars use a separate channel that does not contribute to network congestion in the authors’ model. This is an explicit modeling assumption about feedback traffic, not a general zero-cost communication guarantee.
  • supports Source: Packet Routing in Dynamically Changing Networks: A Reinforcement Learning Approach · Locator: Section 3.2, PDF p. 6 / proceedings p. 676, random-traffic congestion discussion and the separate-channel qualification in the Full-echo paragraph.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The defining paragraph does not specify a separate all-neighbor update equation, how local or untraversed-link delay is obtained, exact query/response/update ordering, tie rules or a complete executable protocol. The baseline update uses experienced queue and transmission delays on the selected link; copying that observed-link target to every alternative would add an unsupported rule.
  • The separate feedback channel is non-congesting in the authors’ model. This does not establish zero bandwidth, latency, processing overhead or resource consumption in a deployed communication network.
  • The distributed environment changes with traffic and with the routing decisions made by other learners. The source does not provide a stationary single-agent Q-learning convergence theorem, universal policy-stability result, optimality certificate or loop-freedom guarantee for this modification.
  • The reported low-load improvement and high-load oscillation concern the paper’s simplified simulation and traffic conditions. They do not establish uniform superiority, universal instability or a numerical benchmark result reproduced here. Refreshing more estimates does not by itself guarantee better end-to-end behavior.
  • The July 1993 CMU-CS-93-165 report is earlier baseline context and does not specify a Full-echo procedure in its eight inspected pages. Its Q_x(y,d) argument order, displayed target without an explicit transmission term and experimental learning rate 0.7 are not silently combined with the selected NIPS account’s Q_x(d,y), explicit queue-plus-transmission term and learning rate usually 0.5 in its experiments. These are source-version boundaries, not repaired equations.
  • This record selects the original Full-echo modification rather than baseline Q-routing, AntNet, source-directed ant-based telephone routing or later predictive, dual, confidence-based and adaptive-parameter routing extensions. A shared reinforcement-learning relation does not make those mechanisms equivalent or resolve their separate scopes.
  • The declared method evidence is the official eight-page NIPS 1993 archive paper; the author CV lists the NIPS 6 proceedings publication in 1994. The separate July 1993 report and workshop references do not establish an independently verified priority date or identical method version. Full native text and selected formula/method images were inspected, without author-code execution, reconstruction of an unspecified protocol, simulator replication or certification of numerical figure values.
