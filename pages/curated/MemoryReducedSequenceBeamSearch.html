Memory-reduced best-first beam search for sequence decoding

Algorithms AIMech:MemoryReducedSequenceBeamSearch DRAFT
A capacity-limited variant of per-length best-first sequence beam search that, when its chosen total queue capacity is reached, removes the worst-scoring active hypothesis from the earliest active sequence length.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the memory-reduced variant in Meister, Vieira and Cotterell Section 3.3. Its eviction first chooses the earliest active length, so it differs from globally removing the worst-ranked hypothesis. It also differs from the base per-length best-first procedure, which can retain hypotheses at several lengths without this extra capacity pruning. Mechanism #search-procedure
Organize active hypotheses in queues for their sequence lengths and prioritize lengths by their best available score. Apply the base per-length beam limits and an additional total active-queue capacity. To make room, evict the minimum-scoring item in the earliest nonempty length queue; then continue decoding the surviving agenda.

Limitation #capacity-boundary
The variant adds pruning to the base decoder by limiting queue capacity and targeting the earliest active time step for eviction. The paper presents it as a separate memory-reduced method, not as the unchanged monotone-beam equivalence construction.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The finite beam is an approximation to exact sequence-model decoding; equality to a specified beam procedure is not global MAP optimality.
  • Sequence length limits, EOS handling and compatible deterministic tie conventions are part of the comparison. Arbitrary implementations with different stopping or tie rules need not return identical hypotheses.
  • Queue counts are counts of retained hypotheses, not total bytes including neural states, model parameters, backpointers and temporary successors. No numerical speedup or benchmark-quality claim is curated.
  • Additional capacity eviction can remove paths retained by the unmodified beam procedure, so its same-output guarantee is not inherited. The memory/quality tradeoff is empirical and problem dependent.
  • The queue cap does not bound the entire decoder or neural model memory. The earliest-length eviction is not a global worst-score eviction rule.
  • No guarantee for arbitrary nonmonotonic scores or globally optimal sequence decoding is asserted.
