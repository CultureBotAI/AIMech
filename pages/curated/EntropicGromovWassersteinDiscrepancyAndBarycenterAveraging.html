Entropic Gromov-Wasserstein Discrepancy and Barycenter Averaging

Algorithms AIMech:EntropicGromovWassersteinDiscrepancyAndBarycenterAveraging DRAFT
Aliases: Entropic Gromov-Wasserstein Distance and Barycenter, Gromov-Wasserstein Kernel and Distance Matrix Averaging, Peyré-Cuturi-Solomon Entropic Gromov-Wasserstein Algorithm
Entropic Gromov-Wasserstein Discrepancy and Barycenter Averaging is a metric-measure alignment and relational matrix averaging methodology introduced by Peyré, Cuturi, and Solomon (2016) that compares and averages pairs of intra-domain distance or similarity matrices (C, p) in R^{N x N} x Sigma_N and (C_bar, q) in R^{M x M} x Sigma_M across incomparable ambient spaces by solving the entropically regularized non-convex quadratic assignment problem GW_epsilon(C, C_bar, p, q) = min_{T in U(p,q)} E(C, C_bar, T) - epsilon H(T), where E(C, C_bar, T) = sum_{i,j,k,l} L(C_{i,k}, C_bar_{j,l}) T_{i,j} T_{k,l}. Proposition 1 proves that for any separable divergence L(a, b) = f_1(a) + f_2(b) - h_1(a) h_2(b) (including squared Euclidean loss L(a,b) = (1/2)|a-b|^2 and Kullback-Leibler divergence L(a,b) = a log(a/b) - a + b), the 4-way tensor contraction L(C, C_bar) (x) T reduces from O(N^2 M^2) to O(N^2 M + N M^2) matrix multiplications c_{C, C_bar}(p, q) - h_1(C) T h_2(C_bar)^T (Equations 5-6). Proposition 2 and Algorithm 1 solve the entropic GW problem via projected gradient / Sinkhorn iterations T^{(k+1)} = Proj_{U(p,q)}^{KL}(K^{(k)}) with K^{(k)} = exp(-(L(C, C_bar) (x) T^{(k)}) / epsilon), and Propositions 3-5 (Algorithm 2) compute Gromov-Wasserstein barycenters of S matrices (C_s, p_s) via closed-form first-order updates C = (1 / p p^T) odot sum_s lambda_s T_s^T C_s T_s (under L_2 loss, preserving the positive semidefinite cone) and C = exp((1 / p p^T) odot sum_s lambda_s T_s^T log(C_s) T_s) (under KL loss, preserving infinitely divisible kernels).
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Distinct from standard linear Wasserstein optimal transport (`AIMech:SinkhornDistancesAndEntropicRegularizedOptimalTransport`) and linear Wasserstein barycenters (`AIMech:FastWassersteinBarycentersAndSmoothedDualSubgradientDescent`), which require a cross-domain ground cost matrix M_{ij} between points in a shared ambient space. Specifically captures the Peyré, Cuturi, and Solomon (2016) entropic regularization of Mémoli's Gromov-Wasserstein discrepancy (Equations 2-4), the Proposition 1 O(N^2 M + N M^2) separable-loss factorization, the Proposition 2 / Algorithm 1 iterative Sinkhorn solver, and the Propositions 3-5 / Algorithm 2 closed-form L_2 and KL relational barycenter updates.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #claim-01
For any similarity loss of the separable form L(a,b) = f_1(a) + f_2(b) - h_1(a) h_2(b) and any coupling T in U(p,q), Proposition 1 proves that the four-way tensor contraction L(C, C_bar) (x) T decomposes as c_{C, C_bar}(p, q) - h_1(C) T h_2(C_bar)^T where c_{C, C_bar}(p,q) = f_1(C) p 1_M^T + 1_N q^T f_2(C_bar)^T is independent of T, reducing each gradient evaluation and Sinkhorn linearized cost matrix construction (Proposition 2, Algorithm 1) from O(N^2 M^2) to O(N^2 M + N M^2) operations.
  • supports Source: Gromov-Wasserstein Averaging of Kernel and Distance Matrices · Locator: Section 2.1, Proposition 1, Proposition 2, Equations 5-9, and Algorithm 1, pp. 3-4
  • supports Source: Gromov-Wasserstein Averaging of Kernel and Distance Matrices (Crossref metadata) · Locator: Bibliographic metadata and abstract for ICML 2016 PMLR 48:2664-2672
Scope #claim-02
For fixed couplings (T_s)_{s=1}^S in U(p, p_s), Propositions 3, 4, and 5 prove that the exact Gromov-Wasserstein barycenter matrix C minimizing sum_{s=1}^S lambda_s E(C, C_s, T_s) is given in closed form by C = (1 / p p^T) odot sum_s lambda_s T_s^T C_s T_s under L_2 loss (which automatically belongs to the positive semidefinite cone S_+^N whenever each C_s in S_+^{N_s}) and by C = exp((1 / p p^T) odot sum_s lambda_s T_s^T log(C_s) T_s) under Kullback-Leibler loss (which preserves conditionally positive semidefinite logarithms and infinitely divisible kernels).
  • supports Source: Gromov-Wasserstein Averaging of Kernel and Distance Matrices · Locator: Section 2.2, Propositions 3, 4, and 5, Equations 13-15, and Algorithm 2, pp. 4-5
Limitation #claim-03
The Gromov-Wasserstein objective E(C, C_bar, T) is a non-convex quadratic form in the coupling T (a continuous relaxation of the NP-hard quadratic assignment problem), so the entropic Sinkhorn iteration (Algorithm 1) and block-coordinate barycenter alternation (Algorithm 2) are only guaranteed to converge to stationary points rather than global minima, and the O(N^3) matrix multiplication cost per iteration remains a bottleneck for very large graphs or meshes.
  • supports Source: Gromov-Wasserstein Averaging of Kernel and Distance Matrices · Locator: Section 2.1, Non-convexity discussion and Remark 1, pp. 3-4; Conclusion, p. 8

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Solves a non-convex quadratic assignment objective where iterative Sinkhorn linearization is only guaranteed to reach local stationary points, and each iteration requires O(N^2 M + N M^2) matrix multiplications.
