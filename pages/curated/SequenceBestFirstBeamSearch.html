Best-first beam search with per-length budgets

Algorithms AIMech:SequenceBestFirstBeamSearch DRAFT
The sequence-decoding beam procedure of Meister, Vieira and Cotterell that prioritizes active hypotheses by decreasing accumulated score, breaks score ties by shorter length, and limits accepted expansions to k hypotheses at each sequence length without a future-score heuristic.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This is the 2020 sequence-decoding meaning of best-first beam search. Its per-length budget is distinct from AIMech:GlobalFrontierBeamSearch, which caps the entire open list. The qualified name avoids treating those different pruning rules as exact synonyms. The h=0 procedure is a specialization of the paper’s A-star beam framework. Claims & Grounded Evidence

3 Claims
Mechanism #search-procedure
Maintain a score-prioritized agenda and a count for each hypothesis length; skip expansion after k hypotheses of that length have been accepted. Under scores that never increase on extension and h=0, the paper shows that the top completed hypothesis agrees with its specified width-k layer beam, with compatible tie and EOS conventions. Once k hypotheses of a later length have been accepted, earlier unfinished layers can be removed under those same monotonicity assumptions.

Limitation #heuristic-boundary
The paper explicitly limits its beam-output equivalence proof to the zero-heuristic case; an optimistic future-score heuristic can change which paths survive finite-beam pruning.
  • supports Source: Best-First Beam Search · Locator: Section 4.1, A Note on Heuristics, printed p. 801 / PDF p. 7
Application #decoding-context
The method is defined for sequential structured prediction and developed in the paper around natural-language sequence transduction, including translation and abstractive summarization. The decoder is separate from the trained model that supplies scores.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The finite beam is an approximation to exact sequence-model decoding; equality to a specified beam procedure is not global MAP optimality.
  • Sequence length limits, EOS handling and compatible deterministic tie conventions are part of the comparison. Arbitrary implementations with different stopping or tie rules need not return identical hypotheses.
  • Queue counts are counts of retained hypotheses, not total bytes including neural states, model parameters, backpointers and temporary successors. No numerical speedup or benchmark-quality claim is curated.
  • The beam-equivalence claim is restricted to nonincreasing scores and h=0. The paper also asserts a nonmonotonic stop-only extension; this record does not adopt that guarantee because a saved curator finite-tree counterexample exposes a conflict with unchanged per-length pruning.
  • The optimized queue-of-beams representation can retain hypotheses at several lengths. The per-length budget is not a global queue-capacity bound.
