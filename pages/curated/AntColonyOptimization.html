Ant colony optimization

Algorithms AIMech:AntColonyOptimization DRAFT
Aliases: ACO, Ant colony optimization metaheuristic
A metaheuristic framework for optimization in which artificial ants construct solutions using local information and communicate indirectly through shared pheromone information that is adapted from their search experience.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The record denotes the ACO framework defined in the 1999 source, not a single fixed update equation. Ant System, ACS, MAX–MIN and AntNet are individual procedures with additional choices. The source distinguishes strict ACO instantiations from the broader class of ant-inspired algorithms, so all ant algorithms are not treated as synonyms. PBIL’s probability-vector update and other population heuristics share some ideas without becoming identical to ACO. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
Represent the problem through states or solution components, feasible local moves and an objective. Artificial ants incrementally construct a solution or a component of one, maintaining private history as needed for feasibility. Their stochastic decisions use locally available pheromone information and, where configured, problem-specific heuristic information. Search experience changes the shared pheromone representation so later constructions can exploit prior discoveries. The framework schedules ant generation/activity, pheromone evaporation or forgetting, and optional globally informed daemon actions such as extra pheromone updates or local optimization. Deposits can occur during construction, after a solution is evaluated, or through the daemon; the ant’s stepwise and delayed deposit routines can both be absent when the daemon supplies deposits. A concrete algorithm must specify the representation, decision policy, update rule, scheduling and stopping criterion. Sequential or concurrent schedules are permitted by the prose; the framework does not prescribe a universal tau-power formula, inverse-cost deposit, elite rule or trail bound.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • A biological analogy is not a guarantee of optimality, exploration or prevention of stagnation. Positive feedback can concentrate search, and stochastic transitions or evaporation alone do not ensure that every feasible solution remains reachable. Constraints, candidate restrictions, zero weights and finite precision matter.
  • Heuristic information and daemon actions are optional or instance dependent. Source prose permits local optimization, lookahead and other extensions; these are not obligatory baseline operations. Its Figure 3 caption refers to an in_parallel statement while the displayed code uses schedule_activities, so the scheduling prose and explicit instantiation must govern an executable implementation.
  • The framework was synthesized from algorithms with varied representations and update schedules. Membership does not transfer one member’s convergence result or benchmark performance to another. The static/dynamic application examples do not define a universal network-routing or machine-learning algorithm.
  • The paper’s broad PBIL/evolutionary/learning-automata comparisons are conceptual parallels, not evidence of exact algorithm equivalence. No experimental outcomes, fixed best parameterization or full field-wide coverage claim is curated.
