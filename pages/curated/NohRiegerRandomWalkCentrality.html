Noh–Rieger random-walk centrality

Aliases: Random walk centrality of Noh and Rieger
A node-scoring method for an undirected random walk that divides each node’s stationary visitation probability by its integrated excess return probability, yielding a centrality whose reciprocal differences determine pairwise mean first-passage-time asymmetry.

The identity is the centrality functional pi_i divided by the complete integrated excess return probability, with its pairwise mean-passage asymmetry interpretation. Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Scope #walk-domain
The maintained scope is a finite connected simple undirected unweighted graph with an odd cycle and no self-loops. A discrete-time walker at i chooses a neighbor uniformly, so P_ij=A_ij/k_i and stationary probability pi_i=k_i/sum_j k_j. Connectivity and the odd cycle make this finite walk irreducible and aperiodic; disconnected components require separate treatment and no cross-component first-passage comparison is asserted.
  • supports Source: Random Walks on Complex Networks · Locator: Model description, PDF pp. 1–2; Equations 1–4, p. 2; note 21, pp. 4–5
Mechanism #integrated-return-score
Define R_ij=sum_{t=0}^infinity((P^t)_ij-pi_j), tau_i=R_ii and C_i=pi_i/tau_i. The t=0 term is included. On the maintained graph domain the deviation series converges. This score depends on return dynamics as well as degree; it is neither the stationary distribution itself nor a probability distribution over nodes. A single dominant spectral mode is only the source’s relaxation approximation, not the definition of tau_i.
  • supports Source: Random Walks on Complex Networks · Locator: Equations 7 and 10–11, PDF p. 2; spectral approximation and Figure 1 discussion, p. 3
Mechanism #mean-first-passage-asymmetry
For distinct i and j, mean first-passage time H_ij=(R_jj-R_ij)/pi_j. The positive return time to i is H_ii=1/pi_i; it is not the zero-time self-hitting convention. Reversibility yields H_ij-H_ji=1/C_j-1/C_i. Thus C_i>C_j implies a smaller expected passage time from j to i than from i to j. This expectation identity does not order every realized arrival time or establish a probability of winning two independent arrival races.
  • supports Source: Random Walks on Complex Networks · Locator: Equations 3 and 5–11 with the pairwise interpretation, PDF p. 2
Limitation #periodic-walk-boundary
For a connected bipartite graph without self-loops, the original walk has eigenvalue -1 and the ordinary deviation series need not converge. The source states this restriction explicitly in note 21 and suggests changing the walk by adding self-loops. In a curator four-cycle example, the diagonal deviation partial sums alternate between 3/4 and 1/2. A generalized inverse or a modified lazy walk must not silently be identified with the original convergent-series definition.
  • supports Source: Random Walks on Complex Networks · Locator: Note 21, PDF pp. 4–5; explicit four-cycle calculation in research/discovery/noh-rieger-boundaries.py
Limitation #finite-algebraic-checks
Exact curator arithmetic on a triangle gives pi_i=1/3, tau_i=4/9 and C_i=3/4 at every node, with distinct-node mean passage time 2 and positive return time 3. On a triangle with a two-edge tail, two degree-two nodes have centralities 15/67 and 5/29; the corresponding mean passage times are 9 and 23/3, whose difference agrees with the reciprocal-centrality identity. The curator calculation uses R=(I-P+1pi^T)^(-1)-1pi^T and independently solves absorbing-target first-step equations. These finite identities are not a replication of the paper’s network simulations.
  • supports Source: Random Walks on Complex Networks · Locator: Equations 8–11, PDF p. 2; explicit finite matrix interpretation and calculations in research/discovery/noh-rieger-boundaries.py
Limitation #interpretation-and-evaluation-limits
The source interprets the score as relative accessibility under random transport and search. Its extension from pairwise mean-passage asymmetry to an ordering of early ensemble visits is explicitly inexact. The reported experiments use a Barabasi–Albert graph with 10000 nodes for degree/relaxation plots, and 512-node Barabasi–Albert and 625-node hierarchical examples with initially one noninteracting walker per node for visit-fraction comparisons. They do not establish universal degree monotonicity, causal influence, learned predictive performance, directed-graph validity or congestion behavior with interacting walkers.
  • supports Source: Random Walks on Complex Networks · Locator: Figure 1 and the explicitly inexact argument, PDF p. 3; Figure 2 and simulation/extension discussion, pp. 3–4

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The maintained scope is the original unweighted undirected non-bipartite walk. The source’s symmetric-weight extension is noted but not adopted here; directed and interacting-walker extensions remain outside scope.
  • Global return dynamics are required; degree alone and a single spectral relaxation scale do not determine the score in general.
  • The exact statement concerns mean passage times. Early-visit ranking and transport interpretations do not provide a universal arrival-probability, causal or predictive guarantee.
  • The arXiv v2 watermark/version history says March 2004, while the inspected PDF prints November 2024 and has five pages versus four in its metadata. The mismatch remains unresolved; no publisher-byte equivalence is claimed.
  • Finite curator matrix checks are mathematical examples, not author software, raw simulation replication or a new general proof. DRAFT remains.
