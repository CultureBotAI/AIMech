Aliases: BBVI
A variational-inference procedure that estimates evidence-lower-bound gradients from samples of a tractable variational distribution using its score function and model log joint, with mean-field local signals and score control variates to manage estimator variance.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For fixed observed x and joint model p(x,z), choose a variational family q(z;lambda), sample z from q, and maximize L(lambda)=E_q[log p(x,z)-log q(z;lambda)] by stochastic ascent. Under the normalized-score and differentiation-under-expectation conditions, the raw gradient is E_q[grad_lambda log q(z;lambda)*(log p(x,z)-log q(z;lambda))], estimated by a Monte Carlo average. The mean-field version factors q into q_i(z_i;lambda_i) and uses for each factor the model log terms that depend on z_i together with -log q_i; unrelated terms cancel in expectation under the stated factorization. For parameter coordinate d in factor i, let h_d be the score and f_d be that score times the local log signal. A fixed control-variate coefficient gives f_d-a_d*h_d because E[h_d]=0; the population variance-minimizing coefficient is Cov(f_d,h_d)/Var(h_d) when these quantities exist and Var(h_d)>0. The source estimates coefficients from samples and averages corrected contributions before a stochastic-ascent update. Its fixed-coefficient expectation identity does not by itself justify arbitrary reuse of samples to estimate both the coefficient and corrected gradient.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Black box still requires sampling from and evaluating the variational distribution, score derivatives with respect to its parameters, and evaluation of the model log joint at sampled latent states. The method does not require differentiation through a discrete latent sample, but support and integrability conditions remain necessary.
  • Raw unbiasedness requires the regularity assumptions behind differentiation under the expectation and zero expected score. A model normalization constant independent of lambda cancels from this gradient; a parameter-dependent term cannot be discarded on that basis.
  • A coefficient fixed independently of the gradient samples preserves expectation when the relevant moments exist. A coefficient estimated from those same samples can correlate with the sample score and introduce bias. The paper reports separate sample counts for coefficient estimation and gradient calculation, but the inspected description does not establish an exact sampling-independence convention; no implementation behavior is asserted.
  • Population-optimal control-variate scaling and conditional-expectation variance reduction do not guarantee that estimated coefficients or every additional removal of zero-mean terms reduces finite-sample variance. Zero or poorly estimated score variance requires handling beyond division by the displayed ratio.
  • The practical step schedule and change-in-parameters stopping rule do not establish global optimality or exact posterior recovery. Mean-field factorization can limit the approximation. The printed hierarchical subsampling formulas need a separate weighting audit and are not curated as a verified extension here.
  • No benchmark result, author-code run, general convergence proof or posterior-quality certification is included.
