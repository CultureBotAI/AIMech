Basis pursuit

Mathematical Methods AIMech:BasisPursuit DRAFT
A signal-representation optimization principle that minimizes the L1 norm of real synthesis coefficients subject to exact reconstruction of a supplied signal from a fixed finite dictionary.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record selects the equality-constrained, real finite-dictionary principle of Chen, Donoho and Saunders. It is not a particular LP solver, a learned dictionary, generic sparse coding or the penalized denoising extension. BP is a source abbreviation but is not asserted as an unqualified alias. Claims & Grounded Evidence

7 Claims
Mechanism #exact-synthesis-objective
For a supplied real n-by-p matrix Phi whose columns are dictionary atoms and a signal s in its column span, choose a real coefficient vector a minimizing sum_j abs(a_j) subject to Phi*a=s. Overcompleteness is typical but not required. The dictionary stays fixed; a need not equal the analysis coefficients Phi^T*s. If s is outside the column span, the equality-constrained problem is infeasible. The optimization principle does not require or define a neural network.
  • supports Source: Atomic Decomposition by Basis Pursuit · Locator: Sections 2.2 and 3.1, Equations 2.2 and 3.1–3.2, PDF pp. 6,11–13 / printed pp. 134,139–141
Mechanism #linear-program-and-dual
Writing a=u-v with u,v>=0 gives the LP minimize 1^T(u+v) subject to [Phi,-Phi](u,v)=s. Its dual is maximize s^T*z subject to abs(Phi^T*z)<=1 componentwise. A feasible primal and feasible dual with equal objective values certify optimality; complementary signs can additionally constrain active coefficients. These formulas use real coefficients and are algebraic specializations of the source LP, not a claim that signed coefficients are nonnegative.
  • supports Source: Atomic Decomposition by Basis Pursuit · Locator: Sections 3.1 and 6.1, Equations 3.2 and 6.1–6.2, PDF pp. 11–12,24 / printed pp. 139–140,152; curator-derived certificates in research/discovery/basis-pursuit-boundaries.py
Limitation #sparse-basic-solution-boundary
The source uses LP geometry to explain why a feasible finite problem has an optimal basic representation with at most n nonzero signed coefficients. Existence of such an optimum does not make every optimum sparse, establish uniqueness, require exactly n active coefficients, or guarantee minimum nonzero count. Source-specific exact recovery results for particular dictionaries in Section 7.1 require additional hypotheses; their cited external proofs were not independently audited.
  • supports Source: Atomic Decomposition by Basis Pursuit · Locator: Section 3.1.1 and Section 7.1, PDF pp. 12,28 / printed pp. 140,156
Limitation #l1-versus-l0-certificate
A curator-derived unit-column example distinguishes minimum L1 from minimum support. Let Phi=[e1,e2,e3,b], where b=(8/9,4/9,1/9), and s=e1-b. The unique BP coefficients are (1/9,-4/9,-1/9,0), with three nonzeros and L1=2/3. The dual z=(1,-1,-1) has correlations (1,-1,-1,1/3) and value 2/3; its strict fourth constraint forces the fourth coefficient to zero at any optimum. Yet (1,0,0,-1) reconstructs s with two nonzeros and L1=2. No single column is collinear with s, so the minimum support is two. Thus even normalized atoms do not make BP an unconditional minimum-L0 method.
  • supports Source: Atomic Decomposition by Basis Pursuit · Locator: Sections 3.1.1 and 6.1, PDF pp. 12,24 / printed pp. 140,152; curator-derived certificates in research/discovery/basis-pursuit-boundaries.py
Limitation #nonuniqueness-and-scaling
For Phi=[1,1] and s=1, (1,0), (0,1) and (1/2,1/2) all minimize L1 at value 1. The last has more nonzeros than the one-dimensional signal space, so the sparse-basic-solution existence result does not describe every minimizer. Scaling the second atom to 2 changes the preferred representation to (0,1/2), with objective 1/2. These exact curator examples show why coefficient attribution, support and dictionary scaling require care; a fixed unweighted coefficient norm is not invariant to independently rescaling atoms.
  • supports Source: Atomic Decomposition by Basis Pursuit · Locator: Sections 2.1,3.1 and 6.1, PDF pp. 2–6,11–13,24 / printed pp. 130–134,139–141,152; curator-derived certificates in research/discovery/basis-pursuit-boundaries.py
Scope #denoising-boundary
Section 5.1 instead defines basis pursuit denoising by minimizing (1/2)*||y-Phi*a||_2^2+lambda*||a||_1 for lambda>0. That penalized problem generally permits a residual and is outside the exact-equality definition here. For scalar Phi=1,y=1,lambda=1/2 its minimizer is 1/2, while exact BP gives 1. Setting lambda exactly to zero discards the L1 preference; it is not the same operation as selecting the limit of positive-penalty optima. Any statement that the residual tends to zero also needs an exact representation to be feasible.
  • supports Source: Atomic Decomposition by Basis Pursuit · Locator: Sections 5.1–5.2, Equations 5.1 and 5.3, PDF pp. 19–20 / printed pp. 147–148; curator-derived certificates in research/discovery/basis-pursuit-boundaries.py
Scope #principle-versus-numerical-implementation
Section 3.1.2 distinguishes the optimization principle from simplex and interior-point implementations. The paper develops a primal-dual log-barrier implementation using approximate conjugate-gradient or LSQR linear solves for dictionaries with fast synthesis and analysis products. Its perturbed programs and finite tolerances approximate the exact problem; nonzero quadratic or residual regularization is not silently an exact BP solution. Runtime depends on dictionary structure, signal and requested accuracy, and the paper explicitly declines to guarantee reaching the optimum in reasonable time. Selected synthetic examples and historical Sun SPARC20 timings do not establish universal sparsity, superresolution, denoising superiority or a nearly-linear exact-solver bound.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The record is limited to real finite dictionaries and exact equality. Complex coefficients, analysis sparsity, weighted/group norms, noisy constrained forms and dictionary-learning algorithms need their own evidence and identity decisions.
  • Optimal L1 need not recover the fewest atoms or a unique representation. Sparse exact-recovery theorems require additional dictionary/signal assumptions; their external proofs were not audited.
  • All 31 pages of the 2001 reprint were visually read. External primary sources, the 1995 precursor and the separate 1998 version remain uninspected in full. The image example in Section 7.2 is expressly a modified BP-inspired method, not validation of this exact implementation.
  • Exact rational examples are curator calculations, not historical experiments. No optimizer, learned model, benchmark or paid provider was run. The record remains DRAFT and has no independent scientific approval.
