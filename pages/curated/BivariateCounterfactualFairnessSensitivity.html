Bivariate counterfactual-fairness sensitivity analysis

Evaluation Methods AIMech:BivariateCounterfactualFairnessSensitivity DRAFT
Aliases: Kilbertus grid-based counterfactual-fairness sensitivity
A model-based sensitivity procedure that varies the correlation between two structural error terms and measures how a predictor built under an unconfounded additive-noise model changes on counterfactual data generated under the alternative confounded model.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the paper’s two-error grid-based sensitivity procedure. The mean squared discrepancy is its output, not another fairness criterion. The multivariate MAXCFU optimizer is separately represented because it searches a matrix of correlations with a different algorithm. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method-scope
Fit an unconfounded working model and a predictor of its residuals. At a selected error correlation, fit an alternative additive-noise model, retain its inferred errors while changing the protected attribute, and propagate the intervention through the causal ordering. Recompute residuals of these counterfactual features using the original working-model coefficients, apply the fixed original predictor and average squared prediction differences. Repeat across correlations to obtain a sensitivity curve.
  • supports Source: The Sensitivity of Counterfactual Fairness to Unmeasured Confounding · Locator: Section 3, Tool #1: grid-based; Sections 3.1–3.4, equations (2)–(6), PDF pp. 3–6; Section 5, PDF p. 8 for grid use

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Alternative Model B is a hypothetical data-generating model, not an identified causal ground truth; the sensitivity parameter and acceptable discrepancy require domain judgment.
  • The inspected construction uses specified additive-noise structural functions, embeddings and Gaussian error covariance, rather than arbitrary hidden confounding.
  • Fitting, covariance invertibility and numerical grid resolution matter. The record does not certify a global maximum over all admissible models or extrapolate the source’s bivariate boundary statement to multiple error terms.
  • Squared prediction differences depend on the prediction scale and assessed sample; they are not automatically calibrated probabilities of discrimination or a universal fairness score.
