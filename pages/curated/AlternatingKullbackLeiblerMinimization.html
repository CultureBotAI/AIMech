Alternating Kullback-Leibler Minimization

Algorithms AIMech:AlternatingKullbackLeiblerMinimization DRAFT
Aliases: Alternating I-Divergence Minimization, Alternating Information Projection
A paired-distribution optimization procedure that alternately minimizes Kullback-Leibler divergence in its first and second arguments over specified convex probability families.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Scoped to the exact two-family KL procedure analyzed by Csiszar and Tusnady. The information-projection alias is qualified here by KL direction and the two-family objective. It is not all block coordinate descent, arbitrary Bregman projection, or the later GAM framework permitting inexact parameter/distribution steps. Iterative proportional fitting and Sinkhorn scaling have their own margin constraints; sharing a projection interpretation does not identify the complete algorithms. Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #two-directed-minima
Start with Q_0 in the second family. At each iteration choose P_n minimizing D(P||Q_(n-1)) over the first family, then Q_n minimizing D(P_n||Q) over the second. For probability laws, D(P||Q)=integral log(dP/dQ)dP, with infinity when absolute continuity fails. The two coordinate projections have different directions. Each displayed argmin must exist and be finite along the realized sequence; this is not supplied by the algorithm name. Exact steps cannot increase the paired objective.
  • supports Source: Information Geometry and Alternating Minimization Procedures · Locator: Section1 Eq1.1, pp205-208 / PDF1-4; Section3 Eq3.1 and Lemmas2-3, pp214-218 / PDF10-14
Scope #accessible-infimum
The convex-family KL construction has the three-point and four-point inequalities used to prove a five-point descent inequality. The objective tends to the infimum over the initialization-accessible first-family laws: those with finite D(P||Q_n) for some iterate. Equality of that infimum with the unrestricted one is an additional support/accessibility condition. Objective convergence is distinct from convergence of the distributions. In finite probability spaces, closed families are compact; analogous arguments for unnormalized measures need their own boundedness or compactness justification.
  • supports Source: Information Geometry and Alternating Minimization Procedures · Locator: Sections1-2, Theorems1-2, pp207-213 / PDF3-9; Section3 Theorem3 and subsequent proof, pp217-218 / PDF13-14
Limitation #finite-measure-extension
The paper also uses uncorrected D(P||Q) for finite measures, which can be negative when their masses differ. Its nonnegative auxiliary is D(P||Pprime)+Pprime(X)-P(X). That extension must not be silently treated as probability KL. The stopping-bound step that integrates log(dQ_n/dQ_(n-1)) against a minimizing P requires its total-mass factor unless P is normalized. A convergent Q sequence alone also does not force successive density ratios to approach one at coordinates whose limit is zero. The saved finite examples exhibit both issues.
  • supports Source: Information Geometry and Alternating Minimization Procedures · Locator: Section3 Eq3.1-3.3 and Eq3.11 discussion, pp214-219 / PDF10-15; curator finite-measure scaling and vanishing-coordinate examples
Scope #likelihood-lifting
For incomplete-data fitting, a family of complete laws with prescribed observed marginal can be paired with a model-distribution family. Projection onto the prescribed marginal rescales the current joint law by the observed-to-current marginal ratio; the other projection fits the model to the completed law. A convex model-distribution family can inherit the global accessible-infimum result. Jointly parameterized nonconvex mixture or neural families do not become convex merely because they use EM.
  • supports Source: Information Geometry and Alternating Minimization Procedures · Locator: Section4 Theorem4 and Eq4.1-4.6, pp220-221 / PDF16-17; Section5A, pp226-230 / PDF22-26

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The maintained identity uses probability families; finite-measure formulas in the source are explicitly separated. On infinite spaces, positivity alone does not ensure finite KL or compactness.
  • The unbounded off-support Q example challenges a compactness step in the finite-measure proof, not the theorem for closed finite probability families. No blanket theorem refutation is asserted.
  • Finite curator calculations check selected support, normalization and convergence boundaries. They are not author software, historical experiments or a proof of every source theorem.
