Aliases: PoE, Products of experts
A probabilistic model family that combines expert factors over the same observations by multiplying their values and normalizing the product over the observation space.

Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes the general normalized-product construction, including latent-variable experts, rather than an arithmetic mixture, a training algorithm or a language-model-specific factorization. The original cooperative-learning setting does not require experts to be separately trained. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For observations x, form p(x)=product_m f_m(x;theta_m)/Z(theta), with Z the sum or integral of the product over x and 0<Z<infinity. Expert factors may be unnormalized; multiplying one by a positive x-independent constant cancels in the final normalization. For parameters local to expert m, the exact log-likelihood derivative is its log-factor derivative at the data minus the expectation of that derivative under the full product model. In the latent-expert construction, f_m(x)=sum_hm f_m(x,h_m), and separate expert hidden variables are conditionally independent given a completely observed x, allowing separate inference when each expert is tractable. This does not imply independence of observed coordinates or independence of experts after some observations are marginalized. Restricted Boltzmann machines instantiate the family with one expert per hidden unit, whereas product HMMs use complete HMM sequence distributions as factors.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The normalizer must be finite and positive. Continuous expert densities being individually normalized does not alone guarantee that their product is integrable; nonoverlapping supports can also make normalization impossible.
  • Tractable inference within each expert does not make the product normalizer, model sampling or likelihood learning automatically tractable. Exact likelihood derivatives involve the full product distribution; short-chain CD is a distinct approximate learning procedure.
  • Experts need not use disjoint input dimensions, be individually well fitted, or produce a sharper or better calibrated distribution in every setting. A product of Gaussian factors remains Gaussian when well defined, so Gaussian factors alone do not represent arbitrary multimodality.
  • Conditional independence is between separate latent expert states given all modeled observations. Missing observations can couple the experts. The original architecture is not defined by a sparse router or an arithmetic average of expert predictions.
  • Unit exponent products and geometric opinion averages are not interchangeable: multiplying duplicate factors can sharpen a distribution, while identical distributions pooled with exponent weights summing to one remain unchanged. No empirical advantage or author implementation is certified.
