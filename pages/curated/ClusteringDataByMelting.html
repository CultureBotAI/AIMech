Clustering Data by Melting

Algorithms AIMech:ClusteringDataByMelting DRAFT
Aliases: Melting Algorithm for Clustering
A scale-dependent clustering procedure that heats Gaussian-weighted center iterations to build a merging membership tree, then selects clusters by persistence of their within-cluster weight fraction across logarithmic scales.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes Wong’s complete melting procedure plus fractional-free-energy persistence selection. At one inverse temperature, the center update is the Gaussian mean-shift weighted mean; the outer heating path, original-point membership tree and selection stage distinguish the named algorithm. Weights are normalized over original observations, not mixture components. Cluster independence means separate center updates using the same data, not statistical independence of clusters. Claims & Grounded Evidence

4 Claims
Mechanism #weighted-center-heating
For each center y and beta>0, assign original points weights P_y,beta(x)=exp(-beta*||x-y||^2)/sum_u exp(-beta*||u-y||^2). Iterate y_new=sum_x x*P_y,beta(x) at fixed beta. Start centers at observations at a sufficiently high initial beta, retain their point memberships, track converged centers, and combine memberships when centers coalesce. Decrease beta and warm-start the retained centers; the printed schedule divides beta by1.05 and uses200inner iterations in demonstrations. The weighting set remains the original observations, not an unweighted replacement by merged centers.
  • supports Source: Clustering Data by Melting · Locator: Section2 Eq2.1-2.5, pp90-91 / PDF2-3; Section3 Eq3.1 and Melting Procedure, pp92-94 / PDF4-6
Mechanism #persistence-selection
For nominal cluster Q at its tracked center, fractional free energy M_Q(beta)=sum_(x in Q)P_y,beta(x) is a membership weight fraction. Choose a threshold M_T; a cluster is good where its fraction exceeds that threshold, and robustness is the corresponding range of logarithmic scales. Greedily select the most robust good cluster, remove its ancestors and descendants from consideration, and repeat. Observations not covered by selected clusters remain in a leftover set; complete coverage is hoped for in the source, not guaranteed. Threshold choice and tie handling affect the result.
  • supports Source: Clustering Data by Melting · Locator: Section4 Eq4.1, Definition2 and Melting Algorithm steps1-6, pp95-98 / PDF7-10
Limitation #fixed-points-and-rate
Stationary centers satisfy the weighted-mean equation for F=-log Z/beta, but not every fixed point is a local minimum or exponentially attracting. For points{-1,+1}, the update is tanh(2*beta*y): at beta=1, y=0 stays at an unstable free-energy maximum if initialized exactly there; at beta=0.5, the critical map tanh(y) has derivative one and no uniform local geometric rate. The one-dimensional bifurcation condition2*beta*weighted_variance=1 does not establish higher-dimensional global cluster recovery.
  • supports Source: Clustering Data by Melting · Locator: Section3 fixed-point and convergence prose, pp92-95 / PDF4-7, Eq3.2 and Figure3; Section6 rate discussion, p101 / PDF13; curator finite/algebraic examples
Limitation #implementation-and-empirical-limits
The printed merge step says more than two coincident clusters, while the surrounding prose describes two-cluster mergers; this record uses the intended two-or-more interpretation explicitly. Exact coincidence tolerance, robust tie policy and a universally adequate initial scale are not specified. Finite inner iteration does not certify convergence or finite exact tree completion. Selected synthetic and SAR demonstrations do not establish universal density/shape insensitivity, semantic cluster-count recovery or superiority to ISODATA under arbitrary initialization.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • High initial beta is a data-dependent choice. Euclidean rotation invariance does not guarantee recovery of arbitrary anisotropic clusters. The method can be costly because each center uses all original points at many scales.
  • The1992thesis, cited SAR original studies and Tam implementation report remain uninspected. Suggested continuation, grouping and adaptive-step speedups are not certified implementations here.
  • No historical software, benchmark or source experiment was reproduced. Saved normalized finite calculations challenge bounded formula/prose claims, not the complete conditional convergence theorems.
