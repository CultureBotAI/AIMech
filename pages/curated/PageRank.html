PageRank

Algorithms AIMech:PageRank DRAFT
Aliases: PageRank citation ranking
A link-analysis ranking algorithm that assigns node scores using a random walk mixing normalized outgoing-link transitions with teleportation to a specified restart distribution, under an explicit policy for nodes with no outgoing weight.

Claims & Grounded Evidence

6 Claims
Mechanism #transition-construction
For a finite nonempty graph with nonnegative edge weights, normalize every positive-outweight row of its adjacency matrix. Replace a zero-outweight row by a specified probability vector q, obtaining row-stochastic P. With restart probability vector v and 0 <= alpha < 1, the selected normalized formulation is the row-vector equation p = alpha p P + (1-alpha) v. Scores sum to one. NetworkX 3.5 defaults q to v and v to the uniform distribution; these explicit conventions do not assert that every historical implementation used them.

Scope #personalization-and-ranking
Uniform restart gives the unpersonalized case; changing the restart distribution gives personalized PageRank within this family. Once the graph, weights, damping and restart policy are fixed, the ranking does not require query text. A retrieval system can filter candidates or combine this score with query-dependent signals. Recursive importance is not simply indegree, alternating HITS authority/hub scores, a cluster assignment or a language model.

Mechanism #iteration-and-convergence
The inspected NetworkX 3.5 public function delegates to a sparse power iteration that propagates link mass, redistributes dangling mass, adds restart mass and stops when the L1 change is below N times tol, or raises after max_iter. For the stated probability domain, the affine update is an L1 contraction with factor at most alpha, so it has a unique fixed point and converges in exact arithmetic even when P is reducible or v contains zeros. This is curator mathematical qualification of the documentation’s stronger irreducibility wording, not a promise that a finite iteration cap meets every tolerance. Alpha equal to one is outside this contraction claim; a two-node cycle can oscillate.
  • supports Source: NetworkX3.5 PageRank implementation and embedded documentation · Locator: public dispatch lines 110–112; google_matrix notes lines 218–225; sparse iteration lines 455–499; curator L1 contraction argument and finite reducible/periodic cases in research/discovery/pagerank-boundaries.py
Limitation #historical-normalization
The Brin–Page paper prints a per-node baseline 1-d while also describing total probability one. On a non-dangling N-node stochastic graph that recurrence instead has total N; unit normalization uses (1-d)/N or divides scores by N. Its random-surfer prose also describes d as the jump probability although the equation uses d on link contributions. The January 1998 report presents additional matrix-orientation and rank-source scaling ambiguities. The normalized equation in this record follows the explicitly versioned implementation convention; the early printed formulas are not silently treated as identical specifications.

Limitation #dangling-policy-boundary
The January 1998 report describes removing links to dangling pages, repeating this removal and reinserting them after the core calculation. The selected modern formulation instead redistributes dangling mass according to q during every update. Their equivalence is not asserted. On a constructed graph with only the link 0 to 1, alpha=1/2 and uniform restart, uniform dangling redistribution yields scores (2/5,3/5), while redistribution to node 0 yields (1/2,1/2). Omitting redistribution entirely gives a leaky fixed vector with total mass 5/8 under the same baseline.

Limitation #evidence-and-interpretation
The primary papers provide historical search examples, graph-dependent computation observations and exploratory usage comparisons. These do not establish that link rank measures factual correctness, calibrated human attention or universal relevance. The January report acknowledges incomplete and biased usage and link data; its optimistic manipulation-resistance discussion is not a guarantee. The Google system combines PageRank with other ranking signals, so its qualitative search results are not an isolated evaluation of this algorithm.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The record selects an explicit damped probability formulation supported by NetworkX 3.5 and preserves differences from the early papers. It does not claim byte-level or behavioral identity across all PageRank implementations.
  • The graph, edge weights, damping, restart and dangling policy affect scores. The probability/contraction claims assume nonnegative normalized inputs and alpha below one; the inspected implementation does not enforce every mathematical precondition.
  • Personalization is represented as a parameterization of this family. Topic-sensitive combinations, scalable personalized solvers and other downstream algorithms require their own evidence and identity assessment.
  • Exact finite substitutions verify the stated examples, not author code, historical experiments or a general finite-precision error guarantee. A successive-iterate tolerance is not itself a universal ranking-accuracy guarantee.
  • The inspected January 29, 1998 mirror differs in version from the inaccessible 1999-66 report. All 17 mirrored pages and the complete 11-page companion paper were read; no unseen version or absent Appendix A screenshot is claimed inspected.
  • The pinned implementation module was read but not executed. No original author code, live crawl, training, paid service, upload or historical benchmark was run. Records remain DRAFT after author self-review.
