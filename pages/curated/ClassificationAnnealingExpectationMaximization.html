Classification Annealing Expectation Maximization

Algorithms AIMech:ClassificationAnnealingExpectationMaximization DRAFT
Aliases: Classification Annealing EM, CAEM
A stochastic hard-clustering algorithm that samples component assignments from current mixture posterior probabilities raised to an inverse positive temperature, maximizes the completed-data classification likelihood, and lowers the temperature according to a chosen schedule.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The Celeux-Govaert CAEM construction changes the random label distribution within classification EM. It differs from deterministic tempered EM, decreasing-step sufficient-statistic SAEM, and Metropolis simulated annealing with an accept/reject transition. Temperature one recovers the SEM assignment law; the low-temperature limit concentrates on maximal posterior components but retains random choice among ties. 3 Claims
Mechanism #powered-posterior
At temperature tau_m>0, set s_ik proportional to [p_k f_k(x_i)]^(1/tau_m), equivalently t_ik^(1/tau_m), and normalize across components. Draw a hard label independently for each observation from s_i and maximize the corresponding completed-data likelihood. The power applies to the entire weight-times-density product. The next iteration uses the updated parameters and scheduled temperature; it is not a fractional-responsibility M-step or a Metropolis acceptance test.
  • supports Source: A classification EM algorithm for clustering and two stochastic versions · Locator: Section 4.2 Eq4.6, p.11 / PDF13; Section 3 M-step, p.5 / PDF7
Scope #endpoint-and-ties
At tau=1, the assignment law equals SEM. Holding positive component scores fixed while tau approaches zero concentrates mass on maximal scores; with multiple tied maxima, their limiting probabilities are equal rather than the smallest-index tie choice specified for deterministic CEM. Literal division by tau=0 is undefined. This fixed-score limit does not establish that a changing-parameter cooling run converges to a global solution.
  • supports Source: A classification EM algorithm for clustering and two stochastic versions · Locator: Section 4.2 Eq4.6 and SEM/CEM interpolation discussion, p.11 / PDF13; Section 3 tie rule, p.5 / PDF7; curator temperature and tie calculations
Limitation #schedule-and-stopping
The report gives geometric cooling tau_(m+1)=a*tau_m as a practical choice and explicitly says its analogous CAEM convergence result has not yet been proved. The printed range includes a=1, which does not cool. Its experiments use a=0.97 and stop when a partition is unchanged; an unchanged stochastic partition over a finite comparison is not proof of future absorption. Neither a geometric schedule nor the simulated-annealing analogy supplies a global-convergence guarantee.
  • supports Source: A classification EM algorithm for clustering and two stochastic versions · Locator: Section 4.2 cooling and convergence discussion, p.12 / PDF14; Section 5 experimental settings, pp.12-13 / PDF14-15

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Positive temperature does not repair zero component scores, empty sampled classes or inadmissible completed-data fits. These require the selected model and explicit restart, constraint or deletion rules; such changes alter the procedure.
  • The source’s informal SAEM blend on p.12 repeats EM in both terms despite referring to a stochastic contribution. The unseen original annealed-SAEM method is not inferred from that printed expression or merged with CAEM.
  • The 1991 report’s empirical settings and tables do not establish an optimal schedule, a universal ranking, statistical uncertainty calibration or semantic correctness of clusters. The final 1992 journal full text and author code were not obtained.
