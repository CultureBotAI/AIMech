Pseudo-Label

Algorithms AIMech:PseudoLabel DRAFT
A semi-supervised neural-network training procedure that recomputes hard labels from the model’s most probable class for unlabeled examples and jointly fits observed and generated targets using a scheduled weight on the unlabeled loss.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope This record denotes Lee’s 2013 named training method and its explicitly described sigmoid-loss configuration. Generic pseudo-labeling also includes other target generators and iterative procedures; DeepCluster’s cluster assignments, SeLa’s balanced transport and FixMatch’s weak-to-strong confidence filter are distinct methods. The method’s entropy-minimization motivation does not establish an identical objective to Shannon entropy minimization. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #method
At each weights update, assign each unlabeled example a one-hot pseudo-target for its argmax predicted class. Treat that chosen target as a label for the update and train the same network on both observed labeled examples and pseudo-labeled examples. The original paper’s Equation15 averages the class-summed supervised loss over the labeled minibatch of size n and separately over the unlabeled minibatch of size n_prime, with multiplier alpha(t) on the latter. The represented original configuration uses independent sigmoid output units and sums binary cross-entropy over classes, as defined in Equation5; it is not silently rewritten as softmax categorical cross-entropy. Equation16 sets alpha(t)=0 before T1, increases it linearly to alpha_f between T1 and T2, and keeps it at alpha_f thereafter. The paper uses Dropout during this training and studies both optional denoising-autoencoder pretraining and training without it. The hard labels are recalculated at each weights update rather than supplied by a separate EMA teacher. The displayed Pseudo-Label rule has no confidence-threshold rejection step.

Limitation #entropy-boundary
Equations5 and15 use hard pseudo-target loss, while Equation17 defines a different entropy expression. For normalized two-class outputs(0.8,0.2) and pseudo-target(1,0), the class-summed binary cross-entropy is−2log(0.8), whereas the printed entropy is−0.8log(0.8)−0.2log(0.2). Their unequal values refute literal objective equality in this example; this finite algebra does not refute the paper’s qualitative confidence-sharpening motivation.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Wrong hard targets can reinforce early prediction errors; the ramp and low-density-separation motivation do not guarantee target correctness or improved generalization.
  • The original paper’s independent sigmoid outputs need not form a normalized class-probability vector. Its classwise binary loss is distinct from later softmax-based reimplementations.
  • The paper describes equivalence to entropy regularization, but its hard-target loss and printed entropy expression are not algebraically equal. This record preserves the shared low-entropy motivation without asserting objective equality.
  • Specific ramp endpoints, final weight, network widths, Dropout rates and pretraining schedules are reported experimental choices, not universal constants. The paper does not specify an argmax tie-breaking policy.
  • The inspected primary paper is a public third-party-hosted copy with an exact hash. Title, author and workshop identity agree with the indexed original and author-linked citation; original-host byte equality and an exact revision date were not established.
