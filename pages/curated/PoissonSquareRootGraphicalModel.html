Poisson square root graphical model

Model Families AIMech:PoissonSquareRootGraphicalModel DRAFT
Aliases: Poisson SQR, Poisson square root Markov random field
A square-root graphical model on unbounded nonnegative integer counts with factorial base measures, unary square-root and linear count terms and pairwise products of square roots of counts, normalizable for all finite symmetric interaction and unary parameters on a finite graph.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope Poisson SQR is the Poisson-base specialization of the SQR construction. It preserves the factorial count base measure and changes the interaction statistic to square roots. It is distinct from the classical auto-Poisson field, finite TPGM, factorial-free QPGM and bounded-statistic SPGM. Taking square roots of observed counts and fitting a Gaussian is a different model: the sample space, base measure and conditional law must be retained. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For X_i in {0,1,...}, a finite symmetric matrix Phi and finite theta, define p(x)=Z^(-1)*exp[sum_i theta_i*sqrt(x_i)+sum_i Phi_ii*x_i+2*sum_(i<j) Phi_ij*sqrt(x_i*x_j)-sum_i log(x_i!)]. The full conditional at i is proportional to exp[Phi_ii*k+eta_i*sqrt(k)]/k!, k>=0, where eta_i=theta_i+2*sum_(j!=i) Phi_ij*sqrt(x_j). All finite parameters are admissible on a finite graph. To see this directly, use 2*sqrt(x_i*x_j)<=x_i+x_j and sqrt(x_i)<=1+x_i to bound the parameter-dependent exponent by C+sum_i d_i*x_i for finite C,d_i. The joint kernel is then bounded by exp(C)*product_i exp(d_i*x_i)/x_i!, whose sum is exp(C+sum_i exp(d_i)). For theta=0 and diagonal Phi, the independent Poisson means are exp(Phi_ii). For nonzero eta_i the conditional is generally not an ordinary Poisson law.

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The factorial base measure is essential to the unrestricted finite-parameter normalization result. Removing it or changing support requires a new validity analysis. No statement about infinite graphs or unbounded parameter limits follows.
  • The conditional normalizer is generally an infinite sum, and existence alone does not certify any cutoff or numerical approximation. exp(eta_i) is not its general conditional mean.
  • The direct factorial-domination argument avoids treating the paper’s directional summations as a complete change-of-measure proof. It is a curator derivation consistent with the stated finite-parameter model, not an empirical reproduction.
  • The graph is finite and Phi is symmetric; an off-diagonal matrix entry contributes twice in the quadratic form. Separately estimated node regressions need not agree on one symmetric joint parameterization.
  • The node conditionals usually have two sufficient statistics and differ from the original univariate base family. A base-family name is not a claim about every conditional or marginal distribution, and an edge parameter is not a universal marginal-correlation or causal-effect statement.
  • The printed radial formulas omit the Jacobian needed to interpret x=z*v as a change of continuous coordinates. In p dimensions the L1-radius transformation contributes z^(p-1); a bare ray kernel is not generally the radius distribution. Discrete directions require a counting-measure treatment. The normalization arguments stated here use direct joint domination instead.
  • The paper proposes nodewise l1-penalized fitting, numerical conditional partitions and annealed importance sampling. Model existence does not establish finite-data graph recovery, sampler mixing, numerical accuracy or optimizer convergence; no author code, empirical fitting or benchmark was reproduced.
  • Reported synthetic and airport-delay experiments are contextual. The paper’s synthetic description prints a positive diagonal while calling the matrix negative definite, an inconsistent sign convention that must be resolved before reproducing that generator. Airport associations and fitted likelihoods do not establish causal effects or held-out predictive performance.
