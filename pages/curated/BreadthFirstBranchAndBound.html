Breadth-first branch-and-bound search

Algorithms AIMech:BreadthFirstBranchAndBound DRAFT
Aliases: BFBnB
A heuristic graph-search procedure that expands states in breadth-first layers and prunes candidate paths whose accumulated cost plus an admissible remaining-cost estimate exceeds a supplied solution-cost bound; the formulation curated here assumes uniform positive edge costs, scaled to one.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The BFBnB search strategy is distinguished from BFIDA*, which obtains bounds by repeated searches, and from width-pruned beam search. Breadth-first heuristic search names the broader family in Zhou and Hansen; that umbrella phrase is not asserted as an exact alias. Frontier, sparse-memory and layered duplicate-detection implementations are alternatives, not mandatory simultaneous components. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #search-procedure
For a unit-cost graph, process nodes by increasing depth g and evaluate f=g+h with admissible h. Under a hypothetical bound U, retain f≤U. If U is the cost of an actual retained incumbent and only improvement is needed, f≥U can instead be pruned; failure to improve then preserves the incumbent. Memory-saving implementations retain enough information for duplicate detection and use relay nodes to reconstruct a found path by recursively solving subproblems.

Limitation #resource-scope
The authors consider frontier, sparse-memory and layered duplicate handling, and relay-based path recovery. The potential reduction in stored search nodes is problem dependent; neither a fixed total-memory bound nor a universal advantage over A* follows. Nonuniform-cost extensions are outlined as future work.
  • supports Source: Breadth-first heuristic search · Locator: Sections 2.2–3.4, 6.1–6.2; printed pp. 387–392 and 405–406 / PDF pp. 3–8 and 21–22

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • The scope is uniform positive costs and an admissible heuristic. A valid upper bound must cover a real solution for a single-pass optimal-solution guarantee; an unsuccessful hypothetical contour does not establish that the original problem is unsolvable.
  • The layered implementation needs enough previous-layer information for the graph structure; retaining one previous layer is not a universal duplicate-elimination rule for directed graphs.
  • Memory-saving implementations can still run out of memory in a large frontier. The paper explicitly does not prove universally lower peak memory than best-first search.
  • The f≥U option is valid for improving an actual incumbent, not for an inclusive hypothetical contour with no stored solution. Preserving the incumbent when no improvement exists is an explicit operational consequence of that distinction.
  • The record does not certify the printed pseudocode for all base cases, graph-locality claims under every pruning policy, or nonuniform-cost extensions.
