RandAugment

Algorithms AIMech:RandAugment DRAFT
An image-augmentation procedure that applies a uniformly sampled sequence of operations sharing one magnitude parameter, reducing augmentation selection to tuning the operation count and shared magnitude on the target task.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope RandAugment is distinct from the learned operation probabilities and per-operation magnitudes of AutoAugment, the feedback-adapted parameter weights of CTAugment, and a particular library’s later RandAugment variant. Reducing the search to N and M does not eliminate hyperparameter tuning or make the procedure parameter-free. Claims & Grounded Evidence

1 Claim
Mechanism #method
For each training image, sample N operations uniformly with replacement from the selected transformation set and apply them in the sampled order, using the same magnitude parameter M with each operation’s own magnitude scale. The paper’s Figure3 uses 14 operations, including identity, geometric and appearance transformations, and permits repeated choices. The sequence therefore has K^N possible operation choices for a K-operation set, rather than sampling N distinct operations. Tune N and M using target-task validation, for example with the reported simple grid search, instead of a separate proxy-task controller search. The source also investigates different schedules for the shared magnitude. Its nominal magnitude scale and exploratory ranges are protocol choices; a shared M does not mean the same physical transformation strength across operation types.
  • supports Source: RandAugment: Practical Automated Data Augmentation with a Reduced Search Space · Locator: Section 4 and Figure 3, PDF/printed pp. 5–6; target-task search context in Sections 3 and 5

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Uniformly sampled transformations still require a suitable operation set and magnitudes for the data; label preservation is an assumption.
  • N and M are hyperparameters, and the paper’s absence of a separate proxy search is not an absence of validation or model training.
  • The source explores magnitude schedules and ranges beyond the nominal scale; no one schedule, numerical range or claimed optimal value is made universal.
  • No benchmark reproduction or general accuracy/robustness guarantee is asserted.
