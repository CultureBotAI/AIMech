Dijkstra shortest-path algorithm

Algorithms AIMech:DijkstraShortestPath DRAFT
Aliases: Dijkstra's algorithm, Dijkstra shortest path
A shortest-path algorithm for nonnegative edge weights that repeatedly settles the unsettled vertex with the smallest tentative distance from a source and relaxes its outgoing edges.
Identity Notes & Concept Scope The shortest-path method in Problem 2 of Dijkstra’s 1959 paper is curated here. Problem 1 in the same paper constructs a minimum spanning tree and is a distinct algorithm. A shortest-path tree need not be a minimum spanning tree; cost-prioritized search is distinct from breadth-first edge-count traversal and heuristic A-star search. Claims & Grounded Evidence

2 Claims
Mechanism #search-procedure
Maintain settled vertices and tentative source distances. A shorter route through the most recently settled vertex replaces a tentative distance and predecessor; the next vertex settled is the one with minimum tentative source distance. For a specified destination, the original method stops when that destination is settled.
  • supports Source: A Note on Two Problems in Connexion with Graphs · Locator: Problem 2, printed pp. 270–271 / PDF pp. 2–3, Steps 1–2 and Remarks 1–2; connected-graph setup on p. 269 / PDF p. 1
Scope #weights-and-output
The versioned implementation applies to directed or undirected graphs with nonnegative weights, maintains tentative labels in a minimum-priority queue, and returns source distances or predecessor data. Its shortest-path tree is not guaranteed to be a minimum spanning tree.
  • supports Source: Boost Graph Library: Dijkstra’s Shortest Paths · Locator: Algorithm description and DIJKSTRA pseudocode; weight_map, predecessor_map and distance_map parameters; Notes [1]

⚠️ Curated Limitations & Negative Results

  • Nonnegative edge weights are a precondition of the standard method; the record does not extend its settling rule to graphs with negative edges.
  • The 1959 presentation assumes a connected graph. The cited implementation documents source-unreachable vertices separately; reaching an arbitrary node does not establish a path to every node.
  • No particular heap implementation, asymptotic runtime, or universal advantage over other shortest-path algorithms is asserted.
